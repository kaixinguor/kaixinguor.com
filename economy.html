US October CPI rises 0.4% m/m, above forecasts: markets price a December hike at 100%

US October CPI was released before the market open on November 10: consumer prices rose 0.4% from a month earlier and core CPI rose 0.3%, both 0.1 percentage point above consensus, after 0.6% and 0.3% in September. Because last October's federal shutdown prevented price collection, the report carried no annual inflation figures for CPI or core CPI.

After the release, traders kept the probability of a December Fed hike at 100%. Yields on two- to five-year Treasuries rose at least 10 basis points, the dollar index jumped and gold and silver fell. Ahead of the open, Dow futures were down 0.10%, S&P 500 futures 0.70% and Nasdaq 100 futures 1.34%.

Why it mattersA 0.4% monthly gain is roughly a 5% annualised pace, more than double the Fed's 2% target, and core inflation matching September's 0.3% shows price pressure is no longer confined to energy. With a December hike fully priced, the Fed's reaction function is effectively locked: only a sharp deterioration in jobs or financial conditions can stop it. For renminbi assets, a firmer dollar and higher two- to five-year Treasury yields hit the exchange rate first and valuation multiples second, while narrowing the room for further domestic easing.

Alibaba to spin off T-Head and list it in Hong Kong after a confidential A1 in September

Alibaba said in a Hong Kong Stock Exchange announcement on November 10 that it plans to spin off wholly owned subsidiary T-Head (Shanghai) Technology Co Ltd and list it separately on the main board of the Hong Kong exchange. The exchange has confirmed the spin-off can proceed; T-Head filed a confidential A1 application on September 11, 2026.

The spin-off is to be carried out through a global offering of T-Head H shares, including a Hong Kong public offer and a placing to institutional and professional investors. T-Head is expected to remain a subsidiary of Alibaba after the deal. The size and structure of the offering and the adjustment to Alibaba's stake have not been determined. Alibaba described T-Head as one of China's leading full-stack data-centre chip suppliers whose chips provide system-level computing solutions. The deal still requires approval from the exchange's listing committee, filing with the China Securities Regulatory Commission, and final decisions by Alibaba and T-Head.

Why it mattersThe spin-off turns an internal cost centre into a standalone balance sheet. Chip development is long-cycle and capital-intensive, and inside an e-commerce group it is valued at a discount; as a listed company T-Head can be priced like a semiconductor firm and raise equity, while Alibaba keeps control and consolidation. For Hong Kong, it creates a rare full-stack data-centre chip listing and a pricing anchor for other hard-tech spin-offs. The risks are approval and dilution: the A1 is only an application, and approval, the CSRC filing, deal size and stake are all still open.
