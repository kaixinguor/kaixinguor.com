10 月 PMI 49.3%：生产与新订单同时回落，价格指数仍在扩张区间

10 月 31 日 9 时 30 分，国家统计局服务业调查中心与中国物流与采购联合会发布 10 月中国采购经理指数。制造业 PMI 49.3%，比上月下降 0.8 个百分点；非制造业商务活动指数 49.7%，下降 0.5 个百分点；综合 PMI 产出指数 49.7%，下降 1.0 个百分点。

分项看，制造业生产指数 49.8%、新订单指数 49.4%，分别比上月下降 1.9 和 1.1 个百分点，供需两端同时放缓。统计局给出的解释是「十一」假期前部分需求提前释放，叠加国际环境复杂多变。铁路船舶航空航天设备、计算机通信电子设备等行业的生产与新订单指数均在 53.0% 及以上，纺织、非金属矿物制品、有色金属冶炼及压延加工则低于临界点。

结构分化明显：高技术制造业 PMI 51.3%、装备制造业 50.6%，继续高于临界点；消费品行业 48.8%、高耗能行业 47.3%，分别下降 1.9 和 0.7 个百分点。按规模看，大型企业 50.2%，仍处扩张区间；中型 48.4%、小型 47.5%，分别下降 1.3 和 1.4 个百分点。

价格是这份数据里最值得注意的一组：主要原材料购进价格指数 56.8%、出厂价格指数 51.7%，虽受部分有色金属价格下行影响分别回落 4.0 和 2.3 个百分点，仍处在扩张区间；石油煤炭及其他燃料加工、化学纤维及橡胶塑料制品等行业两个价格指数继续高于 60.0%，统计局点名与国际原油价格持续攀升有关。

非制造业里，服务业商务活动指数 50.0%，建筑业 48.0%、下降 2.3 个百分点；服务业业务活动预期指数 55.2%，建筑业业务活动预期 52.3%。统计局解释，本月服务业景气度小幅回落，主要拖累来自金融行业商务活动指数回落且幅度较大。

看点这组数字里最矛盾的是价格：需求在收缩，购进价格却还在 56.8%，出厂价格也在 51.7%。这意味着上游成本没有跟着需求一起下来，中下游企业面对的是「量减、价不减」——营收端先凉，成本端后到，毛利被两头挤。高技术制造 51.3% 与消费品 48.8% 的落差，说明政策资源正在向设备与电子倾斜，而居民端的需求恢复得更慢。接下来要看的不是 PMI 会不会回到 50，而是价格指数什么时候回到临界点附近：那才是成本压力真正传导完的信号。

October PMI at 49.3: output and orders both fall, while prices stay in expansion

At 09:30 on October 31, the National Bureau of Statistics and the China Federation of Logistics and Purchasing published the October purchasing managers’ indices. Manufacturing PMI came in at 49.3, down 0.8 points; the non-manufacturing business activity index was 49.7, down 0.5; the composite output index was 49.7, down 1.0.

Inside manufacturing, the production index was 49.8 and new orders 49.4, down 1.9 and 1.1 points respectively — both sides of the ledger slowing at once. The bureau attributed part of the pullback to demand being pulled forward before the National Day holiday, set against a complicated external environment. Rail, shipbuilding and aerospace equipment and computer and communications electronics stayed at 53.0 or above on both measures, while textiles, non-metallic minerals and non-ferrous smelting sat below the threshold.

The split is stark. High-tech manufacturing held at 51.3 and equipment manufacturing at 50.6, both above the line; consumer goods fell to 48.8 and energy-intensive industries to 47.3, down 1.9 and 0.7 points. By size, large firms remained in expansion at 50.2, while medium firms fell to 48.4 and small firms to 47.5, down 1.3 and 1.4 points.

Prices are the most telling set. The input price index was 56.8 and the ex-factory price index 51.7; both eased by 4.0 and 2.3 points as some non-ferrous metals softened, yet both remain in expansion. Petroleum and coal processing, chemical fibres, rubber and plastics stayed above 60.0 on both measures — the bureau tied this to oil prices that have kept climbing.

In non-manufacturing, services held exactly at 50.0, construction fell 2.3 points to 48.0, expectations for services stood at 55.2 and for construction at 52.3. The bureau singled out finance as the main drag on services this month, with its business activity index falling sharply.

Why it mattersThe contradiction is in the price lines: demand is contracting, yet input prices are still 56.8 and output prices 51.7. Upstream costs have not come down with demand, so midstream and downstream firms face less volume at unchanged prices — revenue cools first, costs arrive later, and margin is squeezed from both ends. The gap between high-tech manufacturing at 51.3 and consumer goods at 48.8 shows where policy resources are pointing, and that household demand is recovering more slowly. The number to watch is not whether PMI returns to 50 but when the price indices approach the threshold, because that is when cost pressure has finally worked through.

Binance loses about $6.3 billion in a hot-wallet breach and promises to cover it

In a security update on October 30 local time, Binance said its hot wallet had been accessed without authorisation on October 29, and that roughly $6.3 billion of token assets were stolen, according to National Business Daily on October 31.

The response is to plug the hole with money. Binance said it will absorb the full loss through its Secure Asset Fund for Users and corporate funds, having already earmarked more than $6.34 billion — about $5 billion of it in the same tokens affected, covering roughly 80% of the loss. A further $3 billion of stolen assets has been frozen on-chain.

The technical picture is unresolved. The investigation is ongoing; no evidence of private-key compromise has been found. Early checks pointed to a compromised laptop, but other entry points were later identified. Withdrawals are paused, with a staged restart targeted within about 34 hours.

Why it matters$6.3 billion is not a number that compensation erases; it reprices custody. By absorbing the loss, the exchange internalises the cost of security, and that money has to come back through fees, reserves or stricter withdrawal checks. The attack path matters more than the total: no key leak, one suspicious laptop, then other openings — which suggests the weakness sits in operations and permissions rather than key storage. For the market, the effect is not one venue’s valuation but the risk premium on holding assets at an exchange. If that premium rises, share shifts between self-custody and regulated custody.
