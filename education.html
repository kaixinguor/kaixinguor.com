义乌用「潮汐学校」接住学位排浪：已新增 7,650 个学位，未来 5 年再落 14 所

义乌实有人口从「十四五」初的 248 万增至 342 万，学位需求持续抬升。当地把小学与初中、初中与高中一体统筹，建「潮汐学校」：先按当前学段招生，等学位高峰排浪到下一学段，再整体转设。目前已建成 2 所、转设 3 所，新增潮汐学位 7,650 个。

稠城校区设计规模 72 个班，现有小学 12 个班、初中 34 个班，预计 2030 年变为 72 个班的纯初中；小初分开建预算需 5 亿元，潮汐学校实际投入 3.55 亿元，地下车位从预估 800 个压到 329 个。塔山校区从 26 亩扩到 63 亩，设计小初共 36 个班。

义乌测算全国初中、高中学龄人口分别在 2026、2029 年达峰，当地因人口流入推迟到 2031、2034 年。「十四五」期间义乌投入 90.7 亿元、建成校舍 221.4 万平方米；未来 5 年还有 14 所潮汐学校落地，占教育重点项目计划的 53.85%，预计节省 21.3 亿元。

看点潮汐学校把「建早了浪费、建晚了挤爆」的择时问题，改成「先通用、再转设」的期权：教室按 90 平方米、实验室按可拆分设计，代价是管理复杂度上升。对地方政府，真正的约束不是建安成本，而是师资编制能否跟着学段倒挂走；对家长，转设意味着对口学校与升学路径会变，政策要提前几年说清楚。

河北鼓励高校毕业生创办 OPC 一人公司：给创业补贴、担保贷款，还把 OPC 课程开进高校

河北省政府办公厅印发《河北省支持人工智能 OPC 创新发展若干措施（试行）》，明确鼓励高校毕业生创办 OPC（一人公司），将符合条件的 OPC 项目纳入省大学生创业扶持项目范围，给予创业补贴、创业担保贷款等支持。

配套包括：支持雄安新区打造 OPC 发展高地，以软硬环境一体化带动数字游民集聚；鼓励高校、职业院校增设人工智能相关专业、开设 OPC 课程；组建 OPC 创业导师专家库，推行校企双师制，共建 OPC 创业实践基地；举办大学生 OPC 创业大赛，支持赛事设 OPC 专项赛道。

人才侧，符合条件的 OPC 人才纳入「燕赵英才服务卡」，提供出行、医疗、教育、落户等便利；支持高校科研人员离岗创业，依规保留人事关系，开通职称评审绿色通道。

看点这不是简单的「鼓励创业」，而是把 AI 工具的边际成本下降翻译成一种新的企业形态：一个人加模型加外包，就能注册公司。政策的关键在最后一公里——创业担保贷款谁来审、OPC 没有员工怎么交社保、高校课程由谁教。对高校，OPC 课程如果只讲工具，会变成就业率包装；要能让学生真的跑通一个客户、一张发票、一次报税，才算数。

湖北汽车工业学院把专业「焊」在产业链上：58 个本科专业覆盖汽车全链，产业匹配度 95%

湖北汽车工业学院 1972 年建校，50 余年向汽车产业输送 15 万余名专业人才；目前在猛士科技（襄阳）工作的校友有 163 人。学校 15 个硕士点、58 个本科专业覆盖汽车产业链，产业匹配度 95%。

2024 年学校重组二级学院，新建智能网联汽车、人工智能、机器人与自动化、汽车未来技术四大学院，并以就业率、行业匹配度、产业贡献度动态评估专业；对关联度走低的专业压缩招生、逐步停招。

教学上，国家现代产业学院超过一半教学案例来自企业真实项目，80% 的本科毕业设计来自车企攻关任务；「少农班」30 多名学生在大三结束后，分赴中国汽车工程研究院、东风越野车等 10 余家企业，进行一年岗位实习。

看点95% 的产业匹配度不是天然结果，而是「专业能停招、学院能重组」换来的；多数高校的难点不在设新专业，而在旧专业的师资与编制怎么退出。对做产教融合的学校，可复制的是评估指标和实习时长，不可复制的是东风这样的本地产业密度。对企业，80% 毕业设计来自真实任务意味着能提前筛人，但也要承担带教成本。

上海交大终身教育学院与金山共建低空经济实训基地，开「AI+低空工程」微专业

11 月 5 日，新金山发展公司与上海交通大学终身教育学院签约，为「上海交通大学终身教育学院低空经济实训基地」揭牌；双方将围绕人才培养、实训基地建设、智库资源共享合作，推进《AI+低空工程与产业经济管理微专业》。

课程把无人机适航审定、空域管理、行业应用纳入体系，并联合开展职业技能培训、行业研修、企业定制化内训；实训依托华东无人机基地的试飞起降区、飞行控制与动力实验室。

华东无人机基地董事长薛彬称，行业最缺的不是单一技术岗，而是「技术+管理+运营」复合型人才；企业侧，上海翰动浩翔已参与大型文旅景区无人机编队项目。

看点低空经济的用人缺口在「既懂适航与空域、又能算运营账」的复合岗，微专业是把这层能力压缩成一年的尝试。对高校，真正的成本是课程由谁上、实训空域怎么批；对企业，定制化内训比校招更快，但会把人才绑定在特定机型上。要看的是首届学生能否拿到适航或空域相关证书，以及基地能否稳定拿到飞行窗口。

Yiwu's tidal schools absorb enrolment waves: 7,650 places added, 14 more schools in five years

Yiwu's resident population has grown from 2.48 million at the start of the 14th Five-Year Plan to 3.42 million, pushing demand for school places. The city pairs primary with lower-secondary and lower- with upper-secondary provision in "tidal schools": a campus admits for the stage that needs it now, then converts as the demographic wave moves up. Two have been built and three converted, adding 7,650 places.

The Doucheng campus is designed for 72 classes and currently has 12 primary and 34 lower-secondary classes; by 2030 it is expected to become a 72-class lower-secondary school. Building primary and secondary separately was budgeted at RMB 500m; the tidal school cost RMB 355m, and underground parking was cut from a projected 800 spaces to 329. The Tashan campus expanded from 26 mu to 63 mu, with 36 primary and secondary classes.

Yiwu projects that national lower- and upper-secondary cohorts peak in 2026 and 2029; because of inflows, its own peaks arrive in 2031 and 2034. During the 14th Five-Year Plan the city spent RMB 9.07bn and built 2.214 million square metres of school space. Fourteen more tidal schools are due in five years, 53.85% of its key education projects, with an estimated RMB 2.13bn saved.

Why it mattersTidal schools turn the timing problem of "build early and waste, build late and overflow" into an option: build generic, convert later. Classrooms are set at 90 square metres and labs are designed to be split, at the cost of more complex management. For local governments, the binding constraint is not construction cost but whether teacher establishment can follow the stage inversion. For parents, conversion changes catchment and progression paths, so the policy has to be signalled years ahead.

11/09 China Education Daily ↗

Hebei backs graduate-founded one-person AI companies with subsidies, loan guarantees and university courses

Hebei's provincial government has issued trial measures to support AI OPCs, or one-person companies. Graduates who set one up can have eligible projects included in the provincial student start-up support scheme, with start-up subsidies and guaranteed start-up loans.

The package also calls for Xiong'an New Area to become an OPC hub, using integrated hard and soft infrastructure to attract digital nomads; universities and vocational colleges are encouraged to add AI programmes and OPC courses; an OPC mentor pool, dual-teacher schemes and shared practice bases are planned; and a student OPC start-up competition will add a dedicated track.

On talent, eligible OPC founders can join the "Yanzhao Talent Service Card" scheme, which covers transport, healthcare, education and household registration. University researchers can leave to start OPCs while keeping their personnel relationship, with a fast track for professional-title review.

Why it mattersThis is not just a start-up nudge. It translates the falling marginal cost of AI tools into a new firm form: one person plus models plus contractors can register a company. The policy's real test is the last mile: who approves the guaranteed loan, how an OPC with no employees handles social insurance, and who teaches the courses. For universities, an OPC course that only teaches tools becomes an employment-rate wrapper; it counts only if students can actually land a client, issue an invoice and file a tax return.

11/09 China Education Daily ↗

Hubei University of Automotive Technology welds its programmes to the car chain: 58 majors, 95% industry match

Founded in 1972, Hubei University of Automotive Technology has sent more than 150,000 graduates into the car industry over five decades; 163 alumni now work at Mengshi Technology in Xiangyang. Its 15 master's points and 58 undergraduate programmes cover the vehicle chain, with an industry-match rate of 95%.

In 2024 the university reorganised its colleges, creating four new schools for intelligent connected vehicles, AI, robotics and automation, and future automotive technology. Programmes are assessed on employment rate, industry fit and industry contribution; those losing relevance have enrolments cut and are phased out.

In its national modern-industry college, more than half of teaching cases come from real company projects, and 80% of undergraduate final projects come from carmakers' problems. More than 30 students in the Shaonong class spend a year after their third year interning at more than 10 companies, including the China Automotive Engineering Research Institute and Dongfeng Off-Road Vehicle.

Why it mattersA 95% match is not natural. It comes from being able to stop programmes and reorganise colleges; for most universities the hard part is not adding a new major but retiring faculty and posts from old ones. What other schools can copy is the assessment metrics and the internship length; what they cannot copy is a local industry density like Dongfeng's. For companies, 80% of final projects coming from real problems means earlier talent screening, but also a teaching cost.

Shanghai Jiao Tong and Jinshan open a low-altitude economy training base with an AI+low-altitude micro-major

On November 5, New Jinshan Development Corporation and Shanghai Jiao Tong University's School of Continuing Education signed an agreement and unveiled a low-altitude economy training base. The two sides will work on talent development, the base and shared think-tank resources, and advance a micro-major in AI plus low-altitude engineering and industrial-economy management.

The curriculum brings drone airworthiness certification, airspace management and industry applications into the programme, alongside vocational training, industry seminars and customised corporate courses. Practical work uses the East China UAV Base's test flight area and its flight-control and power labs.

Xue Bin, chairman of the East China UAV Base, says the shortage is not single-skill technicians but people who combine technology, management and operations. On the company side, Shanghai Handong Haoxiang has worked on drone formation projects at large tourism sites.

Why it mattersThe low-altitude economy's skills gap is in hybrid roles that understand airworthiness and airspace as well as operating economics; a micro-major is an attempt to compress that into a year. For universities, the real cost is who teaches and how training airspace is approved. For companies, customised training is faster than campus recruiting but ties people to particular airframes. Watch whether the first cohort earns airworthiness or airspace certificates, and whether the base gets a stable flight window.
