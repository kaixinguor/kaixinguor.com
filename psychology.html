3,816 名中国青少年、相隔五个月两次随访：手机「用出问题」与后来的情绪调节困难相关

西北师范大学心理学院 Xuan Zhao、Tiejun Kang 等（共同第一作者，通讯作者 Xiaobin Ding）对 3,816 名中国青少年做了两轮问卷，间隔 5 个月。测量三块：青少年生活事件量表（ASLEC）的负性生活事件、情绪调节困难量表（DERS）、手机成瘾指数（MPAI）中的手机使用维度。分析先为男女两组分别拟合同期网络，再用交叉滞后面板网络（CLPN）看变量之间的时间先后，边选择用 LASSO 加 EBIC。

同期网络里，男女生最中心的负性生活事件节点都是学习压力。跨时间保留的跨域路径有三类：男生的适应相关压力、女生的人际压力，以及两组都出现的「手机使用导致的生产力损失」。生产力损失在男生身上指向后来的冲动控制困难，在女生身上指向后来的「情绪调节策略获取受限」。

性别差异这块，结论是「没找到」：两轮同期网络的结构与整体强度都没有显著差异；多组模型整体 χ²(240)=304.20、p=0.003，但没有一条具体路径通过 Bonferroni 校正。作者的措辞很克制——五个月内是条件性关联，不是因果；样本为中国青少年，全部自报量表，网络模型本身是探索性的。

看点把「功能受损」而不是「使用时长」当入口，是这条研究对筛查最有用的地方：问「手机让你耽误了什么」比问「每天几小时」更能把人分开。但要小心过度解读——跨域路径在多重比较后全部不成立，说明按性别分层可能已经过拟合，三条路径更像是待验证的假设。下一步需要生态瞬时评估（EMA）加临床端点，否则「生产力损失」会变成又一个笼统标签，谁都能往里装。

11,868 名 9–17 岁青少年：一份 DSM-5 诊断，65%–98% 的情况下只有孩子或父母一方报告

加州大学圣迭戈分校医学院 Laika D. Aguinaldo 等用「青少年大脑认知发展」（ABCD）研究的数据：11,868 名 9–17 岁青少年与他们的照护者，分别接受同一套诊断访谈，各自据 DSM-5 形成诊断，再按病种与发育阶段比较两份报告的重叠程度。论文 11 月 11 日发表于 JAMA Psychiatry。

结果是分歧成为常态：当某个诊断成立时，只有一方报告（而非双方都报）的比例约 65%–98%。青少年自己更常报重性抑郁、睡眠问题、物质使用、自杀意念与尝试、非自杀性自伤；照护者更常报品行障碍、进食障碍、广泛性焦虑与社交焦虑。分歧在年幼时最大，随年龄增长收窄；总体一致性随发育阶段改善，但始终有限。

Aguinaldo 的解释是两种观察窗口不同：青少年更愿意说私密、内化的体验，父母更常报外显的问题。她的原话是，「只问一方，就可能漏掉一个需要帮助的孩子」——这句话同时适用于临床筛查和患病率估计。

看点这条最直接的含义不是「父母不可靠」，而是单一报告人会把同一批孩子算成两种流行病学：按孩子报是一个患病率，按父母报是另一个。对筛查流程，双报告人采集应当成为默认设置而不是加项，代价是门诊时间与量表成本；对研究，用单一口径做的青少年患病率估计需要重新标注。作者说下一步要查的是：不一致本身是否预测后续服务使用与结局——如果成立，「不一致程度」可以变成一个几乎零成本的风险指标。

芬兰 28.3 万名儿童登记数据：12 岁前品行障碍累计发生率 0.8%，其中 40.6% 共病 ADHD

芬兰图尔库大学 Marika Leppänen 等用 2001–2005 年出生儿童的全国登记数据（N=282,773，随访至 12 岁的分析样本 269,490），按 ICD-10 统计品行障碍（F91.x，含对立违抗障碍）与混合性品行与情绪障碍（F92.x）的累计发生率，并核对共病 ADHD 以及围产期、心理社会因素。

12 岁前 F91.x 累计 0.8%（2,208 人），F92.x 1.5%（4,128 人）。队列之间在上升：男孩从 2001 年出生队列的 2.8% 升到 2005 年队列的 3.3%，女孩从 0.8% 升到 1.1%（p<0.0001、p=0.0007）。5,573 名确诊儿童中，40.6%（2,260 人）共病 ADHD；队列间 ADHD 诊断在品行障碍男孩中上升（p<0.05），女孩没有（p>0.05）。

共病的校正比值比：男孩 6.8（95% CI 6.0–7.8）、母亲孕期吸烟 2.6（2.4–2.9）、母亲在孕期至孩子 4 岁间有精神障碍 2.5（2.2–2.8）、单亲 1.8（1.6–2.1）、母亲失业 1.2（1.1–1.3）。要打折的地方同样清楚：登记数据只记录「被诊断到」的人，轻症与从未就诊者不在分母里；发生率上升的绝对幅度只有 0.5 个百分点，区分不了疾病本身变多还是识别与诊断实践变化。

看点40.6% 的共病率说明这两个诊断在真实服务系统里几乎是一起出现的，而现在的服务把它们拆开：行为问题归学校，ADHD 归儿科与精神科，同一个孩子于是有两份互不往来的档案。对路径设计，含义是共病筛查应写进品行障碍的初诊环节，尤其是母亲有精神障碍史的孩子——2.5 是这份数据里除性别外最强的信号。对支付方，一个更实际的问题是：这两类诊断的干预（家长训练、行为治疗、药物）由不同预算出，共病孩子的成本落在谁的表上，目前没有答案。

194 名成年人：每周「入睡抽动」的人，睡眠质量更差、焦虑更高

萨尔茨堡大学（Paris Lodron University Salzburg）Tanja Grünberger、Manuel Schabus 等对奥地利与德国 194 名成年人做在线问卷：96 人每周至少一次在入睡时突然惊醒（sleep starts，俗称入睡抽动）并伴至少一种其他表现，98 人作对照。问卷覆盖睡眠质量、失眠严重度、日间嗜睡、对睡眠的功能失调信念，以及焦虑与抑郁症状。

与对照相比，入睡抽动组的睡眠质量更差、失眠更重、对睡眠的功能失调信念更多、焦虑与抑郁症状更高，差异都显著。发生频率与睡眠质量、睡眠信念、焦虑相关，与抑郁不相关；把年龄、性别一并放入，这些因素解释频率个体差异的 26.9%。参与者报告在心理压力与情绪紧张期抽动更频繁，睡眠剥夺的影响较小。

局限写得很直白：横断面、自报、没有已验证的入睡抽动量表（条目按临床文献自编）、没有客观睡眠记录，因此不能推因果。作者称，对大多数人入睡抽动是入睡过程的无害特征，但对一部分人伴随痛苦与更长的入睡时间。

看点机制上，这更像「睡眠相关高唤醒」的一个可观察标记，而不是一种独立的病：抽动、心悸、坠落感与「必须马上睡着」的压力同时出现，和失眠的认知模型对得上。临床上可操作的是把它当问诊线索（问「快睡着时会不会突然一惊」），而不是新增一个诊断。真正缺的一步是干预试验——CBT-I 这类降低唤醒与睡眠焦虑的方案能不能减少抽动，作者明确说还没测。

40 国 113 份数字心理健康政策：只有 14.2% 专门聚焦数字心理健康

北京大学公共卫生学院全球卫生系 Zuokun Liu、Xiang Zhou 等与国家心理健康中心（北京）合作，比较 40 个国家的 113 份数字心理健康政策文件，世界银行四个收入组各取 10 国。编码框架基于「可得、可及、可接受、质量」（AAAQ），拆成 14 个维度，共得到 1,270 条编码、归并为 66 个子主题。

政策发布在 2020 年后增加，2023 年达峰 21 份。但多数文件并不专：49.6% 只泛谈数字健康、24.8% 只泛谈心理健康，只有 14.2% 专门聚焦数字心理健康。覆盖最多的维度是信息（71.7% 的文件、14.1% 的编码）、服务可得性（69.9%、12.9%）与伦理问题（70.8%、12.8%）；最少的是吸引力（17.7%、2.7%）、连接质量（23.9%、3.4%）、数字素养（32.7%、3.9%）与可用性（31.0%、4.0%）。

收入组之间重心不同：低收入组更强调基础能力，中等收入组强调服务可得性，高收入组强调吸引力、可用性、伦理与科学证据。国别层面，澳大利亚是唯一 14 个维度都有专门内容的国家，俄罗斯的文件一个维度都没有覆盖；印度、泰国、乌克兰等中等收入国家的覆盖范围比部分高收入国家更广。

看点文本的盲区正好落在「用不用得起来」：数字素养、可用性、连接质量三项覆盖率都低，而这三项决定了谁会被排除——老人、低带宽地区、低教育人群。对做数字疗法的人，合规只是入场券，支付方与伦理审查能引用的文本依据更决定能不能落地；对监管者，下一步不是再发一份文件，而是把覆盖率最低的四项写成可考核指标，否则「数字心理健康」会长期停在信息发布层面。

3,816 Chinese adolescents, two waves five months apart: phone-related impairment tracks later emotion-regulation difficulty

Xuan Zhao, Tiejun Kang and colleagues at Northwest Normal University's School of Psychology (joint first authors; Xiaobin Ding corresponding) surveyed 3,816 Chinese adolescents twice, five months apart. Three instrument families were used: negative life events on the Adolescent Self-Rating Life Events Checklist (ASLEC), the Difficulties in Emotion Regulation Scale (DERS), and the mobile-phone dimension of the Mobile Phone Addiction Index (MPAI). The team fitted same-wave networks for boys and girls separately, then a cross-lagged panel network (CLPN) to order variables in time, with edges selected by LASSO and EBIC.

In the contemporaneous networks, learning stress was the most central negative life event node for both sexes. The cross-lagged paths that survived involved adaptation-related stress in boys, interpersonal pressure in girls, and, in both groups, the productivity loss that comes with phone use. That productivity loss predicted later impulse-control difficulties in boys and later limited access to emotion-regulation strategies in girls.

On sex differences, the finding is a null: network structure and global strength did not differ at either wave, and although a multigroup model showed an omnibus difference in cross-lagged coefficients (chi-square(240) = 304.20, p = .003), no individual path survived Bonferroni correction. The authors are careful: these are conditional associations over five months, not causal effects, in a Chinese sample measured entirely by self-report, and the network itself is exploratory.

Why it mattersUsing functional impairment rather than screen time as the entry point is the practical idea here: asking what the phone makes you fall behind on separates adolescents better than asking how many hours. The guardrail is just as important. Every cross-domain path collapsed under multiple-comparison correction, so sex-stratified fitting may already be overfitting and the three paths are hypotheses awaiting confirmation. What would move this forward is ecological momentary assessment plus a clinical endpoint, otherwise "productivity loss" becomes one more catch-all label that absorbs anything.

11,868 adolescents aged 9-17: a DSM-5 diagnosis came from only one informant in 65%-98% of cases

Laika D. Aguinaldo and colleagues at UC San Diego School of Medicine drew on the Adolescent Brain Cognitive Development (ABCD) study: 11,868 young people aged 9-17 and their caregivers each completed the same diagnostic interview, each report was converted into a DSM-5 diagnosis, and the two were compared diagnosis by diagnosis and developmental stage by developmental stage. The paper appeared in JAMA Psychiatry on November 11.

Disagreement turns out to be the rule. Where a diagnosis was present, only one informant reported it, rather than both, in about 65%-98% of cases. Adolescents were more likely to report major depression, sleep problems, substance use, suicidal thoughts and attempts, and non-suicidal self-injury. Caregivers were more likely to report conduct disorder, eating disorders, and generalised and social anxiety. The gap is widest at younger ages and narrows with age, and overall agreement improves across development while staying limited.

Aguinaldo's explanation is that the two informants watch through different windows: teenagers disclose private, internal experience, while parents report what is visible from outside. Her point is that asking only one of them may miss a young person who needs help, a claim that applies to clinical screening and to prevalence estimates alike.

Why it mattersThe lesson is not that parents are unreliable. It is that a single informant turns one cohort of children into two different epidemiologies, one when the child reports and another when the parent does. For screening, dual-informant collection should be the default rather than an optional add-on, and the price is clinic time and questionnaire burden. For research, prevalence figures built on one informant need a label. Aguinaldo's team is now asking whether discordance itself predicts later service use and outcomes; if it does, the degree of disagreement becomes a near-zero-cost risk marker.

282,773 Finnish children: conduct disorder incidence 0.8% by age 12, and 40.6% of cases had ADHD too

Marika Leppänen and colleagues at the University of Turku used national register data on children born in 2001-2005 (N = 282,773; 269,490 analysable through age 12), coding conduct disorder (ICD-10 F91.x, including oppositional defiant disorder) and mixed disorders of conduct and emotions (F92.x), then checking comorbid ADHD alongside perinatal and psychosocial factors.

By age 12, cumulative incidence was 0.8% for F91.x (2,208 children) and 1.5% for F92.x (4,128). It rose across birth cohorts: boys went from 2.8% in the 2001 cohort to 3.3% in the 2005 cohort, girls from 0.8% to 1.1% (p < .0001 and p = .0007). Of 5,573 children with a diagnosis, 40.6% (2,260) also had ADHD, and ADHD diagnoses rose across cohorts in boys with conduct disorder (p < .05) but not in girls (p > .05).

Adjusted odds ratios for comorbidity: boys 6.8 (95% CI 6.0-7.8), maternal smoking in pregnancy 2.6 (2.4-2.9), maternal psychiatric disorder between pregnancy and the child's fourth year 2.5 (2.2-2.8), single parenthood 1.8 (1.6-2.1), maternal unemployment 1.2 (1.1-1.3). The discounts are equally clear: registers only contain children who were diagnosed, so mild and never-referred cases are missing from the denominator, and the absolute rise is half a percentage point, which cannot distinguish more disorder from more detection.

Why it mattersA 40.6% comorbidity rate says these two diagnoses almost always arrive together in the service system, which nonetheless splits them: behaviour problems belong to schools, ADHD to paediatrics and psychiatry, and one child ends up with two case files that never meet. For care pathways, comorbidity screening belongs in the first conduct-disorder assessment, especially for children whose mothers have a psychiatric history, since 2.5 is the strongest signal in these data after sex. For payers the unresolved question is fiscal: parent training, behaviour therapy and medication come out of different budgets, and no one has worked out whose ledger carries the comorbid child.

11/11 20:00 PLOS Mental Health ↗

194 adults: jolting awake at sleep onset goes with worse sleep and higher anxiety

Tanja Grünberger, Manuel Schabus and colleagues at Paris Lodron University Salzburg surveyed 194 adults in Austria and Germany online. Ninety-six reported abrupt awakenings at sleep onset at least weekly plus at least one other symptom; 98 served as controls. The questionnaire covered sleep quality, insomnia severity, daytime sleepiness, dysfunctional beliefs about sleep, and anxiety and depressive symptoms.

Compared with controls, the sleep-starts group reported worse sleep quality, more severe insomnia, more dysfunctional sleep beliefs and higher anxiety and depressive symptoms, all statistically significant. How often the jolts occurred was linked to sleep quality, sleep beliefs and anxiety, but not to depression. Together with age and sex, these factors explained 26.9% of the between-person variation in frequency. Participants reported more frequent jolts during periods of psychological stress and emotional strain, while sleep deprivation mattered less.

The limits are stated plainly: cross-sectional self-report, no validated sleep-starts questionnaire (items were written from the clinical literature), and no objective sleep recording, so causality cannot be inferred. For most people, the authors write, sleep starts are a harmless feature of falling asleep; for a subgroup they come with distress and a longer time to fall asleep.

Why it mattersMechanistically this looks less like a disease than a visible marker of sleep-related hyperarousal: the jerk, the racing heart, the sensation of falling and the pressure to fall asleep now all arrive together, which is what the cognitive model of insomnia would predict. The clinical use is as an interview prompt ("do you ever jolt awake just as you drop off?"), not a new diagnosis. The missing step is an intervention trial: whether CBT-I, which lowers arousal and sleep worry, also reduces the jolts has not been tested, and the authors say so.

113 digital mental health policies across 40 countries: only 14.2% actually focus on digital mental health

Zuokun Liu, Xiang Zhou and colleagues at Peking University's School of Public Health, with the National Center for Mental Health in Beijing, compared 113 policy documents from 40 countries, ten from each World Bank income group. Coding followed a 14-dimension framework built on availability, accessibility, acceptability and quality (AAAQ), yielding 1,270 coded references grouped into 66 subthemes.

Policy output rose after 2020 and peaked at 21 documents in 2023. But most documents are not specific: 49.6% discuss digital health only in general terms and 24.8% discuss mental health only in general terms, while just 14.2% focus on digital mental health as such. The most-covered dimensions were information (in 71.7% of documents, 14.1% of references), service availability (69.9%, 12.9%) and ethical issues (70.8%, 12.8%). The least-covered were attractiveness (17.7%, 2.7%), quality of connection (23.9%, 3.4%), digital literacy (32.7%, 3.9%) and usability (31.0%, 4.0%).

The emphasis shifts with income: low-income countries lean on foundational capacity, middle-income countries on service availability, high-income countries on attractiveness, usability, ethics and scientific evidence. By country, Australia is the only one whose documents cover all 14 dimensions, Russia's cover none of them, and several middle-income countries, including India, Thailand and Ukraine, range wider than some high-income peers.
