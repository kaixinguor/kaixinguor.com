自闭症与非自闭症成人看同一批电影：组平均的脑反应几乎一样，自闭症参与者彼此之间差得更多，社会脑系统上最明显

《Journal of Neuroscience》2026 年 11 月 15 日在线发表（DOI 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0055-26.2026），神经科学学会（SfN）同日发布新闻稿。爱荷华大学的 Dorit Kliemann 与 James Traer 牵头，重新分析了印第安纳大学与加州理工学院此前采集的影像数据。样本为 138 名成人：48 名自闭症成人（13 名女性）与 90 名非自闭症成人（25 名女性）；每人在扫描时观看 6 段 5 至 20 分钟的电影片段，题材覆盖动画、动作、爱情喜剧与希区柯克式惊悚片。

方法上，研究者用计算模型把电影的声音、画面与更抽象的社会特征（例如效价）同脑活动对应起来，再检验模型能否预测不同脑系统的反应。新闻稿的说法是，模型对各类脑系统的预测「准确」，这也正是这套设计的意义：把复杂、动态的电影场景拆成可以被模型检验的脑反应，而不是只用静态图片做刺激。

结果分两层。比较组平均时，模型对两组不同脑系统的预测结果相似；把个体与个体相比，自闭症参与者偏离本组平均的程度更大。这种更高的变异性出现在几乎所有被评估的脑系统中，在社会脑系统（social brain system）上最明显。新闻稿特别提到，团队对「组平均如此相似」感到意外。

作者的判断是：目前理解与治疗自闭症都依赖组平均比较，这项结果说明需要把重点放到个体脑反应的细节上——「它们如何不同、为什么不同」。Kliemann 说，识别自闭症亚组可能有助于更精确地理解这一状况，并发展个体化的支持方式；团队计划继续追查这种变异性与行为差异的关系。该结果随 2026 年神经科学学会年会（华盛顿）发布，11 月 15 日下午做了报告。

看点这条的落点不是「自闭症的大脑不一样」，而是「平均之后看不出来」。组平均相似、个体分散，动摇的是三件依赖平均值的做法：把自闭症当同质群体的试验，效应量会被个体异质性稀释；按组均值训练的影像标记，换一个人未必成立；亚型若真的存在，按行为量表划线可能划错位置。限制要一并读：横断面、复用既有数据集、只有看电影这一种范式；而且论文摘要只给了「变异更大」这个方向，没有效应量，也没有可复现的分组方案——「识别亚组」目前是研究计划，不是结果。
另一个视角若真按「个体化支持」走，交付单位会从「一套方案卖给所有人」变成「一人一套评估」；这笔评估成本由谁支付，现有证据里没有答案。

11/15 Discover Mental Health（Springer） ↗

尼泊尔 Bara 地区 24 人访谈：反复高温被描述为先经缺水、断电、粮食不安全挤压家庭，再传导到儿童的情绪、睡眠与上课状态

《Discover Mental Health》2026 年 11 月 15 日在线发表（DOI 10.1007/s44192-026-00676-1），第一作者为 Alisha Basnet。这是一项探索性描述性质性研究：在尼泊尔 Bara 县受高温影响的城乡社区做半结构化访谈，共 24 人，包括儿童、照护者、教师、卫生工作者与非政府组织人员，用反身性主题分析整理。

受访者描述的高温不是单一的热应激，而是一串环境与社会经济压力：水源短缺、电力不稳、饮用水污染、粮食不安全、住房不安全。这些压力落在家庭上，表现为家庭紧张、冲突与养育中断；受访者把它与儿童遭遇忽视、处在不安全环境等不良经历联系起来。社会经济地位较低的家庭，应对能力更弱。

儿童层面被提到的变化是：身体症状、心理困扰、注意力下降、睡眠被打断、学校参与度下降。家庭使用的应对方式包括行为调整、补水、降温与社区仪式，但对最贫困的家庭缓解有限；机构响应则受制于基础设施不足与心理支持稀缺。作者的结论是，高温把环境、家庭与心理压力连成一条链，而这条链上没有哪一个部门单独负责。

看点这类研究在证据金字塔上位置很低：24 人的访谈报告的是感知与叙述，既不是发病率，也给不出效应量，更不能说高温「导致」了某种精神障碍。但它提供的是一条可供检验的机制路径——高温 → 基础设施失灵 → 家庭压力与养育中断 → 儿童不良经历。对政策的价值在排序：如果路径成立，一台稳定的水泵或备用电源，可能比单开一条心理热线更能减少儿童的心理困扰；而这笔钱记在水利与电力的账上，不在卫生的账上。要验证，需要把高温日、门诊住院数据与学校缺勤连起来的纵向设计，而不是再一轮访谈。

186 人的静息态 fMRI：双相抑郁中「失眠较轻」者的右岛叶自发活动反而更高，与单相抑郁的失眠相关模式不同

《BMC Psychiatry》2026 年 11 月 15 日在线发表（DOI 10.1186/s12888-026-08855-8）。样本为 79 例重性抑郁障碍（MDD）、56 例双相障碍抑郁相（BDD）与 51 名健康对照，共 186 人；研究者按 17 项汉密尔顿抑郁量表的失眠因子，再把患者分成高失眠与低失眠四个亚组（MDDHI、MDDLI、BDDHI、BDDLI）。

方法为静息态功能磁共振：先算静态与动态低频涨落振幅（s/dALFF），再做种子点的静态与动态功能连接，最后用 Spearman 相关检验异常指标与症状严重度的关系。这是一项横断面、单中心的观察研究，没有干预。

结果有三处：BDD 低失眠组的右岛叶 dALFF 显著高于 BDD 高失眠组、MDD 高失眠组、MDD 低失眠组与健康对照；BDD 低失眠组的右岛叶与左侧后扣带/楔前叶静态连接，高于 MDD 低失眠组；在 BDD 高失眠组，这条连接的强度与抑郁严重度负相关。作者据此提出，右岛叶的动态活动及其与后扣带/楔前叶的连接，可能对应双相与单相抑郁中「与失眠有关」的不同机制。

看点临床上真正难的往往不是处理失眠，而是在抑郁发作时判断这是单相还是双相——两者的用药与随访路径不同，判错方向的代价很高。这条研究给出的候选标记是「右岛叶—后扣带/楔前叶」这条通路，但它首先是组水平的差异：落到个体身上，两组分布会大面积重叠，无法当诊断用。把 186 人按诊断与失眠高低拆成四格之后，每格只剩几十人，相关分析又是探索性的，效应很可能不稳。要变成门诊可用的东西，需要前瞻队列、独立样本复现和一个明确的分界值，而不是又一个 p 值。

11/15 Scientific Reports ↗

23 名抑郁者的先导随机试验：四周「心跳—慈悲」训练组抑郁改善 Hedges g=−1.42，但这是一个两臂合计 23 人的探索性研究

《Scientific Reports》2026 年 11 月 15 日在线发表（DOI 10.1038/s41598-026-78490-6），第一作者为 Alessandra Anzolin。研究者把 23 名抑郁者随机分到 Heart-Smile Training 强化入门项目（HST-IIP）或等待名单对照组，干预 4 周，前后各做一次脑电与临床评估。HST-IIP 把内感受注意训练与慈悲练习放在一起。

结局设置值得注意：主要结局是心跳诱发电位（HEP），也就是内感受训练的候选神经指标；临床结局用计算机自适应心理健康测评（CAT-MH）测抑郁严重度；探索性结局包括引导冥想中的 Delta 功率谱密度，以及 MAIA-2 内感受觉察量表与 SCS 自我慈悲量表。

结果：HST-IIP 组的抑郁改善大于对照组（Hedges g=−1.422，p=0.028），内感受觉察提高。脑电上，注意聚焦冥想时右侧额区电极的 HEP 振幅变浅（更不负），慈悲聚焦冥想时 Delta 功率下降；这两项神经指标的变化与抑郁改善相关。论文自述这是「探索性先导研究」「初步证据」，并主张继续研究内感受慈悲训练作为抑郁治疗的可能路径。

看点g=−1.42 在心理治疗文献里属于「大效应」，但两臂合计 23 人的先导试验几乎必然给出偏大的效应估计：样本越小，效应量的抽样分布越宽，阳性结果也更容易被写出来。真正值得记的是它的设计选择——把内感受指标而不是抑郁分数设为主要结局，这在机制研究里合理，在疗效研究里则意味着主结局与患者最关心的事不是同一件。要判断这套训练有没有用，下一步需要的是百人以上、有主动对照（例如放松训练）、随访至少三个月的试验；在此之前，它适合作为一个机制假设。

102,684 名 AI 心理健康聊天机器人用户的行为聚类：52.2% 早期流失、66.9% 有凌晨会话，抑郁改善随使用强度呈梯度

《Scientific Reports》2026 年 11 月 15 日在线发表（DOI 10.1038/s41598-026-78802-w）。第一作者为弗吉尼亚大学心理学系的 Emma C. Wolfe，合作者来自开发该产品的 Slingshot AI 与纽约大学医学院；研究对象是 AI 心理健康聊天机器人 Ash 的 102,684 名真实用户。

研究者对 8 个行为特征做 K-means 聚类，得到 5 种参与表型：早期流失 52.2%、每周使用 25.3%、集中使用 16.8%、高强度使用 4.1%、深度使用 1.6%。全体用户中 66.9% 至少有过一次 21:00 至 05:00 的会话。

临床侧，子样本在基线与第 3 周完成 PHQ-9（n=298）、GAD-7（n=298）与社会支持量表 MSPSS（n=194），11,437 人完成了基线工作同盟量表（WAI）。前后变化为：抑郁 d=−0.51、焦虑 d=−0.57、社会支持 d=0.22。抑郁改善随使用强度呈梯度：高强度与深度使用组分别为 d=−0.50 与 −0.57，早期流失组为 −0.22；用模型预测的 PHQ-9 在 23,813 人上复现了同一梯度。工作同盟越高，抑郁改善越大。

作者划出的边界是：这是自然使用状态下的相关性证据，没有随机分组、没有对照组，改善可能来自回归均值、自选择或同时进行的其他治疗，结论写的是「与临床获益一致，待对照设计确认」。利益冲突需要一并读：6 名作者中有 4 名是开发该产品的公司雇员，另一名作者的研究经费部分来自该公司与美国国立精神卫生研究所（NIMH）。

看点这条研究最有用的部分不是 d=−0.5，而是把「用量」拆成了行为表型：会话次数本身不预测改善，凌晨使用、集中使用、早期流失是三种完全不同的用户。对一个按月活或按席位收费的产品，这意味着「月活」会系统性高估效果；对支付方，真正该问的是「哪一类用户在被服务、他们本来会不会好」。另一个不该被跳过的数字是 52.2% 的早期流失率——真实世界里多数人打开几次就不再来，这和随机试验里八成完成干预的人群不是同一群人。相关性设计能做的到此为止：剂量梯度只是「用得多的好得多」的另一种说法，因果方向尚未分开。

11/16 Society for Neuroscience (EurekAlert) ↗

Autistic and non-autistic adults watching the same films: group-level brain responses looked alike, while autistic participants differed more from one another — most visibly in the social brain

The work appeared online in The Journal of Neuroscience on November 15, 2026 (DOI 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0055-26.2026), with the Society for Neuroscience releasing the findings the same day. Dorit Kliemann and James Traer at the University of Iowa led the collaboration, which re-analysed imaging collected earlier at Indiana University and Caltech. The sample was 138 adults — 48 autistic (13 female) and 90 non-autistic (25 female) — each watching six film clips of five to 20 minutes across genres: animation, action, romantic comedy, Hitchcock-style thriller.

The team trained a computational model to relate the sounds, visuals and more abstract social features of each scene, such as valence, to brain activity, then tested whether it could predict responses across different brain systems. The release describes the predictions as accurate across systems, and that is the point of the design: it turns complex, dynamic film into brain responses a model can be checked against, rather than relying on static images as stimuli.

The results split in two. On group averages, the model predicted activity similarly for both groups. Compared person to person, autistic participants strayed further from their own group average, and the higher variability appeared in nearly every system assessed, most strikingly in the social brain system. The release notes that the team was surprised by how similar the group averages were.

The authors' reading is that because both understanding and treatment of autism rest on group-average comparisons, the field needs to focus on the detail of individual brain responses — how and why they differ. Kliemann says identifying subgroups may support a more precise understanding of the condition and the development of individually tailored support, and the team plans to keep probing how that variability relates to behaviour. The findings were released with Neuroscience 2026 in Washington, DC, and presented on the afternoon of November 15.

Why it mattersThe claim is not that autistic brains differ; it is that the difference disappears once you average. Similar group means with a wider individual spread undermines three practices that depend on means: trials treating autism as one homogeneous group dilute their own effect sizes; imaging markers trained on group means may not transfer to the next person; and if subgroups exist, behavioural scales may cut in the wrong place. Read the limits alongside. The study is cross-sectional, reuses an existing dataset and one paradigm — watching films — and the abstract reports a direction, greater variability, without an effect size or a reproducible grouping. "Identifying subgroups" is a research plan here, not a result.
Another angle If individually tailored support is the destination, the unit of delivery shifts from one programme sold to everyone to one assessment per person, and nothing in the current evidence says who pays for that assessment.

11/15 Discover Mental Health (Springer) ↗

24 interviews in Nepal's Bara district: repeated heat was described as squeezing households through water, power and food before reaching children's sleep, attention and mood

Discover Mental Health published the study online on November 15, 2026 (DOI 10.1007/s44192-026-00676-1), with Alisha Basnet as first author. It is an exploratory descriptive qualitative study: semi-structured interviews in heat-affected rural and urban communities of Bara District, Nepal, with 24 participants — children, caregivers, teachers, health workers and NGO staff — analysed using reflexive thematic analysis.

Heat was not described as a single stressor but as a chain of environmental and socio-economic pressures: water scarcity, unreliable electricity, contaminated drinking water, food insecurity and unsafe housing. Those pressures landed on households as tension, conflict and disrupted parenting, which participants linked to children's exposure to adversity such as neglect and unsafe surroundings. Households with fewer resources were described as coping less well.

For children, participants described physical symptoms, psychological distress, reduced concentration, disrupted sleep and lower engagement with school. Families used behavioural adjustments, hydration, cooling practices and community rituals, but relief was limited for the poorest households, while institutional responses were constrained by inadequate infrastructure and thin mental health support. The authors' conclusion is that heat braids environmental, household and psychosocial stressors into one chain, and no single sector owns it.

Why it mattersOn an evidence hierarchy this sits low: 24 interviews record perceptions and accounts, not incidence, not an effect size, and certainly not proof that heat causes a mental disorder. What they supply is a testable mechanism — heat, failing infrastructure, household strain and disrupted parenting, then childhood adversity. The policy value is in the ordering. If the path holds, a reliable water pump or a backup generator may do more for children's distress than a new helpline, and that money is booked to water and energy, not to health. Testing it needs a longitudinal design linking heat days to clinic and hospital records and to school absence, not another round of interviews.

Resting-state fMRI in 186 people: bipolar depression with milder insomnia showed higher right-insula spontaneous activity, a pattern distinct from unipolar depression

BMC Psychiatry published the study online on November 15, 2026 (DOI 10.1186/s12888-026-08855-8). The sample comprised 79 patients with major depressive disorder (MDD), 56 with bipolar disorder depression (BDD) and 51 healthy controls, 186 people in total. Patients were stratified by the insomnia factor of the 17-item Hamilton Rating Scale for Depression into high- and low-insomnia subgroups: MDDHI, MDDLI, BDDHI and BDDLI.

Methods were resting-state functional MRI: static and dynamic amplitude of low-frequency fluctuation (s/dALFF), followed by seed-based static and dynamic functional connectivity, with Spearman correlations between abnormal indices and symptom severity. The authors propose that right-insula dynamics and its coupling with the posterior cingulate may distinguish insomnia-related mechanisms in bipolar from unipolar depression.

Why it mattersThe hard clinical question is usually not insomnia itself but whether a depressive episode is unipolar or bipolar, because the treatment and follow-up routes differ and a wrong turn is costly. The candidate marker here is the right insula and its connection to the posterior cingulate, but it is a group-level difference first: at the level of one patient the distributions will overlap heavily, so it cannot serve as a diagnostic. Splitting 186 people by diagnosis and by insomnia leaves only a few dozen per cell, the correlations are exploratory, and the effect may not hold. A 23-person pilot: four weeks of heartbeat-and-compassion training produced Hedges g = −1.42 for depression, in a study too small to cash that number

Scientific Reports published the trial online on November 15, 2026 (DOI 10.1038/s41598-026-78490-6), with Alessandra Anzolin as first author. Twenty-three people with depression were randomised to the Heart-Smile Training Intensive Introduction Program (HST-IIP) or a waitlist control, with EEG and clinical assessments before and after a four-week intervention. HST-IIP combines interoceptive attention training with compassion practices.

The endpoint structure is worth noting. The primary outcome was heartbeat-evoked potentials (HEPs), a candidate neural marker for interoceptive training. Depression severity, the main clinical outcome, was measured with the Computerized Adaptive Test-Mental Health (CAT-MH). Exploratory outcomes covered delta power spectral density during guided meditation, the MAIA-2 interoceptive awareness scale and the SCS self-compassion scale.

Depression improved more in the HST-IIP arm than in controls (Hedges g = −1.422, p = 0.028), and interoceptive awareness rose. On EEG, right-frontal HEP amplitude became shallower (less negative) during attention-focused meditation, and delta power fell during compassion-focused meditation; changes in both markers were associated with depression improvement. The paper describes itself as an exploratory pilot offering preliminary evidence, and argues for further research into interoceptive compassion training as a treatment path.

Why it mattersA g of −1.42 counts as a large effect in the psychotherapy literature, but a pilot with 23 people across two arms will almost inevitably produce an inflated one: the smaller the sample, the wider the sampling distribution of the effect, and positive results are the ones that get written up. What deserves more attention is the design choice — making an interoceptive measure the primary outcome rather than the depression score. That is sensible for a mechanism study and awkward for an efficacy study, because the primary endpoint is then not the thing patients care about. Settling whether the training works needs a trial with a hundred-plus participants, an active comparator such as relaxation training, and at least three months of follow-up. Until then it belongs in the hypothesis column.

Clustering 102,684 users of an AI mental health chatbot: 52.2% left early, 66.9% had overnight sessions, and depression gains followed engagement dose

Scientific Reports published the analysis online on November 15, 2026 (DOI 10.1038/s41598-026-78802-w). Emma C. Wolfe of the University of Virginia's Department of Psychology is first author, with co-authors from Slingshot AI, the company that builds the product, and NYU Grossman School of Medicine. The subjects were 102,684 real users of Ash, a purpose-built AI mental health chatbot.

The team ran K-means clustering across eight behavioural features and recovered five engagement phenotypes: early dropouts at 52.2%, weekly users at 25.3%, concentrated users at 16.8%, intensive users at 4.1% and power users at 1.6%. Across all users, 66.9% had at least one session between 9pm and 5am.

On the clinical side, subsamples completed the PHQ-9 (n=298), GAD-7 (n=298) and the MSPSS social support scale (n=194) at baseline and at three weeks, and 11,437 users completed the baseline Working Alliance Inventory. Pre-to-post changes were d = −0.51 for depression, −0.57 for anxiety and 0.22 for social support. Depression improvement followed an engagement gradient — d = −0.50 for intensive users and −0.57 for power users, against −0.22 for early dropouts — and the gradient replicated in 23,813 users using model-predicted PHQ-9 scores. Higher working alliance predicted larger improvement.

The authors set their own boundary: this is naturalistic correlational evidence with no randomisation and no control group, so improvement could reflect regression to the mean, self-selection or concurrent care, and their conclusion is that the findings are consistent with clinical benefit pending controlled designs. The conflicts of interest belong in the same paragraph: four of the six authors are employees of the company that builds the model, and one author's funding comes partly from that company and partly from the National Institute of Mental Health.

Why it mattersThe useful part is not d = −0.5 but the way "dose" gets taken apart. Session counts on their own did not predict improvement; overnight use, concentrated bursts and early exit describe three different kinds of user. For a product billed monthly or per seat, that means the headline activity metric will systematically overstate benefit. For a payer, the question becomes which users are actually being served, and whether they would have improved anyway. The other number that should not be skipped is the 52.2% early-dropout rate: in the real world most people open an app a handful of times and stop, and that is not the population that finishes 80% of sessions in a trial. A correlational design can go no further — a dose gradient is another way of saying that heavier users improved more, with direction of cause still unresolved.
