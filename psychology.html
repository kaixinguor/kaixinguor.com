5.2 万份伦敦女性病历里，非英国出生与更常经急诊进入精神科相关

研究 2026 年 11 月 27 日在线发表于《Social Psychiatry and Psychiatric Epidemiology》。第一作者 Patrick Nyikavaranda，单位包括伦敦大学学院与 Brighton and Sussex 医学院。研究用的是南伦敦与 Maudsley NHS 基金会信托的生物医学研究中心病例登记库。

样本是 18 岁以上的女性。她们在 2007 年 1 月 1 日至 2023 年 9 月 30 日间被成人二级精神卫生服务接收转介。共 52,536 人。16,648 人（31.7%）在英国以外出生。17,699 人（33.7%）由急诊路径进入。

校正年龄、居住安排与诊断等变量后，非英国出生女性经急诊转介的比值比 1.19（95% CI 1.14–1.25）。与白人女性相比，亚裔、黑人和其他族裔女性的急诊转介比值更高，混血/多种族女性更低。

迁移状态与族裔的交互项 p<.001。非英国出生的关联在白人女性中 OR 1.26（1.19–1.35）。混血/多种族女性 1.96（1.47–2.62）。其他族裔女性 1.77（1.42–2.22）。在亚裔与黑人女性中，这一关联不显著。

作者提醒，这是观察性登记数据，不能推出因果。出生国只是迁移状态的粗糙代理，无法区分迁移路径、年龄、居留身份、语言需求与既往就医经验。急诊转介也不等于延迟求助或强制治疗。

看点同一座城市、同一套专科系统，转介入口却随出生地和族裔变化。最该看的不是 1.19 这个总比值。要看它由哪些群体撑起来。白人与混血/多种族女性的差距更大，亚裔与黑人女性不显著，说明“移民”不是一个同质变量。对服务方，转介来源可以当成一个不花钱的公平性指标，按月按族裔与出生地拆开看。对政策方，若只按总人口配资源，会漏掉那些已经在急诊出现的人。登记数据能指出入口，不能解释为什么。

274 人四年追踪发现，大麻使用频率与精神病样症状相关，停用者症状降幅更大

研究 2026 年 11 月 27 日在线发表于《Psychological Medicine》。第一作者 Layla Sadek，单位是伦敦国王学院精神病学、心理学与神经科学研究所。研究追踪 274 名参与者四年。

招募时分为两组：139 名 16–17 岁青少年，135 名 26–29 岁年轻成人。基线的平均年龄分别是 17.1 岁和 27.5 岁。研究每 3 个月测一次，持续 1 年，之后在第 4 年再测。

大麻使用频率平均约每周 2 天。频率越高，大麻使用问题量表分数越高（b=2.05，p<.001）。精神病样症状也越多（b=1.05，p=.003）。空间工作记忆越差（b=−0.04，p=.038）。青少年的问题性使用分数持续高于成人（b=1.17，p=.001）。

在精神病样症状、抑郁、焦虑、淡漠与工作记忆上，年龄组与使用频率的交互项大多不显著。换句话说，青少年没有表现出更强的关联。4 年随访保留率 60.2%，第 1 年结束时为 85.7%。

153 人被分入大麻使用变化组，其中 17 人（11.1%）停用，18 人（11.8%）开始使用。停用者的精神病样症状降幅大于继续使用者（均差 −17.8，p=.004），抑郁降幅也更大（−8.8，p=.010）。

看点这篇研究最容易被误读成“青少年抽大麻没事”。它没有这么说。年龄交互项不显著，意思是青少年与年轻成人在同一频率下的症状关联差不多，不是青少年不受影响。真正有政策含义的是停用：17 人停了，精神病样症状和抑郁分数都降得更多。样本只有 274 人，四年后只剩 60.2%，且以白人为主。它提供的是关联与时间顺序，不是因果。对临床，问使用频率比问“有没有用过”更有用。对公共卫生，减少使用与提供停用支持，可能比单讲青少年脆弱更能落地。

1.7 万名芬兰年轻人中，低剂量抗精神病药标记更高精神病风险，工具变量不支持因果

研究 2026 年 11 月 27 日以接受稿形式在线发表于《European Psychiatry》。第一作者 Colm Healy 与通讯作者 Ian Kelleher 都在都柏林大学学院，合作者来自芬兰奥卢大学。

研究用芬兰全国登记数据，覆盖 1987–2005 年出生的所有人。分析样本是 17,099 名 13–25 岁因抑郁首次入院、此前没有精神分裂谱系诊断、也没有抗精神病药处方的年轻人。

作者先做常规分析，再用工具变量。工具是各医院辖区对阿立哌唑、奥氮平、喹硫平与利培酮的处方倾向。干预窗口按 1、2、3、4 年分别计算，结局是随访结束时以及干预后 1、2、5 年的精神分裂谱系诊断。

常规分析里，用药组的累积风险 11.95%，未用药组 1.43%，风险差 0.11（95% CI 0.10–0.11）。工具变量分析里，16 个估计有 15 个不显著。唯一显著的是 4 年治疗、1 年随访，风险差 −0.10（−0.22 至 −0.03），作者认为多重比较下更像偶然。

看点11.95% 对 1.43% 看起来像“抗精神病药让风险涨了八倍”。但这是把“谁被开药”当成了“药物效应”。开药本身标记的是一群症状更重、已经进入专科系统的人。工具变量利用的是医院辖区处方倾向的差异，15/16 个估计没有显著关系，说明因果信号很弱。它不能证明药物绝对安全，只能说明在这批以抑郁首次入院、没有精神病史的年轻人里，药物不是后来精神分裂谱系诊断的主要推手。真正的临床问题是：这 11.95% 的高风险组，目前没有有效的预防干预。

8,870 名焦虑患者的治疗数据把担心放回机制位置，躯体症状会反向作用

研究 2026 年 11 月 27 日在线发表于《Psychological Medicine》。作者团队来自赫尔辛基大学与 HUS 赫尔辛基大学医院等芬兰机构。研究分析的是 8,870 名接受治疗师指导的互联网认知行为治疗（iCBT）的广泛性焦虑障碍患者。

治疗共 12 次。78.2% 的样本是女性。第 1、6、12 次用 Penn State 担忧问卷（PSWQ）与总体焦虑严重度与损害量表（OASIS）测量。每次治疗都用 GAD-7 测担忧与躯体症状。

作者用了两种方法。非高斯依赖方向分析看 PSWQ 与 OASIS 的全局动态。连续时间结构方程模型看 GAD-7 各条目在治疗过程中的局部动态。

结果支持担忧到功能损害的方向。连续时间模型还发现双向交叉效应：担忧可能推高躯体症状，躯体症状又压低后续担忧。模型提示，未观察到的共同原因可能解释很大一部分动态。

看点这项研究没有比较治疗与不治疗，也没有说 iCBT 对谁都有效。它回答的是治疗里先动的是哪一环。如果担忧先于功能损害，那么把前几次会谈用在识别与打断担忧，比等到第 6 次再测量表更合理。反向路径也提醒：心悸、紧绷、失眠这些躯体症状会暂时压住担忧，制造“我好点了”的假象，随后担忧反弹。8,870 人很大，但仍是单组治疗队列，没有对照，机制结论只能算与数据一致。下一步该做的是把担忧作为中介变量，放进随机试验里检验。

20 名香港青少年说 AI 聊天机器人“安全但没有灵魂”，有人拿它做未经处方的临床指导

研究 2026 年 11 月 27 日以接受稿形式在线发表于《European Psychiatry》。第一作者 Michelle Sze Yiu Yu 来自香港大学医学院精神医学系。通讯作者 Ka Ying Heidi Lo 同时隶属该系与玛丽医院儿童及青少年精神科。

研究访谈了 20 名 15–18 岁的香港华人青少年。招募通过 Instagram 定向广告完成，时间是 2025 年 7 月 13 日至 10 月 23 日。纳入标准是至少 20 次为心理健康目的使用生成式 AI 聊天机器人。访谈 41–88 分钟，平均 61 分钟。

作者用反身性主题分析，得到四个主题：用与不用、安全但没有灵魂、使用中的担忧、未经处方的心理健康使用。青少年用 AI 寻求恋爱与同学关系建议、情绪宣泄，也用它自我激励。有人明确说，面对 AI 不用顾及对方感受。

“安全”来自保密与低负担。他们不想让朋友承担负面情绪，也不想让家人担心。“没有灵魂”来自非互惠：AI 没有语调、肢体接触与真实共情。有人形容这是“对月亮说话”。也有受访者担心依赖、社交退缩与情绪调节能力下降。

更值得警惕的是第四类使用：把通用 AI 当临床指导。研究没有测量症状变化，也没有测试风险识别。作者建议做基于证据的 AI 开发、临床评估使用模式，并建立监管框架。

看点这 20 个人不是“AI 依赖”的临床样本，而是能在 Instagram 上被招募到、已经用过至少 20 次的重度使用者。他们给出的最有用的信息是使用顺序。先找 AI，因为不用顾及对方感受。再发现它没有温度。最后有人把它当成未经处方的心理医生。风险不在单次回答，而在替代。当 AI 成为唯一不用付情绪成本的出口，真实关系就被跳过了。产品方该测的是“它有没有把人推回人”，不是“它像不像人”。监管方目前没有适应症，也没有转介标准。这份质性研究给不出疗效，但给出了需要监测的四种使用模式。

21 名 ADHD 儿童的 VR 试验完成率 91%，症状改善未跑赢主动对照

研究 2026 年 11 月 27 日在线发表于《Frontiers in Psychiatry》。第一作者 Barbara Remberk，单位是波兰精神病学与神经病学研究所儿童与青少年精神科。研究由华沙的 Unicorn Virtual Reality 公司参与开发。

研究纳入 21 名 6–14 岁、已确诊 ADHD 的儿童。他们被随机分到 VR 组（7 人）或主动对照组（14 人）。VR 组完成 8 次 Focus&Calm 训练，对照组完成 8 次以认知行为治疗为框架的治疗师带领课程。

完成率 91%。1 名儿童在第一次对照组课程后退出，原因不明。另 1 名在第一次 VR 课程后因头晕与头痛退出。两组在 ADHD 症状改善上没有显著差异。家长报告的 Conners 3 分数在全样本中有改善，但组间没有拉开。

自我报告的 Conners 3 与 d2 注意力测验没有显著变化。动机量表出现组间差异，但方向不一致，对照组在三个分量表上更高。作者提醒，样本太小，无法检测中等效应，结果只能算初步。

In 52,536 London records, non-UK birth is linked to more emergency entries into psychiatric care

Social Psychiatry and Psychiatric Epidemiology published the study online on November 27, 2026. The first author is Patrick Nyikavaranda of UCL and Brighton and Sussex Medical School. The team used the South London and Maudsley NHS Foundation Trust biomedical research centre case register.

The sample covers women aged 18 and over who were accepted into adult secondary mental health services between January 1, 2007 and September 30, 2023. There were 52,536 women. Of them, 16,648 (31.7 per cent) were born outside the UK, and 17,699 (33.7 per cent) entered through an emergency referral pathway.

After adjustment for age, living arrangements, diagnosis and other variables, non-UK-born women had an odds ratio of 1.19 for emergency referral (95 per cent CI 1.14–1.25). Compared with White women, Asian, Black and Other ethnic group women had higher odds. Mixed or Multiple ethnic group women had lower odds.

The migration-by-ethnicity interaction was significant (p<.001). Non-UK birth was associated with emergency referral among White women (OR 1.26, 1.19–1.35), Mixed or Multiple women (1.96, 1.47–2.62) and Other ethnic group women (1.77, 1.42–2.22). It was not significant among Asian or Black women.

The authors call the design observational and say it cannot establish causation. Country of birth is a crude proxy for migration status. It does not capture route, age at migration, residence status, language needs or earlier contact with care. Emergency referral also does not equal delayed help-seeking or compulsory treatment.

Why it mattersOne city, one specialist system, and the entry door still shifts with birthplace and ethnicity. The number to watch is not the overall 1.19 but the groups that produce it. The gap is larger for White and Mixed or Multiple women and absent for Asian and Black women, so “migrant” is not one category. For providers, referral source is a free equity indicator that can be split by ethnicity and birthplace every month. For policymakers, allocating by total population will miss the women who already appear in emergency departments. Registry data can point to the door. They cannot explain why she came through it.

11/27 Psychological Medicine (paper) ↗

Over four years, more frequent cannabis use tracked more psychotic-like symptoms, and those who stopped improved more

Psychological Medicine published the study online on November 27, 2026. The first author is Layla Sadek of the Institute of Psychiatry, Psychology and Neuroscience at King's College London. The team followed 274 participants for four years.

At recruitment, 139 were mid-adolescents aged 16–17 and 135 were young adults aged 26–29. Mean baseline age was 17.1 and 27.5 years. Participants were measured every three months for one year, then again at about four years.

Cannabis use averaged about two days a week. Higher frequency was associated with higher scores on the cannabis use disorder test (b=2.05, p<.001), more psychotic-like symptoms (b=1.05, p=.003) and worse spatial working memory (b=−0.04, p=.038). Adolescents had persistently higher problematic-use scores than adults (b=1.17, p=.001).

Most age-group by frequency interactions were null for psychotic-like symptoms, depression, anxiety, apathy and working memory. In other words, adolescents did not show a stronger association. Retention was 85.7 per cent at the one-year point and 60.2 per cent at four years.

Of 153 participants in a cannabis-change group, 17 (11.1 per cent) stopped using and 18 (11.8 per cent) started. Those who stopped had a larger fall in psychotic-like symptoms than those who kept using (mean difference −17.8, p=.004). Their depression scores also fell more (−8.8, p=.010).

Why it mattersThe finding is easy to misread as “cannabis is fine for teenagers”. It says no such thing. A null age interaction means the association at a given frequency looked similar in adolescents and young adults, not that adolescents were spared. The policy-relevant result is cessation: 17 people stopped, and both psychotic-like and depressive symptoms fell further. The sample is 274, retention at four years is 60.2 per cent, and participants were mostly White. The design gives association and timing, not causation. In clinic, asking about frequency is more useful than asking whether someone has ever used. In public health, reducing use and supporting cessation may travel further than a message about adolescent vulnerability alone.

The authors ran conventional analyses and then an instrumental-variable analysis. The instrument was each hospital district's propensity to prescribe aripiprazole, olanzapine, quetiapine or risperidone. Intervention windows ran from one to four years. Outcomes were schizophrenia-spectrum diagnoses at the end of follow-up and at one, two and five years after the intervention.

In the instrumental-variable analysis, 15 of 16 estimates were not significant. The one significant estimate covered four years of treatment and one year of follow-up (risk difference −0.10, 95 per cent CI −0.22 to −0.03). The authors attribute it to multiple comparisons.

Why it mattersThe raw contrast looks like an eightfold increase. It is not a drug effect. A prescription marks a group that is already sicker and already inside specialist care. The instrumental variable uses differences in prescribing habits across hospital districts. With 15 of 16 estimates null, the causal signal is weak. This does not prove the drugs are harmless. It does suggest that, in young people first admitted for depression with no prior psychosis, medication is not the main driver of later schizophrenia-spectrum diagnosis. In 8,870 GAD patients, worry predicted impairment, while somatic symptoms fed back into worry

Psychological Medicine published the study online on November 27, 2026. The authors are based at the University of Helsinki, HUS Helsinki University Hospital and other Finnish institutions. They analysed 8,870 patients with generalised anxiety disorder who received 12 sessions of therapist-guided internet-delivered cognitive behavioural therapy (iCBT).

Women made up 78.2 per cent of the sample. Worry and anxiety-related impairment were measured with the Penn State Worry Questionnaire (PSWQ) and the Overall Anxiety Severity and Impairment Scale (OASIS) at sessions 1, 6 and 12. The GAD-7 measured worry and somatic symptoms at every session.

The team used two methods. A non-Gaussian direction-of-dependence analysis modelled the global dynamics between PSWQ and OASIS. Continuous-time structural equation modelling modelled the local dynamics between GAD-7 items across treatment.

The results support a path from worry to anxiety-related impairment. The continuous-time model also found bidirectional cross-effects: worry may raise somatic symptoms, and those symptoms may then lower subsequent worry. The model suggests that unobserved common causes may account for a substantial part of the dynamics.

Why it mattersThis was not a trial of iCBT against no treatment, and it does not show that iCBT works for everyone. It asks which link moves first inside treatment. If worry precedes impairment, then spending the first sessions on identifying and interrupting worry makes more sense than waiting until session six to measure it. The reverse path matters too. Palpitations, tension and insomnia can temporarily suppress worry and create the impression of improvement, before worry returns. The sample is large, but this is still a single-arm treatment cohort without a control. The mechanistic conclusion is only consistent with the data. The next step is to test worry as a mediator inside a randomised trial.

Twenty Hong Kong adolescents describe AI chatbots as “safe but soulless,” and some use them for unbriefed clinical advice

European Psychiatry published the accepted manuscript online on November 27, 2026. Michelle Sze Yiu Yu is first author, in the Department of Psychiatry at the University of Hong Kong. Ka Ying Heidi Lo is corresponding author, affiliated with the same department and the child and adolescent psychiatry unit at Queen Mary Hospital.

The team interviewed 20 Hong Kong Chinese adolescents aged 15–18. Recruitment ran through targeted Instagram advertisements from July 13 to October 23, 2025. Eligibility required at least 20 lifetime conversations with a generative AI chatbot for mental health purposes. Interviews lasted 41–88 minutes, with a mean of 61.

Reflexive thematic analysis produced four themes: use and non-use, “safe but soulless”, concerns about use, and non-prescribed mental health use. Adolescents sought advice on dating and classmates, vented emotions and asked for self-motivation. Some said they did not have to manage the other party's feelings when talking to AI.

“Safe” came from confidentiality and low burden. They did not want to load friends with negative emotion or worry family. “Soulless” came from non-reciprocity: no tone, no touch, no felt empathy. One participant described it as talking to the moon. Others worried about dependency, social withdrawal and weaker emotional regulation.

The fourth theme is the most concerning: using general-purpose AI for clinical guidance. The study did not measure symptom change or test risk detection. The authors call for evidence-based AI development, clinical assessment of use patterns and a regulatory framework.

Why it mattersThese 20 people are not a clinical sample of “AI addicts”. They are heavy users who could be reached through Instagram and had used chatbots at least 20 times. Their most useful information is the sequence. They go to AI first because it costs no emotional labour, then find it cold, then some treat it as a clinical adviser. The risk sits in substitution, not in a single reply. When AI becomes the only outlet that charges no emotional cost, real relationships get skipped. Developers should test whether the bot sends people back to people, not whether it sounds human. Regulators still have no indication or referral standard here. This qualitative study cannot show efficacy, but it maps four patterns worth monitoring.

11/27 Frontiers in Psychiatry (paper) ↗

In 21 children with ADHD, a VR programme had a 91 per cent completion rate but did not beat active CBT on symptoms

Frontiers in Psychiatry published the study online on November 27, 2026. Barbara Remberk is first author, based in the child and adolescent psychiatry department at the Institute of Psychiatry and Neurology in Warsaw. Unicorn Virtual Reality, a Warsaw company, co-developed the programme.

The trial enrolled 21 children aged 6–14 with a confirmed ADHD diagnosis. They were randomised to VR (7 children) or an active control (14 children). The VR group completed eight Focus&Calm sessions. The control group completed eight therapist-led sessions built on a cognitive behavioural framework.

Completion was 91 per cent. One child withdrew after the first control session for unknown reasons. Another withdrew after the first VR session because of dizziness and headache. The groups did not differ significantly on ADHD symptom reduction. Parent-rated Conners 3 scores improved across the whole sample, but not between groups.

Self-reported Conners 3 scores and the d2 attention test did not change significantly. Motivation scores differed between groups, but not in a consistent direction: the control group scored higher on three subscales. The authors warn that the sample is too small to detect a moderate effect and call the results preliminary.

Why it mattersThis is not evidence that VR treats ADHD, and it is not a verdict that VR fails. With 21 children split 7 to 14, the trial could not reliably detect a moderate effect. The authors themselves frame it as a feasibility study. Its useful signal is adherence: 91 per cent finished eight sessions, so an immersive task can hold children. But the active control also finished eight structured sessions, and symptom improvement did not separate. Existing medication and psychotherapy were allowed to continue, so the trial tested an add-on, not a replacement. Being engaging did not automatically make the treatment better. Digital therapeutics often skip that step in the commercial story: first show that people finish, then show what the finished course adds. The next study needs adequate power, blinded assessment and follow-up, not a bigger headset.
