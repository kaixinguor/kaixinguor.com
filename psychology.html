约 2.3 万名成年人、平均 154 天智能戒指数据：睡眠时间越不规律，抑郁与焦虑症状越重

加州大学旧金山分校（UCSF）与加州大学圣迭戈分校的团队做了一项观察性分析，数据来自 TemPredict 研究的可穿戴设备记录：约 2.3 万名参与者，平均每人贡献 154 天睡眠数据，智能戒指根据动作与生命体征推断入睡与醒来时间，据此计算 0–100 的睡眠规律指数（Sleep Regularity Index，SRI）。抑郁、焦虑与孤独感由每月自评问卷测量。论文 11 月 12 日发表在《Scientific Reports》，第一作者 Severine Soltani（原加州大学圣迭戈分校生物信息学与系统生物学项目），资深作者 Ashley Mason（UCSF 精神病学与行为科学系）。参与者平均年龄 46 岁，女性略少于一半。

按症状严重度分层，SRI 单调下降：几乎没有抑郁症状者平均 84，轻度 83，中度 81，重度 75；焦虑正常者 84，重度焦虑者 79；孤独感偏高的人作息也更不规律。作者给了一个可读的换算：SRI 84 大致相当于每天的入睡与起床时间比前一天前后浮动约一小时，SRI 75 接近一个半小时。换句话说，症状最重与最轻两组之间，日常作息的不稳定程度差了约半小时。

方法上有一点值得记：研究者既用全部数据算 SRI，也做了一个「典型一周」的快照，结果是用长期数据时睡眠与心理健康的关联更强——此前多数研究只有一两周数据，可能把关联压小了。局限同样明确：这是横断面关联，作者自己说方向可能相反，睡眠不规律也可能是心理问题的结果；样本是自愿佩戴智能戒指的人，代表不了普通人群。研究由美国国防部医学技术企业联盟（MTEC）与 USAMRDC 资助，另接受 #StartSmall 基金会与 Oura 支持，Mason 与 Smarr 都披露了与 Oura 的顾问或财务关系。

看点效应量要按刻度读：SRI 差 9 分对应的是每天作息漂移半小时这个量级，说明「不规律」要成为临床变量，需要的是幅度足够大的漂移，而不是偶尔熬夜。这条研究真正改写的是测量窗口——用一周数据做的睡眠规律性研究会把关联压小，过去那些「关系不大」的结论，得先看它观察了多久。缺的一步是可干预性：把作息固定下来能不能改善情绪，目前没有试验证据；这类干预的成本几乎全落在个人时间安排上，不由医疗系统出。

11/12 University of Michigan School of Public Health（EurekAlert） ↗

67,477 名美国大学生：中度以上抑郁筛查阳性率降到 34%，但仍有 28% 处于进食障碍风险

密歇根大学公共卫生学院牵头的「健康心智研究」（Healthy Minds Study）发布 2025–2026 学年全国学生报告，样本为 107 所高校的 67,477 名学生。中度及以上抑郁筛查阳性率 34%，上一份报告为 37%；中度及以上焦虑从 32% 降到 30%；过去一年有自杀意念的比例从 11% 降到 10%。这是该项调查连续第三年改善。

改善不只在症状端。以关系、自尊、意义感与乐观衡量的积极心理健康，从 36% 升到 43%。需求仍然密集：四分之一的学生报告过去一年有过非自杀性自伤，28% 筛出进食障碍风险，三分之二说情绪或心理困难影响了近四周的学业表现。筛出抑郁或焦虑的学生里，61% 过去一年接受过咨询、精神科药物或两者；全体学生中 36% 接受过治疗、30% 服过药。障碍主要是时间（23%）、费用（19%）与不知道去哪（12%）。

一个要单独记下的数字：7% 的学生说过去一年曾从 ChatGPT 这类人工智能聊天机器人那里获得咨询或支持。报告同时调查了 16 所院校的 5,557 名教职员：48% 过去一年曾与学生一对一谈过心理或情绪问题，但只有约一半有信心识别学生的痛苦，16% 说支持学生已经损耗了自己的心理健康；教职员中 16% 筛出抑郁、16% 筛出焦虑、28% 报告高或很高的职业倦怠，只有 30% 认为学校重视员工心理健康，而 61% 认为学校重视学生心理健康。

看点连续三年下降容易被读成「危机过去了」，但这是校园自愿参与样本、不是概率抽样，筛阳性也不等于诊断；更稳的解读是：项目投入与求助去污名化同时推进，把「已被识别且愿意报告」的那部分人推到了前台。真正的新信息是 7%——它说明一部分学生的第一接触点已经不在咨询中心，而在一个没有临床监督、没有危机转介义务的界面里；校园要做的不是禁用，而是把「聊天机器人会把你推到哪」写进危机路径。教职员那组数字是成本问题：48% 已经在做一线工作，只有一半觉得自己能识别，倦怠率 28%，这部分劳动目前没有计入任何预算。

2018 到 2024 年，美国成年人过去一年报告遭遇性骚扰的比例从 10.7% 升到 24.3%

《美国公共卫生杂志》（AJPH）11 月 12 日在线发表的一项分析，比较了 2018 与 2024 两轮「#MeToo 全国性骚扰与性侵调查」：5,293 名成年人，加权后代表全国。过去一年报告遭遇过性骚扰的比例从 10.7% 升至 24.3%；男性从 6.7% 升至 15.2%，女性从 14.3% 升至 31.8%。调整社会人口学变量后，2024 年报告过去一年性骚扰的比值比是 2018 年的 2.80 倍。研究由杜兰大学纽科姆研究所 Anita Raj 等完成。

两轮调查的测量方式并不一致，作者在文中明确提示：2024 年间卷把网络骚扰拆成公共与私人网络空间两题，2018 年只有一题；2018 年的「过去一年」是根据终身经历与最近一次发生的时间推算的。地域上，美国南部的增幅大于其他地区；性别之间的变化幅度没有显著差异。作者的原话是，「无法确定这是事件真实增加，还是识别与披露的改善」。研究为横断面自报数据，不能推因果。

背景数字：2017 年 #MeToo 之后，25 个州通过了 80 多项反性骚扰法案，多数针对职场。而 2024 年的问卷覆盖公共场所、职场与网络空间，其中公共场所与线上并不在多数州的反性骚扰立法范围内。

看点六年里比值比 2.8 倍，很难全部用发生率解释——更可能是「什么算性骚扰」的定义、以及说出来的成本同时变了。对心理学研究这是测量学提醒：同一套题连续施测才有趋势可言，中途改题干（比如把网络骚扰拆成两题）会直接改动基线。对政策，含义是保护范围：已有立法集中在职场，而报告增长最快的场景需要固定的举报口径与年度监测。要把「披露增加」和「事件增加」分开，靠的是一个不随舆论改动的题库，外加一个独立的发病率锚点。

新南威尔士州 3 万余名产妇的链接数据：有智力障碍者围产期因精神健康住院的几率高 9 倍

有智力障碍者因精神健康原因住院的几率是无智力障碍者的 9 倍，因精神健康问题到急诊就诊的几率是 7 倍；差距在产后最初几周与几个月最大。研究团队称这是澳大利亚首个针对该人群的州级人口研究，并呼吁：建立无障碍、可协调的围产期精神健康路径，把产科与精神健康服务接起来，为临床人员提供培训，并把智力障碍父母写进国家围产期精神健康与产科指南的优先人群。

一位化名 Amanda 的智力障碍家长在新闻稿中说，希望专业人员知道「智力障碍父母可以做得很好，需要的是被听见，以便得到他们想要的、为家庭的支持」。这项研究给出的路径提示是：产检与产科病房是这类家庭最稳定接触医疗系统的窗口，而急诊与住院往往是漏掉之后才出现的地方。

看点9 倍这个数字有两层：一层是需求——智力障碍者本身精神障碍患病率更高、社会支持更少；另一层是识别与路径——围产期服务最容易漏掉这类需求，而漏掉之后的去处是急诊。可操作的指标是 7 倍的急诊率与「产后头几个月」这个时间点：出院计划里加一次无障碍的心理筛查（图文简易版、支持决策），成本远低于一次精神科住院。真正缺的是分母——登记数据只记录接触过服务的人，没被识别、没就诊的父母不在里面，所以 9 倍更可能是下限。

11/12 UC San Francisco (EurekAlert) ↗

About 23,000 adults with 154 days of wearable data each: less regular sleep-wake timing goes with worse depression and anxiety

Researchers at UC San Francisco and UC San Diego analysed wearable records from the TemPredict study: about 23,000 participants, each contributing an average of 154 days of sleep data. A smart ring inferred sleep and wake times from movement and vital signs, and the team computed a 0-100 Sleep Regularity Index (SRI) for each person. Depression, anxiety and loneliness came from monthly self-report surveys. The paper appeared in Scientific Reports on Nov. 12; Severine Soltani, formerly of UC San Diego's bioinformatics and systems biology programme, is first author, and Ashley Mason of the UCSF Department of Psychiatry and Behavioral Sciences is senior author. Mean age was 46, and just under half of participants were women.

SRI fell steadily with symptom severity: 84 on average for people with no or almost no depressive symptoms, 83 for mild, 81 for moderate and 75 for severe. Participants with normal anxiety averaged 84, against 79 for those with severe anxiety; lonelier participants also kept less regular schedules. The authors offer a translation: an SRI of 84 means bedtime and wake time shift by roughly an hour from the previous day, while 75 is closer to an hour and a half. Between the least and most symptomatic groups, the difference in day-to-day drift is therefore about half an hour.

One methodological detail is worth keeping. The team computed SRI from the full record and also from a "typical week" snapshot, and the associations were stronger with the longer data. Most earlier studies had one or two weeks of data, which may have pushed the association toward zero. The limits are equally clear: this is a cross-sectional association, and the authors note the direction may run the other way, with irregular sleep following poor mental health; participants were people who chose to wear a ring, so they are not representative. Funding came from the Medical Technology Enterprise Consortium and USAMRDC under the U.S. Department of Defense, with additional support from the #StartSmall Foundation and Oura Health; Mason and Smarr both disclosed consulting or financial relationships with Oura.

Why it mattersRead the effect size on its own scale: nine SRI points correspond to about half an hour of daily drift, which means irregularity only becomes a clinical variable when the drift is large, not when someone occasionally stays up late. What this study really revises is the measurement window. Sleep-regularity work built on a single week will understate the association, so earlier null-ish findings need to be re-read for how long they observed. The missing step is manipulability: no trial has yet shown that fixing sleep timing improves mood, and the cost of such an intervention falls almost entirely on the individual's schedule rather than on the health system.

11/12 University of Michigan School of Public Health (EurekAlert) ↗

67,477 U.S. college students: moderate-to-severe depression screens fall to 34%, while 28% remain at risk of an eating disorder

The Healthy Minds Study, led by the University of Michigan School of Public Health, released its 2025-2026 national student report, covering 67,477 students at 107 colleges and universities. Moderate-to-severe depression screens came in at 34%, down from 37% in the previous report; moderate-to-severe anxiety fell from 32% to 30%, and past-year suicidal ideation from 11% to 10%. It is the third consecutive year of improvement in the survey.

The gains are not confined to symptoms. Positive mental health, measured through relationships, self-esteem, purpose and optimism, rose from 36% to 43%. Need remains dense: a quarter of students reported non-suicidal self-injury in the past year, 28% screened at risk of an eating disorder, and two-thirds said emotional or mental difficulties had affected their academic performance in the previous four weeks. Among students who screened positive for depression or anxiety, 61% had received counselling, psychiatric medication or both in the past year; across all students, 36% had received therapy and 30% had taken medication. Time (23%), cost (19%) and not knowing where to go (12%) were the main barriers.

One number deserves its own line: 7% of students said they had received counselling or support from an artificial intelligence chatbot such as ChatGPT in the past year. The study also surveyed 5,557 faculty and staff at 16 institutions. Nearly half, 48%, had a one-on-one conversation with a student about mental or emotional health in the past year, but only about half felt confident recognising distress, and 16% said supporting students had taken a toll on their own mental health. Among employees, 16% screened positive for depression, 16% for anxiety and 28% reported high or very high burnout; 30% agreed their institution prioritised employee mental health, against 61% who said student mental health was an institutional priority.

Why it mattersThree years of decline is easy to read as the crisis being over, but this is a voluntary campus sample rather than a probability sample, and a positive screen is not a diagnosis. The safer reading is that programme investment and de-stigmatised help-seeking have moved the "already identified and willing to report" group to the front. The genuinely new information is the 7%: for part of the student body the first point of contact is no longer a counselling centre but an interface with no clinical supervision and no duty to refer in a crisis. Campuses do not need a ban so much as a written answer to where the chatbot sends someone at 3 a.m. The staff figures are a cost problem: 48% are already doing frontline work, only half feel able to recognise distress, burnout sits at 28%, and none of that labour appears in a budget line.

11/12 American Journal of Public Health ↗

Past-year reports of sexual harassment in the U.S. rose from 10.7% in 2018 to 24.3% in 2024

An analysis published online in the American Journal of Public Health on Nov. 12 compared the 2018 and 2024 waves of the #MeToo National Surveys on Sexual Harassment and Assault: 5,293 adults, weighted to national estimates. Past-year reports of sexual harassment rose from 10.7% to 24.3%. Among men the share went from 6.7% to 15.2%; among women, from 14.3% to 31.8%. After adjustment for sociodemographic characteristics, the odds of reporting past-year harassment in 2024 were 2.80 times those in 2018. Anita Raj of Tulane University's Newcomb Institute led the work.

The two surveys did not measure identically, and the authors flag it. The 2024 questionnaire split cyber harassment into public and private online spaces, while 2018 used a single item; the 2018 past-year estimate was derived from lifetime experiences and the timing of the most recent incident. Geographically, the increase in odds was larger in the U.S. South than elsewhere, and the change over time did not differ significantly between women and men. Raj's own reading: "It is unclear if this is a true rise in incidents or improved recognition and disclosure." The data are cross-sectional and self-reported, so no causal claim follows.

Some context: after #MeToo in 2017, more than 80 anti-sexual-harassment bills passed across 25 states, most of them focused on the workplace. The 2024 survey covers public spaces, workplaces and online settings, and public and online spaces fall outside most of that legislation.

Why it mattersOdds 2.8 times higher in six years is hard to explain as incidence alone; more likely both the definition of harassment and the cost of saying so have shifted. For psychological research this is a measurement warning: trends require the same items administered the same way, and changing a stem mid-series, as with the split cyber-harassment items, moves the baseline. For policy it is a question of scope: legislation has concentrated on workplaces, while the settings the survey covers most broadly have neither a reporting standard nor regular surveillance. Separating disclosure from incidence needs a fixed question bank plus an independent incidence anchor.

Linked data on more than 30,000 births in New South Wales: parents with intellectual disability are nine times as likely to be hospitalised for mental health care

A data-linkage study led by UNSW Sydney's National Centre of Excellence in Intellectual Disability Health, published in the Australian & New Zealand Journal of Psychiatry, analysed linked health records for more than 30,000 people who gave birth in New South Wales, including more than 2,200 with intellectual disability, from pregnancy through the first year after birth. Outcomes covered mental health-related hospital admissions, emergency department presentations, outpatient and out-of-hospital contacts, and psychotropic medication use. Rachael Cvejic is lead author.

People with intellectual disability were nine times as likely to be hospitalised for mental health reasons and seven times as likely to present to an emergency department for mental health care, with the widest gaps in the first weeks and months after birth. The team describes it as Australia's first population-level study of this group and calls for accessible, coordinated perinatal mental health pathways, integration between maternity and mental health services, training for clinicians, and recognition of parents with intellectual disability as a priority group in national perinatal mental health and maternity guidelines.

A parent with intellectual disability, identified as Amanda, said in the release that she wants health professionals to know that parents with intellectual disability can be great parents and need to be listened to so they can get the support they want for their family. The service implication is that antenatal visits and maternity wards are the most reliable point of contact these families have with the health system; emergency departments and inpatient units are where they surface once that contact misses them.

Why it mattersThe ninefold figure carries two things at once. One is need: people with intellectual disability have higher rates of mental disorder and thinner social support. The other is detection and routing, since perinatal services are precisely where this need is easiest to miss, and the emergency department is where missed need lands. The actionable numbers are the sevenfold ED rate and the early-postpartum window: one accessible screening conversation at discharge (easy-read format, supported decision-making) costs far less than a psychiatric admission. What is missing is the denominator, because registers only count people who reached a service; parents never identified and never seen are not in it, which makes nine times a floor rather than a ceiling.
