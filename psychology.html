11/17 Taylor & Francis Group（EurekAlert） ↗

3,777 名法国老人、30 年随访：只有长期孤独把痴呆风险推高 38%，偶发孤独没有关联

《Aging & Mental Health》2026 年 11 月 17 日在线发表（DOI 10.1080/13607863.2026.2733938）。第一作者 Yvanna Simon、共同作者 Karine Pérès 都来自波尔多大学波尔多人口健康中心（Bordeaux Population Health Centre），Pérès 的研究方向是老龄流行病学与孤独对健康的影响。研究用的是法国一项老年队列：1988 到 2018 年、3,777 名 65 岁及以上老人，整整 30 年。

第一步只用前 8 年，把重复测量到的孤独分成三条轨迹：「从不」占 48.3%、「偶发」（1 至 2 次）占 34.4%、「长期」（3 至 4 次）占 17.3%。第二步从 1995–96 年起算，1,540 人仍符合条件并进入随访，其中 36.4%（560 人）在此后二十年按标准医学诊断标准被判定为痴呆。

三条轨迹的痴呆发生率分别是 33.1%（246/743）、34.5%（183/530）与 49.1%（131/267）。与「从不孤独」相比，长期孤独组的痴呆风险高 38%，偶发组没有统计学上的显著关联。模型调整了年龄、性别、教育、居住状况、健康状况、用药与抑郁，排除了基线已有认知下降的人，并把死亡作为竞争风险记录下来。

两处口径要一并读。孤独只由一道题测量，分不出关系质量、也分不出有没有有意义的活动；另外，长期孤独组里有 29.6% 的人并不是独居——作者据此提醒，医疗与政策不该把「独居」当作社会风险的代理变量，照护负担、不令人满意的关系、缺少有意义的活动，都可能藏在同一间屋子里。

看点这条真正推翻的是一个线性假设：不是「越孤独越危险」，而是持续性才有信号，偶发孤独在这份数据里没有抬高风险。证据强度要分层看——8 年 4 次测量定轨迹、20 年结局、竞争风险与基线认知都处理过，比一次性横断面强很多；但它仍是观察性队列，反向因果排除不掉：临床前期的淡漠与社交退缩可能先于诊断若干年出现，调整抑郁只能减轻、不能消除这层混杂。真正能改变筛查的是测量方式：资源有限时，问「过去一年你多久感到孤独」比问「是否独居」更接近风险，而一次「最近有点孤单」不该进高危名单。
另一个视角把 38% 换成绝对数更清楚：长期孤独组二十年里 49.1% 得了痴呆，从不孤独组 33.1%，差 16 个百分点；WHO 预计 2050 年全球痴呆患者达 1.5 亿，落到具体国家，这 16 个百分点意味着预防窗口在 65 岁之前那 8 年，而不是确诊之后的照护床位。

11/17 University of Kansas（EurekAlert） ↗

6–17 个月婴儿的居家录像：情绪研究里那套夸张表情几乎不出现，快乐脸也只占看全脸时间的 12%

这项研究发表在《Communications Psychology》（Nature 旗下，文章页）上，由堪萨斯大学心理学助理教授 Katie Hoemann 领衔、同校的 Jeffrey Girard 共同主持，罗格斯大学参与数据采集与编码。它问的是一个很基础的问题：情绪研究普遍假设「大笑=快乐、皱眉=愤怒、夸张下垂=悲伤」这套面部配置是演化保留下来的通用信号，那婴儿在日常生活里到底见过多少次？

团队没有做新实验，而是分析一套既有的家庭录像：6 至 17 个月的婴儿，每月录一小时居家活动，逐段标注婴儿看到的脸与表情，再统计与理论假设「完全匹配」的面部配置占了多少时间。

结果是「很少」。完全匹配的配置出现频率很低，快乐是相对最常见的一种，但即便只算婴儿看到完整正脸的时间，也只占 12%；而婴儿看到完整正脸的时间本身就有限。同一组面部动作在不同情境里可以表示不同意思，同一种情绪也可以由许多不同的动作组合表达。

口径：新闻稿没有给出婴儿人数、录像总时长、编码者一致性，也没有跨文化样本；测量的是「暴露频率」，不是「婴儿能不能识别表情」。结论最后落在一句方法学提醒上——刺激材料如果在真实信息环境里几乎不出现，用它测出来的能力就要重新定义。

看点这条打的不是情绪理论，是测量效度。婴儿情绪识别研究几十年来的标准刺激，是一组在屏幕上被放大的静态表情；如果这套刺激在真实信息环境里占比只有一成上下，那么测出的组间差异更可能反映「对实验室材料的熟悉度」或「任务投入度」，而不是日常的社交理解。对三类人有直接后果：做婴儿眼动与脑电的研究者需要报告刺激的生态频率，否则跨研究比较没有意义；做家长指导与早教产品的人，「多给宝宝看夸张表情」缺少暴露基础；做发展筛查的人，把「认不出夸张的悲伤脸」当预警指标，假阳性会很高。缺口也很具体——这项研究没有把暴露频率与后来的语言、社会能力连起来，样本也不跨文化，所以它能不能改写教科书，要看有没有纵向数据接上。

120 人随机试验：先练 12 周再开始 CBT，高强度组的信息加工速度提升最大，认知获益到治疗期才巩固

这项国际临床试验发表在《Mental Health and Physical Activity》上。第一作者 André de Oliveira Werneck 同时在威斯康星大学麦迪逊分校与圣保罗大学流行病学研究中心（NUPENS-USP）任职，临床部分在柏林完成，由德国研究基金会（DFG）与 Dalla Vecchia 抑郁研究基金会支持，Werneck 的数据分析与撰稿工作由 FAPESP 资助。

设计是「先运动、后心理治疗」：120 名确诊抑郁障碍的成人入组，先做 12 周训练，再接 12 周认知行为治疗（CBT）。三组分别是高强度（最大心率的 55%–80%，骑行、跑步、循环训练）、低强度（20%–30%，步行、轻骑、拉伸）与对照组（前 12 周不运动）。期间做三轮体能测试、血液指标与神经认知评估。

结果：高强度组心肺适能提升最多，信息加工速度与视觉注意改善也最明显；两个运动组在特定记忆测验上差别不大。IGF-1（胰岛素样生长因子 1）随训练上升，与心肺适能改善同步出现。认知改善主要在 CBT 阶段巩固，且与第一阶段的体能与激素变化相关——作者的原话是，运动没有直接放大治疗的所有效果，而是「为认知的某些领域做了生物学准备」。

口径：120 人入组、74 人走完全部阶段，脱落约四成；样本是轻中度抑郁；新闻稿与摘要都没有给出认知结局的效应量与置信区间。Werneck 已在美国启动一项 100 人的新试验，改测「CBT 前 30 分钟运动」，用来比较两种排序。

看点这条的价值在排序，不在叠加。运动与 CBT 各自有效早有共识，真正没答案的是「先后」和「剂量」；把运动放在治疗之前，等于把运动当成进入治疗的准备状态，而不是另一种平行疗法。判断它能不能进指南，要看三件事。一是脱落：38% 的人没走完，而恰恰是抑郁最重、最需要干预的人最容易掉队，这会把效果高估。二是设计：三组最后都接受 CBT，没有「只做 CBT 不做运动」的对照，所以「运动放大 CBT」严格说只是相关。三是成本：12 周有监督训练要的人力与场地，换成直接多做 8 次 CBT，哪个更划算，这项研究没有回答。

11/17 University of Colorado Anschutz（新闻稿） ↗

20 人的双盲试验：25 毫克裸盖菇素在 8 周时改善快感缺失，但 1 周的主要终点没有分开

科罗拉多大学 Anschutz 医学院 2026 年 11 月 17 日公布这项研究，论文发表在《Journal of Affective Disorders》。20 名难治性重性抑郁（对既往治疗反应不足）成人随机、双盲接受 25 毫克或 1 毫克裸盖菇素，两组都配心理支持，在第 1 周与第 8 周评估快感缺失与总体抑郁严重度。

结果要分两层读。主要终点——第 1 周的快感缺失——两组没有统计学差异。到第 8 周，高剂量组的快感缺失改善显著更大，但这是次要分析。总体抑郁严重度上，高剂量组在第 1 周和第 8 周都更好。

口径：样本 20 人；1 毫克并不是惰性安慰剂——在致幻剂试验里，受试者往往能猜到自己拿的是低剂量，盲法容易破。研究方称这是首个专门针对快感缺失的裸盖菇素随机对照试验；作者 Andrew Novick 强调结论属初步，需要更大样本确认，也要看效果能否维持。

这项研究的位置因此有点微妙：它没有推翻什么，也没有确立什么，而是把一个笼统的结局拆开——难治性抑郁的「改善」，可能有一部分只发生在兴趣与愉悦这一层，而且比整体分数出现得更晚。

看点「主要终点没中、次要终点中了」这八个字，决定了这条该被读成信号而不是结论。20 人的试验里，第 8 周一个量表上的差别，两三个人的波动就能造出来；而 1 毫克对照组在主观体验上与 25 毫克差距明显，期待效应会系统性地偏向高剂量组。真正值得记的是它换了结局：从「抑郁总分」拆到「快感缺失」——这个症状对现有抗抑郁药反应差，也最影响功能恢复，如果它真的对致幻剂敏感，辅助治疗的选择顺序会变。但要让这句话成立，至少需要一次样本在两百量级、有活性对照、报告盲法评估与脱落、并随访三个月以上的试验。在那种证据出现之前，把它读成「难治性抑郁的一个可能的症状靶点」是上限。

WHO 欧洲办事处发布 2027–2035 数字心理健康路线图：把数字当默认入口，会先关掉最需要的人那扇门

2026 年 11 月 17 日，WHO 欧洲办事处与爱尔兰卫生部、HSE 发布《Digital mental health roadmap for the WHO European Region (2027–2035)》。文件在 11 月 16 日都柏林的一场高级别欧洲会议上启动（爱尔兰担任 2026 年欧盟理事会主席国）。WHO 明确说明：这是系统层面的治理框架，不评估、也不背书任何具体工具、App 或产品。

数字：区域内逾 1.43 亿人、也就是每 6 人中有 1 人，患有精神健康问题，而精神健康仍是卫生支出中投入最不足的领域之一。数字化的速度很快——2015 年只有 7% 的受调查国家报告建立了远程精神医学服务，到 2023 年这一比例是 42%。

文件点名了会被落下的人：某些残障人士与严重精神疾病患者、老年人，以及难民、农村居民、贫困人群——他们的数字技能更弱，发一台设备不等于能看上病。已经在运行的三例被写进文件：丹麦的全国互联网精神科提供免费文本治疗，针对轻中度抑郁与焦虑、无需转诊、30 天内开始，完成治疗者中 93% 症状下降；拉脱维亚在公立系统试点在线 CBT；苏格兰的数字治疗服务去年收到逾 7.6 万例转介，与面对面心理服务的量相当。

六项行动：把数字心理健康放进国家精神健康与数字转型议程；建立透明、可问责的治理结构；产品须符合安全、质量、伦理、证据与数据保护标准；建设可规模化的数字与数据基础设施；培养使用技能、文化与伙伴关系；让证据、数据与亲历经验进入决策。文件经 7 轮咨询（2025 年 4 月至 2026 年 5 月）形成，由欧盟资助。

看点这份文件真正改的是采购语言。过去买一个 App 看功能清单，现在要求证据、安全、数据保护三项同时达标，并且把「是否加深不平等」写进治理指标；对面向欧洲公共系统的数字疗法公司，报价里要能回答「不用数字工具的人怎么办」，否则在治理审查这一关就会被问住。但它的效力有限：路线图不是法规，六个行动领域没有量化目标、没有时间表、没有违约后果，能不能改变支付，取决于各国把哪几条抄进本国医保目录。验证方式很具体——一年后看丹麦、拉脱维亚、苏格兰这三套已在跑的服务，有没有被写进本国报销清单；如果还是靠项目经费，这份路线图就仍停在纸面。

马德里团队把推荐算法搬进心理健康：按心理画像推短视频，公司称 8 万人用过、80% 症状下降

看点推荐系统的目标函数和临床目标不是一回事。前者优化停留时长与完播率，后者优化症状与功能，而「什么内容让人愿意继续看」与「什么内容真的有用」经常相反——这正是这类产品最该被检验的地方。三个可核查的问题：那 80% 用的是哪张量表、谁填的；没坚持下来的人算不算进分母；系统识别出高危用户后，转介链条是否真的接得上。对支付方与雇主，这类产品的位置应该是「低门槛入口 + 转介漏斗」，该买的指标是转介率、首诊等待时间与高危识别的准确率，而不是自报改善比例；对监管，它一旦宣称改善症状，就该按医疗器械的证据标准来要求。

11/17 Taylor & Francis Group (EurekAlert) ↗

30 years, 3,777 older adults: only chronic loneliness raised dementia risk, by 38%, while occasional loneliness showed no link

The study appeared online in Aging & Mental Health on November 17, 2026 (DOI 10.1080/13607863.2026.2733938). Yvanna Simon is first author; Karine Pérès, a senior researcher in the epidemiology of ageing at the Bordeaux Population Health Centre, is a co-author. The data come from a French cohort of 3,777 people aged 65 and over followed from 1988 to 2018 — thirty years.

The first step used only the first eight years, sorting repeated loneliness reports into three trajectories: never lonely at any visit (48.3%), occasionally lonely at one or two visits (34.4%) and chronically lonely at three or four (17.3%). The second step began in 1995–96, when 1,540 participants remained eligible; 36.4% of them, 560 people, met standard medical criteria for dementia over the following two decades.

Dementia incidence ran from 33.1% (246 of 743) in the never-lonely group to 34.5% (183 of 530) among the occasionally lonely and 49.1% (131 of 267) among the chronically lonely. Against the never-lonely group, chronic loneliness carried a 38% higher risk; the occasional group showed no statistically significant association. Models adjusted for age, sex, education, living situation, health status, medication and depression, excluded anyone already showing early cognitive decline, and recorded deaths as a competing risk.

Two caveats belong with the numbers. Loneliness was measured with a single question, so it cannot separate relationship quality from a lack of meaningful activity. And 29.6% of the chronically lonely were not living alone — which is why the authors argue that living arrangements are a poor proxy for social risk when caregiving burdens or unsatisfying relationships can sit inside the same household.

Why it mattersWhat this displaces is a linear assumption: not "the lonelier, the worse", but "only the persistent kind carries a signal". Occasional loneliness raised no risk in these data. The evidence is stronger than a cross-section — four measurements over eight years to define trajectories, twenty years of follow-up, competing risks and baseline cognition handled — but it remains observational, and reverse causation cannot be excluded: apathy and social withdrawal in the preclinical phase may precede diagnosis by years, and adjusting for depression reduces that confounding without removing it. What could change screening is the measurement itself: with limited resources, "how often have you felt lonely in the past year" tracks risk more closely than "do you live alone", and one lonely month should not put anyone on a high-risk list.
Another angle Absolute numbers are clearer than 38%: 49.1% of the chronically lonely developed dementia over two decades against 33.1% of the never-lonely, a gap of 16 percentage points. With WHO projecting 150 million dementia cases worldwide by 2050, that gap puts the prevention window in the eight years before 65, not in the care beds that come afterwards.

11/17 University of Kansas (EurekAlert) ↗

In hour-long home videos, the exaggerated faces emotion research relies on almost never appear, and a happy face fills 12% of the time a baby sees a full face

The paper is in Communications Psychology (the article), led by Katie Hoemann, assistant professor of psychology at the University of Kansas, with Jeffrey Girard of KU as co-lead and Rutgers University handling much of the data collection and coding. The question is basic. Emotion research assumes that a handful of facial configurations — a broad toothy smile for happiness, a scowl for anger, an exaggerated frown for sadness — are evolutionarily conserved signals. How often do infants actually encounter them?

Rather than run a new experiment, the team analysed an existing dataset: monthly, hour-long home recordings of infants aged 6 to 17 months, coded for the faces and expressions each infant was exposed to, and then searched for exact matches to the hypothesised configurations.

The answer is rarely. Exact matches were scarce. Happiness was comparatively the most common, and even so it accounted for 12% of the time an infant spent seeing a full face — which is itself a limited share of the day. The same set of facial movements can mean different things in different contexts, and one emotion can be expressed through many different combinations of movements.

The qualifiers: the release gives no infant count, no total video hours and no inter-coder reliability, and the sample is not cross-cultural. What was measured is exposure, not whether infants can read expressions. The conclusion is a methodological warning — if a stimulus barely occurs in the real information environment, then whatever a test built on it measures has to be defined again.

Why it mattersThis is an argument about measurement validity, not about emotion theory. For decades the standard stimuli in infant emotion research have been enlarged, static faces on a screen. If that kind of stimulus accounts for roughly a tenth of the real information environment, a group difference is more likely to reflect familiarity with lab materials, or engagement with the task, than everyday social understanding. Three groups feel it directly. Infant eye-tracking and EEG researchers need to report the ecological frequency of their stimuli, or cross-study comparisons mean little. Parenting advice and early-years products promising that babies should see big expressions lack an exposure base. And developmental screening that flags an infant who does not recognise an exaggerated sad face will produce false positives. The gap is specific: exposure was never linked to later language or social ability, and the sample is not cross-cultural, so whether this reaches a textbook depends on longitudinal data that does not exist yet.

11/17 FAPESP (EurekAlert) ↗

A 120-person trial: 12 weeks of exercise before CBT, with the largest gains in processing speed in the high-intensity arm

The trial is published in Mental Health and Physical Activity. First author André de Oliveira Werneck splits his time between the University of Wisconsin–Madison and the University of São Paulo's Center for Epidemiological Research in Nutrition and Health (NUPENS-USP). The clinical work ran in Berlin with support from the German Research Foundation and the Dalla Vecchia Foundation for Depression Research; FAPESP funded Werneck's analysis and writing.

The sequence was exercise first, therapy second: 120 adults with a depressive disorder enrolled, trained for 12 weeks, then received 12 weeks of cognitive behavioural therapy. The three arms were high intensity (55–80% of maximum heart rate: cycling, running, circuit training), light activity (20–30%: walking, easy cycling, stretching) and a control group that did no training in the first phase. Three rounds of fitness testing, blood work and neurocognitive assessment ran alongside.

Those in the high-intensity arm gained the most cardiorespiratory fitness and improved most on information-processing speed and visual attention; on specific memory tests the two active arms differed little. IGF-1 rose with training, in step with fitness. Cognitive gains consolidated mainly during the therapy phase and tracked the fitness and hormonal changes from the first phase — in the authors' framing, exercise did not amplify every effect of therapy but acted as a biological preparation for particular cognitive domains.

The qualifiers: 74 of 120 completed every phase, so attrition was close to 40%; the sample had mild to moderate depression; and neither the release nor the abstract reports effect sizes or confidence intervals for the cognitive outcomes. Werneck has started a 100-person trial in the United States that gives exercise 30 minutes before CBT, to compare the two orderings.

Why it mattersThe value here is in sequencing, not stacking. That exercise and CBT each work is settled; what is not settled is order and dose. Putting exercise first treats it as preparation for therapy rather than a parallel treatment. Three things decide whether it reaches a guideline. Attrition: 38% did not finish, and the people most likely to drop out are the most depressed, which inflates the result. Design: all three arms eventually received CBT, so there is no therapy-only comparison, and "exercise amplifies CBT" is strictly correlational here. Cost: twelve weeks of supervised training consumes staff and floor space, and whether that buys more than eight extra CBT sessions is a question the study does not answer.

11/17 University of Colorado Anschutz ↗

A 20-person double-blind trial: 25 mg of psilocybin eased anhedonia at eight weeks, but the one-week primary endpoint did not separate

The University of Colorado Anschutz announced the findings on November 17, 2026; the paper is in the Journal of Affective Disorders. Twenty adults with treatment-resistant major depression were randomised double-blind to 25 mg or 1 mg of psilocybin, both alongside psychological support, and assessed for anhedonia and overall depression severity at one and eight weeks.

The results read in two layers. The primary endpoint — anhedonia at one week — showed no statistically significant difference between groups. By week eight the higher dose produced significantly greater improvement in anhedonia, but that was a secondary analysis. Overall depression severity favoured the higher dose at both one and eight weeks.

The qualifiers: twenty people, and 1 mg is not an inert placebo — in psychedelic trials participants can usually guess that they received the low dose, so blinding is fragile. The study is described by its authors as the first randomised placebo-controlled trial aimed specifically at anhedonia in treatment-resistant depression; Andrew Novick, the psychiatrist quoted in the release, calls the findings preliminary, pending larger samples and evidence that effects persist.

That places the study awkwardly: it overturns nothing and establishes nothing. What it does is split a composite outcome — part of what "improvement" means in treatment-resistant depression may sit in interest and pleasure specifically, and may arrive later than the total score does.

Why it matters"Primary endpoint missed, secondary endpoint hit" is the whole story, and it makes this a signal rather than a finding. In twenty people, a difference on one scale at week eight can be produced by two or three participants; and because 1 mg feels clearly different from 25 mg, expectancy runs systematically in favour of the high dose. What is worth keeping is the change of outcome: from a total depression score to anhedonia, a symptom that responds poorly to existing antidepressants and gates functional recovery. If that sensitivity is real, the order in which adjuncts are tried would change. Establishing it needs a trial in the low hundreds, with an active comparator, published blinding assessment and attrition, and follow-up past three months. Until then, the ceiling is "a possible symptom target in treatment-resistant depression".

11/17 22:12 WHO Regional Office for Europe ↗

WHO Europe publishes a 2027–2035 digital mental health roadmap, warning that a digital default shuts out the people who need care most

On November 17, 2026, the WHO Regional Office for Europe released the Digital mental health roadmap for the WHO European Region (2027–2035) with Ireland's Department of Health and the HSE. The document was launched at a high-level European conference in Dublin on November 16, during Ireland's presidency of the Council of the EU. WHO is explicit that this is a system-level governance framework: it does not assess or endorse any specific tool, app or product.

The numbers behind it: more than 143 million people in the region — one in six — live with a mental health condition, and mental health remains one of the most underfunded areas of health care. Digital delivery is spreading fast: in 2015 only 7% of surveyed countries reported an established telepsychiatry programme; by 2023 the figure was 42%.

The roadmap names who gets left behind — people with certain disabilities and severe mental health conditions, older people, and refugees, rural residents and people living in poverty. Their digital skills are weaker, and handing someone a device is not the same as giving them care. Three running services are cited: Denmark's national internet psychiatry service offers free text-based therapy for mild to moderate depression and anxiety without a referral and starts treatment within 30 days, with 93% of those who complete treatment seeing symptoms fall; Latvia is piloting online CBT in its public system; and Scotland's digital therapy services took more than 76,000 referrals last year, matching the volume of face-to-face psychology.

Six areas for action: place digital mental health inside national mental health and digital transformation agendas; build transparent, accountable governance; require products to meet agreed standards on safety, quality, ethics, evidence and data protection; build digital and data infrastructure that can scale; develop skills, culture and partnerships; and let evidence, data and lived experience drive decisions. The document went through seven rounds of consultation between April 2025 and May 2026 and was funded by the European Union.

Why it mattersWhat this changes is the language of procurement. Buying an app used to mean reading a feature list; the roadmap asks for evidence, safety and data protection at once, and turns "does this deepen inequity" into a governance metric. For companies selling digital therapeutics into European public systems, the pitch now has to answer what happens to the patient who will not use a digital tool, or the governance review will ask it for them. Its force is limited, though: a roadmap is not regulation, and the six areas carry no targets, no timetable and no consequences. Whether it moves money depends on how much of it member states copy into reimbursement schedules. The test is concrete — in a year, check whether the Danish, Latvian and Scottish services cited in the document appear in national benefit packages; if they are still project-funded, the roadmap remains on paper.

11/17 Universidad Carlos III de Madrid ↗

A Madrid team points a recommendation engine at emotional health, as the company reports 80,000 users and an 80% symptom drop

On November 17, 2026, the robotics lab at Universidad Carlos III de Madrid (UC3M) and Psiconnea, a Spanish psychotechnology company, presented EMOTINET: a streaming-style recommendation system rebuilt for mental health. It reads a user's psychological profile, in-platform behaviour and self-reported needs, then recommends pre-validated "micro-learning" videos aimed at easing anxiety and organising emotion.

The product has four layers: self-assessments to catch early distress; short videos and exercises the company says it has pre-validated; guided exercises and an anonymous peer community; and a referral prompt when a problem persists. José Carlos Castillo, of UC3M's social robotics group, is direct about the boundary — the app does not replace a professional, it is a first step, and unresolved problems are routed to a specialist.

Every number is the company's: Psiconnea CEO Rosa Becerril says more than 80,000 people have used the platform and that 80% of users reduce their levels of stress, anxiety or depression. The release itself is a university knowledge-transfer announcement, not a peer-reviewed paper.

The qualifiers have to be stated plainly: no control group, no indication of which scale produced the 80%, who filled it in, over what period, or how many people dropped out — and nothing connecting self-reported improvement to clinical remission.

Why it mattersA recommender's objective function is not a clinical one. One optimises time-on-task and completion; the other optimises symptoms and function, and what keeps people watching is often the opposite of what helps them. That is precisely what products like this should be tested on. Three checkable questions: which scale produced the 80%, and who answered it; are the people who quit in the denominator; and when the system flags a high-risk user, does the referral chain actually connect. For payers and employers, the right slot for a product like this is a low-threshold front door with a referral funnel, and the metrics worth buying are referral rates, time to a first appointment and the accuracy of high-risk detection — not a self-reported improvement rate. For regulators, the moment symptom improvement is claimed, the evidence bar becomes a medical device one.
