10/31 09:30 National Bureau of Statistics ↗

October PMI at 49.3: output and orders both fall, while prices stay in expansion

At 09:30 on October 31, the National Bureau of Statistics and the China Federation of Logistics and Purchasing published the October purchasing managers’ indices. Manufacturing PMI came in at 49.3, down 0.8 points; the non-manufacturing business activity index was 49.7, down 0.5; the composite output index was 49.7, down 1.0.

Inside manufacturing, the production index was 49.8 and new orders 49.4, down 1.9 and 1.1 points respectively — both sides of the ledger slowing at once. The bureau attributed part of the pullback to demand being pulled forward before the National Day holiday, set against a complicated external environment. Rail, shipbuilding and aerospace equipment and computer and communications electronics stayed at 53.0 or above on both measures, while textiles, non-metallic minerals and non-ferrous smelting sat below the threshold.

The split is stark. High-tech manufacturing held at 51.3 and equipment manufacturing at 50.6, both above the line; consumer goods fell to 48.8 and energy-intensive industries to 47.3, down 1.9 and 0.7 points. By size, large firms remained in expansion at 50.2, while medium firms fell to 48.4 and small firms to 47.5, down 1.3 and 1.4 points.

Prices are the most telling set. The input price index was 56.8 and the ex-factory price index 51.7; both eased by 4.0 and 2.3 points as some non-ferrous metals softened, yet both remain in expansion. Petroleum and coal processing, chemical fibres, rubber and plastics stayed above 60.0 on both measures — the bureau tied this to oil prices that have kept climbing.

In non-manufacturing, services held exactly at 50.0, construction fell 2.3 points to 48.0, expectations for services stood at 55.2 and for construction at 52.3. The bureau singled out finance as the main drag on services this month, with its business activity index falling sharply.

Why it mattersThe contradiction is in the price lines: demand is contracting, yet input prices are still 56.8 and output prices 51.7. Upstream costs have not come down with demand, so midstream and downstream firms face less volume at unchanged prices — revenue cools first, costs arrive later, and margin is squeezed from both ends. The gap between high-tech manufacturing at 51.3 and consumer goods at 48.8 shows where policy resources are pointing, and that household demand is recovering more slowly. The number to watch is not whether PMI returns to 50 but when the price indices approach the threshold, because that is when cost pressure has finally worked through.

10/31 15:20 National Business Daily ↗

Binance loses about $6.3 billion in a hot-wallet breach and promises to cover it

In a security update on October 30 local time, Binance said its hot wallet had been accessed without authorisation on October 29, and that roughly $6.3 billion of token assets were stolen, according to National Business Daily on October 31.

The response is to plug the hole with money. Binance said it will absorb the full loss through its Secure Asset Fund for Users and corporate funds, having already earmarked more than $6.34 billion — about $5 billion of it in the same tokens affected, covering roughly 80% of the loss. A further $3 billion of stolen assets has been frozen on-chain.

The technical picture is unresolved. The investigation is ongoing; no evidence of private-key compromise has been found. Early checks pointed to a compromised laptop, but other entry points were later identified. Withdrawals are paused, with a staged restart targeted within about 34 hours.

Why it matters$6.3 billion is not a number that compensation erases; it reprices custody. By absorbing the loss, the exchange internalises the cost of security, and that money has to come back through fees, reserves or stricter withdrawal checks. The attack path matters more than the total: no key leak, one suspicious laptop, then other openings — which suggests the weakness sits in operations and permissions rather than key storage. For the market, the effect is not one venue’s valuation but the risk premium on holding assets at an exchange. If that premium rises, share shifts between self-custody and regulated custody.

10/31 16:22 Cailian Press (CCTV Finance) ↗

42 listed banks: revenue up 7.79%, profit up 3.78%, margin at 1.55%

All 42 A-share listed banks had reported third-quarter results by the evening of October 30. Aggregated by Cailian Press on October 31: revenue for the first nine months totalled RMB 4,657.714 billion, up 7.79% year on year, and net profit reached RMB 1,746.790 billion, up 3.78%.

Net interest margin is the binding constraint. Of the 36 banks that disclosed it, the average for the nine months was 1.55%, and 18 were flat or higher year on year.

Read the two growth rates together: revenue rose 7.79% while profit rose 3.78%, less than half the pace. Balance sheets are still growing, spreads are not recovering, and the difference went to provisions and costs.

Why it mattersA bank income statement is a mirror of the rate environment: revenue can still be held up by volume, but profit has to absorb provisions first. With margin at 1.55% and only half the banks stable year on year, the decline in funding costs has not fully passed through to asset yields. For borrowers, that limits how much further lending rates can fall; to keep profit growing at a 1.55% margin, banks must push deposit costs lower or grow fee income. For savers, deposit rates still face downward pressure. The variable for the fourth quarter is provisions: if margin keeps narrowing and provisioning does not ease, the ordering of next year’s dividends and capital raising has to be redone.

Top-100 developers sell RMB 2.537 trillion in ten months; only six remain above RMB 100 billion

On October 31, the China Index Academy published its January–October 2026 developer sales review. According to Securities Times, sales at 100 developers totalled RMB 2,537.06 billion, with the year-on-year decline narrowing 0.9 percentage points from the January–September pace.

The number of survivors keeps shrinking: six developers exceeded RMB 100 billion in sales in the ten months, one fewer than a year earlier, and 58 exceeded RMB 10 billion, four fewer. The academy said the count of large developers continues to trend down.

A narrowing decline alongside a shrinking field points to concentration: in the same market, fewer firms held their scale, but those that did fell less sharply.

Why it matters“Narrowing decline” reads like recovery, but it comes with one fewer RMB 100 billion developer — the numerator is the leaders’ sales, the denominator is a smaller set of players. For developers, access to financing and land is concentrating further; for local governments, the buyer list for land auctions is shorter and bargaining power weaker. What decides the fourth quarter is not the year-on-year decline but how much longer cash collection takes as presale rules tighten. Sales are a paper number; collection is cash flow.

Northeast China trade hits RMB 995.26 billion, up 8.7%, with seven straight months above RMB 100 billion

Citing Shenyang Customs on October 31, goods trade in Northeast China reached RMB 995.26 billion in the first nine months, up 8.7% year on year and a record for the period.

The composition is balanced: exports of RMB 499.55 billion and imports of RMB 495.71 billion, roughly half each. Monthly growth has stayed positive all year, and monthly trade has exceeded RMB 100 billion for seven consecutive months.

Against a pressured national trade backdrop, the value of this curve is stability: not one category surging, but exports and imports moving together without a gap in the monthly series.

Why it mattersThe Northeast trades mainly with Russia, Japan, South Korea and the EU, and part of the 8.7% comes from routes being redrawn by geopolitics — same goods, different ports and settlement currencies. Exports and imports at almost exactly one to one reflect energy, grain and mineral imports alongside equipment, vehicles and electronics exports, a mix more resilient than a single-resource region. The question is not whether the full year tops RMB 1.3 trillion, but how long the run above RMB 100 billion a month lasts: seven months is resilience, twelve is a trend.

10/31 21:00 China Securities Journal ↗

ChangXin Memory talks long-term contracts: contract liabilities at RMB 7.04 billion

On October 31, ChangXin Memory Technologies published the record of its third-quarter results briefing. At the October 30 meeting, management said long-term supply agreements are progressing well, with several major customers in negotiation to lock in medium-term demand, and guided that gross margin should hold steady in the fourth quarter given continued tight supply and lean manufacturing.

Contract liabilities quantify the locking. At the end of September 2026 the balance was RMB 7.036 billion, nearly 15 times the RMB 447 million at the end of 2025. Cash received from sales of goods in the first nine months reached RMB 277.386 billion.

On margin: the nine-month gross margin was 86.85%, up 2.11 points from the first half, and the third quarter alone reached 89.53%, up another 1.94 points from the second quarter. The company attributed the improvement to product mix and higher selling prices.

By product, DDR revenue in the first half was RMB 69.472 billion, or 46.22%, and LPDDR RMB 78.191 billion, or 52.02%; DDR’s share rose through the nine months on the ramp of DDR5, in volume production since late 2024. Management expects revenue to grow again next year and sees automotive memory still undersupplied industry-wide in 2027.

Why it mattersThe point of a long-term contract is not a higher price but shifting cycle risk to the customer. Contract liabilities rising from RMB 447 million to RMB 7.04 billion means downstream buyers are prepaying to reserve capacity, moving part of the price exposure away from the memory maker. A 89.53% quarterly gross margin shows the price increases fell almost entirely to gross profit, but the 3% sequential gain in net profit says pricing has shifted from rising to holding. Locking long contracts is the best move at that point: write today’s high price into a contract rather than bet on the next leg up. The risk sits on the same page — if supply loosens and spot prices fall next year, the contract price becomes grounds for default or renegotiation.

10/31 15:57 Qilu Yidian (CCTV News) ↗

Gold jewellery down nearly RMB 550 a gram this year, and small pieces move first

CCTV News reported on October 31 that gold fell to a one-year low on October 30, pressured by higher oil prices, firmer US inflation and rising expectations of further Federal Reserve tightening. COMEX gold futures fell 3.63% to $3,884.90 an ounce and spot gold 3.61% to $3,875.19, both below $3,900.

Chinese retail prices followed: some brands have cut jewellery prices by nearly RMB 550 a gram from this year’s high.

The mix of buyers is shifting. Li Yang, a senior analyst at Caibai in Beijing, said younger shoppers account for about half of jewellery volume and prefer hard gold, traditional craftsmanship and enamelled pieces with cultural meaning; during wedding season, the traditional “three golds” and “five golds” sets take a clearly larger share. Investment demand is warming too, with one buyer purchasing five 30-gram bars at about RMB 26,000 each.

Shenzhen’s Shuibei market tells a similar story: wedding gold and zodiac novelty pieces lead, one store saying sales doubled from last month and another citing growth of 30% to 40%; sheep-themed pieces have overtaken last year’s horse designs at half of that category’s volume. Silver has fallen further — spot silver was around $53 an ounce on October 29, down more than 50% from its late-January high of $121.79.

Why it mattersWith gold down 30% this year, the first buyers are not bargain hunters but two well-defined use cases: wedding sets, and small, low-ticket everyday pieces. That shows pricing power at retail sits with the gram weight of a piece, not the gold price — shoppers look at what this item costs, not the per-gram rate. For brands, margin gets squeezed first: inventory was bought at higher prices while shelf prices follow the market, so the markdown lands on the company. On the investment side, bars selling means some money is treating gold as a hedge against rates rather than an ornament. The number to watch is whether gold holds $3,800, which decides fourth-quarter inventory write-downs for jewellers.
