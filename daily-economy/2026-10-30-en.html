Money & Policy

Liquidity
10/30 09:20 People's Bank of China ↗

RMB 1 trillion overnight: the PBoC bridges month-end without cutting rates

On October 30 the People's Bank of China ran RMB 15 billion of seven-day reverse repos by fixed-rate, quantity-tender auction at an operation rate of 1.40%, fully meeting primary dealers' demand, and at the same time RMB 1 trillion of overnight reverse repos. Securities Times reports that RMB 1 trillion of reverse repos matured the same day.

So the net injection was RMB 15 billion. The maturing trillion was rolled over almost entirely in the overnight tenor; the seven-day leg stayed small while the overnight leg went to a trillion.

Month-end is when funding traditionally tightens: tax payments, reserve requirements and month-crossing buffers land together. Choosing overnight rather than a reserve-ratio cut or the medium-term lending facility changes one thing — the tenor. The shorter the money, the faster it can be taken back.

It is not the first such operation this month. Open-market injection has leaned increasingly on tools of seven days or less, with longer money handled separately through outright reverse repos and the MLF.

Why it mattersProviding liquidity and easing are two different things. The first answers "is there cash to settle today"; the second changes the price of money. Doing the first with overnight paper keeps the decision daily — the central bank can decide again tomorrow. The cost is that the market has to re-borrow every day, and short rates get noisier. Noise is itself a cost.

10/30 Xinhua Finance ↗

The Bank of Japan holds at 1.25% on an 8-1 vote, confirms inflation is on target, and starts selling ETFs

The BoJ kept its policy rate at 1.25% on an 8-1 vote, as expected. Board member Hajime Takata proposed 1.5% and was outvoted.

The statement said underlying inflation is now consistent with the 2% target and is expected to stay near it, and repeated that rates will rise as the economy and prices warrant — but used "in a timely manner", signalling no fixed timetable.

It also warned, more directly than before, about inflation overshooting. Core-core CPI forecasts were nudged up to 2.7% for fiscal 2027 and 2.3% for fiscal 2028, with food prices attributed to Middle East tension and global weather.

A less-noticed decision: from January, ETF sales will run at about ¥860 billion a year, which would take roughly 43 years to unwind the holdings — down from a previously projected 112 years.

Why it matters43 years is the BoJ's honest quote for exiting unconventional policy: direction certain, speed glacial. It also shows that balance-sheet time and rate time are different clocks. Rates can move twice a year; a balance sheet moves over two generations.

Growth & Structure

Output, prices, credit
10/30 Xinhua Finance ↗

Inside the 3.4%: AI equipment carried US growth, net exports took 3.3 points away

US third-quarter GDP grew 3.4% annualized, up from 2.2% in the second quarter and above the roughly 3.2% consensus. The concentration of that growth is the more important detail.

Business investment rose 12.7% annualized, the strongest since 2020. Equipment spending contributed the most to GDP since 2011 (excluding the pandemic rebound), driven by AI infrastructure. But net exports subtracted 3.3 points, the biggest drag since early last year — data-centre equipment is imported, and firms pulled shipments forward ahead of tariffs. Inventories added 1.6 points, offsetting part of it.

Inflation did not cooperate. September PCE rose 3.6% year on year and 0.5% on the month, the highest since May; core PCE ran at 3.1%, a four-month high. PCE has now been above the Fed's 2% target for 68 straight months.

Households are thinning out. The savings rate fell to 3.8%, the lowest since October 2022. Real personal spending rose less than 0.1% in September. Retail sales rose 0.8%, but only 0.2% excluding petrol stations. Thirty-year mortgage rates sit at 7.24%, above 7% for six straight weeks, and the median home price fell for the first time in more than three years.

Why it mattersStrip out AI spending and this quarter is nowhere near 3.4%. Growth carried by one sector, imports dragging net exports, inventories plugging the gap — together that looks like a pull-forward, not a broad expansion. Alphabet's slide yesterday is the same fact from the other side: when growth depends on capex, the price of capex — the rate — becomes the denominator of the whole income statement.

10/30 Xinhua Finance ↗

Eggs back to RMB 4: a 28-month supply cycle and what it teaches farmers

The Ministry of Agriculture's monitoring put the national egg price at RMB 9.49 per kilogram in the week of October 16–22, down 3.4% on the previous week. Zhuochuang data put the main producing areas at RMB 4.07 per jin on October 28.

It fell faster than it looks. Before the National Day holidays the main regions averaged RMB 5.36 per jin; after the holiday demand lull, the price was RMB 3.73 per jin on October 19 — about a 12% monthly drop.

The cause sits in the supply lag. A chick takes about four months to start laying, and a flock runs from placement to culling in 550–600 days; the full capacity cycle is roughly 28–30 months. Early culling at around 500 days in late 2025 and early 2026 opened a gap, while chicks placed in the second quarter of 2026 reached peak laying exactly in October — and older hens were kept longer. Supply came back all at once.

Farmers did not rush back in. Several said the memory of last year's losses still shapes decisions: restocking depends on feed costs, chick prices and what the market will look like when the new flock lays. Some are already using egg futures and soymeal basis contracts to manage price risk.

Why it mattersThe cycle in farm prices is not "expensive this year, cheap next year" — it is "today's decision becomes supply 550 days from now". That lag means every high price carries the seed of the next low. Whether that can be broken depends on having tools that lock in a future price: futures, delivery, insurance-plus-futures. Small farms without access to those tools simply absorb the full amplitude.

Shanghai's credit grows RMB 96.9 billion, and the mix moves 10 points toward markets

The PBoC's Shanghai headquarters said on October 29 that social financing in the city rose RMB 96.9 billion in the first nine months of 2026.

Within that, RMB loans to the real economy rose RMB 44.58 billion. Direct financing rose RMB 36.05 billion, 37.2% of the increase and up 10.1 percentage points year on year.

Inside direct financing, corporate bond issuance rose RMB 26.61 billion, an extra RMB 12.01 billion versus last year; equity financing rose RMB 3.58 billion, an extra RMB 1.04 billion; local government bond financing rose RMB 5.86 billion, down RMB 5.36 billion year on year.

The other side is shrinking: entrusted loans, trust loans and undiscounted bankers' acceptances — the off-balance-sheet channel — added just RMB 4.94 billion combined, RMB 10.88 billion less than a year earlier.

Why it mattersThe same financing carries risk for different people depending on the route: a loan keeps the risk on a bank's balance sheet, a bond puts it in the hands of whoever buys it. A 10-point jump in direct financing and an RMB 10.88 billion drop in off-balance-sheet lending say money is moving from "the bank backstops it" to "the investor bears it". That is not good or bad by itself — it changes who prices the risk and who eats the loss when the price is wrong.

Who books the growth
10/29 23:32 China Fund News ↗

2,862 earnings reports, 1,332 with growth: optics up 200%, chip equipment up 5%

As of 20:00 on October 29, 2,862 companies listed in Shenzhen had reported third-quarter results; 1,332 of them posted growth — fewer than half.

Among the large caps that reported that evening, the AI compute and memory chain stood out. Innolight's nine-month revenue reached RMB 69.55 billion, up 178.13%, with net profit of RMB 22.24 billion, up 211.81%. Longsys made RMB 14.32 billion, up 1,909.68%. DapuStor's revenue rose 678.13% to RMB 9.84 billion and it swung to a RMB 2.54 billion profit.

But the same chain produced growth rates an order of magnitude apart depending on position. NAURA, the chipmaking-equipment leader, grew revenue 15.26% to RMB 31.47 billion while net profit rose just 5.10% to RMB 5.39 billion. Packaging and testing firm Tongfu Microelectronics lifted net profit 255.68% to RMB 3.06 billion.

Non-AI names grew too, only slower: Bank of Ningbo made RMB 25.15 billion, up 12.05%, with assets crossing RMB 4 trillion and a 0.76% bad-loan ratio; GF Securities made RMB 16.03 billion, up 46.58%, on brokerage, fund management and investment gains.

Why it matters"AI is driving growth" is a conditional statement. What ran hardest were the chokepoints — optics and memory — not the whole chain. Equipment grew 5%, meaning the capex money has not yet become equipment orders; brokers grew 47%, meaning trading is hotter than production. Put those three together and you get the real shape of this earnings season: growth concentrated in a few gatekeepers, everything else held up by turnover and spreads.

10/29 17:06 Xinhua Finance ↗

Foxconn Industrial Internet earns RMB 40.38 billion: shipments outran revenue

Foxconn Industrial Internet's third-quarter report shows nine-month revenue of RMB 941.41 billion, up 55.88%, net profit of RMB 40.38 billion, up 79.55%, and adjusted net profit of RMB 39.45 billion, up 82.15%. Third-quarter revenue was RMB 383.55 billion, up 57.73%, with net profit of RMB 16.63 billion, up 60.35%.

By segment, cloud revenue grew 75% year on year and 25% on the quarter. AI-ASIC server shipments rose 1.5 times year on year and 80% on the quarter; AI-GPU rack shipments rose 70% and 15%; 800G-and-above switch shipments rose 80% and 25%.

Cash flow turned as well: operating cash flow for the nine months was RMB 3.22 billion, against negative RMB 4.14 billion a year earlier, which the company attributed to faster collection. The stock closed at RMB 45.22 on October 29, for a market value of about RMB 897.3 billion.

Why it mattersA contract manufacturer sells capacity and delivery, and the buyer holds the pricing power, so "shipments up, price down" is normal. Which means you should not read revenue growth alone — read the gap between shipment growth and revenue growth. That gap is the discount given away. Here, AI-ASIC server shipments rose 1.5 times while total revenue rose less than 60%.
