The PBoC re-anchors policy at overnight: 1.25% reverse repos, every working day from November 2

At 5 p.m. on October 30, the PBoC’s open-market operations room issued Announcement No. 13 of 2026: to further improve the short-end interest-rate framework, open-market operations will change from November 2, 2026.

First, an overnight reverse repo will be tendered every working day at a fixed rate and quantity, with the current rate at 1.25%. Second, the seven-day reverse repo becomes a fixed-quantity, rate-based auction with multi-price allotment, used mainly around tax periods and month-end. Third, tools that previously added or subtracted a spread to the seven-day rate — including temporary repo and reverse repo, and the repo facility for foreign central-bank-type institutions — will be priced off the overnight rate instead, with the spread unchanged.

In practice, the daily price of money will be set at the overnight tenor, while the seven-day operation becomes a tool for tax dates and month-end. The transmission chain from the policy rate to market rates loses one maturity.

Why it mattersMoving the anchor changes more than a quoted rate: it changes who sets the daily price of money. Overnight funding matures and rolls every day, so the central bank can adjust daily and the market must re-borrow daily. Short-end volatility becomes more frequent, and liquidity management shifts from locking in a week to rolling a day. The pricing effects are concrete: negotiable certificates of deposit, short bonds and floating-rate loans all need a new reference, and the rate corridor moves with the spread. The real test comes at the first month-end under the new regime — if overnight rates jump at tax time, the framework will have to answer whether the tool was insufficient or transmission was blocked.

30-year T-bond futures drop 0.94% as ultra-long duration meets the new framework

Chinese bonds sold off sharply on October 30. Treasury futures fell across the curve: the 30-year main contract lost 0.94% to 117.42, its biggest one-day drop since March; the 10-year fell 0.21% to 109.84, the five-year 0.17% to 106.70 and the two-year 0.06% to 102.668.

Interbank cash yields rose 2–3bp across the board: the 30-year special treasury bond “26 Ultra-Long Special Treasury 04” rose 3.5bp to 2.088%, the 10-year CDB bond “26 CDB 05” rose 2.5bp to 1.683%, and the 10-year government bond “26 CGB 16” rose 2.3bp to 1.634%.

The move was not confined to rates. The CSI convertible-bond index fell 2.10% to 443.12 on RMB 70.7 billion of turnover, with Weidao and Kema convertibles down 15.32% and 15.28%. The PBoC injected a net RMB 15 billion; short-term funding rates rose.

Why it mattersThis looks less like a fundamentals-driven selloff than a position adjustment: ultra-long bonds had rallied hard all year, profit-taking clustered, and it landed just before the overnight framework took effect. The 30s/10s spread widened and AAA credit yields rose 5–7bp, so what was sold was not only the most expensive segment but duration along the whole curve. The question is not the one-day move but whether the long end can still lean on “asset scarcity” once the short end is anchored to a daily rate — if short-end volatility rises, carry trades in the long end unwind first.

10/30 Xinhua Finance ↗

New trust rules: no principal guarantees, no expected returns, no pooled funds

On October 30, the National Financial Regulatory Administration issued the Measures for the Administration of Asset-Management Trusts. Trust companies may not promise investors that principal will not be lost or guarantee a minimum return in any way, and may not set an expected rate of return.

The rules also ban four practices: using trust products to run investment-banking business that raises money for financiers; operating or joining pooled funds characterized by rolling issuance, collective operation and separated pricing; facilitating illegal or non-compliant activity by other institutions or individuals; and re-delegating management or ceding management responsibility to another institution or person.

Trust companies must also follow fair dealing and may not transfer benefits illegally between trusts, or between a trust and the company or a third party. Where other parties are lawfully engaged to handle trust affairs, the trust company must supervise them and cannot be relieved of liability by delegating.

Why it mattersPrincipal guarantees, pooled funds and channel business were the three legs of the trust industry, and one document removes all three at once. For borrowers, raising money through trusts gets more expensive and shorter-dated; for investors, the psychological anchor of an “expected return” disappears and products genuinely move to net asset value. Risk does not vanish, it changes address — from behind trust products to the holders of bonds and loans. The same day the exchange approved RMB 36.6 billion of developer project bonds: one door closes, another opens, and both point the same way — financing moves on-balance-sheet and from entity credit to project cash flow.

SOEs: revenue down 3.2%, profit up 3.0%, leverage up 0.4 points

The Ministry of Finance published January–September results for state-owned and state-controlled enterprises on October 30. Combined operating revenue was RMB 60.006 trillion, down 3.2% year on year; total profit was RMB 3.310 trillion, up 3.0%.

Taxes payable came to RMB 4.659 trillion, up 4.7% — faster than profit. At end-September the debt-to-asset ratio stood at 65.5%, up 0.4 percentage point from a year earlier.

With revenue falling and profit rising, the difference can only come from the cost side: lower operating costs, expenses or impairment charges, not stronger demand.

Why it mattersThe three numbers belong together. Profit growth of 3.0% on a 3.2% revenue decline means the improvement was saved, not sold; taxes growing 4.7% faster than profit means companies keep less of it; and leverage still rose 0.4 points, so the profit did not repair balance sheets. For the budget, SOE profit is one source of dividends; for banks, higher leverage means rollover and rate-cut pressure persists. Without revenue coming back, cost compression has a floor.

Euro-area growth slows to 0.4%: France contracts, Ireland grows 5.5%

Eurostat’s preliminary estimate, published October 30, put third-quarter euro-area GDP growth at 0.4% quarter on quarter, with the EU at 0.5%; in the second quarter the figures were 0.6% and 0.7%.

Year on year, the euro area grew 1.3% and the EU 1.5%. Among members with data, Ireland grew 5.5% quarter on quarter, the fastest, while Lithuania contracted 1% and France 0.1%.

The data landed a day after the European Central Bank left its three key rates unchanged, saying higher energy prices had increased both upside inflation risks and downside growth risks.

Why it matters0.4% is not a recession number, but it pushes the European recovery out by another quarter. The dispersion matters more: Ireland at 5.5% and France at -0.1% in the same table mean the average no longer describes any major economy. For China, Europe is an export market behind ASEAN and the US; a contracting France and a slowing euro area show up first in machinery, appliances and solar-component orders, then in freight rates and the currency. The external leg gets softer in the fourth quarter than it was in the third.

Brent above $117: imported inflation gets another notch

Oil rose on October 30. December WTI settled at $104.12 a barrel, up $2.12 or 2.08%; December Brent settled at $117.64, up $2.76 or 2.4%, Xinhua reported from New York.

Both benchmarks were for December delivery. Brent’s premium to WTI held above $13, a reflection of shipping and geopolitical risk pricing rather than a simple supply-demand gap.

Higher crude feeds first into the petroleum-processing and chemical-raw-material components of PPI, then into jet fuel, logistics and plastics; for households it shows up in transport fuel and the freight cost embedded in food.

Why it mattersChina is a net crude importer; every $10 on Brent adds several billion dollars to the annual import bill at current volumes. The distribution matters more than the level: when crude rises, refining margins are squeezed first because domestic fuel pricing follows a mechanism with a lag — upstream gains, midstream and downstream absorb. The energy component of US PCE already rebounded in September, and this move will reach China through import price indices. The number to watch is not the daily gain but whether Brent holds above $115; if it does, fourth-quarter PPI has to be recalculated.

RMB 36.6 billion of developer bonds approved: property finance moves to project-level accounting

China Resources Land and China Merchants Shekou have won registration approvals for corporate bonds totaling RMB 36.6 billion in planned proceeds, part of which will fund property projects that meet policy requirements, Securities Times reported on October 30.

The two deals were structured on a “project financing” logic, with RMB 14.05 billion earmarked for projects that comply with the Residential Project Standards. The projects are mainly in Beijing, Shanghai and Hangzhou and follow the “good housing” criteria of safety, comfort, greenness and smart features.

The backdrop: in August the housing ministry and others launched a reform of pre-sale management and pushed finished-housing sales, and the CSRC issued opinions on capital-market support for a new property development model. In the first three quarters, developers issued RMB 11.4 billion of commercial real-estate REITs and RMB 2.6 billion of inter-institutional REITs on the exchanges.

Why it mattersEntity credit and project credit are two different risk systems: one asks whether the parent will backstop, the other whether the project can repay on its own. Once the switch happens, a developer’s funding capacity depends less on ranking than on whether it holds projects that pencil out — location, absorption, and the capital tied up under finished-housing sales. That favors the largest players and raises the bar for smaller ones. The REIT channel is the exit: sell down existing assets to recover cash before starting new ones.

Foxconn Industrial Internet offers 12,000 staff a RMB 2 billion plan with a RMB 240 billion profit pledge

On the evening of October 30, Foxconn Industrial Internet (FII) published a draft 2026 employee stock ownership plan: up to RMB 2 billion raised, no more than 12,000 participants, shares bought in the secondary market at market prices.

For scale: net profit was RMB 35.29 billion in 2025 and RMB 23.22 billion in 2024. The stock closed at RMB 45 on October 30, down 46% from its 2026 high of RMB 83.99, for a market value of RMB 893 billion.

Why it mattersThe number that matters is not RMB 2 billion but RMB 240 billion: management is wagering three years of profit on the durability of AI server and rack orders. The contradiction is that the earnings are already here and the stock has halved — the market believes this year’s profit, not the three-year pledge. Contract manufacturing earns on the gap between volume and price; AI-ASIC server shipments rose 1.5 times while total revenue rose less than 60%, which is the price concession. The higher the target, the more it reads as a public endorsement of the capex cycle; and the deeper the lock-in, the slower the adjustment if the cycle turns.

CXMT: RMB 54.4 billion quarterly profit and an 89.53% gross margin — the memory cycle on paper

CXMT said on the evening of October 30 that first-nine-month revenue reached RMB 269.024 billion, up 738.51% year on year, with net profit attributable to shareholders of RMB 132.04 billion, turning profitable.

Third-quarter revenue was RMB 118.714 billion, up 613.18%; net profit was RMB 54.435 billion, turning profitable and up about 3% quarter on quarter. The quarterly gross margin was about 89.53%, up 1.94 points from the prior quarter. The company cited global DRAM shortages, higher volumes and prices, and lower unit costs.

R&D spending in the first nine months was RMB 11.071 billion, up about 73.03%; a DRAM product on its sixth-generation platform has completed engineering sample validation. Impairments on credit and assets totaled RMB 921 million.

CXMT plans to buy back RMB 1–2 billion of shares at no more than RMB 72.69 each, with at least 50% of the repurchased shares to be cancelled to reduce registered capital.

Why it mattersAn 89.53% gross margin says the price increase fell almost entirely to gross profit, with no matching cost increase. But profit rose only 3% quarter on quarter, which means prices have stopped rising and started holding — the most delicate point in a cyclical stock. R&D up 73% and sixth-generation samples out mean the next capex round is already under way; depreciation returns in about two years and compresses margins then. Cancelling more than half the buyback is cash for earnings per share, and a statement that the cycle has not peaked. The turning signal is not quarterly profit but contract prices.
