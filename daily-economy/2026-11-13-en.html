Money & Policy

货币与政策
11/12 13:08 Xinhua Finance ↗

CSRC: Rmb100bn of the third swap facility is deployed as long-term money buys over Rmb600bn of A-shares

Speaking at the Shanghai Stock Exchange's 2026 international investors conference on November 12, the vice-chairman of the China Securities Regulatory Commission opened with the macro anchor: gross domestic product topped Rmb107trn in the first nine months of the year, 4.6% more than a year earlier.

On funding, pension funds, annuities and insurers have raised both the size and the share of their equity allocations, buying more than Rmb600bn of A-shares since the start of the year. Dividends plus buybacks now exceed IPO and refinancing proceeds, and newly disclosed buyback plans, executed buybacks and shares cancelled all reached record levels. "Securities firms and fund managers have carried out Rmb100bn of the third tranche of the swap facility," he said.

Securities Times reported that 31 firms bid in the third tranche against 20 across the first two rounds, that the winning fee was 10bp versus 20bp in the first round, and that CICC estimates an all-in cost of about 1.5% for winning bidders, down from roughly 2%. By November 11 more than a dozen brokers, including Guolian Minsheng, Industrial, CITIC Construction, Guosen, Zhongtai, Zheshang, CITIC, Guotai Haitong, CICC, China Galaxy, Orient, CMS and Shenwan Hongyuan, had announced purchases.

The innovation and opening-up ledger was also on display: more than 600 STAR Market companies spent Rmb147.1bn on research in the first nine months, up 26%; QFII investors may now trade onshore treasury futures, and RMB treasury futures list in Hong Kong. The next steps are to refine the market-stabilisation mechanism, use both structural monetary tools to the full, widen the sources and channels of long-term money, and let margin trading lean against the cycle.

Why it mattersStabilisation now comes with three numbers that can be checked month by month: Rmb100bn of swap capacity is central-bank-funded collateral financing, Rmb600bn of net buying is long money already in the market, and dividends plus buybacks above IPO and refinancing turn the equity market from a net absorber of savings into a net payer. Together they change supply and demand: new listings and placements used to drain cash, while cancelled shares shrink the float and slow money adds to holdings. Watch the price of the tool. A 10bp fee and a 1.5% all-in cost mean the dividend yield on whatever gets bought has to clear that bar; once red-chip yields fall below funding cost, the facility stops stabilising the market and starts arbitraging it — which is exactly why the third tranche widened the eligible universe instead of deepening one trade.
The other viewOn accounting, "dividends and buybacks above IPO and refinancing" puts two ledgers side by side: dividends distribute profit, buybacks for cancellation cut registered capital and lift earnings per share. Both sit in financing cash flow and neither adds a productive asset. For valuation that raises the earnings attached to each share; for the macro picture it says corporate cash is moving from expansion to distribution, and the gap between that and manufacturing investment is a better indicator than the index level.

11/12 National Development and Reform Commission ↗

Petrol and diesel cut by Rmb645 and Rmb625 a tonne from midnight on November 12

The National Development and Reform Commission issued its fuel pricing notice on November 12: from midnight, retail prices of petrol and diesel at the standard grade fall by Rmb645 and Rmb625 a tonne respectively.

The notice spells out the arithmetic. Since the previous adjustment on October 29, crude prices in international markets drifted lower, leaving the average of the ten working days before this window below the average of the ten working days before the last one. The commission told PetroChina, Sinopec, CNOOC and other refiners to keep production, transport and supply steady, asked local authorities to step up price inspections, and reminded consumers that violations can be reported on the 12315 hotline.

Crude went the other way the same day. Xinhua reported that December WTI rose US$2.21 to US$88.74 a barrel, up 2.55%, while January Brent rose US$3.06 to US$95.82, up 3.3%.

Why it mattersThe mechanism matters more than the number. Retail prices are anchored to a trailing ten-session average, so a cut at midnight and a 3.3% jump in crude can both be true: the cut prices the past fortnight, the jump prices the next one. Three consequences follow. The cut reaches the transport-fuel line of CPI with roughly a two-week lag, so October's soft inflation print does not contain it yet. Refining margins are squeezed on the day, because product prices fall while crude is bought at current prices, trapping the difference in the middle of the chain. And because excise on refined products is levied per unit rather than per yuan, it is value-added tax that falls with the price — which is why the hit to the budget is smaller than the relief to households.

11/12 23:01 Cailianshe ↗

US existing-home sales drop to a 3.75m pace, the weakest since 2010

US existing-home sales ran at an annualised 3.75m in October, against expectations of 3.85m and a prior reading revised down to 3.97m from 3.99m, Cailianshe reported on November 12 — the lowest since 2010. The National Association of Realtors put the monthly decline at 5.5%.

The labour data released the same day was firmer. Initial claims for the week to November 7 came in at 200,000, against 195,000 expected and a prior figure revised to 191,000 from 189,000; continuing claims for the week to October 31 were 1.697m, against 1.71m expected.

According to Wallstreetcn, mortgage rates above 7% and sticky inflation are the binding constraint, with the 30-year rate having touched a near three-year high in early October. Supply and price tell the second half of the story: inventory rose 4.6% from a year earlier to 1.59m homes, or 5.1 months of supply at the current pace, the highest since late 2015, while the median sale price of US$416,700 was up just 0.4%. NAR chief economist Lawrence Yun said suppressed demand could be substantial once rates come down.

Why it mattersThe rate-sensitive sector is breaking first, but what is breaking is volume, not price — and the two adjustment paths carry different macro consequences. A frozen market clears through time; a price-clearing market gets there through discounts. Rising inventory with a median price that barely moves says the US is on the first path, with the cost carried by sellers who wait and agents who lose commissions rather than by lenders. For inflation, shelter is the largest component of CPI through owners' equivalent rent, so a frozen market pushes rents and then the index lower with a six-to-twelve-month lag: it will not rescue the December meeting, only the path through 2027. The real test comes in next spring's selling season, when pent-up listings hit the market if rates are still above 7%.

11/12 15:11 Xinhua ↗

Commerce ministry: a "30 billion for 30 billion" tariff-cut framework would steady China-US trade

Ministry of Commerce spokesman He Yadong told the ministry's regular November 12 briefing that a "30 billion for 30 billion" reciprocal tariff-cut arrangement would help stabilise and expand China-US trade, and that businesses on both sides expect a lot from it. The two governments remain in close contact on implementing the arrangement.

He said Beijing hopes Washington will work with it to bring the arrangement into effect quickly and to create better conditions for agricultural trade, and that the two sides are in close touch on trade, investment and other concerns, with progress to be announced in due course.

The design is reciprocal and matched in size: each side sets aside 30 billion of tariff reduction, swapping market access and trade volumes rather than making a one-way concession. No product list, tariff schedule, timetable or currency denomination was published at the briefing.

Why it matters"Matched size" is what shapes the negotiation: because both sides put up the same amount, each can sell the deal at home as reciprocal, and what actually changes hands is trade volume. Agriculture will move first — soybeans and corn are the most tariff-sensitive items on the cost side, and the pass-through runs through port stocks and feed margins before it reaches the food line of CPI about a quarter later. For the budget, tariff revenue falls but the tax base recovers; for exporters, the only thing priceable today is expectation, because two parameters — the list and the rate — are still missing from the model.

11/12 Xinhua Finance ↗

UK GDP grows 0.7% in the third quarter as services carry the economy

Britain's economy grew 0.7% in the third quarter from the previous three months, the Office for National Statistics estimated on November 12, faster than the 0.5% recorded in the second quarter and the strongest quarter since the first three months of 2024. Output was 1.9% higher than a year earlier, and September alone grew 0.2% against expectations of no change.

Services output rose 0.9%, led by business-facing services; production edged up 0.1% and construction fell 0.7%. Xinhua reported ONS chief economist Grant Fitzner saying computer programming recorded its fastest growth in three years and that "we continue to see evidence that artificial intelligence linked businesses helped boost this sector".

Rate expectations tightened behind the data. The Bank of England delivered its first hike in more than three years last week, and Xinhua Finance reported that investors now lean towards another increase in December, with the benchmark possibly rising three more times by July 2027. Sterling held just above a four-week low of 1.3251 against the dollar as the dollar index climbed to a 19-month high.

Why it mattersA 0.7% quarterly expansion gives the December meeting its justification, but underneath it is a two-speed economy: AI-related capital spending and professional services are carrying growth while construction, the most rate-sensitive and most labour-intensive sector, contracts. That puts the cost of another hike on small firms' investment and on housing supply rather than on the part of the economy that is growing — and both the British Chambers of Commerce and the CBI say investment is concentrated in a handful of large firms while most smaller ones have stopped. For the fiscal side, faster nominal GDP improves the debt-to-GDP denominator, which is the space the chancellor needs. For the Bank, services prices rather than excess demand are the source of inflation, and wages, not GDP, set the limit on how far this cycle goes.

ChiNext slides 2.44% as A-share turnover swells to Rmb1.93trn

A-shares reversed early gains on November 12, with ChiNext having risen almost 2% at the open before closing 2.44% lower. The Shanghai Composite fell 0.67% and the Shenzhen Component 1.68%. Combined turnover in Shanghai and Shenzhen reached Rmb1.93trn, Rmb255.8bn more than the previous session, and more than 4,600 stocks declined.

Computing chips ran against the tape: Cambricon added more than 8%, Enflame more than 13% to a record since listing, and Tianpu Gear held a third straight limit-up. Gas utilities churned higher, with Dongfang Huanyu on a fourth limit-up and Hongtong Gas at its cap, while retailers Guangbai and Ningbo Zhongbai both hit their limits.

Precious metals weakened, with Shanjin International, Chifeng Gold, Western Gold and Shandong Gold all lower, and AI-animation plays sold off: Shanghai Film hit its downside limit, Huace Film & TV fell more than 10% and Wento Holdings gave up its gains.

Why it mattersA four-fold gap between the index move and the breadth of decliners is the classic shape of weights defending the tape while small caps are distributed — the extra Rmb255.8bn was turnover, not new money. The same day the CSRC disclosed more than Rmb600bn of net buying by long-term money this year, but that is slow money rebalancing by quarter and will not absorb this kind of supply within a session; only read together do the two show the shape of the market, a floor under indices and none under individual stocks. Money rotated out of precious metals and AI applications into computing hardware and gas — from narrative to cash flow. Chips have orders and price letters; animation has a story. Gas has winter demand. For households, equity wealth is concentrated in a few industries and a few accounts, and this rotation produces no consumption.

Tinci to build a 500,000-tonne electrolyte plant for up to Rmb5bn, and buy back Rmb50-100m for cancellation

Tinci Advanced Materials disclosed on the evening of November 12 that its board had approved a 500,000-tonne-a-year electrolyte and ancillary materials project to be built by subsidiary Chizhou Tinci or its own subsidiary with self-raised funds. The plant sits in the Dongzhi Economic Development Zone in Anhui, runs 24 months, and carries a total investment of no more than Rmb5bn, of which construction accounts for up to Rmb3.5bn.

Output will cover electrolyte, lithium salts, LiFSI and sodium-battery electrolyte, feeding lithium-ion and sodium-ion cells for electric vehicles, storage and consumer electronics. The filing lists the risks plainly: industry cycles, a long approvals chain for hazardous chemicals, rival capacity landing at once, and substitution if solid-state or other new chemistries industrialise faster than expected.

The same board meeting approved a buyback of Rmb50m to Rmb100m at no more than Rmb45 a share, funded from cash and borrowings including bank buyback loans, with every share cancelled to reduce registered capital. It still needs shareholder approval. At the upper limit the company would repurchase about 2.22m shares, or 0.1090% of the 2.039bn shares outstanding.

On the balance sheet, total assets stood at Rmb34.305bn at September 30, with Rmb1.995bn of cash, Rmb21.029bn of net assets attributable to shareholders and a debt ratio of 38.22%. A Rmb100m buyback equals about 0.29% of total assets and 0.48% of net assets.

Why it mattersRmb1.995bn of cash against a Rmb5bn project leaves the gap to borrowing, and a 38.22% debt ratio is a starting point rather than an endpoint. A 24-month build means the capex straddles the lithium and electrolyte processing-fee cycle of 2027-2028, and electrolyte is a tolling business: whoever adds capacity first cuts his own price. Spending Rmb100m to cancel shares lifts earnings per share for shareholders; spending Rmb5bn on capacity answers customers. The two cheques point in opposite directions — one shrinks the share count, the other adds depreciation and interest. Watch two numbers: utilisation after ramp-up, and how far the debt ratio travels from 38.22%.
