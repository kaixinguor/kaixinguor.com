11/10 09:20 People's Bank of China ↗

Overnight reverse repos keep draining: RMB 190bn lent on November 10, with RMB 1.8tn of MLF and outright repos maturing in November

The People's Bank of China said at 09:20 on November 10 that it conducted RMB 190bn of overnight reverse repos through fixed-rate, quantity-tender operations at an unchanged 1.25%. Bids and allotments were both RMB 190bn, fully meeting the demand of primary dealers.

The previous day was a net drain. China Securities Journal reported that the central bank lent RMB 182bn overnight on November 9 against RMB 226bn maturing, a net withdrawal of RMB 44bn; short-tenor repos have now drained cash for several sessions, in line with the usual start-of-month pattern.

The calendar matters more than the daily print. Medium-term lending facility loans and outright reverse repos mature to the tune of RMB 1.8tn in November, so the rollover ratio is the number to watch. Net government bond financing is expected to exceed RMB 1.5tn, a clear step up from October, while maturing negotiable certificates of deposit and seasonal credit demand add to bank funding needs. The tax payment window runs from November 16 to 18.

The toolkit is being re-sorted. Since the policy rate re-anchoring, seven-day reverse repos have stepped back from being the daily workhorse to a fill-in tool for tax periods and month-end, while overnight operations handle routine liquidity and government bond trading handles the long end: net purchases of government bonds reached RMB 150bn in October, the highest since the tool was reactivated.

Why it mattersThe rate has not moved; the tenor has. The more routine liquidity is supplied overnight, the shorter banks' liability duration becomes, and the constraint shifts from price to rollover: one quiet day from the central bank is felt immediately. If none of the RMB 1.8tn is rolled over and banks absorb all of the RMB 1.5tn of net government bond issuance, the two together withdraw roughly RMB 3.3tn of reserves — on a RMB 300tn deposit base, close to what a 1.1 percentage point cut in the reserve requirement ratio would release. The rollover decision will therefore decide whether the 10-year yield keeps falling through November or stalls.
Another viewFor a bank treasurer the cost is not the 1.25% overnight rate but the certainty of having to roll it every morning; for a duration-taking bond fund, the leverage cost tracks that overnight rate, and a tax-period spike eats the carry quickly.

11/09 Ministry of Finance / PBOC ↗

Savings bonds on sale: 1.63% for three years, 1.7% for five, RMB 55.343bn across two tranches

The Ministry of Finance and the central bank published circular Cai Zhai [2026] No. 44 on November 9. The ninth electronic savings bond of 2026 carries a three-year fixed coupon of 1.63% with a maximum issue of RMB 24.904bn; the tenth carries a five-year coupon of 1.7% with a maximum of RMB 30.439bn. Together they total RMB 55.343bn, sold from November 10 to November 19, with interest accruing from November 10 and paid annually.

The allocation rules are precise. A single bond account may buy no more than RMB 500,000 per tranche; a dedicated pension quota equals 10% of the initial discretionary underwriting allocation; on November 10 electronic channels may sell up to 40% of a member's base allocation, unsold base and pension quotas are zeroed after the close on November 13, and the released amount becomes discretionary allocation available from November 14. CNR confirmed on November 10 that both tranches opened that morning.

Comparisons need the right base. The previous electronic tranches, sold from July 10, also carried 1.63% and 1.7%, with maximum issues of RMB 27bn and RMB 33bn, or RMB 60bn combined. So the coupons have not moved in four months, while this issue is RMB 4.657bn smaller than July's, a 7.8% reduction. October's sale was the certificate form (RMB 13.5bn plus RMB 16.5bn), a different channel that cannot be compared on size with the electronic product.

Early redemption is priced in the circular: from November 10, holdings of less than six months earn no interest; six to 24 months earn the coupon less 180 days of interest; 24 to 36 months less 90 days; and for the five-year tranche, 36 to 60 months less 60 days, with underwriters allowed to charge 0.1% of principal.

Why it mattersSet 1.63% and 1.7% against October CPI at 0.7% year on year and the real return is roughly 0.93 and 1.0 percentage points: that is the true price at which the government borrows household savings. The money moves from deposit accounts to the finance ministry's account and is recorded as government debt at a fixed, rigid cost. Unchanged coupons with a slightly smaller quota suggest a decision to keep the issuance rhythm rather than to pull in more savings. For banks, savings bonds substitute for deposits, and a five-year 1.7% will pull money out of long-term time deposits; for households, 1.7% locks in a nominal return that turns negative in real terms if inflation returns above 2%.

11/10 02:44 Xinhua ↗

Brent touches $92.80, down 5.61%, as markets price US-Iran talks

Xinhua reported from New York on November 9 that crude futures fell sharply in early trade, at one point more than 5%, before paring losses, after US President Donald Trump played down the prospect of a new large-scale military strike on Iran. As of 11:00 am Eastern time, December light sweet crude on the New York Mercantile Exchange had fallen as low as $86.19 a barrel, down $4.64 or 5.11%, while January Brent touched $92.80, down $5.52 or 5.61%.

The trigger was a single remark. In a telephone interview with CNN on November 8, Trump said the United States was "highly unlikely" to resume large-scale military action against Iran as long as talks make progress, while stressing that US forces in the Middle East were "ready" and not ruling out force.

Analysts disagree on supply rather than on the headline. Ole Hansen, head of commodity strategy at Saxo Bank, said expectations of a peace deal were pushing prices down and Trump's comments added pressure. EBW Group said it was sceptical that talks would produce a result, but that if Middle East crude exports stay strong the US may have little reason to restart military action. UBS analyst Giovanni Staunovo pointed to high Middle East export volumes and a soft physical market as separate drags.

This is the second such move in five sessions. Our November 5 issue recorded the November 4 settlement slump: Brent at $94.62, down 8.67%, and WTI at $88.12, down 8.29%. What is new today is a lower intraday print — Brent $92.80, WTI $86.19 — and a different trigger, from post-election bets on diplomacy to the talks themselves.

Why it mattersOil is the only price that reaches CPI and PPI within two months. October producer prices showed oil and gas extraction up 35.0% year on year and oil and coal processing up 29.9%, and that curve is what fed them. Brent moving from $94.62 to $92.80 trims the marginal cost of imported energy first, then works down the cost sheets of refiners, chemicals, airlines and logistics. Who pays: producer-state budgets and shale hedges if a deal materialises, and anyone shorting into it if talks collapse. On duration, the durability of this drop depends less on statements than on two things — whether Middle East export volumes fall, and when inventory restocking begins.

11/09 13:28 Securities Times ↗

October passenger car retail falls 27.1% to 1.643m units, with combustion models falling faster than the market

Data from the CPCA passenger car market committee shows national retail sales of passenger vehicles at 1.643m units in October 2026, down 27.1% year on year and up 1.5% from September. Retail sales for January to October totalled 14.978m units, down 22.3% year on year.

Break it down and new energy is the only support. The committee's earlier preliminary count for October 1-31 put new energy retail at 1.148m units, down 10% year on year and up 6% month on month, with cumulative retail of 8.907m units, down 12%. Wholesale of new energy vehicles by manufacturers was 1.807m units, up 12%, taking the year-to-date figure to 13.256m, up 10%.

Put the two together and the shortfall is almost entirely combustion: total retail down 27.1% while new energy fell only 10% means, arithmetically, that combustion models must have fallen by more than 27.1%. Wholesale also ran ahead of retail, by 659,000 new energy units, a gap that corresponds to exports and channel turnover; the CPCA flash note does not break it out.

A note on definitions: 1.643m is the committee's final monthly figure while 1.148m is the earlier preliminary count, so dividing them to say new energy is "about 70% of the market" is indicative only, not an official penetration rate. Nor is the CPCA measure the same as the CAAM measure, let alone insurance registrations.

Why it mattersUp 1.5% month on month and down 27.1% year on year says the October peak season only stopped the sequential slide; it did not repair the annual comparison. With the market down 27.1% and new energy down 10%, combustion is exiting faster, which shrinks the denominator for capacity utilisation. The cost lands in two places: fixed-cost absorption on joint-venture and combustion lines, and inventory plus interest on dealer balance sheets. For the budget, purchase tax revenue tracks combustion sales and is falling; upstream, the retreat hits parts makers and dealer cash flow first. Watch November and December: if new energy retail cannot return to growth, the 14.978m year-to-date base will be marked down again.

11/10 Shanghai Securities News ↗

Harvest Nasdaq-100 ETF closes 21% above NAV and is suspended until 10:30 on November 10

Harvest Fund said in a notice dated November 10 that the secondary market price of its Harvest Nasdaq 100 exchange-traded fund (QDII, ticker 159501) was materially above its reference net asset value. On November 9 the ETF closed at RMB 2.055 while the reference NAV (IOPV) at the close was RMB 1.6961.

On those two figures the secondary price sat about 21.2% above reference NAV. The fund was suspended from the open on November 10 and resumes at 10:30, with redemptions processed as normal during the halt. If the premium does not narrow effectively after resuming, the manager may ask the Shenzhen Stock Exchange for an intraday halt, an extension, or a series of suspensions.

It is not isolated. On the same disclosure page of the newspaper, Guotai's S&P 500 ETF (QDII) published its own premium warning. The notices also remind investors that secondary prices reflect supply and demand, systemic risk and liquidity as well as NAV.

The mechanism is simple. Primary creation in a cross-border ETF is limited by foreign exchange quota, so when quota is tight and subscriptions are restricted, buyers can only bid up the secondary market, and the price decouples from NAV. The notice does not disclose the fund's current subscription status, saying only that operations are normal and there is nothing else material to disclose.

Why it mattersA 21% premium is not a 21% gain in the portfolio; it is 21% paid for access. That money does not enter fund assets or the index constituents. It sits in the buyer's cost basis, and the buyer can only recover it by finding someone willing to pay a larger premium — a partly zero-sum position. A one-hour halt interrupts trading without changing quota-bound supply and demand, so the premium usually narrows after the reopen, and that narrowing is the loss of those who bought it. For the manager, the premium brings scale and attention but weakens the stability of the holder base; for regulators, how cross-border quota is allocated determines whether this premium keeps recurring.

11/09 21:45 Shanghai Securities News ↗

Wus Printed Circuit plans a RMB 500m-1bn buyback at up to RMB 156.70 a share, half for employee ownership

Wus Printed Circuit (002463.SZ) said after the close on November 9 that it plans to buy back A-shares on the centralised bidding market using its own funds or self-raised money, spending no less than RMB 500m and no more than RMB 1bn, at a ceiling of RMB 156.70 per share.

The use is split down the middle: half of the repurchased shares will fund an employee ownership plan or equity incentives, and the other half will be held to support the company's value and shareholders' interests.

At the ceiling price, RMB 500m buys roughly 3.19m shares and RMB 1bn roughly 6.38m, before accounting for actual execution prices during the buyback window.

Why it mattersWhere the cash comes from defines what the buyback is. Funding it from internal cash, or from new borrowing, moves money out of the capital expenditure and R&D pool and into the equity market; the RMB 156.70 ceiling is the price management is willing to pay and a public statement about intrinsic value. The more interesting half is the employee allocation: shares destined for ownership and incentive plans are effectively cash converted into compensation, expensed over the vesting period, and are not the same instrument as the half held for value. For shareholders the test is not the headline amount but the average purchase price, whether shares are cancelled, and whether capital spending is squeezed in the same period — those three decide whether earnings per share actually rise.
