US payrolls add 165,000 in October, unemployment falls to 4.1% and December hike odds reach 76.8%

The US Labour Department said on November 6 that nonfarm payrolls rose by 165,000 in October and the unemployment rate was 4.1%, down from 4.2% a month earlier. The gain topped the 48,000 monthly average of the past 12 months.

Revisions went the same way: August was lifted to 186,000 from 133,000 and September to 60,000 from 29,000, adding 84,000 jobs across the two months.

Hiring was concentrated in professional and business services (+70,000, after less than 10,000 the month before), health care (+42,000) and transport and warehousing (+25,000).

Pay accelerated: average hourly earnings for private workers rose 14 cents to $38, up 3.1% from a year earlier.

By the time the report was filed, CME's FedWatch tool showed a 76.8% probability of a December hike, up nearly 10 percentage points in a day. The Fed raised rates in September for the first time in more than three years and held in October.

Why it mattersThe report turns December from a political question into arithmetic: with wages at 3.1% and unemployment at 4.1%, the last mile to 2% inflation is set by pay, not by election results. The cost lands on floating-rate debt and mortgages repricing in 2027; for emerging markets, a 76.8% hike probability means another step up in dollar funding costs, starting at the short end.
The other view For compute investment, $38 an hour and 3.1% wage growth raise both the build and the operating cost of data centres, weakening the case for replacing labour with machines. If the Fed does hike, long-duration projects get another notch on the discount rate and every capex payback gets recalculated.

11/06 21:05 gov.cn ↗

State Council puts cash flow first: policy funds to reach the grassroots on time, market-stabilisation mechanism to be formalised

Premier Li Qiang chaired a State Council executive meeting on November 6 to study the leadership's read on the economy in the first three quarters and priorities for the remaining months, and to hear a report on social assistance.

The meeting called for faster progress on major projects and the "six networks", support for firms expanding equipment upgrading and intangible investment, and better links between online and offline and between urban and rural consumption. Policy funds must be used fully and paid out to grassroots governments and firms in full and on time, so that fiscal and financial policy reinforce domestic demand.

On risk, it asked for continued work on local-government debt and small financial institutions, and for improving the capital market's stabilisation mechanism so that equity prices are more stable internally and the market develops steadily. It also asked for early thinking on next year's economic priorities and better policy continuity across years.

The same day, CICC, China Galaxy, CITIC, Guotai Haitong, Orient Securities and China Merchants Securities said they had completed repurchase financing and made their first share purchases, several of them under the third batch of the central bank's swap facility. On November 2 the PBOC, the financial regulator and the CSRC held meetings on the swap facility and on the buyback-and-increase loan programme (per Shanghai Securities News).

Why it mattersThe order matters more than the wording: pay out first, then work down debt, then steady the market. That puts cash flow ahead of balance-sheet repair. The lag between Beijing's policy funds and the grassroots decides how much physical construction and how much corporate receivables turnover show up in November and December. Making stabilisation permanent shifts the cost first to institutions holding long-duration assets, and ultimately to the fiscal and central-bank balance sheets.

11/07 SAFE ↗

FX reserves reach $3.4052 trillion in October, up $4.9 billion as valuation offsets a stronger dollar

The State Administration of Foreign Exchange said on November 7 that China's foreign exchange reserves stood at $3.4052 trillion at the end of October, up $4.9 billion, or 0.14%, from $3.4003 trillion a month earlier.

SAFE attributed the rise to a stronger dollar and mixed moves in major financial assets, with currency translation and asset-price changes combining to lift the stock. It said a broadly stable economy and progress in high-quality development continued to support reserve stability.

Why it mattersA stronger dollar should have cut the dollar value of reserves through translation; instead the stock rose by $4.9 billion, which means non-dollar asset prices contributed positively. That is not capital inflow and should not be read as foreign money returning. Reserve valuation changes do not create central-bank liquidity either; what matters for interbank conditions is FX settlement and PBOC operations. Watch the dollar and Treasury yields in November: if the December hike lands, translation pressure returns.

Hong Kong stocks fall 1.27% on November 6 as the Hang Seng closes at 24,481.86

The Hang Seng Index fell 315.12 points, or 1.27%, to 24,481.86 on November 6, with HK$247.43 billion traded on the main board. The H-share index lost 94.98 points, or 1.13%, to 8,274.1, and the Hang Seng Tech Index fell 76.06 points, or 1.83%, to 4,078.23.

Why it mattersThe selling hit the denominator, not earnings: tech fell 1.83% against 1.27% for the index, so the longest-duration growth names took the damage. With HK$247 billion still changing hands while prices fall, the move looks like rotation rather than liquidation, with money going from tech into high-dividend banks and oil. For dividend assets the pricing anchor is the gap between yield and dollar rates; with December hike odds at 76.8% that gap is narrowing, which lowers the appeal mechanically rather than fundamentally.

October excavator sales rise 11.8% to 20,225 as exports of 10,724 top domestic sales

Chinese manufacturers sold 20,225 excavators in October, up 11.8% from a year earlier, according to the China Construction Machinery Association's survey of major producers.

Domestic sales were 9,501 units, including 183 electric machines, up 12.2%. Exports were 10,724 units, including 104 electric machines, up 11.4%.

On 20,225 units, exports were 53.0% of the total and domestic sales 47.0%. Electric machines accounted for 287 units (183 domestic, 104 exported), or about 1.4% of sales.

Why it mattersWith exports at 53%, pricing power sits with overseas infrastructure and mining capex, not Chinese property. Only 183 of 9,501 domestic machines were electric, about 1.9%, so electrification is early and the next leg of demand is replacement, not new starts. Whether 11.8% reflects a high base or genuinely weaker demand will only be clear with November data; on cost, steel prices and the exchange rate set margins, and a strong dollar is a headwind on export settlement.

US stocks split on November 6: Nasdaq up 1.09%, Dow down 0.46%, seven of 11 sectors lower

US equities closed mixed on November 6. The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 237.33 points, or 0.46%, to 51,285.73; the S&P 500 rose 23.13 points, or 0.31%, to 7,376.86; and the Nasdaq Composite rose 264.427 points, or 1.09%, to 24,579.42.

Seven of the S&P's 11 sectors fell, led by consumer staples (-1.21%) and financials (-0.68%), while technology (+1.26%) and communication services (+0.51%) led the gainers. Nvidia rose 2.78%, AMD 11.04%, Tesla 4.23% and SpaceX 8.62%.

A preliminary University of Michigan survey put November consumer sentiment at 51, above the 47.5 expected and October's 46.3, while 12-month inflation expectations fell to 4.3% from 4.7%.

Why it mattersThe index rose but the breadth did not: the Nasdaq gain came from heavyweights while seven S&P sectors fell. This is day two of the discount-rate trade — rate expectations moved up, yet money went back into the longest-duration technology, treating it as earnings certainty rather than a rate-sensitive asset. The real test is the December meeting: if the hike lands, multiple compression starts with mid-caps and low-cash-flow companies, not with Nvidia.

CNOOC signs $13.6 billion of CIIE purchase deals with 20 suppliers; nine-year total tops $100 billion

CNOOC signed import contracts and cooperation agreements worth $13.6 billion with 20 global suppliers at its CIIE procurement event in Shanghai on November 6, a record for the company.

The purchases span the oil and gas chain — crude, natural gas, deepwater equipment and technical services — as the mix continues to shift.

In nine years at the expo, CNOOC has struck more than 250 contracts and agreements with over 100 suppliers in more than 30 countries and regions, with a cumulative value above $100 billion. It has traded more than 1 billion tonnes of oil products and imported more than 240 million tonnes of LNG, making it the world's third-largest and China's largest LNG importer.

In the first nine months, net oil and gas production was 593.7 million barrels of oil equivalent, up 2.7%, with five exploration discoveries and nine new projects brought on stream.

Why it mattersThe $13.6 billion is a multi-year purchase commitment priced in dollars: it fixes import costs for coming quarters and moves currency risk from seller to buyer. LNG term contracts usually track oil with a slope, and with crude down from its October highs the next round of terms will be renegotiated. The real cost is not the headline number but the minimum take-or-pay volume — once the volume is fixed, the price matters.

CIFI units default on 7.12 billion yuan across 20 subsidiaries, all bank project loans

CIFI Group said in a filing that 20 subsidiaries had overdue debt totalling 7.1246 billion yuan as of November 2, 2026, including Liuan Jinjin Real Estate Development.

All of the overdue debt is bank project loans, the company said, blaming falling sales and cash collection in a weaker property market.

On bonds, the 22 CIFI Group MTN001 has been extended and several notes including HPR CIFI 1 have passed restructuring plans; outstanding credit bonds have been granted waivers on cross-default clauses or related default language.

Why it mattersThe 7.12 billion yuan sits in banks' project-loan books, not in public bonds, so the loss hits the non-performing loan ratios of the city and joint-stock banks that lent for construction before it hits bondholders. Waiving cross-default bought time: restructuring stretches principal over a longer duration and pays interest out of sales proceeds. If 2027 sales do not recover, a second extension only pushes loss recognition into another fiscal year. For the banks, the collateral is land and unfinished buildings, valued off the same local housing market they cannot diversify away from.
