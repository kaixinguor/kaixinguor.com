Democrats win both chambers of Congress: 258 House seats projected, at least seven Senate seats flipped

Democrats won control of both the House and the Senate in the November 3 midterms, according to projections, delivering a sweeping rebuke to President Donald Trump.

In the House, Democrats had secured 224 seats with 78 still undecided, against 133 for Republicans, according to the latest tally; 218 is the majority threshold. NBC projects they will finish with 258. In the Senate, they flipped at least seven Republican-held seats, more than the four-seat net gain needed.

Texas: James Talarico beat Attorney General Ken Paxton, becoming the first Democrat elected to the state’s Senate since 1988; Gina Hinojosa beat Governor Greg Abbott, the first Democratic governor in more than 30 years. Democrats also flipped Senate seats in North Carolina, Maine, Ohio, Iowa, Kansas and South Carolina, and Keisha Lance Bottoms won the Georgia governorship.

Trump acknowledged the result and said he would work with Democrats, while retaining the veto.

Why it mattersDivided government does not mean policy paralysis so much as a higher legislative threshold: tax, spending and the debt ceiling need bipartisan deals, while tariffs remain largely within executive power. Who pays: states, contractors and households dependent on federal spending feel a funding delay first; importers and consumers carry the tariff if it continues. On duration, the House is up every two years and the Senate map does not turn over again until 2028, so uncertainty has not disappeared — it has shifted from one-party control to mutual veto. What reprices is the deficit path and the legal basis for tariffs, not election-night sentiment.
Another angleFor importers, the uncertainty shifts from the tariff rate to its legal basis: Congress can legislate limits, while the president defends executive discretion with the veto. That is harder to price because it changes the duration of policy.

PBOC’s 30 billion yuan outright reverse repo is a rollover, not an injection: 50 billion matures, 20 billion withdrawn

The People’s Bank of China said on November 4 that it would conduct a 30 billion yuan three-month (91-day) outright reverse repo on November 5, allocated by fixed quantity, rate auction and multiple prices, maturing on February 4, 2027.

It is a shrinking rollover. Some 50 billion yuan of three-month outright reverse repos mature in November, so the operation withdraws a net 20 billion yuan; October’s operation added 20 billion. Jiemian News reported that the central bank chose to drain liquidity while the rate-anchor shift was under way and funding was already loose.

The money market had already moved. DR001 averaged 1.28% in October, 10 basis points below September, and fell to 1.2591% on November 4, the lowest in four months.

The framework changed the same month. From November 2, the PBOC moved overnight reverse repo to daily tenders at 1.25% and shifted seven-day repo to multiple-price auctions at tax and month-end dates. The main policy rate is moving to overnight, while outright reverse repo supplies medium-term liquidity.

Why it matters30 billion looks like an injection; the balance sheet says otherwise — 50 billion matures, 30 billion is rolled, 20 billion is withdrawn. That gap is the story: DR001 at 1.2591% is already low, and the PBOC still chose to shrink, a signal that it does not want the rate-anchor shift read as a rate cut. Who pays: banks get 20 billion less medium-term funding and must roll year-end needs through interbank deposits and overnight; for bonds, the three-month price matters more than the seven-day because it crosses New Year and sits near Spring Festival. On duration, the 91-day maturity is February 4, 2027; the PBOC is expressing its stance through tenor, not the rate. An outright repo transfers bond ownership, so the 20 billion cut is about quantity, not eligibility.

Services trade rises 8.5% to 5.77 trillion yuan as transport leads

China’s services imports and exports totaled 5.77 trillion yuan in January-September, up 8.5% year on year, the Ministry of Commerce said on November 4. Exports rose 14.8% and imports 4.8%, leaving a services trade deficit of 1.23 trillion yuan.

Transport led all categories with growth of 33.9%. Knowledge-intensive services accounted for 43.9% of the total and grew 7.4%.

The deficit narrowed because exports grew 14.8% while imports grew 4.8%, not because imports fell. Transport is a large import category, tied to goods trade and energy shipping volumes.

Definition: the figures are in renminbi, cumulative for January-September, and year-on-year. The improvement is a flow, not a stock; it says nothing yet about the durability of the export mix.

Why it mattersServices exports growing at three times the pace of imports is a rare external cushion for domestic demand. But the mix is narrow: transport depends on goods trade and rerouted shipping, while knowledge-intensive exports lean on a few fast-growing lines such as culture and insurance. Who pays: transport imports are large and tied to goods trade and energy shipping, so the cost lands on manufacturers and consumers. On duration, an improvement driven by freight prices fades with oil and rates; one driven by knowledge-intensive exports lasts longer.

Ninth CIIE opens with 4,140 overseas exhibitors and a record 440,000 square metres

The ninth China International Import Expo opens in Shanghai on November 5 with more than 4,140 overseas companies, participants from 162 countries, regions and international organizations, and an exhibition area above 440,000 square metres for the first time, according to CCTV.

More than 300 Fortune Global 500 and industry-leading companies have registered. Nearly 200 companies and institutions have attended all nine editions, more than 2,000 exhibitors are first-timers, and more than 480 products are expected to debut in China, Asia or globally.

This is an import platform: foreign exhibitors bring supply, buyers bring demand. The six-day show is the visible part; the orders and shelf space it produces are the real test.

Why it matters440,000 square metres and 4,140 exhibitors are a supply-side commitment; the price signal is in the 480 debuts — whether they can earn a premium in China decides how many companies return. Who pays: exhibitors front booth, logistics and compliance costs, buyers pay later with orders; if orders disappoint, the cost stays on the exhibitor’s books. On duration, the show lasts six days, but bonded warehouses, the debut economy and regional headquarters are year-round assets; the value is not the signed deal value but turning a fair into a channel.

Brent settles 8.67% lower at $94.62 as traders bet on an end to the Iran war

Oil fell sharply on November 4. December WTI settled $7.97 lower at $88.12 a barrel, down 8.29%; January Brent settled $8.98 lower at $94.62, down 8.67%. Both closed at their lowest since August 31.

The trigger was political. Vice-President JD Vance blamed the midterm defeat on the Iran war, saying “we lost the election because of this war”; investors bet the US may seek a diplomatic exit. A Democratic Congress could constrain funding for military operations in the Middle East.

The conflict is not over: Iran and the US remain in a standoff over the Strait of Hormuz, and Iran is on high alert. US crude inventories fell 865,000 barrels to 409.8 million last week, against expectations of a 877,000-barrel build, the EIA said.

Prices settled well above the intraday low, as buyers narrowed the loss into the close; the settlement is the number that feeds inflation and hedging, not the session’s worst print.

Why it mattersThis is not a demand collapse; it is the war premium coming out. Brent fell from above $100 to $94.62, and the Hormuz standoff has not ended, so the premium can return. Who pays: oil-exporting governments, shale producers’ hedges and investors who bought energy stocks high; consumers, airlines and logistics gain. On duration, a diplomatic deal would keep prices soft until inventories rebuild; a post-election statement alone would not. For inflation, oil is the only input that moves within two months, and the December FOMC will reprice along this curve.

Qualcomm revenue falls 8% to $10.32 billion as handset chips drop 26%

Qualcomm reported fiscal fourth-quarter revenue of $10.322 billion on November 4, down 8% year on year; non-GAAP revenue was $10.442 billion, down 7%; adjusted earnings per share were $2.17, down 28% but slightly above expectations.

Handset chip revenue fell 26% to $5.137 billion from $6.961 billion and missed the $5.25 billion consensus. Automotive rose 89% to $1.990 billion and IoT 4% to $1.888 billion; QCT fell 8% to $9.015 billion and QTL 7% to $1.304 billion.

Full-year automotive revenue was $6.004 billion, up 52%. Qualcomm expects non-handset revenue to grow more than 60% in fiscal 2027 and reiterated a $5 billion data-centre revenue target; first-quarter guidance is $10.4-11.2 billion in revenue and $2.15-2.35 in non-GAAP earnings per share.

Demand and cost are tightening at once. Counterpoint says global smartphone shipments fell 20% in the third quarter, the lowest for the period since 2012. Qualcomm said wafer, packaging and memory costs are rising across the board; it has raised prices, with the benefit reaching QCT gross margin in the second half of fiscal 2027. Apple’s in-house modem is cutting Qualcomm’s Apple revenue, and the first-quarter guide excludes potential Apple sales.

Why it mattersHandsets down 26% and autos up 89% are the two ends of one revenue curve: the handset is the stock, the car is the flow. What matters for pricing is cost pass-through — wafer, packaging and memory are all rising, Qualcomm is raising prices, but the benefit does not reach gross margin until the second half of fiscal 2027; the 18 months in between are carried by Qualcomm. Who pays: handset makers and consumers if the increases stick, Qualcomm if they do not. On duration, automotive design wins run five to seven years and the $5 billion data-centre target is a fiscal 2027 goal; both are longer than the handset cycle, and both require upfront investment.

October NEV retail falls 10% to 1.148 million while wholesale rises 12%

Preliminary CPCA data for October 1-31 show retail sales of new-energy passenger vehicles at 1.148 million, down 10% from a year earlier but up 6% from September; cumulative retail sales fell 12% to 8.907 million.

Wholesale volumes were 1.807 million, up 12% year on year and 8% from September; cumulative wholesale rose 10% to 13.256 million.

The 22-percentage-point gap between wholesale growth and retail decline has to go somewhere: dealer inventory or exports. The cumulative gap is similar: retail down 12%, wholesale up 10%.

Definition: these are CPCA preliminary figures for October 1-31, not CAAM data or insurance registrations. Wholesale is factory shipments; retail is the dealer end. The gap is not all inventory — it also includes exports and channel turnover.

Why it mattersWholesale up 12% and retail down 10% leave a 22-point gap that does not disappear; if it stays domestic, it lands on dealer inventory and cash flow. Who pays: factories ship to dealers and collect first, so the receivable sits with the dealer, who carries inventory, interest and discounts. On duration, if retail does not catch up in November-December, inventory days keep rising and manufacturers either cut output or deepen discounts; both compress margins. Exports are the third variable: how much of wholesale is export decides whether the gap is inventory or external demand.
