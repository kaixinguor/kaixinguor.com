11/03 Xinhua Finance ↗

RBA raises the cash rate to 4.85% on a 6-3 vote and lifts its inflation peak to 4.5%

Australia’s central bank raised the cash rate target by 25 basis points to 4.85% on November 3, with six of nine board members in favour and three preferring to hold at 4.60%. It was the fifth increase this year, after February, March, May and September, and takes the cash rate to its highest level since December 2008.

The bank marked its inflation path up across the horizon. Headline CPI is now expected to peak at 4.5% in the fourth quarter of 2026, ease to 3.7% by mid-2027, 2.7% by the end of 2027 and 2.4% by late 2028. Inflation stays above 3% through 2027, and the return to the top of the 2–3% band is about three quarters later than the August forecast.

It listed three sources of pressure: fuel and other commodity prices lifted by the Middle East conflict; domestic capacity running tighter than previously judged; and AI-related investment pushing up prices for technology goods. Many firms, it said, are already raising prices or planning to, and higher fuel costs are passing into other goods and services.

Growth and employment are set to absorb the adjustment. GDP growth is forecast at 1.6% in late 2026 and 1.3% in late 2027; unemployment rises from 4.7% in late 2026 to 5.1% in late 2028, a bigger increase than the August forecast. Housing prices have fallen sharply and unemployment has risen, the statement said, while business investment is stronger, including data-centre spending.

Before the meeting, Australia’s September CPI was running at 4.3% year on year, up from 4.0% in August, and the trimmed mean at 3.6% from a revised 3.5%. The Australian dollar fell after the decision, Xinhua Finance reported.

Why it mattersThe 25 basis points are the least interesting part; the balance-sheet signal is the news. Three of nine votes wanted a hold and the statement offers no explicit next-step guidance, which shifts duration risk on the rate path from the central bank back to mortgage holders and firms. Australian housing prices have already fallen sharply and unemployment is rising; 4.85% only reaches existing loans as they reprice, so much of the cost lands in 2027. With the inflation peak marked up to 4.5% and the return to target three quarters later, the high-rate regime lasts longer: the fiscal interest bill, bank margins and household repayments all get recalculated over a longer horizon.
Another angleWriting “AI-related investment” into the inflation case is a new attribution: compute capex is now competing with households and manufacturers for power, equipment and land, which lifts the cost curve for data-centre operators rather than merely changing one project’s valuation.

11/03 17:35 Xinhua ↗

Financial work report to the NPC: 33 trillion yuan of new social financing and 247.2 billion yuan of IPO proceeds

The State Council’s report on financial work was submitted to the 25th session of the 14th NPC Standing Committee on November 3. It sets out five next steps: keep monetary policy appropriately loose, strengthen supervision, improve how finance serves the real economy, deepen reform and opening, and defuse risks in key sectors.

The figures: social financing rose by 33 trillion yuan between October 2025 and September 2026. Over the same period, 88 companies listed on the Shanghai and Shenzhen exchanges raised 247.2 billion yuan, nearly 90% of them high-tech firms. At the end of September, loans under the “five major articles” framework were up 10.1% year on year, and the bond market’s technology board had issued 3.3 trillion yuan of tech bonds since its launch in May 2025.

The risk numbers are starker. By the end of September, the number of remaining local-government financing vehicles was 92% below its March 2023 level, and outstanding operating financial debt was down 79% by the end of June, People’s Daily reported.

On property, banks have approved more than 8.4 trillion yuan under the whitelist, and the report cites a lower minimum down payment for commercial property purchases, a mortgage subsidy for households and the takeover of Zhongbang Bank.

The next-step instruments include a mix of tools to keep liquidity ample, further review of the monetary-policy framework, better statistics and assessment for the five articles, more support for the six networks, technology and small firms, wider international use of the renminbi through a self-controlled cross-border payment system, a crackdown on illegal finance and more use of AI in supervision.

Why it matters33 trillion yuan is a twelve-month flow, not a balance growth rate; the 247.2 billion yuan of IPO proceeds is less than 1% of it. The harder numbers are on the stock side: financing vehicles down 92% from March 2023, operating debt down 79%, and more than 8.4 trillion yuan approved under the property whitelist. Debt resolution has moved from naming entities to restructuring the stock, and the price is a longer duration and a lower yield on bank assets. The part that changes bank behaviour is the statistical and assessment framework for the five articles: once loans are classified and scored, capital charges, tolerance for bad debt and branch targets follow. Who pays: depositors through low deposit rates, banks through net interest margins, and the fiscal authority as the final guarantor.

11/03 12:30 People’s Bank of China ↗

PBOC runs a 100 billion yuan SFISF operation at a 10bp fee, with 31 bidders

The People’s Bank of China’s open-market operation room said on November 3 that it had conducted a securities, fund and insurance company swap facility (SFISF) operation worth 100 billion yuan. The facility was allocated by fee auction, with 31 securities and fund companies bidding and a winning fee of 10bp.

It was the third operation since the facility was created in October 2024 and the largest: the single injection came close to the first two combined. Bidders rose to 31 from 20, and the 10bp fee was half the 20bp charged in the first operation, Shanghai Securities News reported.

Disclosed allocations show large and mid-sized brokers moving together: Guotai Haitong and CITIC Securities took 10 billion yuan each, Guotou Securities 6 billion, Soochow 5 billion, Great Wall 2 billion and Huafu 1.2 billion; the disclosed total alone exceeded 34 billion yuan. GF, Orient, China Merchants, Ping An and Guolian Minsheng also won allocations without disclosing amounts.

The facility had 105 billion yuan outstanding before this operation. Buyback and increase-in-holdings loans have signed about 440 billion yuan and disbursed more than 280 billion, with the relending rate cut from 1.25% to 1%. Mechanically, institutions pledge bonds, equity ETFs and CSI 300 constituents for government bonds or central-bank bills and then fund themselves in the interbank market; a 10bp fee puts the funding cost close to the risk-free rate.

Why it mattersA 10bp fee is a price, not a quantity. SFISF does not create base money; it changes the asset structure of non-banks — equities become government bonds, government bonds become repo funding — shortening duration and raising liquidity, with leverage set by the haircut. The disclosed 34 billion yuan is only part of the 100 billion yuan operation, and Guotai Haitong and CITIC Securities took 10 billion each, which says quotas still follow balance-sheet size and collateral rather than being spread evenly. Who carries the risk: the central bank holds the equity, the institutions hold the repo liability, and the ultimate exit is interbank liquidity; a fall in equity prices cuts collateral value first and the usable quota second. Read with the cut in buyback relending to 1% the day before, the common thread is cheaper institutional funding, not a direct change in household or corporate balance sheets.

11/03 14:28 China Federation of Logistics & Purchasing ↗

Logistics confidence slips 0.3 points to 51.2% as new orders rise for a second month

China’s logistics confidence index came in at 51.2% for October, down 0.3 percentage points from September and above the 50% line for a sixth month, the China Federation of Logistics and Purchasing said on November 3.

The volume index was 51.2%. New orders rose 0.3 points to 51.5%, a cumulative gain of 0.7 points over two months. The business-expectations index was 55.3%, its third month above 54%, and the fixed-asset investment index 55.4%.

CFLP chief engineer Liu Yuhang pointed to consumer and industrial demand reinforcing each other: the long National Day and Mid-Autumn holiday lifted travel and services spending, while bulk energy, smart equipment and new-energy vehicles drove freight. A CFLP analysis published the same day put the sector’s main-business profit index at 47% for October, up 0.3 points but still below 50%, with the express-delivery volume index above 65% for a fourth month.

Firms reported that fuel and management costs are still rising, though more slowly, and the profit index improved across large, medium, small and micro enterprises.

Why it mattersAt 51.2%, the index is only 1.2 points above the boom-bust line — expansion, but thin. The structure carries more information: new orders at 51.5% are above volume at 51.2%, and expectations at 55.3% and investment at 55.4% are above current activity, so firms are betting on the year-end. Yet the main-business profit index is 47%, still contracting: volumes are rising, margins are not. The squeeze is easy to see: fuel costs are rigid, while freight rates are set by shippers’ tenders, so operators front the diesel bill and recover it from drivers and depreciation. On duration, holiday spending is one-off; if new orders do not carry into November, the expansion looks fragile, while continued growth in express delivery and EV logistics would give the restocking cycle a longer tail.

October account openings fall 35% while broad ETF turnover jumps 42% in a day

New A-share account openings were 1.5006 million in October, down 21.51% from September and 35.04% from 2.3099 million a year earlier, according to Shanghai Stock Exchange data. Openings in the first ten months of 2026 totalled 28.6265 million, up 27.46% year on year.

Money took another route on November 3. Turnover in the 387 broad-based ETFs came to 183.955 billion yuan, up 54.436 billion yuan or 42.03% from the previous session and 2.31 times the 79.71 billion yuan daily average of the prior 30 sessions, Securities Daily reported. Six broad ETFs traded more than 10 billion yuan each.

The CSI 300 ETF (Huatai-PineBridge) traded 19.007 billion yuan, the STAR 50 ETF (ChinaAMC) 22.456 billion and the ChiNext ETF (E Fund) 19.511 billion. The SSE 50 ETF (ChinaAMC) traded 12.905 billion yuan, up 397% from 2.596 billion the day before.

The two measures are not the same thing: turnover is secondary-market trading, not new money. Wind data show broad ETFs took in a net 374.235 billion yuan in October, and the category held about 1.33 trillion yuan of assets as of November 3.

Why it mattersPut the two numbers together and the channel is changing: account openings fell 35% year on year in October, while broad ETFs drew 374.2 billion yuan of net inflows and traded 183.96 billion yuan on November 3. Openings measure people; ETFs measure money. One is retreating, the other advancing, which says incremental capital prefers index tools to single-stock accounts. Mind the definition: the report itself notes that higher turnover mainly reflects secondary trading and is not new money; what supports valuations is share growth and net subscriptions. Who pays: ETF fees are low, but tracking error, premiums and discounts, and creation/redemption frictions fall on holders; for brokers, commissions shift from stocks to ETFs, lowering revenue per trade and raising the scale needed. On duration, ETF money is more concentrated in a few benchmarks, so redemptions arrive together when the index falls and stability depends on the index rather than the holder.

AMD posts $13.38 billion in revenue, up 45%, and guides the fourth quarter to $17 billion

AMD reported third-quarter 2026 revenue of $13.377 billion after the US close on November 3, up 45% year on year and 16% sequentially. GAAP gross margin was 54% and non-GAAP 56%; non-GAAP earnings per share were $2.09, up 74%.

Data centres supplied the growth: segment revenue was $8.5 billion, up 97%, on EPYC processors and Instinct MI450 GPUs. Client and gaming revenue was $3.7 billion, down 8%, with gaming at $773 million, down 40%. Embedded revenue was $1.1 billion, up 29%.

GAAP operating income was $2.832 billion, up 123%, for a 21% margin; non-GAAP operating income was $3.923 billion, up 75%, for a 29% margin. Operating expenses rose 25% to $4.378 billion, slower than revenue.

For the fourth quarter, AMD guided revenue to about $17 billion, plus or minus 3% — roughly 66% year-on-year growth and 27% sequential growth at the midpoint — with non-GAAP gross margin of about 55%. Anthropic, Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI and Oracle Cloud Infrastructure are already running workloads on Helios racks, the company said.

Why it mattersThis is where AI capex stops being a story and shows up in an income statement: data-centre revenue of $8.5 billion, up 97%, and a 29% non-GAAP operating margin say the supply side has not been competed away. What moves pricing is the fourth-quarter guide: a $17 billion midpoint lifts growth from 45% to 66%, and the company attributes the acceleration to ramping Helios and Venice. It is not free: operating expenses rose 25%, and the 55% gross-margin guide is a point below the 56% just reported, so scale did not all reach the margin. Who pays: cloud capex is recovered through depreciation and compute rents; if rents fall, the pressure travels back up the order book into AMD’s margin. On duration, the ramp decides whether this growth lasts quarters or years.

11/03 16:28 Xinhua ↗

Aramco’s adjusted profit rises 9% to $31.2 billion on refining spreads

Saudi Aramco reported third-quarter 2026 adjusted net income of $31.2 billion on November 3, up 9% from $28.6 billion a year earlier. Operating cash flow was $38.7 billion against $36.1 billion, and free cash flow $28.2 billion against $23.6 billion.

Downstream drove the improvement: adjusted earnings before interest and tax there rose 234% year on year on stronger refining margins. Upstream, the Zuluf crude increment came on stream, while Jafurah phase two, the Fadhili gas plant expansion and the Dammam development stayed on schedule.

The East-West pipeline continues to provide export flexibility, the company said, and it is assessing additional overseas storage and alternative export routes. Geopolitical uncertainty and attacks on key energy facilities increased pressure on global supply in the quarter.

On capital allocation, Aramco is taking a 49% stake in HUMAIN, with about $2.6 billion of cash plus asset transfers, to expand into artificial intelligence.

Why it mattersThe profit repair came mainly from downstream spreads, not from the direction of oil alone. Upstream earns the Middle East risk premium; downstream earns the refining margin. Both improved in the same quarter, which is what produced $38.7 billion of operating cash flow. The real constraint is capital allocation: $28.2 billion of free cash flow has to cover dividends, upstream expansion and the $2.6 billion AI stake, and more of any one squeezes the others. Who pays: if the risk premium fades, upstream profit falls first and downstream spreads are unlikely to hold alone; the safety of the dividend then depends on whether capex can be deferred. On duration, Jafurah phase two and Fadhili are long-cycle projects that lock in a decade of gas prices and feedstock costs, not this quarter’s earnings.
