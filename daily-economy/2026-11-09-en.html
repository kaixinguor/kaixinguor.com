11/09 09:30 National Bureau of Statistics ↗

China's October CPI rises 0.7% y/y and PPI 5.7%, a five-point scissors gap

The National Bureau of Statistics reported at 09:30 on November 9 that China's consumer price index rose 0.7% year on year in October and was flat month on month; the January-October average was up 0.9%. Food prices fell 2.9% year on year while non-food rose 1.5%; core CPI, excluding food and energy, rose 0.8%.

Food remained the drag: fresh vegetables fell 16.9% year on year, subtracting about 0.31 percentage points from CPI; pork fell 8.6% and fresh fruit 3.8%. The upward pressure sat in transport and communications, up 5.5%, with vehicle fuel up 20.2% and communication equipment up 16.1%. Healthcare rose 2.4%, education, culture and recreation 1.5%, while housing fell 0.3%.

Producer prices rose 5.7% year on year and 0.7% month on month; the January-October average was up 2.7%. Producer goods rose 7.4%, with mining up 27.9%, raw materials 11.6% and processing 3.5%; consumer goods fell 0.1%. By industry, coal mining rose 36.6%, oil and gas extraction 35.0%, oil and coal processing 29.9%, chemicals 16.4% and non-ferrous smelting 18.6%; car making fell 1.8% and power and heat 3.8%.

Why it mattersThe five-point gap between PPI and CPI says price pressure is concentrated upstream, while consumer goods and utilities are still absorbing costs. For policy, core CPI at 0.8% says domestic demand prices remain weak, while PPI at 5.7% says imported costs are not. For companies, margins are squeezed in between: whether an 11.6% rise in raw materials can be passed on depends on industry concentration and contract duration.
Another viewFresh vegetables at -16.9% and pork at -8.6% held CPI down; once the base reverses, the 0.7% reading can rise quickly. The number to watch is whether core CPI can hold above 1% for several months.

11/09 09:06 Ministry of Finance Japan ↗

Japan's MOF: three days of FX intervention totalled ¥15.4tn in July-September, including ¥7.98tn on July 30

Japan's Ministry of Finance published daily intervention data for July-September on November 9: the total was ¥15.3993tn, all of it selling US dollars and buying yen, across three trading days.

The breakdown is ¥7.9756tn on July 30, ¥5.5523tn on July 31 and ¥1.8714tn on August 3. Earlier reporting said the operations pushed the yen toward 164 per dollar.

Why it mattersSpending ¥15.4tn in three days is not routine smoothing; it is a direct fight against one-way depreciation. As long as the US-Japan rate gap persists, intervention can change the speed but not the direction. For carry traders, the real risk is not the intervention day but the catch-down after it stops. Watch the ministry's next monthly disclosure and the Bank of Japan's rate path.

11/09 09:15 Guandian ↗

Lagarde signals another December hike: barring a major change, policy will meet expectations

In an interview with Japan's Yomiuri Shimbun, ECB President Christine Lagarde said inflation data will stay high over the coming quarters. On the December meeting, she said: "Based on the information we have today, unless there is a major change, the policy path will be in line with my expectations."

It was her first media interview since announcing she will step down in February, eight months before her October term ends. She linked the departure to France's presidential election next spring, saying many key issues are "either not discussed, or discussed on the basis of wrong facts and wrong principles".

Why it mattersThe remark turns a December hike from possible into the default, barring a large data surprise. For the euro area, inflation is still high while growth is weak, so policy has to pick a side in the stagflation trade-off. For emerging markets, further ECB tightening would weigh on the euro and lift the dollar, sending imported inflation back to commodity importers. Lagarde's early exit adds uncertainty about policy continuity; her successor's path matters more than this one meeting.

Central SOEs sign over $100bn at the import expo, with Sinopec at $41.3bn and PetroChina at $18.8bn

On November 8, the fourth day of the ninth China International Import Expo, the central SOE delegation signed deals worth more than $100bn. Sinopec signed with 37 partners from 18 countries and regions across 14 categories and 29 products, for $41.3bn. PetroChina signed 38 agreements with 35 partners from 16 countries and regions, worth more than $18.8bn.

Sinochem signed with more than 30 partners from nearly 20 countries and regions, including Saudi Arabia and Oman, for more than $12.8bn. China North Industries Group signed 18 import contracts with partners from 11 countries and regions, worth RMB 40.1bn. COFCO's total signings exceeded $10bn, and the supply-and-marketing cooperative delegation signed agricultural input and food purchase agreements with 17 companies from the US, France and Australia.

Beijing's 21 trading delegations will hold procurement talks, investment promotions and company exchanges during the expo. Shanghai's Hongkou delegation signed intent orders of $190m in pharmaceuticals and agricultural products.

Why it mattersThe $100bn is a signing figure, not goods landed this quarter and not final execution. Its immediate macro effect depends on customs clearance and payment timing. Energy and agricultural products still dominate, which says the expo's core function is locking in long-term resources rather than launching new products. For importers, large SOE orders pre-empt shipping slots, warehousing and FX hedging, so smaller buyers may find more bargaining room after the event than during it.

Hudong-Zhonghua delivers a 174,000-cubic-metre LNG carrier as Chinese yards hand over a batch of ships

According to the China Association of the National Shipbuilding Industry, several Chinese ships have recently been delivered: a 174,000-cubic-metre LNG carrier from Hudong-Zhonghua, a 307,000-deadweight-tonne very large crude carrier from Dalian Shipbuilding, and a 210,000-tonne LNG dual-fuel bulk carrier from Beihai Shipbuilding.

The first Chinese very large trailing-suction hopper dredger with distributed in-hold pumps, Xin Hai Jing 1, was also delivered. The association said several key technical indicators beat expectations, with strong energy-saving and carbon-reduction results, and that the dredger marked a new step in intelligent dredging equipment.

Why it mattersThe three ship types map to LNG, crude and dry-bulk transport, and the delivery pace shows Chinese yards converting high-end order books into output. For shipping lines, LNG dual-fuel bulkers are a practical hedge against carbon costs, but retrofit expense and bunkering networks remain constraints. For yards, profit is less about tonnage than about local content in high-value ships such as the 174,000-cubic-metre LNG carrier. Next come newbuilding prices and steel costs, which will set delivery margins in 2027.
