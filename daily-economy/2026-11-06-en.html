US stocks crash on November 5: Nasdaq down 5.52%, Dow down 1,833 points

US stocks suffered a sharp selloff on November 5, with all three major indices posting their worst single-day drops since April 2025. The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 1,833.06 points to 51,523.06, down 3.44%; the S&P 500 fell 334.89 points to 7,353.73, down 4.36%; and the Nasdaq Composite fell 1,421.52 points to 24,315.00, down 5.52%.

All 11 S&P sectors fell. Technology led with a 7.30% drop, while consumer staples, the best performer, still lost 0.67%. The Nasdaq 100 fell 6.36%; Nvidia lost 7.3%, while Micron and AMD each fell more than 14%; the VIX closed at 28.18, its highest since late March.

Xinhua attributes the move to two forces: Democrats won both chambers in the midterms and have been signalling tougher regulation, raising fears that AI data-centre buildouts and frontier development will be slowed; and a market already carrying high valuations and leverage saw long-term AI believers join the selling.

The report quotes Senator Bernie Sanders saying voters in most districts backed a pause on AI data-centre construction, and Representative Yassamin Ansari saying the next Congress's first priority is to pause advanced AI development, regulate it and ban 'superintelligence'. SYZ's chief investment officer said the 'blue wave' has investors worried the AI peak has passed.

Why it mattersWhat fell was not this quarter's earnings but the discount rate and the regulatory premium. The VIX at 28.18 and tech at -7.30% are the two lines to track; next come data-centre financing spreads, power contracts and 2027 capex guidance, not the share price itself. The cost lands on whoever borrowed to build compute, and on valuation models that booked AI capex as perpetual growth.
The other view For engineers, the regulatory signal changes where data centres are sited, powered and interconnected before it changes model parameters. Compute does not disappear; it moves, and it pays a different power price.

11/05 20:00 Bank of England ↗

Bank of England raises Bank Rate 25bp to 4% on an 8-1 vote as Mann pushes for 50bp

The Bank of England's Monetary Policy Committee raised Bank Rate by 25 basis points to 4% on November 5, with eight votes in favour and one against. Catherine Mann preferred a 50bp increase to 4.25%. The meeting ended on November 4.

Energy is the driver. UK CPI inflation was 3.5% in September, with direct energy effects contributing about 1 percentage point. The Bank expects inflation around 4.5% in Q1 2027 and around 5% in Q2 before it fades. Over the 15 working days to October 27, front-month Brent averaged $108 a barrel and UK front-month gas 216 pence per therm, 38% and 81% above the July report.

Second-round effects have not shown up yet: food inflation is lower than expected, and some firms are absorbing costs through hedges, fixed-price contracts and thinner margins. But two-year mortgage rates have risen nearly 50bp since early September, and the stock of gilts held for monetary policy was £362 billion on November 4.

Governor Andrew Bailey said that without news easing energy risks there is a case for a further increase, while Alan Taylor stressed this is 'not the start of a hiking cycle' but a 'one-for-now' insurance move. The next meeting is on December 17.

Why it mattersThis is an insurance hike: energy pushes inflation toward 5%, second-round effects are still missing, and the Bank moved the price rather than the balance sheet. The cost lands on households refinancing mortgages in 2027 and on the 2027 wage round. Duration matters more than size: if energy retreats, the 25bp may be reversed; if not, 4% is not the end.

A-shares close lower: ChiNext down 3.96% as turnover shrinks by 460 billion yuan

Chinese equities opened lower on November 6 and stayed weak. The Shanghai Composite fell 40.73 points, or 1.1%, to 3,649.18; the Shenzhen Component fell 295.31 points, or 2.57%, to 11,175.78; the CSI 300 fell 59.13 points, or 1.44%, to 4,054.28; ChiNext fell 101.57 points, or 3.96%, to 2,465.23; and the STAR 50 fell 33.48 points, or 2.6%, to 1,253.20.

Turnover across the two exchanges was 1.66 trillion yuan, 460.2 billion yuan below the previous session, and more than 4,700 stocks declined. Compute-hardware names led the drop: Cambridge Technology and Aohong Electronics hit limit-down, while Foxconn Industrial Internet and Shengyi Technology fell sharply. Banks, utilities and agriculture rose against the trend, with Shanghai Rural Commercial Bank up more than 4%.

The same day, the People's Bank of China conducted 226 billion yuan of overnight reverse repos at a fixed rate of 1.25% through a quantity auction; bids and awards were both 226 billion yuan, fully satisfying primary dealers.

Why it mattersWhat sold off was the duration in compute hardware: high multiples, orders far in the future and sensitivity to overseas capex. Banks, utilities and agriculture rising shows money hunting cash flow and policy certainty rather than growth stories. The PBOC fully satisfied 226 billion yuan at 1.25%, so quantity is not the constraint; price is. Watch whether DR001 holds near 1.25% into the tax period.

11/06 15:07 Xinhua Finance ↗

SCFI falls 53.90 points to 3,633.45 while CCFI gains 0.3%

Data from the Shanghai Shipping Exchange show the composite Shanghai Containerized Freight Index at 3,633.45 on November 6, down 53.90 points from the previous reading, while the China Containerized Freight Index rose 0.3% to 1,881.82.

The two indices point in opposite directions: SCFI tracks spot bookings, while CCFI includes contract rates and is smoothed by them. For exporters, spot costs are falling, but contract resets lag.

Why it mattersSpot down and contracts up means carriers are still using contract prices to lock in earlier highs. Exporters will not see landed costs fall until the next contract reset; for now the burden sits with whoever books on spot. Watch whether SCFI falls again in late November, and whether pre-Chinese-New Year shipping pushes rates back up.

11/06 14:23 Xinhua ↗

Six ministries issue 14 measures on elderly-care products, naming embodied AI and brain-computer interfaces

Six ministries, including Civil Affairs, the NDRC, MIIT, Commerce, the market regulator and the national ageing office, issued 14 measures on November 4 to develop consumption scenarios for products used by older people. The document was published on November 6.

It explicitly calls for accelerating embodied AI, large language models, brain-computer interfaces and exoskeletons in elderly care, including smart devices for disability care, rehabilitation and companionship, and smart service robots.

On the consumer side it promotes rent-to-own and combined rental-sale models to lower the cost of use. On the supply side it encourages using state-owned buildings and community facilities as existing sites, and supports public-build/private-operate and 'site partnership plus revenue sharing' arrangements.

The document also promises a standards framework for the elderly-products industry, a certification scheme for senior products, and clearer market entry, exit and complaint handling.

Why it mattersThe policy offers scenarios, not subsidies: it lowers entry costs with existing assets and leaves affordability to rental and revenue-sharing. Whether it lasts depends on whether local governments give up income from those assets, and on how long-term care insurance and medical insurance pay for smart devices. For companies, work out the payback period on the site before talking about the robot.

11/06 14:02 Securities Times (Sina mirror) ↗

10 Shanghai-listed companies announce buyback loans; 550 deals and 140 billion yuan over two years

Ten Shanghai-listed companies announced on November 6 that they plan to use buyback and increase loans: six from the STAR Market, including Hygon Information, Gaoche, Lianxun Instruments, Qiangyi, Xi'an ESWIN and Guanggang Gas, and four from the main board, including Kingfa, Haixing Power, Hengtong Optic-Electric and Haitong Development.

Since October, Shanghai-listed companies have disclosed 21 such loan announcements worth 2.7 billion yuan. The facility was created in October 2024; as of November 5, 2026, more than 550 announcements and more than 140 billion yuan had been disclosed in Shanghai.

Why it mattersAt a 1% funding cost, buybacks shift from cash-flow decisions to balance-sheet operations: replacing pledged shares, substituting for dividends, or supplying stock for incentive plans. A buyback used for incentives does not shrink the share count; it swaps cash for treasury stock. Only cancellation reduces the float. Read the stated use and cancellation terms, not the headline size.
