11/11 18:00 People's Bank of China ↗

PBOC Q3 report: loan pricing to move beyond a single LPR reference as DR001 averages 1.34%

The People's Bank of China published its third-quarter 2026 monetary policy report on November 11. GDP grew 4.6% in the first nine months, the overnight money-market rate DR001 averaged 1.34%, and at end-September the stock of total social financing was 7.1% higher than a year earlier while M2 was up 7.2%.

On prices, new corporate loans were priced just under 3.0% in September, about 0.2 percentage point below a year earlier, and new mortgages averaged around 3.1%, roughly flat. The report said the PBOC cut the rate on pledged supplementary lending by 0.25 point, on top of January's cuts to structural tool rates, and kept pressing lenders to disclose all-in borrowing costs so that fees in the middle of the chain come down.

On structure, the relending quota for technology innovation and industrial upgrading was raised by Rmb200bn with the share of eligible costs covered lifted from 60% to 100%; the agricultural and small-business quota rose by Rmb500bn, including Rmb300bn earmarked for private firms; and construction of the "six networks" was added to PSL-eligible projects. At end-September, technology, green, inclusive, elderly-care and digital-economy loans were up 12.0%, 13.0%, 6.5%, 16.2% and 15.7% respectively, every one of them faster than total lending.

Looking ahead, the report pledged to "steadily reform the monetary policy operating framework" so that short-term money-market rates trade around the policy rate, and to shift loan pricing "from a relatively single reference to the loan prime rate toward a more diversified set of benchmarks". The yuan closed at 6.7053 per dollar at end-September, 4.1% stronger than a year earlier, and the CFETS basket index stood at 103.20, up 5.3%.

Why it mattersChanging the benchmark changes the transmission chain, not the level of rates. Cutting LPR used to be shorthand for cheaper corporate credit; from here the outcome depends on the policy rate, deposit costs and the spread lenders add. With corporate loans just under 3.0% and mortgages near 3.1%, the gap is only about 10bp, so there is little room left to squeeze loan rates; instead the report cut PSL by 0.25 point and doubled the coverage ratio on tech relending — a subsidy replaces a price cut. One denominator deserves attention: social financing at 7.1% and M2 at 7.2% both outrun real GDP growth of 4.6%, and debt is measured against nominal output.
The other viewFor a bank treasurer, a multi-benchmark regime means LPR stops being the only anchor and the repricing gap has to be managed through internal funds transfer pricing; for a bond fund, a 1.34% DR001 average caps short-rate volatility, so carry depends on certainty of funding rather than duration.

11/11 16:20 Ministry of Finance ↗

63-day T-bill clears at 1.05% as the MOF sells Rmb40.61bn against a Rmb40bn plan

The Ministry of Finance said in announcement No. 193 of 2026, published on November 11, that the 73rd discount T-bill of the year had been auctioned: Rmb40bn was planned and Rmb40.61bn of face value was actually issued.

The bill runs 63 days and was sold at 99.819 per 100 of face value, equivalent to an annualised yield of 1.05%. Interest starts accruing on November 12, distribution runs through the same day, trading begins on November 16, and the bill is repaid at par on January 14, 2027, or the next business day.

The same morning the PBOC lent overnight cash at 1.25%, and its quarterly report put the DR001 average for the first nine months at 1.34%.

Why it mattersA Rmb40bn plan that drew Rmb40.61bn, 1.5% more, shows solid demand for two-month safety. At 1.05% the bill yields 20bp below the 1.25% overnight repo rate and 29bp below the 1.34% DR001 average — the very front of the curve is inverted. For the sovereign, short funding is cheap but has to be rolled often: repayment falls due on January 14, 2027 and another bill follows. For a bank, buying a 1.05% bill against its funding cost leaves almost no carry; what it buys is liquidity and collateral that counts at quarter-end, not return.

11/11 09:20 People's Bank of China ↗

PBOC injects Rmb187bn of overnight cash at 1.25%, draining a net Rmb3bn

The People's Bank of China said on November 11 that it had lent Rmb187bn through overnight reverse repos at a fixed rate of 1.25% in a quantity tender; bids and allotment both totalled Rmb187bn and primary dealers' demand was met in full.

With Rmb190bn of overnight cash placed on November 10 maturing the same day, the two sessions netted to a Rmb3bn drain. November 9 had drained Rmb44bn, when Rmb182bn was lent against Rmb226bn maturing, while November 10 added a net Rmb8bn, lending Rmb190bn against Rmb182bn maturing.

The format is still a fixed-rate, quantity-based tender: the PBOC sets the price and meets demand in full, so the initiative over draining or adding liquidity sits with maturities rather than with the rate.

Why it mattersA Rmb3bn drain is noise; "met in full" is the signal. The price does not move and quantity follows demand, so the PBOC manages the water level through maturities instead of the rate. The larger the share of overnight money, the shorter banks' average liability duration and the more the fragility shifts from price to rollover: a heavy maturity day with a smaller allotment moves the overnight rate at once. For non-banks, 1.25% anchors leverage costs; for corporates, a stable overnight price puts a floor under loan rates.

Hong Kong turnover shrinks to HK$180.7bn as the Hang Seng loses 0.98% and Chinese banks slide

Hong Kong equities tracked a softer Asia session on November 11, with the Hang Seng Index closing at 24,315.92, down 241.12 points or 0.98%.

Underneath, the Hang Seng Tech Index fell 1.41% (down 57.39 points to 4,027.06) and the H-share index lost 1.14% (down 94.36 points to 8,171.45). Main-board turnover was HK$180.749bn, roughly 27% less than the HK$247.428bn recorded on November 6.

Tencent closed at HK$409.6, down 1.96%, and HSBC at HK$141.6, down 1.19%. Chinese banks were uniformly weaker: China Construction Bank at HK$10.5, down 1.32%, ICBC at HK$7.89, down 1.31%, and Bank of China at HK$6.22, down 0.64%.

Why it mattersChiNext gained 3.11% in the same session while the Hang Seng lost 0.98% and Hang Seng Tech 1.41%, a split that says pricing power sits with mainland money and industrial themes rather than with the foreign-owned index heavyweights. Turnover of HK$180.7bn compares with HK$247.4bn on November 6, so the decline came without volume: the selling was in weights, not a broad exit. For Hong Kong the discount rate still dominates; with odds of a December Fed hike rising and the dollar firm, high-dividend names and platform weights get squeezed together, and the denominator story drowns out the earnings story.

China's October NEV sales hit 1.94m units, 64.3% of the market, as vehicle exports rise 60.2%

Data from the China Association of Automobile Manufacturers on November 11 showed October output of 2.017m new-energy vehicles and sales of 1.94m, up 13.8% and 13.1% from a year earlier, leaving NEVs at 64.3% of monthly new-vehicle sales.

Total vehicle output was 3.063m and sales 3.016m, up 2.5% and 2% from September but slightly lower year on year. In the first ten months output reached 26.318m and sales 26.286m, with NEV output of 14.509m and sales of 14.411m, up 11.5% and 11.3%, lifting the cumulative NEV share to 54.8%.

Exports held up: 1.066m vehicles shipped in October, up 60.2%, including 606,000 NEVs, up 1.4 times. Cumulative exports in the first ten months were 9.271m, of which 4.612m were NEVs, up 1.3 times.

Why it mattersDomestic sales slipped year on year while exports rose 60.2%, so the increment now sits in foreign demand: October exports equal 35% of monthly sales, and NEV exports equal 31% of NEV sales. For the macro account, that revenue is booked on overseas buyers' balance sheets, which makes tariffs, freight and the exchange rate the next variables. For the industry, a 64.3% monthly share and a 54.8% cumulative share is close to the ceiling that policy-driven penetration can deliver; growth from here has to come from exports and content per vehicle rather than from taking share from combustion cars.

Enterprise SSD contract prices set to rise another 15-20% as the memory rally narrows

Several Chinese memory makers told Shanghai Securities News, in a report published on November 11, that fourth-quarter official prices from the big wafer producers are edging up, but the models involved are concentrated in enterprise storage; consumer products show no clear sign of a parallel increase.

Third-party data points the same way. A TrendForce report dated October 30 said North American customers are chasing orders and accepting higher prices, with enterprise SSD contract prices expected to rise another 15-20% in the fourth quarter; in China, however, limited AI revenue and tight GPU supply have left customers resisting quotes even as prices grind higher.

In the spot market, the best-selling 6000MHz consumer modules with c30 or c28 timings were quoted at Rmb3,500-4,500 in Shenzhen's Huaqiangbei in early November, with some premium brands above Rmb5,000, while enterprise modules remain 10-20% below their highs earlier this year. Memory shares rose on the afternoon of November 11: Puran 9.73%, Biwin 6.95%, Demingli 6.61% and Shannon Chip 5.54%.

Why it mattersThe rally has narrowed from everything to one segment. Producers allocate wafers to high-margin contract customers first, small module makers are forced into the spot market at high prices, and finished-goods pricing cannot keep up with input costs — the cost sits in the middle of the chain while profit moves from modules to wafers. Enterprise customers will carry a 15-20% contract increase into 2027 server depreciation and cloud costs; on the consumer side, Rmb3,500-4,500 spot quotes for 6000MHz modules say channels are not short of goods — only the AI server line is rising.
