11/18 09:20 People's Bank of China ↗

PBOC injects Rmb700.2bn of overnight cash at 1.25% and drains Rmb13.8bn net against Rmb714bn of maturities

The People's Bank of China said in open market operations announcement No. 223 of 2026 that it injected Rmb700.2bn through overnight reverse repos on November 18 at a fixed rate of 1.25%, using quantity bidding. Bids and allotment were both Rmb700.2bn, and the announcement said primary dealers' demand was met in full.

The previous session, November 17, saw Rmb714bn at the same 1.25% under announcement No. 222. With that Rmb714bn maturing on November 18, the operation drained a net Rmb13.8bn, a figure confirmed by the financial data provider JRJ.

Across the two sessions the central bank lent Rmb1.41trn overnight, and November 16 was the monthly deadline for tax filings. The tax period is over; the overnight book is still turning over in the hundreds of billions rather than shrinking.

Why it mattersA Rmb700.2bn operation that nets to minus Rmb13.8bn looks neutral, but the gross number carries the information. Rmb1.41trn of overnight money over two days means banks are financing themselves by rolling short liabilities with the central bank every morning. Overnight cash is cheap and short; the cost is that it must be renewed daily, and a smaller allotment would show up in funding rates immediately. Keeping the size after the tax deadline says the gap is not tax but the pace of credit expansion and government bond settlement. Watch how far DR001 sits from 1.25% and whether one-year interbank certificate of deposit rates follow; if CD rates stay put while overnight stays large, banks are short of stable funding, not of temporary cash.
Another angle For banks and savers, 1.25% is both a funding cost and the yield that money market funds and cash-management products can earn; the larger the overnight book and the lower the rate, the closer those products' seven-day yields sit to 1.25%, which weakens the pull of deposit migration — the asset-side face of a quantity instrument.

11/17 Ministry of Finance ↗

Market-making sale clears two- and three-year bonds at the same 1.25% yield as overnight cash

The Ministry of Finance published government bond operation notice No. 196 on November 17 with the results of that day's market-making support: Rmb320m of the 2026 book-entry issue 13, a two-year, sold at 99.94 for a 1.25% yield, and Rmb330m of issue 17, a three-year, sold at 99.95 for 1.25%.

The operation totalled Rmb650m, with competitive bidding between 11:05 and 11:35 on November 17. The bonds sold will merge with the existing issues for trading from November 20.

The result matters more than the size: both maturities cleared at exactly the same yield, and that yield equals the 1.25% rate on the central bank's overnight operation. A normal curve puts three years above two, and two above overnight. This sale flattened all three onto one price.

Why it mattersRmb650m cannot move a market of this size, but it can set a reference point. Two- and three-year paper trading at 1.25% strips the term premium out of the front end. For portfolio managers the implication is plain: holding two- to three-year government bonds against overnight funding earns no positive carry, so banks have little reason to extend duration and cash prefers to sit in overnight and certificates of deposit — a self-reinforcing pile-up at the front end. For dealers, the sale provides an outlet for inventory and makes continuous quotes possible, but continuous quotes need a spread to earn, and a pinned yield leaves only small adjustments on either side. Judge the operation on November 20, when the bonds merge: look at bid-ask spreads and trade counts, not at the 1.25% itself.

11/17 23:59 Xinhua Finance ↗

US retail sales slow to 0.2% in October as cars and furniture drag, with seven of 13 categories higher

US retail sales rose 0.2% in October from September, above the 0.1% consensus but well below the 0.8% gain a month earlier, Xinhua Finance reported. Seven of 13 retail categories expanded: non-store retailers, health and personal care stores, petrol stations and building materials rose, while furniture, motor vehicles and hobby goods fell.

Analysts read the release as a solid headline, a sharp slowdown and wide dispersion. Rate-sensitive durables — cars and household goods — were the main drag, while online retail, personal care and building materials held up. Goldman Sachs argued that underlying demand remains supported once autos are excluded; Morgan Stanley noted that only just over half of the categories grew, pointing to tighter household budgets and selective spending.

Both readings agree on the conclusion: the data rule out a consumer hard landing but confirm that high rates are still biting on rate-sensitive goods, which argues against aggressive rate-cut bets without reversing the baseline easing path.

Why it mattersThe 0.2% is nominal. With prices still rising, a 0.2% nominal gain implies weaker real spending — which explains why a print "better than expected" coincided with a lower stock market. Investors were not buying the idea that the consumer is fine; they were selling the idea that earnings are safe. Composition matters more than the total: cars and furniture are durables financed on credit, and their decline shows that high rates are still squeezing big-ticket purchases through instalments and mortgages, while the rise at petrol stations is a price effect rather than volume. For Chinese exporters, weaker US durable demand usually reaches appliance, furniture and auto-parts orders with about a quarter's lag.

Wall Street falls across the board, the Dow losing 848.75 points as financials and utilities lead

All three main US indexes fell sharply on November 17, Xinhua reported from New York. The Dow Jones industrial average lost 848.75 points to close at 50,759.42, down 1.64%; the S&P 500 fell 76.06 points to 7,340.53, down 1.03%; the Nasdaq composite dropped 181.37 points to 24,328.38, down 0.74%.

All 11 S&P sectors closed lower. Financials led with a 1.87% decline and utilities followed at 1.60%, while energy lost the least at 0.53%.

The same day's data showed US retail sales up 0.2% in October, 0.6 percentage point slower than September's 0.8%.

Why it mattersThe order of the declines says more than their size. Financials and utilities led while energy lagged — that is not a technology correction but a repricing of rates and credit. Utilities are bond proxies and the most sensitive to long yields; financials are sensitive to credit costs and the shape of the curve. Both falling at once says the market is cutting the weight it gives to the idea that rate cuts will support valuations. The Dow's 1.64% drop against the Nasdaq's 0.74% confirms it: the selling was in the old economy, not in AI. The next signals are two spreads rather than an index level — whether investment-grade credit spreads widen, and which way the two- to ten-year Treasury spread moves. If credit moves first, this is no longer only a valuation story.

Turnover of Rmb1.46trn is not a retreat, so this looks like repositioning rather than exit. The question is whether the metals rally can persist: gold rising with real yields is hedging, gold rising as real yields fall is a bet on money. For the index, support near 3,584 depends less on sentiment than on the pace of credit supply and fiscal spending in late November.

Nvidia (NASDAQ: NVDA) reported third-quarter fiscal 2027 results after the US close on November 17 for the period ended October 25: revenue of $115.84bn, up 103% from a year earlier and 20% on the quarter; data centre revenue of $107.6bn, up 110% and 21% respectively; a GAAP gross margin of 74.8%; and diluted earnings per share of $2.70.

Guidance for the fourth quarter is $130bn, plus or minus 3%, and explicitly assumes no data centre compute revenue from China. Gross margin is guided to 71-72% on both a GAAP and non-GAAP basis, below this quarter's 74.8%, with operating expenses of about $11.0bn GAAP and $10.7bn non-GAAP.

On capital returns, the board added $150bn to the buyback authorisation in September; the company returned $28.1bn to shareholders in the quarter and had about $227.3bn left on the authorisation. The next quarterly dividend is $0.25 a share. Vera Rubin NVL72 has begun shipping, which Nvidia calls its fastest product ramp; chief financial officer Colette Kress is also interim chief executive.

Why it mattersThree numbers together explain the quarter. The $130bn guide implies roughly 12% sequential growth, slower than the 20% just delivered but still about $14bn of absolute addition — AI capex has not turned. The 71-72% margin guide is about three points below the quarter, the cost of ramping a new platform: high-bandwidth memory, advanced packaging and liquid cooling all cost money, and whether Nvidia can pass that on depends on its pricing power. Excluding China data centre revenue from guidance removes that business from the valuation base: the policy risk no longer sits in near-term earnings, and it no longer cushions the downside either. For China's compute supply chain, the $107.6bn of data centre revenue is the order book, with boards, power, cooling and connectors needing capacity a quarter ahead; last issue's Rmb70.7bn credit line at Victory Giant to pre-fund Malaysian capacity is the other end of that chain. The three-point margin decline points the other way: part of what Nvidia gives up in gross margin may become the supply chain's pricing room.

JD.com revenue rises 3.5% to Rmb309.6bn as service income reaches a record 24.7% of sales

JD.com reported third-quarter 2026 results on the evening of November 17: revenue of Rmb309.6bn, up 3.5% from a year earlier; operating profit of Rmb5.2bn against a Rmb1.1bn loss a year earlier; net income attributable to ordinary shareholders of Rmb6.5bn versus Rmb5.3bn; and non-GAAP net income of Rmb8.3bn versus Rmb5.8bn.

Service revenue reached Rmb76.4bn, or 24.7% of the total, which the company called a record, while quarterly active customers and PLUS members kept growing at double-digit rates. Management attributed the profit improvement to the resilience of JD Retail, narrower losses in new businesses and a significant year-on-year fall in marketing expenses.

The same report disclosed that on November 12 JD completed its voluntary public takeover offer for CECONOMY AG, the parent of European retailer MediaMarktSaturn, paying €1.335bn for 59.8% of shares and voting rights, or 85.2% including shares retained by Convergenta. CECONOMY has been consolidated since the acquisition date.

Why it mattersRevenue growth of 3.5% is below nominal GDP, which tells you this is no longer a growth stock; the profit improvement comes from three reusable levers — narrower losses in new businesses, lower marketing spend and a richer service mix. The Rmb76.4bn of service revenue, at 24.7% of the total, is the number to remember: service margins exceed those on physical goods, so every point of mix shift improves the gross margin structure. The €1.335bn deal puts European retail on the balance sheet at the cost of goodwill and currency exposure: CECONOMY's euro revenue and costs translate into renminbi and move with the exchange rate. The fourth quarter is the first full quarter of consolidation, and the test is whether the acquired contribution covers the integration cost.

Why it mattersOne day's small gain matters less than how long the $85-93 range has held and how it feeds producer prices.
