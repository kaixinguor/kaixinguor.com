Spot gold slides to about $3,500, a 14-month low, as Chinese jewellery tags fall just over Rmb600 a gram from the peak

Spot gold traded near $3,500 an ounce on November 21, its lowest in about 14 months. CCTV Finance reported that London spot broke $3,600 during the Asian session on November 20 and went as low as about $3,514 intraday. Over eight sessions it lost five round numbers between $4,000 and $3,600, fell close to 9% on the week and is about 36% below its January record; Shanghai gold touched Rmb755 a gram intraday.

China News Service attributed the move to monetary policy expectations. After October inflation data, several Federal Reserve officials signalled a tighter stance, lifting real yields and the dollar. Qu Rui, a senior deputy director at Orient Golden Credit, added a second link: volatility in risk assets tightened liquidity, some holders sold gold to meet margin calls, and the break of round numbers set off a rush for the exits, a short-term negative feedback loop. "In a disorderly de-risking, gold can be cashed in first because it is the most liquid asset, rather than serving as the hedge," he said.

Banks are already flagging the risk. Industrial and Commercial Bank of China, China Construction Bank and China Merchants Bank all said that volatility in precious metals has increased sharply and advised clients to keep position sizes under control and avoid short-term speculation or following the crowd. Retail prices adjust more slowly: on November 21 several brands cut pure-gold jewellery to Rmb1,056–1,068 a gram, Rmb20–34 below the previous day, Brokerage China reported. Against the year's high, mainstream brands are down more than Rmb600 a gram.

Demand does not support the buy-the-dip story. The World Gold Council's third-quarter Gold Demand Trends, published this month, showed global jewellery demand down 17% year on year as buyers shifted to lighter, lower-purity pieces, and the drag from high prices has not fully worn off. Goldman Sachs expects gold to return to $5,600 an ounce by the end of 2028, provided the Fed pivots back to easing and central banks keep buying.

Why it mattersGold pays no coupon, which makes its price a mirror of the real rate, and what fell this time was not hedging demand but liquidity. The sequence belongs in the ledger: inflation data re-priced a December hike, higher real yields raised the opportunity cost of holding metal, and then falling risk assets forced margin calls so gold was sold first because it could be sold. A hedge becomes an ATM in the first stage of deleveraging; that is a change of role, not a failure of function. Who pays: households who bought jewellery near the top (more than Rmb600 a gram), leveraged precious-metals accounts (the audience for the bank notices) and allocation books that treated gold as a deposit substitute. Durability depends on the December meeting: if a hike lands with inflation still firm, real yields keep rising and $3,500 will not hold simply because it has already fallen a long way. A rally needs hike expectations to peak, not a lower price.
Another angleFor bank precious-metals desks the risk sits less in proprietary positions than in client and accumulation products: concentrated buying at the top and concentrated redemption on the way down raise both liquidation volume and complaint volume. Three large banks issuing notices within days suggests they are treating this as a suitability question, not a market call. For jewellery retailers, sticky tags are a short-term margin source, but a 17% fall in third-quarter jewellery demand says high prices suppressed volume — and cheaper tags may not bring it back.

11/21 09:05 Securities Daily ↗

PBOC to auction Rmb50bn of central bank bills in Hong Kong on November 24: Rmb20bn for three months, Rmb30bn for one year

The People's Bank of China said on November 20 that it will sell the 10th and 11th batches of 2026 central bank bills on November 24, a Tuesday, through the Hong Kong Monetary Authority's CMU bidding platform. The stated aim is to broaden the range of high-grade renminbi products in Hong Kong and improve the offshore renminbi yield curve, under the PBOC's memorandum with the HKMA on issuing bills through the debt-tools central clearing system.

The 10th tranche runs three months, or 91 days, pays a fixed coupon at maturity and totals Rmb20bn, with value on November 26 and maturity on February 25, 2027. The 11th runs one year, pays semi-annually and totals Rmb30bn, with the same value date and a maturity of November 26, 2027.

Both carry a face value of Rmb100 and will be issued by Dutch auction with the yield as the bidding target; maturities roll to the next business day if they fall on a holiday. The combined size is Rmb50bn.

Why it mattersCentral bank bills are the sluice gate on offshore renminbi liquidity, and this is an exchange-rate tool rather than a rate tool. Issuing bills drains CNH from the offshore pool, pushes up CNH funding rates and therefore the cost of financing a short renminbi position. It also adds three-month and one-year risk-free points to the offshore curve, without which offshore institutions have no benchmark for dim-sum bonds, trade finance or FX hedges. Who pays: exporters and offshore institutions that need CNH funding, whose short-end cost rises. Who earns: the central banks, banks and funds that win the paper, which get high-grade, short-duration assets. Durability is about the stock, not the tranche: bills that mature without a rollover are an injection, so the signal sits in the maturity profile while Rmb50bn is a flow. With the dollar firm and gold down about 9% in a week, this is more about the exchange rate than about the amount.

11/21 Jiemian News ↗

SCFI slips 1.9% to 3,503.78 as the US west coast falls 5.6% and South America 20-foot rates drop 15.2%

The Shanghai Shipping Exchange's containerised freight index came in at 3,503.78 on November 20, down 1.9% from the previous reading. The exchange said China's export container market was broadly stable, with transport supply and demand in balance, and that declines on several main deep-sea routes dragged the composite lower.

Europe: $3,356 per FEU, down 1.4%, and $2,253 per TEU, down 1.7%. The Mediterranean was the exception, at $4,871 per FEU, up 2.6%, and $3,678 per TEU, up 1.6%.

North America fell further: $6,711 per FEU to the US west coast, down 5.6%, and $8,776 per FEU to the east coast, down 6.5%. The exchange pointed to US initial jobless claims of 203,000 in the latest week, above both the prior reading and the 198,000 consensus, as evidence that transport demand lacked further momentum.

Elsewhere the routes diverged: the Persian Gulf at $6,670 per TEU, up 1.3%; Australia and New Zealand at $2,165 per TEU, down 7.2%; South America at $3,336 per FEU, down 4.8%, and $3,110 per TEU, down 15.2%. The Southeast Asia container index was broadly flat at 5,659.97.

Why it mattersA 1.9% fall in the composite hides the information, which sits on individual routes. The US west coast at -5.6% and the east coast at -6.5% say the front-loaded cargo has already shipped; South America's 20-foot rate down 15.2% is an emerging-market route catching down; and the Mediterranean at +2.6% with the Gulf at +1.3% reflects geopolitical risk premia rather than demand. Freight is a function of tariff timing: expected duties pull volume forward and rates rise first, then fall first when the rush ends. Who pays depends on the Incoterm, and under FOB the buyer pays the freight, but the rate returns to the exporter's order book through CIF quotes and landed cost. For carriers, whether December blank sailings and rate notices can hold next year's contract talks matters more than this week's print.

11/21 Xinhua Finance ↗

NDRC presses provinces on "fund payments" for government projects as six regions report

The National Development and Reform Commission held a video conference on the payment and use of government investment funds and on project pipelines, Xinhua Finance reported on November 21. Vice-chairman Yue Xiuhu attended and spoke. The meeting reviewed payment progress and reserves of major projects, and the development commission officials of Zhejiang, Henan, Guangdong, Heilongjiang, Anhui and Shaanxi exchanged remarks.

The commission asked local counterparts to play the coordinating role of the investment authority, improve factor guarantees, speed up construction and payment of government projects, tighten scheduling and coordination, and generate more physical work volume as soon as possible. It also called for stronger preliminary work and a higher-quality project pipeline.

The context is budget execution. Ministry of Finance data show general public budget spending of Rmb22.80tn in January–October, up 1% and only about 76% of the full-year plan, against revenue growth of 4.9% (see the ministry's release). The gap is timing, not savings.

Why it matters"Fund payment" and "physical work volume" are two different ledgers: the first is cash flow, the second is the investment that shows up in the national accounts. Pressing both at once says the blockage is in between — money sitting on project accounts without turning into construction output, or projects not yet at a payable milestone. The difference is the accounts receivable of contractors and suppliers: whoever fronts the cash is lending to the budget for a duration that carries no interest line. Durability rests on the pipeline, and putting project quality next to payment concedes that first-quarter infrastructure growth depends on the projects filed now, not on the size of the quota. For companies selling into government projects, the cash-flow turn comes when work volume is certified, not when the quota is issued.

11/21 12:36 Cailian Press ↗

EVE's Liu Jincheng says the battery consumption tax will push "a good number" of cell makers out, with early 2027 the transition

Speaking at the 11th International Summit on Power Battery Applications on November 21, EVE Energy chairman Liu Jincheng said a good number of companies will leave the market once the lithium-battery consumption tax is in force. He cited the lead-acid industry, where 90% of producers exited after a 10% consumption tax was imposed.

Applied to lithium cells, he expects a substantial share of makers to exit within three years, with the first quarter of 2027 as the transition period. His advice to peers was not to be a follower — "the gaps are hard to find" — and to look for innovative niches and technologies instead.

The policy dates to July. Announcement No. 20 of 2026 by the Ministry of Finance, the customs administration and the tax administration imposed a 2% consumption tax on mercury-free primary batteries, nickel-metal-hydride, lithium primary, lithium-ion and vanadium flow batteries from September 1, 2026, rising to 4% from September 1, 2027. Sodium-ion, solid-state and fuel-cell batteries, along with some perovskite and stacked photovoltaic cells, are exempt until December 31, 2028, China Securities Journal reported.

EVE is not slowing down. According to Guandian, Liu said at the same event that he expects the company's cylindrical cell deliveries to exceed 100GWh in 2027–2028.

Why it mattersA consumption tax is levied on the sales price, so 2% goes straight into cost. For a tier-2 cell maker running a single-digit net margin, 2% of revenue is a large share of net profit; for a leader it is a repricing exercise. Who bears it depends on bargaining power: makers with tax pass-through clauses in long-term contracts push it onto carmakers and storage customers, while makers who quote and close at the same number absorb it themselves. Liu's 90% analogy describes that mechanism, not demand. This is an administered shakeout of supply with a published timetable — the year between 2% and 4% is a countdown for tail capacity, so exits will cluster around early 2027 while leaders gain both share and pricing power. Downstream, the cost shows up first in quotes and then in the internal rate of return on storage projects.

11/21 09:35 China Securities Journal ↗

Cement price letters and traded prices diverge: Rmb20 to Rmb80 a tonne sought while the national average falls

Cement producers have issued a wave of price-increase letters this month, with regional increases of Rmb20 to Rmb80 a tonne, China Securities Journal reported on November 21. The increases are concentrated in southern China and driven mainly by high coal costs, but downstream buyers are resisting them.

Traded prices went the other way. Monitoring by Zhuochuang Consulting showed the national average price lower in the week to November 20 than in the week before.

Several listed producers said this year's prices are below last year's. Cold weather has effectively ended the season in the north, leaving only sporadic shipments; the south is relatively better, with plants chasing incremental demand from urban renewal and public facilities. Some listed companies are pivoting into new businesses or expanding overseas.

Why it mattersA price letter is a quote, not a trade. Its job is to move the starting point of the next negotiation with distributors and ready-mix plants, while the clearing price is still set by supply and demand. Here the cost push comes from coal while demand contracts — the north is shut for the season and property and infrastructure volumes have not returned — so the pass-through rate is low, and Zhuochuang's weekly number already gives the verdict: letters up, average price down. Who pays: if the pass-through works, contractors and project owners; if it does not, the producers' own gross margin, which is the norm at low utilisation. Durability depends on how long staggered production can hold the quote into the spring restart, but next year's price will be set by the pace of urban-renewal and water projects, which leads back to the NDRC's ledger.

11/22 07:59 Brokerage China ↗

First 18 active ETFs list on Monday with Rmb36bn raised and more than 300,000 holders, disclosing positions daily

Eighteen active ETFs will list on the Shanghai and Shenzhen exchanges on November 23, nine on each. Listing documents filed on November 17 show that all 18 hit their Rmb2bn fundraising caps and closed early, with 17 allocating pro rata after oversubscription, raising Rmb36bn in total from more than 300,000 valid subscribers.

Positioning was disclosed in detail: average equity exposure was 56.71% as of November 16, with the lowest below 30%, and the products had to lift exposure above 80% during the build-out window of November 17–20. Twelve of the 18 beat their benchmarks over that window, and seven moved more than 1% while four moved more than 2%.

The real change is disclosure. Active ETFs publish holdings through the daily portfolio composition file before each session, against six periodic reports a year for conventional active equity funds. Positions go from quarterly to daily, letting investors compute an indicative net asset value, judge premiums and discounts, and check for style drift.

The format is not unique to China. Morningstar puts US discretionary active ETFs at about $1.03tn and systematic active ETFs at about $650.8bn at the end of June, together 83% of active ETF assets.

Why it mattersTransparency reallocates cost; it is not a free good. A daily composition file makes pricing more efficient and premiums harder to sustain, but it also prices the marginal information in a strategy faster, so front-running and replication get more expensive for the manager and excess return decays sooner than in a pooled fund. Who pays: the manager gives up secrecy and buys intraday liquidity and lower subscription friction; arbitrageurs earn the convergence of premiums and discounts; long-term money gets a vehicle it can see through. Rmb36bn is a rounding error against roughly $1.7tn in the United States, and what decides the future is not disclosure frequency but whether net-of-fee excess return stays positive — if it does not, daily disclosure only speeds up the exit. One cost is easy to miss: when every position is visible every day, the rebalancing itself becomes information, and the larger the portfolio the sooner the impact cost lands in the NAV.
