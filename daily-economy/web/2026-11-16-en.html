Hormuz stays shut: two tankers hit mines, and fertiliser prices are already about 30% above pre-war levels

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps said on November 15 that two tankers struck mines in the southern approaches to the Strait of Hormuz before dawn, exploded and caught fire. Its navy said both vessels had switched off navigation and automatic identification systems to slip through unauthorised channels, and that two more tankers attempting the same thing turned back to the Gulf after warnings. The same day the Guard said its aerospace force flew six drones against a US air-defence radar at Riffa in Bahrain, declared the American escort mission a failure and repeated that the strait is closed.

Prices moved first in weekend dark trading: the report put New York crude up 0.673% and Brent up 0.378% at the time of writing, with futures exchanges closed. On volumes, US Central Command said on November 14 that Gulf partners had shipped more than 1.7bn barrels of crude through the strait under US protection; a day earlier the Guard said it had intercepted and destroyed a US MQ-1 drone above Qeshm island.

The real transmission runs through fertiliser and food. UNOPS executive director Da Silva said on November 14 that about a third of the world's fertiliser passed through Hormuz before the conflict, and that global fertiliser prices have risen roughly 30% since fighting began. He called for at least five fertiliser vessels a day, said existing UN mechanisms could restore shipments within seven to ten days, and warned that missing more planting seasons could leave 45 million people acutely food insecure and push 30 million into poverty.

Diplomacy has left one channel open. Xinhua reported that Kaskhavi, spokesman for the Iranian parliament's national security and foreign policy committee, said on November 15 that the committee had finished its expert review of a Hormuz package and had reached agreement with the two littoral states on shipping lanes — even as the Guard kept insisting the strait is shut. The military and diplomatic tracks are moving at the same time.

Why it mattersThe price that matters here is not crude but fertiliser. Oil has inventories, strategic reserves and shale supply that respond at the margin; fertiliser has no such buffer, because a third of global volumes travel one blockaded waterway. Once that is 30% more expensive, the next word is not "dear" but "absent". The cost lands on three balance sheets: farmers' planting budgets in importing countries, their governments' subsidy lines, and next season's food prices. Five fertiliser vessels a day and seven to ten days to restore shipments are the only operational thresholds anyone has published, and they are easier to price than "when will the blockade end". Watch urea and phosphate prices ex-works, and arrivals ahead of the northern-hemisphere spring planting, not the oil tape.
Another angle For China's fertiliser and pesticide chain, a 30% premium abroad resets the trade-off between exports and domestic supply: the marginal return on exporting rises while domestic prices feed straight into grain costs, so both constraints hit the same policy window. For pricing, the cost-to-price spread in fertiliser, pesticides and farm products will say more about how long this lasts than the crude complex does.

11/16 China Securities Journal ↗

China's securities regulator lists six research priorities, with A-share market value above Rmb100trn

China Securities Regulatory Commission chairman Wu Qing used the opening of the first China Capital Markets Academic Conference on November 14 to name six areas for deeper study: the particular rules of China's market, a long-term stability mechanism, the inclusiveness and adaptability of listing rules, two-way AI adoption, market microstructure, and China's role in global financial governance. He called for "a stable mechanism, stable forces and a stable ecosystem" to prevent abnormal swings.

The stocktaking came first. Shanghai Securities News reported that Wu put A-share market value above Rmb100trn, the bond market above Rmb210trn and futures and options products at more than 170. Social security, insurance and annuity funds have bought a net Rmb1.5trn of A-shares since 2024; in the first nine months of this year direct financing through equities and bonds reached nearly 50% of the increase in total social financing; dividends since the 2024 "nine-point" reform total nearly Rmb7trn, about three times equity fundraising over the same period; and qualified foreign investors now cover more than 40 countries and regions, with over 140 futures and options contracts open to them.

Structural problems were written into the research list. Under microstructure he asked for a review of "the optimisation of rules on quant trading and share reductions and fair, reasonable supervisory arrangements", and for better expectation management to curb herd behaviour and short-termism. Under inclusiveness he singled out the listing-rule fit and risk response for large and new-economy technology companies. The China Securities Journal report also cited his point that A-shares have more than 200 million stock investors and over 800 million fund holders — a retail base without parallel anywhere.

Why it mattersTwo of the six will actually change rules: the quant-and-reduction review under microstructure, which sets the marginal trader's cost, and the listing fit for large tech companies, which sets the duration of new supply. The hardest number in the speech is Rmb7trn of dividends at roughly three times equity fundraising, because it retires the "financing market" story and replaces it with a "return market" one — but dividends merely redistribute existing cash and price nothing on their own. What reprices the market is the duration behind Rmb1.5trn of net buying by pension, insurance and annuity money: the longer the holding period, the more tolerant the market is of a single quarter's results, and the more the anchor shifts from quarterly profit to cash flow. The test is not the speech but fourth-quarter equity allocations at mutual funds and insurers, and when the draft rules on quant trading surface.

Rail freight reaches 3.42bn tonnes in ten months, up 1.1%, while rail-road volumes jump 48.4%

China State Railway Group said on November 15 that national rail freight came to 3.42bn tonnes in January-October, with average daily wagon loading of 189,200, up 1.1% and 1.8% respectively. Coal accounted for 1.75bn tonnes, including 1.16bn tonnes of power-station coal; since October the group has issued supply-guarantee dispatch orders for eight power plants as cold air pushed the north into the heating season; grain shipments exceeded 100 million tonnes, up 11.7%.

The growth sits in the product mix. Operators signed 1,675 bundled logistics contracts covering 1.32bn tonnes of volume; 41,900 inter-regional freight trains ran in the period, up 22.5%, carrying 45.92 million tonnes, up 37.0%; the 95306 platform added 317 rail-water "single bill" projects and container rail-water volumes rose 8.9%; rail-road combined volumes reached 385 million tonnes, up 48.4%.

International services and the financing tools around them grew alongside. China-Europe freight trains ran 18,600 services, up 14.6%; Central Asia services ran 12,100, up 1.9%; the New International Land-Sea Trade Corridor moved more than 1.13 million TEU by rail-sea. So-called freight loans, freight certificates and freight bills — railway logistics finance products — have extended a cumulative Rmb55bn, and 17 driver rest stations have opened at freight yards, offering free rest, water and washing facilities to the truckers who handle first and last miles.

Why it mattersFreight volumes grew 1.1% while rail-road combined volumes grew 48.4%: a slow total with a fast mix means the railway is switching cargo, not waiting for it. Bundled contracts — 1,675 of them, 1.32bn tonnes — pull medium and long-haul road freight onto rails, and freight loans finance the customer while they switch. Who pays? The railway gives up tariff and payment terms to buy load factors; what gets displaced is the road carrier's long-haul mileage and some drivers' income. For the macro read, separate two sets of numbers: 1.75bn tonnes of coal, 1.16bn tonnes of power coal and 100 million tonnes of grain are supply-guarantee data, about energy and food security. The demand data are the 22.5% jump in inter-regional trains, 48.4% in rail-road volumes and 8.9% in containers. Whether those hold in November and December decides whether cheaper logistics are actually cutting inventories on corporate balance sheets.

Five years of the Beijing Stock Exchange: 355 companies, Rmb800bn of value, daily turnover near Rmb20bn

The Beijing Stock Exchange marked its fifth anniversary on November 15. As of November 13 it had 355 listed companies and about Rmb800bn of market value, against 81 companies and less than Rmb300bn at launch. More than 60% are national-level "little giant" specialists, over 90% are high-tech enterprises, 20 have become manufacturing champions in their niche, and nearly 80% of the companies listed since 2025 are little giants.

On the financing side, the exchange has raised nearly Rmb90bn for small and mid-sized companies since opening, with more than 80% of proceeds going to green and low-carbon, digital economy, high-end equipment and new materials. Forty-three listed companies have announced 50 follow-on offerings and five have completed major asset restructurings. Since taking over corporate bond review, the exchange has registered more than Rmb120bn and issued Rmb27.48bn.

On the investing side, qualified investors exceed 11.8 million, nearly three times the number at launch. Institutional accounts exceed 200,000 and the number of mutual funds investing in the board has grown from about 100 to more than 3,000. Market makers have expanded from 13 to 24, covering more than 50% of listed names. Average daily turnover has risen from under Rmb1bn at launch to almost Rmb20bn this year, and institutions now account for 25% of trading, up from 9%.

The wider ecosystem moved too. The New Third Board has served 15,000 small and mid-sized companies, helped more than 200 regional equity-market companies list through green channels, and passed nearly 850 companies up to the Shanghai, Shenzhen and Beijing exchanges. Private equity has participated in private placements at about 400 New Third Board companies, investing more than Rmb26bn, and nine in ten Beijing-listed companies have taken venture capital.

Why it mattersRmb800bn of market value against Rmb90bn of cumulative fundraising is a ratio of about 11%, and that number explains the board better than "355 companies" does: it is a market built for small, repeated raises, not an IPO factory. The real change is in who sets prices. Institutional share of trading rose from 9% to 25% at the same time as daily turnover went from under Rmb1bn to almost Rmb20bn, which means the liquidity came from institutions rather than retail. The risk is in the same article: Zhu Haibin of Kaiyuan Securities notes that trading is concentrated in the leaders while the tail still lacks depth — one rulebook, 355 companies, and not one kind of liquidity. Watch two things: whether BSE 50 volatility falls as institutions take over, and whether bid-ask spreads in the tail narrow now that market makers cover more than half the board.

Dutch court sets a EUR300m penalty ceiling for Wingtech, which refuses any forced sale of Nexperia

*ST Wingtech (600745.SH) said in a November 15 evening filing that the enterprise chamber of the Amsterdam Court of Appeal heard additional interim applications from its offshore units Nexperia Holding B.V. and Nexperia B.V. on October 30 Dutch time and ruled on November 11. The court ordered the company and related parties to withdraw some claims filed in Chinese courts and attached penalties to breaches of its orders, capped at EUR300m in total.

The penalty structure is worth reading line by line: four obligations carry EUR5m per breach, per day of continuation or per day of delay, each capped at EUR100m; the duty to file later pleadings carries EUR1m per breach, capped at EUR10m; all payments go to Nexperia Holding B.V. The court also ordered the company, Yucheng Holding and Nexperia China to withdraw two claims within seven days from a suit filed with the Guangdong High People's Court on May 7 and now heard by the Dongguan Intermediate People's Court, and barred interference with Nexperia's dealings with Tata Electronics and Polar Semiconductor, with capacity expansion, IP licensing and financing.

Two legal tracks are now live at once. Dongguan Intermediate People's Court issued a conduct-preservation order on November 2 barring the offshore Nexperia entities, ITEC B.V. and three individuals from disposing of the disputed equity, intellectual property or other assets, with daily penalties for breach. *ST Wingtech says the Dutch ruling has no legal effect inside China, will appeal to a Dutch court, has heard nothing back since proposing talks at the end of September, and will not accept any forced sale of its Nexperia stake; it also says court-appointed officers have repeatedly floated an outcome in which the company holds only a minority.

Why it mattersThe EUR300m is the memorable number, but it is a compliance and litigation cost: it lands first on the listed company's accounts, as a non-operating charge or a provision, and is ultimately borne by shareholders — and the real price is not the penalty but the equity itself. Once the court's demand to "negotiate with potential counterparties" and its refusal to let the company approach them directly convert into a minority stake, Nexperia stops consolidating: revenue, profit and cash flow are restated, and valuation shifts from an operating multiple to an equity-method return. For the supply chain, Nexperia supplies automotive-grade power devices, and a frozen ownership structure locks expansion, IP licensing and financing behind a docket. Customers will price a second source into the next tender, in lead times and in price.

11/15 19:51 CCTV News ↗

China's 2026 box office passes Rmb32bn, with industry output value estimated near Rmb500bn

CCTV News reported on November 15 that the year's total box office, including presales, had passed Rmb32bn as of that day. The top five films of the year are all domestic: Pegasus 3, Welcome to the Dragon Restaurant, Kung Fu Women's Football, Eight Immortals! and A Love Letter to Grandma.

The same report said that, by its estimate, output value across the film chain has reached nearly Rmb500bn this year, with "film plus" taking new forms.

The two figures use different definitions: Rmb32bn is end-market ticket revenue including presales, while Rmb500bn is industry output value spanning production, distribution, exhibition, licensing and film-related tourism. Dividing one by the other does not produce a box-office share of output. The report gives no year-on-year growth rate, and the Rmb32bn figure includes presales, so it will differ from final settled receipts.

Why it mattersFilm is one of the few consumer industries where price and volume are both visible: box office equals average ticket price times admissions, and only the split tells you whether more people came or tickets got dearer. Rmb32bn alone says nothing about heat; what says something is that the top five are all domestic. A smaller import share hands more of the box-office swing to domestic release calendars, and the more clustered the calendar, the higher the per-film risk and marketing spend. The gap between Rmb500bn of output value and Rmb32bn of tickets sits in production, equipment, cinema rents and film-linked tourism — revenue that never passes through box-office splits and is therefore free of the revenue-share formula, which is exactly the increment cinemas and producers are chasing. For 2027 earnings, watch the share of non-ticket revenue, not the box-office total: tickets are the price of traffic, non-ticket revenue is the rate at which that traffic is monetised.
