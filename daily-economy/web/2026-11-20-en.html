Money & Policy

货币与政策
11/20 09:00 People's Bank of China ↗

China leaves the LPR unchanged for an 18th month at 3.0% and 3.5%, with overnight repo still at 1.25%

The People's Bank of China authorised the National Interbank Funding Center to publish the loan prime rates for November 20, 2026: 3.0% for one year and 3.5% for five years and above. Both tenors were left where they were last month and hold until the next release. Chinanews reported that the two rates have now gone 18 consecutive months without a change, dating to June 2025.

The same morning produced the other price. The central bank supplied Rmb379.5bn of overnight reverse repos through a fixed-rate, quantity-based tender at 1.25%, with bids and allotments both at that amount, fully meeting the demand of primary dealers (see open market operation notice No. 225 of 2026).

The benchmark itself is being reworked. The central bank's third-quarter monetary policy report proposes moving loan pricing away from a near-exclusive reliance on the LPR toward a wider set of benchmarks, and asks institutions to experiment with floating-rate loans linked to DR001, Chinanews reported.

Now compare that with the government curve. Seven-year recapitalisation bonds sold on November 19 carry a 1.40% coupon — 210 basis points below the five-year-and-over LPR at 3.5%, and only 15 basis points above the 1.25% overnight operation rate. From overnight money to a seven-year bond, 15 basis points is the entire price the market charges for time.

Why it mattersEighteen months without a move has changed what the LPR is: less a monthly policy signal than the anchor for contracts already on the books, while pricing power for new lending shifts toward short rates such as DR001. Two spreads tell the story. The 175 basis points between the one-year LPR at 3.0% and the 1.25% overnight rate is the gross space between bank assets and liabilities. The 210 basis points between the five-year-and-over LPR and the 1.40% seven-year bond is what banks earn for transforming duration. How long that structure holds depends on deposit costs: if deposits keep repricing lower while the LPR stands still, margins recover passively and savers pay; if deposit costs are sticky, an unchanged LPR means corporate funding costs cannot fall either. The real variable is not the level of rates but the frequency of repricing — mortgages adjust annually, DR001-linked loans adjust daily, and interest rate risk moves off bank balance sheets into borrowers' cash flow.
Another angleFor anyone financing data centres, that matters more than the LPR itself. These loans typically run eight to ten years; if new contracts switch to a DR001-plus spread, project cash flow becomes immediately sensitive to the overnight rate, and the fixed-rate assumption in the model has to go. Daily DR001 volatility is far larger than the LPR's, and over a year it can eat 20 to 30 basis points of a project's buffer.

11/20 Ministry of Finance ↗

Finance ministry sells $2bn of three-year dollar bonds in Hong Kong at 4.821%, with orders 36 times the deal

The Ministry of Finance's press office said on November 20 that the sovereign issued $2bn of three-year dollar bonds in Hong Kong on November 19, at a yield of 4.821%.

Demand was heavy: total orders reached $72.3bn, 36 times the amount on offer. By region, Asian investors took 52%, Europe and the Middle East 34% and the Americas 14%. By type, sovereign accounts took 59%, funds and asset managers 29%, banks and insurers 11% and dealers 1%. The bonds will list on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange.

Set the two sovereign prices side by side: seven-year renminbi recapitalisation bonds priced at 1.40% on November 19, while three-year dollar paper cleared at 4.821% — a 342 basis point gap for the same issuer in two currencies, spanning both the dollar-renminbi spread and the four-year difference in tenor.

The buyer mix matters for what happens next. With 59% in sovereign hands, this is reserve allocation rather than a trading position; the 41% left over is what will provide liquidity in the secondary market.

Why it matters4.821% is not China's sovereign rate; it is the rate on Chinese sovereign risk denominated in dollars, and the 342 basis points are what the market charges for the two currencies. Thirty-six times coverage says demand is not the constraint — supply of high-grade dollar assets that can sit in reserve portfolios is. Outside US Treasuries, there are few three-year sovereign deals of this size. For the ministry, $2bn is too small to change the debt structure; the point is to draw a dollar curve for Chinese issuers, so that the next corporate deal offshore prices off it rather than off Treasuries plus a guessed spread. Judge the deal not on the 36 times but on where the three-year trades after listing: above 4.821% means the primary market priced it too cheaply and the next deal has to pay more.

11/20 Xinhua Finance ↗

Japan's October core CPI rises 2.2%, the most since December 2025, as swaps price an 86% chance of a December hike

Japan's statistics bureau said on November 20 that core consumer prices, which exclude fresh food, rose 2.2% in October from a year earlier, up from 2.1% in September and the fastest since December 2025. Headline CPI rose 2.3%, Xinhua Finance reported.

Core-core inflation, which also strips out energy and is watched as a gauge of underlying pressure, rose 2.2%, down from 2.4% in September. Core inflation has now held above 2% for two straight months, and the October reading came in just below the 2.3% consensus.

Policy has already turned. The Bank of Japan raised its policy rate to 1.25% in September, the highest in 31 years, held in October and confirmed for the first time that underlying inflation had reached the 2% target. Governor Kazuo Ueda told parliament on two consecutive days that the bank remains ready to keep raising rates, arguing that short-term real rates are still low and financial conditions accommodative.

Market pricing is more specific than the rhetoric: swaps assign roughly an 86% probability to a 25 basis point increase to 1.50% at the December 18 meeting. In a Reuters poll of 71 economists conducted November 11-18, 61, or 86%, expected a December move. If it happens, it would be the first calendar year with three hikes in 37 years.

Growth is the constraint. Cabinet Office data published on November 16 showed third-quarter real GDP contracting 0.1% annualised, with private consumption down 0.3% on the quarter for a second consecutive decline.

Why it mattersWith policy at 1.25% and core inflation at 2.2%, Japan's real policy rate is minus 0.95% — the entire case for hiking into a contracting economy. A negative real rate subsidises borrowers and penalises savers, so standing still while inflation holds above target amounts to passive easing. The argument is therefore not whether to move but where to stop: a December rise to 1.50% still leaves the real rate at minus 0.70%, so the carry that funds yen-funded positions narrows rather than disappears. For global assets, the transmission channel is not Japanese demand but the cost of unwinding those positions, a quantity that appears in cross-border bank balance sheets rather than in the statistics bureau's CPI. For importers of Japanese equipment, higher yen rates show up in the exchange rate first and in procurement costs second; the list price moves last.

11/19 21:42 Xinhua Finance ↗

US jobless claims rise to 203,000 against a 197,000 forecast, as the prior week is revised up to 202,000

Initial claims for unemployment benefits came in at 203,000 in the week to November 14, against expectations of 197,000, with the prior week revised up to 202,000 from 200,000, Xinhua Finance reported.

The week before was also above forecast: 200,000 in the week to November 7 against 195,000 expected, with the earlier figure revised up to 191,000 from 189,000. The week to October 10 printed 199,000 against a 200,000 forecast.

Across 2026 the weekly series has mostly run between 190,000 and 210,000: 209,000 in late January, 206,000 in mid-February and 196,000 in mid-September, with a low of 189,000 in late October that was later revised up to 191,000. At 203,000 the level is still low, but 7,000 above the mid-September reading.

Why it mattersClaims are a flow, not a stock. In a labour market the size of America's, 203,000 describes a combination of low layoffs and slow hiring: firms are not cutting, but they are not adding either, so new entrants wait longer and claims drift up from a low base. For policymakers the implication is direct — unemployment will not spike and wage pressure will not fade quickly, so this reading does not weaken the case for a December hike; if anything, the fact that it is not weaker keeps the case intact. The 6,000 miss is noise. Separating slow hiring from real job losses requires continuing claims and the gap to job openings, not the weekly headline. For exporters, it means US orders will not vanish suddenly, but the inventory cycle stretches, and the slope of restocking matters more than the level of orders.

Philadelphia Fed manufacturing index drops to 6.3 from 20.9 against a 20.55 forecast, and October leading indicators fall 0.3%

The Philadelphia Fed's manufacturing index for November came in at 6.3, against a forecast of 20.55 and a prior reading of 20.9, Cailian Press reported — a fall of 14.6 points in a month.

Released the same evening, the Conference Board's leading economic index for October fell 0.3% month on month, against expectations of 0% and a prior reading of 0% (Wallstreetcn).

The two point the same way: one measures current regional manufacturing conditions, the other is designed to lead activity by six to nine months. The first fell sharply, the second turned from flat to negative.

The reading is still positive, so expansion has not ended. Diffusion indices of this kind use zero as the dividing line, not 50: 6.3 means more respondents reported growth than contraction, but the margin narrowed from 20.9 points to 6.3.

Why it mattersThis is a trap worth naming. The Philadelphia Fed index uses zero as its break-even, so reading 6.3 the way one reads a PMI at 50 produces a verdict of deep contraction; read as a diffusion index, the correct verdict is still expanding, with more than half the momentum gone. The same number, a different line, and two opposite policy implications. What matters now is the slope, not the level: a further move toward zero in December would say the manufacturing recovery has peaked under the combined weight of tariffs and rates. For the leading index, count the consecutive negative months — one month at minus 0.3% is a hint, three is a signal. For manufacturers selling into the US, 6.3 means orders are still there but the increment has flattened, so plan for stable volumes rather than rising volumes and prices.

Anthropic closes its first session at $97.17, up 17.07% from the $83 offer, on an $81.3bn IPO

Anthropic, the developer of Claude, listed on Nasdaq on November 19 and closed its first session at $97.17 a share, up 17.07% from an offer price of $83, Securities Times reported.

The deal raised about $81.3bn, surpassing SpaceX's $75bn in June to become the largest IPO on record.

Strategic buyers were disclosed alongside the listing: Google, Nvidia, AMD, Broadcom, Micron, Microsoft and TSMC will buy about $26bn of stock at the same price, with Google and Nvidia taking $10bn each.

The prospectus shows revenue of $11.6bn in the second quarter, up 1,368% from a year earlier, and operating profit of $974m — its first profitable quarter at the operating line. Revenue rose to $17bn in the third quarter, but the company posted a net loss of $80.7bn for the first half.

Why it mattersRead the $81.3bn raise together with the $26bn of strategic money and the structure becomes a public offering plus a private placement: public markets bought the growth story, industrial capital bought compute commitments, at the same price but with very different duration. Financially, two figures are true at once — $974m of operating profit in the second quarter and an $80.7bn net loss in the first half. Operating profit contains only the business; net income also carries everything outside it, and a gap of more than $80bn says the loss was not produced by selling model access. The report does not break that gap down, which is why operating profit and free cash flow matter more here than net income — and why the years in which those $26bn of commitments turn into purchase obligations matter too. A 17% first-day gain does not settle whether the stock is expensive; it says the underwriters set $83 between clearing the book and leaving something on the table. The real test is the lock-up expiry and the next quarterly report. For suppliers into data centres, the $26bn means volumes for the next two years are contracted before prices are.

Pinduoduo's third-quarter revenue is flat at Rmb108.3bn, below the Rmb115.2bn expected, with Rmb29.5bn of profit

Pinduoduo reported results for the quarter to September 30 on November 19: revenue of Rmb108.3bn, unchanged from a year earlier and below the Rmb115.2bn consensus, with net profit of Rmb29.5bn against Rmb29.3bn a year earlier, the China Securities Journal reported.

The shares first fell in US pre-market trading and then rallied, gaining 8.5% by 20:30 Beijing time.

Management put the emphasis on spending. Co-chairman and co-CEO Zhao Jiazhen said the company's "Rmb100bn support" programme continues to invest in the ecosystem, extending from merchants to the whole supply chain. Co-chairman and co-CEO Chen Lei said regulatory requirements, compliance rules and tariff policies across global markets keep changing and bring new demands.

The arithmetic of the quarter: revenue flat at Rmb108.3bn, profit of Rmb29.5bn, a net margin of about 27.2%, and just Rmb0.2bn more profit than a year ago on the same revenue.

Why it mattersRevenue stalled at Rmb108.3bn while profit held at Rmb29.5bn, which together say the subsidies have not stopped and the growth has. A 27% net margin was not bought with higher prices; it was bought with the pace of spending. The market's 8.5% rebound read "profit held" rather than "revenue missed", and that reading carries a cost: if revenue stays flat next quarter while profit is defended by shrinking support, 27% is a cyclical peak rather than a norm. Where the support money is booked decides the shape of the next two years of statements — booked at the merchant end it is a revenue concession, booked at the platform end it is an expense that hits margin, and the two pull operating cash flow in opposite directions. For factories selling on the platform, zero growth in revenue means traffic allocation shifts from acquiring new buyers to fighting over existing ones, and the cost of buying traffic only rises.

11/19 Xinhua Finance ↗

Alibaba grows second-quarter revenue 10% with adjusted EBITA up 260%, as cloud external revenue rises 64%

Alibaba Group reported results for the second quarter of its 2027 fiscal year on November 19: group revenue rose 10% from a year earlier and adjusted EBITA rose 260%, Xinhua Finance reported.

Cloud carried the quarter. External revenue at Alibaba Cloud grew 64%, external recurring revenue, which excludes revenue from compute-equipment solutions, grew 67%, adjusted EBITA rose 121% and the EBITA margin reached 14.5%. Revenue from AI-related products grew at a triple-digit rate for a 13th consecutive quarter.

On the commerce side, adjusted EBITA at the Alibaba e-commerce group rose 243%, instant retail revenue grew 22%, Taobao Flash Sale held its share with narrower losses and improving unit economics.

Two definitions are worth separating. External recurring revenue at 67% is higher than external revenue at 64%, and the gap is compute-equipment solutions — strip that out and the number reflects subscriptions and usage. Group revenue at 10% and adjusted EBITA at 260% are not points on the same curve either.

Why it mattersRevenue up 10% and adjusted EBITA up 260% describe two different things: scale on one side, and the operating leverage that arrives when spending stops outrunning revenue on the other. The quality sits in the cloud disclosure. Recurring external revenue, which excludes compute-equipment solutions, grew faster at 67% than headline external revenue at 64%, which tells you that low-margin hardware sales are diluting the mix and that the underlying subscription and usage trend is stronger than the headline. An EBITA margin of 14.5% with adjusted EBITA up 121% means profit grew an order of magnitude faster than revenue, which is more likely to come from scale and product mix than from pricing. Watch capex next: if cloud revenue keeps growing above 60% while capex growth slows, the business shifts from buying capacity with cash to earning on capacity already bought — that is when a re-rating becomes possible. If revenue growth instead depends on capex staying high, the problem has only moved from the income statement to the balance sheet. For data-centre, power and cooling suppliers, the capex rhythm at cloud providers matters more for orders than the revenue line does.
