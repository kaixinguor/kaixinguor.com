Monetary Policy

货币与政策
11/27 16:11 Securities Times ↗

Beijing deploys the “national team” to put a floor under A-shares; ChiNext jumps 11.61% on Rmb2.02tn turnover

China’s State Council met on November 27 and published its readout before the afternoon session. It said capital markets matter to the broader economy and promised to improve the stabilisation mechanism, implement existing policies and prepare new ones. It also said the “national team” should play a bigger role, and that Central Huijin’s quasi-stabilisation-fund function should be improved to provide ample liquidity.

Xinhua’s readout called it the first time the phrase “national team” had appeared in a State Council meeting statement. The meeting also asked listed companies to pay more dividends and buy back more shares, and called for easier entry for insurance, annuity and social-security funds.

The market did the rest. The Shanghai Composite rose 3.73% to 3,606.49 and the Shenzhen Component rose 7.24%. ChiNext rose 11.61% and the STAR 50 rose 15.07%.

Turnover across the three exchanges reached Rmb2.02tn, up Rmb682.4bn or 52.17% from the previous session. 5,382 stocks rose, 181 fell and 426 closed limit-up, the most since October 8, 2024.

Hard-tech names led; banks were the main sector to fall. The STAR 50 had fallen 25.26% between November 12 and November 26, so short covering amplified the rebound.

What to watchThe “national team” supplies a buyer, not earnings. Central Huijin’s quasi-stabilisation-fund role puts the tail risk of equity prices on the central bank and fiscal balance sheet. The Rmb2.02tn turnover and 426 limit-ups show private money adding risk because policy signalled a floor, not because profits improved. The cost falls on households and leveraged funds that chase the rally if prices run ahead of earnings; the first gains go to brokers, exchanges and early holders. Two tests matter: whether turnover stays above Rmb2tn, and whether limit-up stocks hold the next day. If either fails, this looks more like a policy-triggered short squeeze than a re-rating driven by profits.
Another angle The rally was led by semiconductors, optical modules and memory, not banks. The AI capex outlook did not change on November 27; the discount rate and risk appetite did. A 25.26% drawdown in the STAR 50 was largely erased in one session, which says more about positioning than about earnings.

11/27 20:02 People’s Bank of China ↗

PBOC stability report keeps financing conditions loose while warning against “blow-ups”

The People’s Bank of China published its Financial Stability Report for 2026 on the evening of November 27. The report reviews 2025: GDP reached Rmb140.19tn, up 5%, and foreign-exchange reserves stayed above $3.3tn.

It lists six areas of work. The PBOC cut the reserve-requirement ratio by 0.5 percentage points in 2025, releasing about Rmb1tn of long-term liquidity, and lowered policy and structural-tool rates. The number of financing platforms and their operating financial debt fell sharply from early 2023, and high-risk small banks were reduced well below their peak.

For the next stage, the report says liquidity should stay ample and social-financing conditions should remain “relatively loose”. It also says new risks must be controlled, legacy risks handled and “blow-ups” prevented. On the currency, it repeats that the market should play a decisive role and the renminbi should stay basically stable at a reasonable equilibrium.

The report also says work on the financial-stability law and the PBOC law has accelerated, a macroprudential and financial-stability committee has been set up, and the deposit-insurance and financial-stability funds are still being built up.

What to watchThe report reviews 2025, but the phrase “relatively loose” is what matters for pricing. The Rmb1tn released by the RRR cut is base money, not free money: it lowers banks’ funding costs first and lending rates later. Fewer financing platforms and high-risk small banks mean risks are converging on the books, but the deposit-insurance and financial-stability funds will keep expanding, and the ultimate backstop is the fiscal authority. The policy order has changed: preventing blow-ups now comes before deleveraging. That should compress tail spreads on local-government financing vehicles and small-bank debt, at the cost of moral hazard and future resolution bills. Watch two things: progress on the financial-stability law, and whether asset write-offs actually happen rather than bad loans being rolled over.

11/27 INSEE (France) ↗

French inflation falls to 2.9%, Spain holds at 4.1%, and the ECB still looks set to hike

France’s INSEE reported a November flash estimate of 2.9% annual CPI inflation on November 27, down from 3.4% in October, with prices down 0.7% on the month. Harmonised inflation was 3.2%, down from 3.7%.

Spain’s INE reported November CPI at 4.1%, down from 4.9%, and core inflation at 2.6%, down from 3.1%. Prices fell 0.6% on the month and harmonised inflation was 4.1%, also down from 4.9%.

Fuel prices drove both declines. French energy prices were still up 19.2% from a year earlier, but that was below October’s 24.1%; services inflation slowed to 2.3% from 2.6%.

At the same time, the ECB’s consumer expectations survey showed euro-area households sharply raising their one-year inflation expectations. Xinhua reported that the ECB has effectively pre-announced a hike on December 17, which would be its third this year.

What to watchOne currency, two inflation rates: Spain at 4.1% and France at 2.9%, a 1.2-point gap. Both fell for the same reason, cheaper fuel, but French energy prices are still up 19.2% and Spanish core inflation is still 2.6%. The ECB watches the second number, because wages and services can turn an energy shock into second-round inflation. The cost of a hike falls first on southern European households and firms refinancing debt, then on import prices through a stronger euro. Two tests: whether euro-area services inflation falls below 2.5% after the December 17 meeting, and whether Brent holds $95. If energy rises again, one hike will not be enough; if services keep cooling, December may be the last.

Growth & Structure

增长与结构
11/27 09:30 National Bureau of Statistics ↗

Industrial profits rise 16.0% as electronics adds 9.7 points and autos fall 26.0%

China’s industrial profits rose 16.0% year on year to Rmb6.74tn in January-October, the National Bureau of Statistics said on November 27, 0.5 percentage points faster than in January-September. October profits alone rose 20.1%, 5.7 points faster than September.

Mining profits rose 38.8%, manufacturing 17.5%, and utilities fell 14.8%. Electronics profits doubled and contributed 9.7 percentage points to the overall increase, according to the NBS interpretation.

The split is wide. Computer manufacturing profits rose 410%, electronic materials 220% and optoelectronic devices 69.7%. Auto profits fell 26.0%, non-metallic minerals 46.7% and ferrous metals 14.2%.

Revenue rose 6.7% to Rmb118.41tn, and the operating margin improved 0.45 points to 5.69%.

But receivables rose 10.5% to Rmb30.45tn, and the average collection period lengthened by 1.4 days to 72.2 days. Finished-goods inventory rose 12.0% to Rmb7.54tn.

What to watchElectronics alone contributed 9.7 of the 16.0 points of profit growth. That is not a broad recovery: AI computing orders are concentrating profits in memory, optical components and electronic materials. Autos fell 26.0% and non-metallic minerals 46.7%, as price wars and the property chain kept eating margins. The cash-flow line matters more: revenue grew 6.7%, receivables grew 10.5%, and collection took 72.2 days. The profit is on the income statement; the cash is still in customers’ accounts. Suppliers and smaller manufacturers carry the cost, paying for materials before they get paid. Two tests: whether electronics orders spread beyond AI servers, and whether the auto price war ends before 2027. As long as receivables outrun revenue, the quality of the profit growth deserves a discount.

11/27 18:13 Ministry of Finance (CCTV) ↗

Local governments issue Rmb4.58tn of new bonds and pay Rmb1.33tn of interest in ten months

China’s local governments issued Rmb4.58tn of new bonds in January-October, the Ministry of Finance said on November 27: Rmb686.6bn of general bonds and Rmb3.89tn of special bonds.

They also issued Rmb4.89tn of refinancing bonds, more than the new-bond total, for overall issuance of Rmb9.47tn. Maturing principal of Rmb3.28tn was repaid and interest of Rmb1.33tn was paid.

Outstanding local-government debt stood at Rmb61.02tn at end-October, of which general debt was Rmb18.07tn and special debt Rmb42.95tn. The stock had an average remaining maturity of 11.0 years and an average rate of 2.69%.

New borrowing is cheaper. The average issuance rate was 2.00% with an average maturity of 15.2 years; in October alone the rate was 1.84% and maturity 16.2 years.

What to watchInterest of Rmb1.33tn equals about 29% of the Rmb4.58tn of new bonds issued in the same period. Refinancing bonds exceeded new bonds by Rmb316.6bn, so this year’s issuance is first about rolling over existing debt, not net new investment. New bonds cost 2.00% against a 2.69% average on the stock, so rolling over lowers the coupon; but new bonds carry a 15.2-year average maturity, longer than the stock’s 11-year remaining life, so the risk moves forward. The cost falls on future taxpayers; the gains go to banks and bondholders, who hold an asset with a government credit backstop. Two tests: whether special-bond projects generate enough cash to cover debt service, and whether land-sale revenue stops falling. If neither holds, next year’s refinancing will be larger.

11/28 04:57 Xinhua ↗

OPEC+ set to keep Q1 2027 targets, Brent closes at $95.44

Oil fell modestly on November 27, Xinhua reported. West Texas Intermediate for January delivery lost $0.60 to $83.08 a barrel, down 0.72%; Brent for January lost $1.13 to $95.44, down 1.17%.

OPEC+ is set to keep first-quarter 2027 output targets unchanged at its November 29 meeting, three sources told Reuters. Uncertainty over the Iran war and disputes over members’ capacity estimates are complicating the talks.

The fight is over capacity baselines. Iraq is unhappy with a preliminary assessment of 4.9m barrels a day, and Kazakhstan wants a higher number. Rystad Energy forecasts a global surplus of about 5m bpd next year, roughly 5% of demand.

Supply is shifting elsewhere. Iraq restarted the Kirkuk-Ceyhan pipeline on November 27 after 12 years, with first-phase capacity of 300,000 bpd. Russia’s oil output is set to fall to a record low of 494m tonnes this year, Deputy Prime Minister Alexander Novak said.

Iran said the Strait of Hormuz would stay closed, and a tanker hit a mine near Qanaem Island the same day, according to Iranian media cited by Jinshidian.

What to watchBrent only fell from $96.57 on November 26 to $95.44, so the market did not treat “no change” as the end of the supply story. The real variable is the Strait of Hormuz: Iran said on November 27 that the strait would stay closed, and a tanker hit a mine near Qanaem Island the same day. The risk of interruption remains; OPEC+ is simply not adding supply. The cost falls on Asian refiners and importers, who pay more for freight and war-risk insurance; the gains go to producers with spare capacity. Two tests: whether the November 29 meeting actually holds quotas, and whether Iraq’s pipeline restart adds 300,000 bpd. As long as Hormuz stays shut, $95 oil is unlikely to return to $80.

11/27 08:29 HKEX (China Shenhua filing) ↗

China Shenhua raises HK$6.73bn in H-share placement to repay onshore debt

China Shenhua said on November 27 that it would place up to 177m new H shares at HK$38.10 each. The price is a 7.70% discount to the November 26 close of HK$41.28 and a 7.85% discount to the five-day average of HK$41.34.

If fully placed, gross proceeds would be about HK$6.74bn and net proceeds about HK$6.73bn. About 90%, or HK$6.05bn, will repay onshore debt and improve the capital structure; about 10%, or HK$673m, will fund working capital.

The new shares equal 5.24% of existing H shares and 0.82% of total shares; after the placement they would be 4.98% of the enlarged H-share base and 0.81% of total shares.

The placement follows this year’s acquisition. Shenhua completed the purchase of 12 companies from National Energy Group and others in the first half, paying Rmb93.52bn in cash. At June 30, borrowings were Rmb157.63bn, including Rmb67.55bn of short-term bank and other loans, up Rmb54.34bn from end-2025.

In April, the company had already raised Rmb20bn by issuing 458m A shares.

What to watchThis placement sits at the end of a financing chain. Shenhua paid Rmb93.52bn in cash for the acquisition, short-term borrowings rose Rmb54.34bn, and now it is raising HK$6.73bn of equity to repay debt. The cost falls on existing H-share holders, whose stake is diluted by about 5.0% and who see the new shares priced 7.70% below the market. The gains go to creditors, as leverage and interest expense fall. The HK$6.73bn is only about one-ninth of the increase in short-term borrowings, so this is more a signal than a deleveraging. Two tests: whether the 12 acquired companies generate enough cash, and whether the equity cost is below the return on those assets. If not, the dilution is a net loss.
