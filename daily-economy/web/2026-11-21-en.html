11/21 07:38 Yicai ↗

Nasdaq falls 5.28% and Nvidia about 14%, leaving the Dow and S&P 500 more than 10% below their recent highs

US stocks closed sharply lower on November 20. The Dow fell 949.73 points, or 1.93%, to 48,147.33; the Nasdaq lost 1,261.47 points, or 5.28%, to 22,617.73; and the S&P 500 dropped 265.44 points, or 3.70%, to 6,910.39. The Nasdaq 100 fell 5.61%, the Russell 2000 2.76%, and the Cboe volatility index traded above 40 during the session, Yicai reported.

The week made the day worse. The Dow lost 8.02%, the S&P 500 8.61% and the Nasdaq 10.02%, the worst weekly declines for the Dow and the S&P since April 2025. The S&P 500 fell for a seventh straight session, its longest losing run since February 2020. Both the Dow and the S&P 500 are now more than 10% below their recent closing highs, while the Nasdaq has given up all of its gains for the year and sits 2.7% lower.

Chips bore the selling. Nvidia fell about 14%, its seventh consecutive decline and a weekly loss of roughly 23%. The Philadelphia semiconductor index dropped 10.73%, SanDisk about 16%, Micron and Arm about 14%, AMD more than 13%, Broadcom about 12% and TSMC's ADR more than 9%. Anthropic gave back its debut gain, falling 8.5% to $88.91; with the underwriters' overallotment exercised in full, the offering totalled about $93.5bn, alongside roughly $26bn of private financing.

Regulation was one trigger. US Securities and Exchange Commission staff are reportedly examining potential accounting issues at Nvidia, though the report did not specify the matters, when any inquiry began, or whether enforcement action is likely. Nvidia this week reported third-quarter fiscal-2027 revenue of $115.8bn, up 103%, with $107.6bn from data centres and a fourth-quarter revenue guide of $130bn, plus or minus 3% — results and guidance both above expectations. President Trump said the same day that he would tax companies that build data centres outside the United States, without specifying a rate, a start date or a legal mechanism.

Rates told the opposite story. Treasury constant-maturity yields closed at 4.63% for two years, down 13 basis points, 4.92% for 10 years, down 12, and 5.31% for 30 years, down 9. CME's FedWatch put the odds of a 25 basis point December hike at 91.4%, down from 100%, and the probability of 50 basis points of tightening by the end of January at 57.7%, down from more than 70% a day earlier. Fed governor Lisa Cook said inflation remains above target and financial conditions too loose, questioning whether a single 25 basis point hike in September was enough.

Why it mattersThis was a sell-off in which the denominator did not help. December hike odds only moved from 100% to 91.4% and the 10-year yield fell 12 basis points, so the discount rate barely changed; what was repriced was the cash flow. Nvidia grew revenue 103% and guided to $130bn, yet lost 23% in a week. The difference is that the market has started pricing the financing cost of capital spending rather than the demand for compute — and the accounting inquiry plus the threat to tax companies that build data centres abroad point at the same thing: AI spending is moving from the income statement to the balance sheet and the tax return. Bonds rallied while stocks fell, which says money is not leaving risk assets; it is demanding more compensation for the same cash flows.
Another angleFor anyone financing data centres, this matters more than the index level. Once equity investors demand a return, the weighted cost of capital rises and the leverage, power-price and exit assumptions all have to be redone. Twelve basis points off the risk-free rate offsets only a fraction of the debt cost and none of the risk-premium repricing — which is the arithmetic behind "rate cuts cannot save valuations". The other side: if the December hike lands while long yields keep falling, the curve flattens further, banks earn less for transforming duration, and that is the next balance sheet to be marked down.

11/20 Ministry of Finance ↗

China's first ten months: budget revenue up 4.9% to Rmb19.57tn, spending up just 1%, land revenue down 25.8%

The Ministry of Finance published January–October budget data on November 20. General public budget revenue reached Rmb19.5671tn, up 4.9% from a year earlier. Tax revenue rose 6% to Rmb16.2622tn while non-tax revenue slipped 0.2% to Rmb3.3049tn. Central revenue rose 7.5% to Rmb8.8036tn and local revenue 2.9% to Rmb10.7635tn.

Spending barely moved: Rmb22.8042tn, up 1%. Central spending rose 5.4% to Rmb3.6608tn; local spending rose 0.2% to Rmb19.1434tn. Education spending reached Rmb3.459tn, up 1.4%; social security and employment Rmb3.9544tn, up 4.8%; health Rmb1.8191tn, up 7.8%; debt interest Rmb1.1365tn, up 3.8%. Transport fell 5.2% to Rmb871.6bn and energy conservation and environment 11.8% to Rmb373.5bn.

The mix matters more than the total. Domestic VAT brought in Rmb6.1996tn, up 5.3%; corporate income tax Rmb4.148tn, up 5.9%; personal income tax Rmb1.6428tn, up 22.9%; import VAT and consumption tax Rmb1.6988tn, up 13.2%; and the securities transaction stamp duty Rmb251.1bn, up 54.1%. Property-linked taxes went the other way: deed tax fell 14.9% to Rmb310.2bn and land appreciation tax 16.6% to Rmb299.1bn.

The second budget shows the land hole. Government fund revenue fell 15.5% to Rmb2.9123tn, with local government fund revenue down 20.4% to Rmb2.4566tn and land-use-right revenue down 25.8% to Rmb1.8526tn. Government fund spending fell 13.8% to Rmb6.9746tn, including a 50.6% drop in central spending to Rmb422.9bn.

Execution is the other lens. Spending reached about 76% of the full-year budget in ten months, according to Securities Times, with October outlays of roughly Rmb1.78tn, below September. Personal income tax rose 89.6% in October alone, helped by tighter collection on offshore trusts (see Securities Times).

Why it mattersRevenue up 4.9% against spending up 1% is not a surplus; it is a timing gap. Ten months delivered about 76% of the annual spending budget, so the fourth quarter has to catch up — that is the arithmetic of back-loaded fiscal policy. The composition is the real story: the general budget's growth came from personal income tax, up 22.9% with an 89.6% jump in October tied to offshore-trust collection, and from the securities stamp duty, up 54.1%. Both ride asset prices and market turnover, which makes them flow taxes. The government fund shortfall comes from land, down 25.8%, which is the sale of a stock. Two tax bases on different cycles mean next year's revenue cannot be projected off this year's growth rates. For local governments, a 25.8% fall implies last year's land revenue was about Rmb2.50tn, so roughly Rmb640bn is missing — the same pot that services LGFV debt and funds infrastructure. Central spending up 5.4% against local spending up 0.2% is the mirror image of that squeeze.

11/20 23:00 University of Michigan Surveys of Consumers ↗

US consumer sentiment ends November at 51.5, up 7.3% on the month, as one-year inflation expectations fall to 3.7%

The University of Michigan's final November survey, released on November 20, put its consumer sentiment index at 51.5, up from 48.0 in October — a 7.3% monthly gain and 1.0% above November 2025's 51.0. The current conditions index rose to 49.4 from 46.7 and the expectations index to 52.9 from 48.8. Interviews were completed between October 20 and November 16.

Inflation expectations fell sharply. The median one-year expectation dropped to 3.7% from 4.7% in October, though it remains above the 3.4% recorded in February before the Iran conflict and above every 2024 reading. The long-run expectation edged down to 3.3% from 3.5%, above the 2.8%–3.2% range of 2024.

Surveys director Joanne Hsu said sentiment rose about 7% this month, led by post-midterm gains in expected business conditions. Lower-wealth households saw personal finances improve on falling petrol prices, middle-wealth households less so, while those with the largest stockholdings reported a decline — consistent with continued AI-related turbulence in equities.

Politics split the sample: expectations jumped among independents, rose moderately among Democrats and fell among Republicans. Sentiment is still 9% below February. And despite softer inflation expectations, consumers overwhelmingly expect inflation rather than unemployment to cause more hardship over the next year or two.

Why it mattersThe 51.5 carries two opposite signals. The 7.3% monthly gain is an expectations repair driven by a political event; the 1.0% annual gain and the 9% shortfall against February say this is a bounce off a low, not a recovery. Current conditions at 49.4 are 3.3% below a year ago, so the present still feels worse than it did. What matters for policy is the inflation number: one-year expectations falling from 4.7% to 3.7% adds a data point against the case that September's hike was sufficient, yet 3.7% is still far above the 2% target, which keeps the real rate positive and rising. The drop in finances among stock-holding households tracks the equity sell-off, so the wealth channel is now working through expectations — slower than petrol prices, but stickier, and more likely to amplify the next drawdown. For exporters selling into the US, better sentiment is not the same as more orders: expectations improved while conditions did not, and restocking still depends on real income.

11/20 15:00 East Money ↗

Shanghai falls 1.23% to 3,533.79 while the SSE 50 gains 0.45% and the big four state banks hit year highs

Chinese equities split on November 20. The Shanghai Composite fell 1.23% to 3,533.79, the Shenzhen Component 3.09% to 10,256.39 and the ChiNext 3.54% to 2,212.13, while the SSE 50 rose 0.45% to 2,785.92. Turnover across the Shanghai, Shenzhen and Beijing markets reached Rmb1.43tn, Rmb116.5bn more than the previous session.

Sectors were almost uniformly lower, with only nuclear power, insurance and banks advancing. Rare earths, semiconductors, defence electronics, aerospace equipment, power equipment, new metal materials, telecom equipment and electronic chemicals led the declines. Just over 300 stocks rose, with 30 hitting the daily limit.

The split ran through single names: Industrial and Commercial Bank of China, Agricultural Bank, Bank of China and China Construction Bank all closed at year highs, carrying the SSE 50 into the green. Semiconductors saw Rmb10.95bn of net outflows, while components drew Rmb1.872bn and liquor and agricultural processing also attracted money.

Why it mattersThe index fell just 1.23%, but only about 300 stocks rose and turnover expanded by Rmb116.5bn — that is not a quiet drift lower, it is a rotation. Money left semiconductors and went into banks, insurers and dividend payers, which is why the SSE 50 closed higher. The big four hitting year highs is not a profit story; it is a relative-value story. With the risk-free rate low and growth stocks carrying long duration, the banks' short-duration cash flow becomes quasi-fixed income for insurers and wealth products matching liabilities — they buy because their liabilities require it, not because they like the assets. The cost is market breadth: a handful of weights hold the index while equal-weighted and growth gauges fall further, so holders feel much worse than the index suggests. For financing, heavy turnover says liquidity has not disappeared; what has shrunk is risk appetite. The same volume is now buying shorter-duration cash flow.

11/20 14:55 21st Century Business Herald ↗

NetEase's third-quarter revenue falls 1.8% to Rmb27.9bn, games slip 1.7%, and the Hong Kong shares drop as much as 19%

NetEase reported third-quarter 2026 results on November 19: revenue of Rmb27.9bn, down 1.8% from a year earlier, with net profit attributable to shareholders of Rmb8.4bn and non-GAAP net profit of Rmb9.2bn, both lower year on year. Its Hong Kong shares opened lower on November 20 and fell as much as 19% during the session, according to Shanghai Securities News; 21st Century Business Herald put the drop at 17.92% at 14:55.

The drag came from the core. Games and related value-added services revenue fell 1.7% to Rmb22.9bn, the first year-on-year decline since the third quarter of 2024, after growth of 6.9% and 9.7% in the first two quarters of this year. Cloud Music revenue fell 0.9% to Rmb1.9bn, ending three quarters of growth; Youdao fell 12.3% to Rmb1.4bn; innovation and other businesses rose 7.1% to Rmb1.5bn, the only segment to grow.

Profit went the other way. Gross profit rose 4.8% to Rmb19bn, operating expenses fell 5.4% to Rmb9.6bn and operating cash flow was Rmb10.5bn for the quarter. Net cash stood at Rmb175.2bn at the end of September.

Management pointed to competition for attention. On the November 19 earnings call, chief executive William Ding said short dramas would have a short-term impact but that the two formats differ over time: games are about control and feedback, short drama about narrative pull. He argued AI will raise production efficiency and diversify content. On the pipeline, open-world title "Infinite" (《无限大》) is set for a global launch on January 15, 2027, with more than 27 million pre-registrations.

Why it mattersRevenue down 1.8%, gross profit up 4.8% and expenses down 5.4% together say this quarter's profit was saved, not earned: gross margin came from the cost structure, the expense line from the pace of investment, and Rmb175.2bn of net cash means the company can sit through a product gap. The 19% drop in Hong Kong is not about this quarter's profit — that fell only a few percent. It prices the combination of games revenue turning negative again and short drama taking viewing time, which changes the assumed duration of future cash flow. For NetEase the next test is "Infinite" in January 2027; for the industry, the real cost line is not R&D but the price of attention. When short drama takes hours, customer acquisition costs rise first in selling expenses, then in revenue growth, and only last in gross margin.

11/21 07:41 CCTV Finance ↗

Global Electronics Association: PCB orders rose 56.1% in the year to September while shipments rose 13.3%, with shortages into 2027

CCTV Finance reported on November 21 that the Global Electronics Association, based in Illinois, has warned of a severe shortage of printed circuit boards that could slow artificial intelligence and the wider electronics industry.

The gap the association describes: in the year to September 2026, PCB orders rose 56.1% while shipments rose only 13.3%, so orders are running well ahead of deliveries. It expects the imbalance to last at least through the first half of 2027, with no meaningful relief before mid-2028.

The bottleneck sits upstream. Asia makes more than 90% of the world's boards, but new capacity is constrained by supplies of fibreglass cloth, resin, copper foil and laminates. A typical PC board has about 12 layers; AI server boards run to 40, 60 or more, and every extra layer consumes more of those materials. Hyperscale cloud providers have already locked up most high-end capacity, leaving automotive, industrial and medical customers in the queue.

The association warned that almost every electronic device depends on PCBs, so delays would hit both semiconductor deployment and final assembly, and judged the supply risk comparable to the 2021 global chip shortage.

Why it mattersOrders up 56.1% against shipments up 13.3% is a 43-point gap, and it is not a forecast — those orders have been placed. It will show up in two ways over the next few quarters: longer lead times that squeeze assembly schedules, and higher prices that pass from copper foil and laminates up to the board makers. The constraint is upstream materials rather than board capacity, which makes "build more capacity" an ineffective prescription before the first half of 2027: the beneficiaries are the materials suppliers, not the midstream manufacturers. For hardware companies, PCB will take a larger share of the bill of materials, and securing supply will matter more than the price — whoever locks capacity gets to ship. The risk on the other side is double ordering, which the association itself flags: how much of that 56.1% is insurance inventory will only be clear once lead times come back down.
