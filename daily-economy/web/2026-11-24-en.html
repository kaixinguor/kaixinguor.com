Money & Policy

11/23 20:12 Jiemian News ↗

Insurers' investable assets reach Rmb41.63tn, with bonds at 51.5% and a Rmb800bn quarterly build

The financial regulator's third-quarter data on insurance fund deployment, reported by Jiemian News on November 23, put investable assets at Rmb41.63tn at the end of September, up 8.2% from the start of the year and 2% from the end of June. Equity holdings rose Rmb146.5bn in the quarter and equities plus funds rose Rmb143.6bn, leaving Rmb4.2tn in stocks and Rmb2.3tn in funds.

Equities and funds together were 16.3% of invested assets, up 0.9 percentage points this year and roughly flat on the second quarter, with equities alone at 10.6%, up 0.2 points. Insurers stood among the top ten holders of 637 listed companies at the end of September, with combined positions up 526m shares from June.

Bonds remain the core: Rmb20.73tn, up about Rmb2.03tn this year and more than Rmb800bn in the third quarter, equal to 51.5% of invested assets, one point higher than in June. Bank deposits and other assets both fell as a share.

The equity book is defensive. Insurers made 10 stake disclosures in the first nine months, 20 fewer than a year earlier, of which three were non-bank financials, two defence and two banks, the rest transport, environmental services and computing. Analysts cite stable, long-dated liabilities and falling fixed-income coupons; accounting adds a third reason, because under the new standards FVOCI equity marks run through other comprehensive income while dividends land in profit.

Why it mattersRmb800bn in a quarter and a one-point gain in the bond share say that the marginal buyer of ultra-long paper is still an insurer's liability, not a trading book. The Rmb146.5bn of equity buying gets described as contrarian, yet the share moved only 0.2 points: it scaled with total assets rather than marking a shift in risk appetite. Who pays: policyholders get the guaranteed rate, shareholders get the spread and the OCI volatility. Keeping marks out of the income statement while letting dividends through is the accounting reason high-dividend stocks are preferred — not taste. Durability comes down to the gap: rigid liability costs against falling coupons can only be filled with equity and alternatives, and volatility in those assets lands in OCI first and in solvency capital next.

11/24 08:18 Shanghai Securities News ↗

Rmb4.13tn of special-purpose bonds issued, 93% of quota, as the 2027 limit heads for a 60% pre-allocation

Local governments have issued Rmb9.98tn of bonds this year, of which about Rmb4.13tn are new special-purpose bonds — 93% of the annual quota, Shanghai Securities News reported on November 24, citing the special bond information platform.

The quota is Rmb4.4tn, with a possible Rmb200bn addition in the fourth quarter that would take full-year issuance to about Rmb4.6tn. Guangdong, Shandong, Jiangsu, Sichuan and Zhejiang lead, together above 40% of the total; proceeds go mainly to municipal and industrial-park infrastructure, transport, affordable housing, social programmes, agriculture and water.

Debt swaps are counted separately: by November 22, Rmb1.170451tn of "special" special-purpose bonds had been disclosed, a slice that may be used to retire hidden local-government debt. Zhao Wei of Shenwan Hongyuan put the unused local-bond carry-over quota at about Rmb600bn by the end of 2026.

Next comes the pre-allocation. Under the NPC's authorisation the Ministry of Finance may pre-allocate up to 60% of the following year's new local quota in the fourth quarter, and economists surveyed expect the full 60% again, which would put more than Rmb3tn of 2027 quota into local hands this year. The ministry has said it wants bond sales to start in January 2027, and cities such as Weihai have begun building project pipelines.

Why it mattersSpecial-purpose bonds sit in the government fund budget and are meant to be serviced by project revenue, so they do not add to the general-budget deficit — which is why "93%" measures issuance, not outlays. The blockage sits on the spending side: between selling a bond and pouring concrete come approvals, land clearance and revenue forecasts. Pre-allocation changes timing rather than the total: next year's quota is sold this quarter so work can start in January, at the cost of a smaller issuance peak later and a quota already spent. Who pays: project revenue and land sales, topped up by fiscal subsidy, which is to say taxpayers; the Rmb1.17tn of swap bonds moves hidden, short-dated, expensive liabilities onto the government fund balance sheet as explicit debt with longer duration and lower coupons. Watch two things — whether project revenue covers debt service, and how much counter-cyclical room is left in 2027 once the quota has been front-loaded.

11/24 12:47 21st Century Business Herald ↗

PBOC sells Rmb50bn of bills in Hong Kong at 1.32% for three months and 1.41% for a year

On November 24 the People's Bank of China sold Rmb20bn of three-month bills in Hong Kong at a 1.32% yield and Rmb30bn of one-year bills at 1.41%, 21st Century Business Herald reported. The Rmb50bn total and the maturity split matched the earlier announcement; what settled on the day was the price.

The two yields differ by 9bp (1.41% minus 1.32%). The one-year paper costs 9bp more, and that is what the offshore market charges for locking renminbi up for a year.

Mechanically, a central-bank bill is a short-dated renminbi liability sold offshore: it drains renminbi from the offshore pool at issue and returns it at maturity. Onshore liquidity is untouched; what changes is the price of offshore renminbi funding, and a higher borrowing cost raises the carry cost of being short the currency.

One caveat on the number: the yield is a price set by the issuer, not a risk-free rate discovered in the market, so it cannot be lined up against government bond yields or the LPR. At Rmb50bn the sale is also modest against the offshore pool; its function is a price signal, not a quantity.

Why it mattersThe operation prints no money and drains nothing onshore; it converts Rmb50bn of instantly available offshore renminbi into three-month and one-year paper. Who pays: banks that need offshore renminbi and anyone short the currency, through higher borrowing costs. Who earns: the central bank, at 1.32% and 1.41%, while locking the liquidity until maturity. The real constraint is arbitrage — if offshore short rates are pushed above onshore levels, cross-border money will close the gap itself, so how high the bill yield can go depends on how much spread is left. Watch whether maturities are rolled, and whether the Rmb30bn one-year tranche is refinanced or allowed to run off; the second is the signal.

11/24 11:09 Securities Times ↗

Digital-industry revenue rises 14.8% to Rmb32.41tn in nine months, with profit up 23.4%

Ministry of Industry and Information Technology data reported by Securities Times on November 24 put revenue in the digital industries at Rmb32.41tn in the first nine months, up 14.8% and 5.2 percentage points faster than a year earlier. Manufacturing contributed Rmb19.32tn, up 20.5%, and services Rmb13.09tn, up 7.3%.

Profit outran revenue: Rmb2.78tn, up 23.4% and 14.9 points faster than a year earlier, on a margin of 8.6% that was 0.4 points wider. The ministry credits strong AI demand and strong export growth.

The arithmetic holds together. Rmb19.32tn plus Rmb13.09tn is Rmb32.41tn, and Rmb2.78tn over Rmb32.41tn comes to 8.6%, matching the reported margin. The 8.6-point gap between revenue growth of 14.8% and profit growth of 23.4% is what the wider margin contributes.

By segment, manufacturing grew 20.5%, nearly three times the 7.3% in services — the increment in this cycle sits in hardware and equipment rather than in software and services.

Why it mattersThis is a revenue table, not a value-added table: Rmb32.41tn includes intermediate inputs, so it cannot be set against GDP or divided by population. What matters is the gap between revenue and profit — 14.8% against 23.4%, margin up 0.4 points — which points to mix and pricing rather than a broad demand recovery. With manufacturing at 20.5% and services at 7.3%, the chain leans harder on exports and capital spending. Who pays: the capex of cloud and equipment makers, billed in the end to enterprises that use AI per token or per project. Who earns: components, equipment and foundries. Two things to watch — whether export growth holds, since the ministry itself lists exports as a driver, and whether hardware inflation squeezes margins downstream in software and services.

11/24 14:41 Beijing News ↗

Oil falls to $80.17, a fresh low for the move, as China's 23rd pump-price window opens on November 26

Crude fell sharply on November 23 and broke a key level, setting a new low for the decline: NYMEX January light crude dropped $3.24 to $80.17 a barrel, down 3.88%, and January Brent fell $3.20 to $89.37, down 3.46%, Beijing News reported on November 24.

China's 23rd retail fuel pricing window is set for 24:00 on November 26. Under current rules the adjustment tracks the ten-working-day average of crude across two pricing cycles rather than a single session, so a 3.88% one-day fall does not pass through one-for-one to the pump. Surveys of analysts currently point to a cut of more than Rmb0.4 a litre — a forecast, not a decision, with the official number due on November 26.

The previous adjustment came at 24:00 on November 12, when the NDRC cut gasoline and diesel by Rmb645 and Rmb625 a tonne, the first reduction since late August. If the current forecast lands it would be the second cut in November and the seventh this year.

The supply story is turning. Monthly reports from the EIA, OPEC and the IEA shifted tone, the report said: the restart of Saudi Arabia's east-west pipeline and heavier ship-to-ship transfers off Oman brought Gulf exports back quickly, global supply rose sharply within three months, and the market moved into surplus for the first time since the Iran conflict. The EIA balance sheet projects a 4m barrel-a-day surplus in 2027, with the IEA close behind. On demand, gasoline is in its seasonal trough after a round of restocking by distributors, while diesel remains tight.

Why it mattersChinese pump prices are anchored to a ten-day average, so a single session never sets direction — the average does. That also means the 3.88% drop on November 23 will only reach the forecourt over the following pricing cycle. The distribution happens in the middle: as crude falls, refiners and traders mark inventory down first while retail prices move only on November 26, and the gap is split between stations and wholesalers. If the Rmb0.4 forecast lands, a 50-litre tank saves about Rmb20 — real money for households and a subtraction from the transport-fuel component of CPI. Who pays: with three agencies writing a 2027 surplus into their balances, the cost lands on high-cost producers and inventory holders, not consumers. Watch whether OPEC+ cuts to defend price, and whether tight diesel eats into the gasoline cut.

Nvidia adds a record $952bn of market value in a day as the stock jumps 31.48% to $165.05

Nvidia closed up 31.48% at $165.05 on November 24 Beijing time, its biggest one-day gain since May 9, 2003, taking market value to $3.98tn — a one-day increase of $952bn, or about Rmb6.39tn, a record, Economic Daily reported.

The trigger was a management announcement: the board unanimously reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive.

The political calendar follows. Huang is due before the Senate Banking Committee on December 3 to testify on oversight of US export controls, and at a closed-door interview with three House committees on December 1, Cailian Press reported. The governance question is settled; the export-control questions are only starting.

One definition to keep straight: the $952bn is the change in market value, closing capitalisation of $3.98tn minus the previous session's, computed as price times shares outstanding. It is not turnover, and it is not money flowing in.

Why it mattersMarket value is price times share count, so a $952bn gain needs no $952bn of inflows: only the shares that traded changed hands, and the rest is a mark-up on what holders already owned. The money is recorded in three places — unrealised gains for shareholders, realised losses for short sellers and option writers, and forced buying for index products weighted by market value. Who pays: shorts and volatility sellers pay cash; index and active fund investors pay a higher entry price. One more definition: this does not enter GDP, because it is a revaluation of a stock, not current output. Two things decide durability — whether the reinstatement is a governance event or an operating one, with two congressional appearances in December likely to hint at which, and whether the earnings implied by $3.98tn can be met by the next set of accounts.

11/24 14:16 Shanghai Securities News ↗

Congo (DRC) halts all cobalt exports from November 30 as Chinese electronic cobalt jumps 11.95% to Rmb318,500 a tonne

On November 23 the Democratic Republic of Congo's mining regulator, ARECOMS, said it would suspend all cobalt exports from November 30 until further notice, to ease oversupply and restore a sustainable balance in the market, Shanghai Securities News reported on November 24. Congo is the world's largest producer, with 74.2% of global output in 2025.

Prices moved in the same session. By 13:15 on November 24 the cobalt 2701 contract on the Wuxi Zhonglianjin electronic exchange traded at Rmb318,500 a tonne, up 11.95%; the Tonghuashun cobalt index rose as much as 11.44%, with Tengyuan Cobalt, Hanrui Cobalt and Huayou Cobalt up 11.4%, 8.92% and 6.76%.

It is the second export halt used to support prices. ARECOMS stopped exports of mined cobalt in February 2025 and switched to a quota system on October 16 that year; the quota already caps exports below half of the country's 2024 output. After the first ban cobalt spiked to Rmb483,000 a tonne, roughly three times the pre-ban price, before easing back to around Rmb260,000 as supply pressure faded.

The details, and the disagreement: ARECOMS will review the ban after four months, weighing the market, available stocks and global supply and demand, and will use the pause to revise the quota allocation, Cailian Press reported. An executive at a listed company told Shanghai Securities News that physical inventories are lower than they were after the 2025 ban, but that consumption has shifted to essential demand and that electronics and EV demand is soft — near-term price support, little long-term effect on demand.

Why it mattersAn administrative halt moves the supply curve, not demand. Congo first capped exports below half of 2024 output and has now stopped them outright, taking marginal supply off the market; with inventories lower than last time, sellers can hold back, which is why the price went from Rmb260,000 to Rmb318,500 in a single session. Who pays: Chinese smelters and cathode makers buy at higher feedstock prices and pass the cost down the battery chain into vehicles and storage. Who earns: miners and the traders holding metal. The fiscal ledger is the other half — a halt cuts export tax revenue, and the four-month review will weigh price against revenue. Substitution sits in Indonesia, where cobalt comes as a by-product of nickel, which is why this spike is unlikely to repeat the threefold move.
