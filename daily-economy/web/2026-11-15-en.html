11/13 17:00 People's Bank of China ↗

Social financing stock grows 7.2% and M2 7.1%, but ten-month issuance is Rmb2.29trn below last year

The People's Bank of China published its October credit statistics on November 13. At the end of the month, social financing outstanding stood at Rmb469.16trn, 7.2% higher than a year earlier; M2 was Rmb359.06trn, up 7.1%; M1 was Rmb116.9trn, up 4.4%; M0 was Rmb15trn, up 10.7%; and the stock of yuan loans reached Rmb283.33trn, up 4.7%.

The flow figures are where the structure shows. Social financing rose a cumulative Rmb28.61trn in the first ten months, Rmb2.29trn less than a year earlier. Yuan loans to the real economy added Rmb10.91trn, down Rmb3.61trn; net government bond issuance reached Rmb11.49trn, down Rmb459.2bn; net corporate bond issuance was Rmb3.1trn, up Rmb1.27trn; and onshore equity financing came to Rmb570.5bn, up Rmb184.2bn.

By borrower, yuan loans rose Rmb11.42trn in ten months, but household loans fell Rmb1.46trn — short-term loans down Rmb1.41trn and medium- and long-term loans down Rmb50.1bn — while corporate loans rose Rmb12.39trn. Deposits rose Rmb21.97trn, including Rmb8.47trn from households and Rmb5.99trn from non-bank financial institutions. On price, the weighted average interbank lending rate was 1.3% in October, 0.1 point below September, and the pledged repo rate 1.33%, down 0.07 point.

Government bonds keep gaining weight: Rmb106.41trn outstanding, up 14.4%, or 22.7% of the social financing stock, 1.4 points more than a year earlier, while yuan loans to the real economy slipped to 59.5% of the stock, 1.5 points less. Securities Times reported that new corporate loans were priced at about 2.9% in October, some 0.2 point below a year earlier, and new mortgages at about 3.1%, broadly unchanged.

Why it mattersThe flow number is the honest one: the stock still grows 7.2%, but issuance is Rmb2.29trn short of last year, and the gap splits three ways. Yuan loans to the real economy fell Rmb3.61trn short, government bond issuance Rmb459.2bn short, and corporate bonds plus equity took up part of the slack with Rmb1.27trn and Rmb184.2bn of extra supply. The household line is the one to watch: loans down Rmb1.46trn, almost all of it short-term, while deposits rose Rmb8.47trn. The borrowing side is contracting and the saving side is expanding, which is no longer a question of whether rates are low enough — corporate loans at 2.9% are 0.2 point cheaper than a year ago — but of whether anyone wants to borrow. With government bonds at 22.7% of the stock, the monthly swing in social financing is set by the fiscal calendar, not by credit demand.
Another angle For banks, a 2.9% lending rate minus 1.3% of interbank funding is what has to cover the capital consumed by 4.7% loan growth, and that spread is the net interest margin. For households, Rmb1.46trn of net repayment swaps interest expense for deposit income — a voluntary balance-sheet repair, paid for out of current consumption.

11/13 People's Bank of China ↗

PBoC to roll over only Rmb200bn of six-month outright repos on November 16, a net drain of Rmb300bn this month

The central bank's open market operations room published tender notice No. 22 of 2026 on November 13: to keep banking-system liquidity ample, on November 16 it will conduct Rmb200bn of outright reverse repos at a fixed quantity with rate bidding and multiple price awards, for a term of six months (180 days) maturing on May 15, 2027, or the next business day if that falls on a holiday.

Securities Times reported on November 14 that Rmb300bn of six-month outright repos mature in November, so the operation is a partial rollover that drains Rmb100bn. The bank had already conducted Rmb300bn of three-month operations on November 5 against Rmb500bn maturing, a net drain of Rmb200bn. Across both tenors, November's outright repo operations withdraw Rmb300bn.

Money market prices explain why. Citing Wind data, the report put the October average of DR001 at 1.28%, 10 basis points below September's 1.38%, and said the rate has stayed low in November at a month-to-date average of 1.3% through November 13. Wang Qing, chief macro analyst at Orient Jincheng, attributed the trimming to recently loose liquidity; Dong Ximiao, chief economist at Lianlian, called it a fine-tuning "peak shaving and valley filling" that does not signal a tightening turn, and said it helps anchor expectations while the policy rate is re-pegged, preventing the market from reading a plumbing reform as a rate cut.

Why it mattersThe signal here is in the price, not the quantity. Outright repos supply banks with medium-term money; draining Rmb300bn across two tenors while DR001 averages 1.3% for the month and 1.28% in October simply stops pressing the short end lower, which matters because an artificially low short rate would distort the signal during the policy-rate re-pegging. Two pricing consequences follow. With less medium-term money, banks have to fund themselves through interbank lending and negotiable certificates of deposit, so the decline in CD rates stops first. And with the short end shaved, the curve's steepness is capped. The date to watch is December, when government bond issuance peaks and the matching loans behind the Rmb800bn of new policy financial instruments arrive; only a return to larger outright repo and MLF operations then would mark a change in direction.

11/13 22:39 Cailianshe ↗

US October PPI inflation reaches 6.5%, the highest since November 2022, as a December hike becomes consensus

The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported on November 13 that producer prices rose 0.3% in October, below the 0.4% consensus, while September's gain was revised up to 1.4% from 1.3%. The annual rate climbed to 6.5% from 6.3% in September, the highest since November 2022. Core PPI, excluding food and energy, rose 0.3% on the month, in line with expectations.

The detail shows the pressure is not only energy: goods prices rose 0.5% on the month and services 0.2%, their fifth consecutive monthly increase. Excluding food, energy and trade services, PPI still rose 0.5%, and goods prices were up 10.2% from a year earlier, the largest annual gain since October 2022.

Consumer prices had already flashed a warning: October CPI rose 0.4% on the month and core CPI 0.3%, each a tenth of a point above consensus. Chicago Fed President Austan Goolsbee said on November 13 that US inflation had shown worrying signs for several months and that some price categories had stopped improving even excluding oil and tariffs. Markets now broadly expect a 25 basis point hike at the December 8-9 meeting, with an assessment of further tightening early next year.

Prices settled the same way. Xinhua Finance reported that December gold on the New York exchange closed at US$3,886.206 an ounce, up 0.02%, while December silver fell 2.87% to US$48.628 an ounce; the 10-year Treasury yield sat near 5.13% and the 2-year around 4.86%, with the dollar index holding above 103.

Why it mattersA 0.3% monthly print and a 6.5% annual one are telling different stories: the monthly figure below consensus means September's 1.4% outlier is rolling out of the base, while the annual figure still rising means twelve months of cost have settled into prices. The 10.2% annual gain in goods is the key number, because it says energy and tariffs are turning from a one-off level shock into a cost base. For rates, a 27 basis point gap between the 2-year at 4.86% and the 10-year at 5.13% is a nearly flat curve: the market is pricing a hike, not a higher neutral rate. For companies, whether the 0.5% monthly rise in input costs can be passed on depends on demand; whatever cannot be passed on comes out of the income statement, and that is the first deduction from 2027 earnings.

China and the US hold the first meeting of their trade council by video, pledging to lengthen the cooperation list

Xinhua reported on November 14, in a dispatch by reporter Xie Xiyao, that the two governments held the first meeting of the trade council under the China-US economic and trade consultation mechanism by video conference on November 13, according to the Ministry of Commerce.

The readout said the two sides, guided by the consensus reached by their leaders, discussed implementing earlier consultation outcomes and exchanged views on tariffs, the "30 billion for 30 billion" reciprocal tariff reduction arrangement, agricultural trade and other concerns in what it called a candid, in-depth and constructive exchange. They will keep using the council to narrow the list of problems and lengthen the list of cooperation.

This is a procedural session, not a deal. The readout records an exchange of views and publishes no product list, tariff rate or timetable; the meeting itself announced no new tariff cut, effective date or next round.

Why it mattersA council is a mechanism, not a result, and its value is in fixing what gets negotiated: tariffs, reciprocal cuts and farm trade now sit in a single readout, which means they are being bargained as a package and any one of them landing alone moves the other two. On prices, the first transmission runs through the landed cost of farm goods, via port stocks and feed margins. On the fiscal side, tariff revenue is the price paid for a recovering tax base. For exporters, the only thing priceable is still expectation, because the list and the rate — the two parameters the model is missing — have not been published. When the next readout appears matters more than what it says: as long as it only records an exchange of views, the two sides have not reached the stage of swapping lists.

11/13 15:30 National Development and Reform Commission ↗

Two ministries target 300GW of new-type storage by 2030 with three-hour duration

The National Development and Reform Commission and the National Energy Administration issued the 15th five-year plan for new-type energy storage on November 13, document No. 1565 of 2026, dated October 29 and addressed to provincial development and reform commissions, energy bureaus and central enterprises.

Securities Times reported on November 14 that the plan sets out 16 measures covering diversified applications, technology research and the industry's management system, and calls for storage to move from a scale-building phase to a market-priced one by 2030, with national installed capacity of 300GW and average duration of about three hours.

The starting point sets the slope. At the end of 2025, 136GW/351GWh of new-type storage was in operation, more than 40 times the level at the end of the previous five-year plan. By the end of September 2026 that had reached 167GW/435GWh, up 63% from a year earlier, with average duration of 2.61 hours, about 0.27 hours longer. Lithium-ion batteries held 131GW, or 96.1% of the 2025 total, while the plan names sodium-ion, compressed air, flow batteries, flywheels, gravity storage, molten salt heat storage and hydrogen.

Capital arrived first. Citing IT Juzi data, Securities Times reported 42 primary-market financing deals in the sector worth Rmb16.204bn in the year to November 13, nearly four times the whole of 2025.

Why it mattersThree hundred gigawatts against 167GW means roughly doubling again in five years, but the operative word in the plan is "market": it pushes the industry from an installation race toward realised returns, because what keeps a project alive is utilisation hours and the peak-valley spread, not the headline capacity number. For the supply chain, lithium-ion's 96.1% share is the starting point and the named alternatives are bets on the next cost curve. For capital, 42 deals and Rmb16.2bn say the money is ahead of the orders, and the open question is which balance sheet the revenue comes from. Watch the move in average duration from 2.61 to three hours: every extra hour raises cost per kilowatt, and the return has to be covered by a longer window of price spreads.

11/14 National Healthcare Security Administration ↗

China's 2026 drug list adds 107 medicines, 51 of them class-1 novel drugs, for a total of 3,331

The National Healthcare Security Administration and the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security published the 2026 editions of the national basic medical insurance drug list and the commercial health insurance innovative drug list on November 14. It is the ninth adjustment since the agency was created, following application, formal review, expert evaluation and negotiation or bidding.

The list adds 107 medicines, 51 of them class-1 innovative drugs, a record, covering oncology, rare diseases, chronic conditions and mental illness. Thirty-four medicines were removed for limited clinical value, better alternatives, or missing label information that made supply impossible. Net of additions, removals and merged formulations, the 2026 list gains 78 item numbers for a total of 3,331 — 1,935 western medicines, 1,396 Chinese patent medicines and 885 herbal decoction pieces.

The pricing signal sits on the payer side: CAR-T therapies for lymphoma that had cost more than Rmb1m, and non-factor treatments for haemophilia, both entered basic insurance. The commercial list added 12 medicines, including the world's first CAR-T for solid tumours and China's first gene therapy for haemophilia, for a total of 28, with two 2025 commercial-list products moving into basic insurance. The new list takes effect on January 1, 2027.

Why it mattersOne hundred and seven in, 34 out, 78 net item numbers and 3,331 in total: for the insurance fund that is an addition to spending, for patients a subtraction from out-of-pocket costs, and the difference is filled by the payment standard set in negotiation. When CAR-T moves from a Rmb1m self-pay item into basic insurance, the anchor for pricing expensive therapies shifts from what patients can afford to what the fund's actuarial model can bear: economics and fund modelling set the standard first, and companies then decide whether to accept it. Once that rule holds, every high-priced innovative drug is priced the same way. For industry, 51 class-1 novel drugs entering the list cashes in the volume-for-price trade and underpins pipeline valuations; for the budget, the binding constraint is not publication day but the volumes actually prescribed after January 2027.
