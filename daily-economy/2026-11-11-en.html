US October CPI rises 0.4% m/m, above forecasts: markets price a December hike at 100%

US October CPI was released before the market open on November 10: consumer prices rose 0.4% from a month earlier and core CPI rose 0.3%, both 0.1 percentage point above consensus, after 0.6% and 0.3% in September. Because last October's federal shutdown prevented price collection, the report carried no annual inflation figures for CPI or core CPI.

After the release, traders kept the probability of a December Fed hike at 100%. Yields on two- to five-year Treasuries rose at least 10 basis points, the dollar index jumped and gold and silver fell. Ahead of the open, Dow futures were down 0.10%, S&P 500 futures 0.70% and Nasdaq 100 futures 1.34%.

Why it mattersA 0.4% monthly gain is roughly a 5% annualised pace, more than double the Fed's 2% target, and core inflation matching September's 0.3% shows price pressure is no longer confined to energy. With a December hike fully priced, the Fed's reaction function is effectively locked: only a sharp deterioration in jobs or financial conditions can stop it. For renminbi assets, a firmer dollar and higher two- to five-year Treasury yields hit the exchange rate first and valuation multiples second, while narrowing the room for further domestic easing.

China's insurers post Rmb5.26trn of premiums, up just 0.9%, while claims rise 3.9%

The Insurance Association of China held its third regular press conference of 2026 on November 10. Wang Simiao, the association's chief officer, said premium income reached Rmb5.26 trillion in the first nine months, up 0.9% year on year, while claims and benefits paid rose 3.9% to Rmb1.94 trillion. Fees and commissions fell 5.2%, with property insurers down 3.1% and life insurers down 6.4%.

By the end of the third quarter, life insurers held more than Rmb28 trillion in reserves for life and long-term health policies, up 12.7% since the start of the year. The association also released five reports: technology insurance premiums were Rmb263.589 billion in 2025, up 28.21%; green insurance premiums were Rmb390.4 billion with a three-year compound growth rate of 33.4% and claims of Rmb243.5 billion; commercial pension premiums were Rmb1.4 trillion with annuity payouts of Rmb278.3 billion.

Why it mattersPremium growth of 0.9% against claims growth of 3.9% is the gap where underwriting margin is being squeezed. The 5.2% fall in commissions shows the "report-and-execute" rule is cutting expenses, but the savings are absorbed by claims and reserve costs rather than turning into profit. The Rmb28 trillion reserve pool, up 12.7% this year, is what life insurers have built by locking in long-term liabilities in a low-rate environment, and it is one of the bond market's most important sources of long-term money. Growth has shifted away from traditional car and life cover to policy-driven technology, green and pension lines.

CIIE closes with $85.45bn in intended annual deals, up 2.3% to a record

The ninth China International Import Expo closed on November 10. Xiao Lu, deputy party secretary of the CIIE Bureau, told the closing briefing that intended deals measured on an annualised basis reached $85.45 billion, up 2.3% from the previous edition and a record high.

Some 162 countries, regions and international organisations took part, with total exhibition space above 440,000 square metres. A total of 4,142 companies exhibited, the enterprise exhibition area reached 370,000 square metres for the first time, more than 300 Fortune Global 500 and industry-leading firms attended, and 485 products were launched globally, in Asia or in China for the first time; five core metrics, including exhibitor numbers and exhibition area, hit records. The expo organised 51 trading delegations and more than 700 sub-delegations, registered more than 470,000 visitors and held 80 side events; 69 countries and international organisations mounted pavilions, with five countries and one organisation taking part for the first time. For the tenth edition, 143 companies and institutions have already signed up, booking more than 100,000 square metres.

Why it mattersThe $85.45 billion is "intended" annualised deal value, not actual imports, and 2.3% growth suggests purchasing appetite is expanding only modestly amid tariffs and geopolitical friction. The more telling signal is structural: 4,142 exhibitors, a 370,000-square-metre enterprise show, 485 launches and 143 early sign-ups for next year show companies voting with renewals, not just one-off orders. For the macro outlook, those intentions will be spread over coming quarters of imports and consumption.

11/10 18:23 Securities Times ↗

Tmall's Singles Day "peak price" lifts first-hour sales 400% from the prior hour; 30,000 brands double

On November 9, Tmall launched a "peak price" promotion for Singles Day with discounts of up to 32% across the platform. In the first hour, transaction value rose 400% from the previous hour and more than 30,000 brands doubled sales year on year; after four hours, space-heater sales were up 400%.

A cold snap across much of China pushed shoppers towards warm clothing: more than 300,000 down jackets, including those from Adidas and Bosideng, were sold that evening. In the pet category, 1.5 million cans of cat food, 300,000 doses of pet deworming medicine, 170,000 dog coats and 235,000 pet beds were sold. By category, children's clothing sales rose 432% year on year and children's shoes 486%. Tmall also said three beauty products from Proya, La Roche-Posay and FAN BEAUTY DIARY would return to Li Jiaqi's livestream on November 11, and that Uniqlo's lightweight down jackets and other apparel would get a final restock.

Why it mattersThe 400% first-hour gain is a timing effect: pre-sales and the opening burst concentrate transactions into a few hours against a very low base, so it is not a year-on-year number. The informative figures are the 30,000 brands that doubled sales and the 432%/486% growth in children's apparel and shoes, which point to deeper discounts and a wider assortment rather than a larger pool of consumers. Cold-weather categories are a weather pulse that fades. For platforms, the shift from a long pre-sale campaign to immediate discounting trades average order value and margin for volume.

Alibaba to spin off T-Head and list it in Hong Kong after a confidential A1 in September

Alibaba said in a Hong Kong Stock Exchange announcement on November 10 that it plans to spin off wholly owned subsidiary T-Head (Shanghai) Technology Co Ltd and list it separately on the main board of the Hong Kong exchange. The exchange has confirmed the spin-off can proceed; T-Head filed a confidential A1 application on September 11, 2026.

The spin-off is to be carried out through a global offering of T-Head H shares, including a Hong Kong public offer and a placing to institutional and professional investors. T-Head is expected to remain a subsidiary of Alibaba after the deal. The size and structure of the offering and the adjustment to Alibaba's stake have not been determined. Alibaba described T-Head as one of China's leading full-stack data-centre chip suppliers whose chips provide system-level computing solutions. The deal still requires approval from the exchange's listing committee, filing with the China Securities Regulatory Commission, and final decisions by Alibaba and T-Head.

Why it mattersThe spin-off turns an internal cost centre into a standalone balance sheet. Chip development is long-cycle and capital-intensive, and inside an e-commerce group it is valued at a discount; as a listed company T-Head can be priced like a semiconductor firm and raise equity, while Alibaba keeps control and consolidation. For Hong Kong, it creates a rare full-stack data-centre chip listing and a pricing anchor for other hard-tech spin-offs. The risks are approval and dilution: the A1 is only an application, and approval, the CSRC filing, deal size and stake are all still open.

11/10 19:52 Cailian Press ↗

TSMC board approves a $40.9bn capital budget for advanced nodes and packaging

TSMC's board approved a capital budget of about $40.927 billion on November 10, mainly for installing and upgrading advanced-node capacity and for expanding advanced packaging, mature and specialty process capacity, as well as fab construction and facility systems.

The budget also covers 2027 R&D capital investment, maintenance capital expenditure and capitalised lease assets. Cailian Press noted that this is not TSMC's first long-term capacity plan but a continuation of the board's regular approval of capital budgets.

Why it mattersThe $40.9 billion is a budget authorisation, not a single year's spending, and it locks in the supply curve for advanced nodes and advanced packaging over the next two to three years. For AI chips, advanced packaging remains the bottleneck, so TSMC's expansion pace sets the shipment rhythm for Nvidia, AMD and cloud providers. For mainland fabless companies, access to leading-edge nodes and advanced packaging remains constrained by geopolitics, leaving mature nodes and packaging as the more realistic space for import substitution. For equipment and materials suppliers, the budget extends order visibility, but approval is not the same as spending.
