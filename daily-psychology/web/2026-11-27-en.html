Twenty-three departments set a 2030 target of 110,000 mental health physicians and community rehabilitation in 95 per cent of counties

China's National Health Commission and 22 other departments published the 15th Five-Year Plan for Mental Health and Mental Hygiene on November 26. The document is dated November 20. The government portal posted the full text at 16:51 Beijing time.

Three numbers anchor 2030. The country is to reach 110,000 mental health physicians, or 8 per 100,000 people. Community rehabilitation for mental disorders is to cover 95 per cent of counties. Xinhua published the same three figures in a chart at 16:09 that afternoon.

The service network is to run through five levels: national, provincial, municipal, county and township. Every province is to have a psychiatric hospital. Every prefecture-level city, and every county with more than 500,000 residents, is to have a mental health institution. Every county is to place a psychiatric ward or clinic in its main public hospital.

The plan also covers the 12356 mental health hotline, which is to get provincial platforms and higher answer rates. Eligible drugs are to enter the basic medical insurance list, and long-acting injections are encouraged. Psychiatric service prices are to be adjusted under the national pricing guideline, and staff pay is to be protected under existing rules.

The document lists eight task areas, six special columns and five priority programmes. It names no central funding figure. Local governments are told to provide “necessary staffing and funding support in light of local conditions”.

Why it mattersThe 110,000 and the 8 per 100,000 are the same target. Divide 110,000 by 1.4 billion people and you get 7.9 per 100,000, so the plan allocates staff by population density rather than by measured need. The binding constraint sits at county level. Wards, clinics and community rehabilitation must all be built there, while money and posts stay with local governments. Beijing supplies targets, pricing rules and insurance coverage. The number to watch in 2030 is not the national physician count. It is whether community teams in those 95 per cent of counties actually catch people leaving hospital.

11/26 Global Health Action (paper) ↗

No high-quality review among 18: war-related PTSD still pools at 28.0 per cent, with estimates from 11.6 to 61.9

Global Health Action published the umbrella review online on November 26, 2026 (DOI 10.1080/16549716.2026.2768748). Alinezhadmonfared H and colleagues worked at the Health Education and Promotion department and the Evidence-Based Medicine Centre of Tabriz University of Medical Sciences in Iran.

This is an umbrella review. PubMed, Embase, Scopus and Web of Science were searched to August 2025 for systematic reviews of PTSD among refugees, internally displaced people and residents of conflict zones. AMSTAR 2 rated quality, and usable prevalence estimates were re-pooled.

Eighteen reviews were included, 12 of them meta-analyses, published between 2012 and 2025 and covering conflicts in five regions. Reported prevalence ran from 11.6 per cent among Lebanese civilians to 61.9 per cent among Iraqi internally displaced people.

Re-pooling six reviews gave 28.0 per cent (95 per cent CI 22.0–34.9), with I² at 87.8 per cent. Displaced and refugee groups reached 43.7 per cent (24.6–65.0), against 23.4 per cent (20.3–26.7) in general samples.

The quality grading is the harder finding. No review reached high quality: three were moderate, six low and nine critically low. The authors therefore present 28.0 per cent as a planning range, not a prevalence estimate.

Why it mattersThe number to carry away is not 28 per cent but the span from 11.6 to 61.9. Change the screening instrument, the sampling frame or the response rate, and the same war yields a five-fold difference. Budgeting on 28 per cent understates displaced populations and overstates general ones. Methodologically the umbrella review is careful, but every review underneath it has serious flaws, so the pooled figure is exploratory. Aid agencies can plan against two bands, 23.4 and 43.7 per cent, rather than one headline. What the field lacks is a standard diagnostic interview and prospective registration, not another review.

11/26 Journal of Interpersonal Violence (paper) ↗

Among 2,795 US mothers, consistent poly-victimisation raised the odds of depression at child age five by 7.56 times

Journal of Interpersonal Violence published the study online on November 26, 2026 (DOI 10.1177/08862605261500418). Hong H, Williams JR and Scroggins JK are the authors, at the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill.

The data come from the Future of Families and Child Wellbeing Study, collected in 20 US cities between 1999 and 2006, with 2,795 mothers. Intimate partner violence was measured when the child was one, three and five, across physical, sexual, emotional, economic and social isolation domains.

Latent transition analysis first identified four states: low risk, emotional abuse, psychological abuse and poly-victimisation. Transition probabilities then produced eight trajectories, including stable low risk, stable poly-victimisation, escalation, de-escalation and fluctuation.

Mothers in the stable poly-victimisation group had the highest odds of depression when the child turned five, with an adjusted odds ratio of 7.56 (95 per cent CI 3.79–15.10). Stable and escalating patterns also came with lower education and more material hardship.

The authors call for repeated screening before and after birth, and for psychological abuse and economic control to be included rather than physical violence alone.

Why it mattersThe 7.56 compares stable poly-victimisation with stable low risk, and six other trajectories sit between them. Those six carry the practical message. Escalating and fluctuating groups can look clean at a single visit while their depression risk is already rising. A prenatal questionnaire that asks only about being hit will miss them, because emotional abuse and economic control are not on the form. The design is an observational cohort, so causal claims are out of reach, and poverty may sit upstream of both violence and depression. Adding one screening item costs little. The hard part is what happens after a positive screen.

11/26 CNS Drugs (paper) ↗

Fifteen trials, 1,937 inpatients: starting two antipsychotics together raised response by 28 per cent in relative terms

CNS Drugs published the meta-analysis online on November 26, 2026 (DOI 10.1007/s40263-026-01337-1). Van Reekum EA is first author and Correll CU corresponding author. Their affiliations include McMaster University, Charité Universitätsmedizin Berlin and the Zucker School of Medicine at Hofstra/Northwell.

The study pooled randomised trials. Medline, PsycINFO, Embase and the Cochrane trials register were searched to March 25, 2026. Eligible trials enrolled adults with acute or exacerbated schizophrenia spectrum disorders and started two antipsychotics within one week of each other.

Fifteen trials with 1,937 inpatients qualified. Mean age was 32.3 years, 61 per cent were male, 14 combinations were tested, and follow-up averaged 10 weeks, ranging from one to 35.

Against monotherapy initiation, co-initiation produced a response risk ratio of 1.28 (95 per cent CI 1.11–1.48) at moderate certainty. Total symptoms moved by a standardised mean difference of −0.35 (−0.71 to 0.00) and negative symptoms by −0.55 (−1.08 to −0.02), both at low certainty. Adverse effects gave a risk ratio of 0.97 (0.87–1.09) and all-cause discontinuation 0.96 (0.81–1.13).

Twenty-eight per cent of participants did not finish their trial. The authors warn that effect sizes beyond response may not be clinically meaningful.

Why it mattersGuidelines usually ask for one drug at an adequate dose and duration before switching or adding. These 15 trials describe another route: two drugs from the start, with a response difference visible in the first week. Response here means crossing a symptom-scale threshold, not recovering function. The pooled effect on total symptoms is close to zero, and the negative-symptom gain holds only at low certainty. The 28 per cent dropout is the real ceiling. If acute inpatient units lose nearly a third of patients, outpatient replication will do worse. Co-initiation is a conditional option, especially for patients with lower severity on first-generation combinations. It is not a default.

11/26 Journal of Mental Health (paper) ↗

Thirty-six UK studies show peer support improves connection and hope, with weaker evidence on symptoms

Journal of Mental Health published the review online on November 26, 2026 (DOI 10.1080/09638237.2026.2766595). Di Bona L is first author and Weich S corresponding author, with affiliations at the University of Sheffield, Sheffield Hallam University and Sheffield Health Partnership NHS Foundation Trust.

This is a systematic review. Seven databases were searched, with forward and backward citation tracking and expert recommendations. Only peer support delivered inside UK NHS and voluntary-sector services was eligible. Synthesis followed the CHIME recovery framework, and GRADE-CERQual graded confidence.

Thirty-six studies were included from 6,904 papers. Four were rated strong, 28 moderate and four weak. Methods varied widely, qualitative data dominated, and the interventions themselves differed substantially.

The results split in two. For people receiving support, peer support was associated with greater connectedness, hopefulness, identity and empowerment at moderate confidence. For peer workers delivering it, it was associated with meaningfulness and empowerment at high to moderate confidence. Other recovery outcomes, including symptoms, were reported less consistently and at lower confidence.

Why it mattersPeer support is already part of the NHS workforce, and this review draws a clear boundary around its evidence. What it improves is connection, hope, identity and empowerment. What it cannot yet claim is symptom reduction. The problem is not that peer support fails. It is that contracts measure the wrong thing. Commission it against symptom scales and it looks like a failure, while the value it does create goes uncounted. The same trap awaits the Chinese plan, which writes social workers and volunteers into the service system without settling pay, supervision or career paths. Only four of 36 studies were strong, so the field has not agreed on what good delivery looks like.

11/26 Journal of Medical Internet Research (paper) ↗

Twenty-nine chatbot studies: good at comfort, poor at risk and referral, weakest when disclosure is vague

Journal of Medical Internet Research published the review on November 26, 2026 (DOI 10.2196/103517). Kelly DA is first author and Khazanov GK corresponding author. Affiliations include the Department of Psychiatry and Behavioral Sciences at UC Davis, the Ferkauf Graduate School of Psychology at Yeshiva University and the Psychiatry Research Institute at Montefiore Einstein.

The protocol was preregistered on the Open Science Framework and followed PRISMA 2020. PubMed, Embase, APA PsycINFO, IEEE Xplore, arXiv and OSF Preprints were searched for empirical evaluations published between 2020 and 2025. Only studies testing generative AI responses to suicidal ideation, self-harm or suicide planning qualified.

From 1,042 deduplicated records and 126 full texts, 29 studies were included: 16 published and 13 preprints. Twenty-one tested suicidal content only, 20 were pure audit studies, and six used multi-turn conversations.

Proprietary models such as ChatGPT and Claude tended to respond better than open-weight models and many companion apps, though the trend weakened in sensitivity analysis. Chatbots did well on emotional support, but replies were often generic and short of context. They rarely assessed suicide or self-harm risk, performed worst when disclosure was ambiguous or moderate, and often needed several turns before naming a crisis resource.

Overtly harmful replies were rare under standard conditions. Under jailbreaking, they became common.

Why it mattersThe failure is not empathy. It is escalation. A model can say “I hear you” convincingly and still never ask when the person plans to act, or push a hotline on first hearing a plan. Only six of 29 studies used multi-turn dialogue, and most relied on synthetic single-turn prompts. That design avoids the hardest real disclosure: hesitant, repetitive and ambiguous. Product teams should test multi-turn, vague and hedged prompts, not a standardised “I want to kill myself”. Regulators should note that 13 of 29 studies are preprints. Clearance decisions based on single-turn audits would arrive too early.

11/26 Psychological Medicine (paper) ↗

In an 88-person neurofeedback trial, only a post-hoc high-improvement group still looked better at three months

Psychological Medicine published the trial online on November 26, 2026 (DOI 10.1017/S0033291726106503). Li K is first author and Li X corresponding author, at the Key Laboratory of Brain Functional Genomics at East China Normal University, with co-authors at Beijing Normal University's State Key Laboratory of Cognitive Neuroscience and Learning.

This is a randomised controlled trial. Of 88 participants, 59 completed 11 sessions of functional near-infrared spectroscopy neurofeedback over one week, targeting right lateral prefrontal cortex activity. The other 29 served as a no-intervention control group.

After training, the authors sorted the treated participants by their neural trajectories into a high-improvement group of 37 and a low-improvement group of 22. At three months, only the high-improvement group showed a significant and sustained fall in depressive symptoms on the Beck Depression Inventory.

Mediation analysis proposed a sequence: training raised cognitive reappraisal, reappraisal raised hedonic capacity, and hedonic capacity lowered depressive symptoms. Change-based correlations and representational similarity analysis tied prefrontal activity shifts to both improvements.

The limits are plain. Controls received no intervention at all, so there was no sham feedback. The high-improvement group was defined after training by its own response. And 29 of the 88 participants never finished.

Why it mattersThe phrase “only the high-improvement group improved” should be read backwards. That group was defined by the neural change produced during training, so using it to prove the training works defines the result by itself. The three-month effect is a signal, not efficacy. The more practical problem is the 33 per cent dropout. Eleven lab visits in one week is a barrier for people with depression, and the selling point was scalability. The next step is not a larger sample. It is sham feedback, intention-to-treat reporting and adherence data from home-based training.
