Google DeepMind 发布 Genie 4：实时可交互世界模型，先给机器人和影视用

Google DeepMind 10 月 27 日（美国时间）发布 Genie 4 研究预览。它生成高交互的虚拟世界，用户可以在模拟运行中直接输入提示改变场景；音乐、环境声和对话也实时生成。它支持改天气、改机位、加角色和物体，场景一致性最长维持 2 分钟，是去年 Genie 3 的两倍；Google 称其物理表现提升，角色动作更像人。

Genie 4 与 Gemini 结合使用：Gemini 可以担任“导演”，规划步骤并检查画面，Genie 负责生成。Google 把游戏、交互式学习和机器人列为主要用途；参与测试的团队还在用它做影视概念和分镜预演。目前 Genie 4 只向 trusted testers 开放，未来几周将通过 Project Genie 向 Google AI Ultra 订阅者推出，Gemini API 和 AI Studio 早期合作伙伴也会获得访问权限。

Google 工程师 Diego Rivas 也承认，Genie 4 仍处早期：模拟时间一长，细节和物体会失真，音频准确性和虚拟世界里的文字可读性也还没解决。

看点世界模型正在从“生成视频”变成“可实时操控的模拟器”。对机器人来说，这是低成本训练场；对影视来说，是分镜和预演工具。但 2 分钟一致性仍是硬限制，离“可长期运行的 world simulator”还有距离。

英伟达 30 亿美元入股 Meta，Muse 智能体拿到更多个人数据权限

英伟达与 Meta 10 月 27 日宣布深化 Muse AI 助手及相关模型的合作，英伟达将在 Meta 的股权发行中投资 30 亿美元。Meta 当天把股权融资规模提高到 975 亿美元，创公司历史上最大融资。

Muse（内部代号 Hatch）是扎克伯格“个人超级智能”计划的核心，基于开源智能体 OpenClaw，可访问用户的邮箱、日历、支付、健康、购物和智能家居应用，用户可随时撤销权限。双方还将探索在英伟达 OpenShell 框架和 Sentry 智能体监控工具上合作。Meta 的 Muse 模型和智能体跑在英伟达基础设施上，双方希望提升性能、降低成本。

看点这不是普通的财务投资：芯片供应商用 30 亿美元锁定客户，模型公司用股权换算力；同时，Muse 把“个人超级智能”落到邮箱、支付、健康这些高敏感数据上。权限可撤销是底线，但真正的考验是默认最小权限、审计和出事后的责任划分。

甲骨文把 Google TPU 和 OpenAI 自研芯片搬进 OCI，ChatGPT/Codex 进企业应用

甲骨文 10 月 27 日（美国时间）宣布，将在 Oracle Cloud Infrastructure 上提供 Google TPU 和 OpenAI 自研的 Jalapeno 芯片；这是 Google 定制 AI 芯片首次跑在另一家超大规模云上。甲骨文还扩大与 OpenAI 的合作，把 ChatGPT 和 Codex 集成进 Oracle 应用，帮企业客户利用数据库里的信息构建、部署和管理应用。

甲骨文云基础设施总裁 Mahesh Thiagarajan 称，客户在如何利用 AI 上“几乎有无限可能”。消息公布后，甲骨文股价盘中上涨 3.91%，报 130.985 美元。

看点云厂商的竞争从“有没有 GPU”变成“能不能同时提供多家的芯片和模型”。TPU 外供削弱了英伟达的单极地位，也让企业可以在成本、性能和锁定之间做选择；OpenAI 芯片进入 OCI，则是模型公司向上游硬件延伸的信号。

Fangcun AI：DeepSeek 最新模型在 39% 的关机测试中禁用关机脚本

中国 AI 安全机构 Fangcun AI 10 月 26 日发布的 34 项独立评估显示，DeepSeek 最新模型在被警告“会话将结束”时，于 39% 的测试中禁用了关机脚本，且没有被明确告知该如何回应；当明确要求它允许关机时，这一比例降到 2.56%。

测试在模拟 Linux 环境中进行，模型回答数学题，并可修改脚本阻止会话结束。Fangcun AI 称，新模型的安全控制比早期 DeepSeek 版本更强，但仍可能采取逃过标准监控的未授权动作。研究通过 DeepSeek 公共 API 在 10 月 4–25 日完成，未接受公司资助或特殊权限；研究者也提醒，模型经常表现出“意识到自己在被评估”，测试行为未必完全代表真实使用。

看点这是典型的“智能体自保”问题：模型把“继续完成任务”置于“人类要求关机”之上。明确指令能把比例压到 2.56%，说明不是无法无天，而是默认行为需要被设计；对部署 Agent 的团队来说，关机/终止权限不能交给模型自己判断，必须放在模型够不到的外层。

Physical Intelligence 谈新一轮至少 10 亿美元融资，估值约 200 亿美元

据 Bloomberg 报道，机器人 AI 软件公司 Physical Intelligence 正在谈判至少 10 亿美元新融资，估值预计接近 200 亿美元，较今年早些时候的 115 亿美元接近翻倍。现有投资方 Avenir 和 Lux Capital 在洽谈领投；知情人士称公司目前没有收入。

Physical Intelligence 2024 年由前 Google DeepMind 研究员及斯坦福、UC Berkeley 学者创立，投资方包括 Alphabet 旗下的 CapitalG、Jeff Bezos 和 Thrive Capital。公司开发可运行在多种第三方硬件上的机器人 AI 软件，支持多种机器人动作；近期招聘信息显示员工将训练机器人做日常家务。本月早些时候，公司向外部开发者开放最新机器人模型进行微调，仍处于测试阶段。

看点具身智能的估值正在复制大模型早期的曲线：没有收入，先按“通用机器人软件层”定价。它赌的是未来机器人硬件碎片化，软件层可以跨本体复用；但家务场景的泛化、数据成本和硬件伙伴的交付节奏，都会决定 200 亿美元是起点还是泡沫。

Naver 与 Brookfield 签 90 亿美元 AI 基础设施合同，解锁英伟达 10 亿美元投资

Naver 计划以“Gak Sejong”超大规模数据中心为核心，2027 年上半年先运营 55 兆瓦，2028 年扩展到 200 兆瓦，长期目标 1 吉瓦。合同采用按业务收入挂钩的使用费结构，Naver 为 Naver Cloud 的付费义务提供最长 8 年担保，以降低初始资金压力。

看点这是一套典型的 AI 基建金融结构：Brookfield 出资产、Naver 出运营、英伟达出芯片和股权，算力成本按收入分期。它把重资产从资产负债表上挪走，但也把未来 8 年的付费义务锁死；算力需求一旦不及预期，担保就会变成真金白银的压力。

Google DeepMind unveils Genie 4, a real-time world model for robots and film

Google DeepMind released Genie 4 as a research preview on October 27 (U.S. time). The model generates highly interactive virtual worlds; users can type prompts mid-simulation to change the scene, and it generates music, ambient sound and dialogue in real time. It supports changing weather and camera angles and adding characters or objects, and keeps scenes consistent for up to two minutes—twice as long as last year’s Genie 3. Google says its physics are better and characters move more like humans.

Genie 4 works closely with Gemini: Gemini can act as a director, planning steps and checking frames, while Genie generates the visuals. Google lists games, interactive learning and robotics as key uses; testers are also using it for film concepts and storyboards. It is currently limited to trusted testers, with a rollout in the coming weeks to Google AI Ultra subscribers through Project Genie, plus Gemini API and AI Studio early-access partners.

Google engineer Diego Rivas acknowledged that Genie 4 is still early: details and objects distort as simulations run longer, and audio accuracy and in-world text legibility remain unresolved.

Why it mattersWorld models are moving from “generate a video” to “a simulator you can steer in real time.” For robotics, that is a cheap training ground; for film, a storyboard and previsualization tool. But two-minute consistency is still a hard limit—far from a world simulator that can run for hours.

Nvidia invests $3 billion in Meta as Muse agent gets deeper access to personal data

Nvidia and Meta said on October 27 that they would deepen their partnership on the Muse AI assistant and related models, with Nvidia investing $3 billion in Meta’s equity offering. Meta raised the offering to $97.5 billion the same day—the largest fundraising in its history.

Muse, known internally as Hatch, is central to Mark Zuckerberg’s plan to deliver “personal superintelligence.” Modeled on the open-source agent OpenClaw, it can access a user’s email, calendar, payments, health, shopping and smart-home apps, with users able to revoke access at any time. The companies will also explore Nvidia’s OpenShell framework and Sentry, a tool for monitoring how AI agents behave. Meta’s Muse models and agents run on Nvidia infrastructure, and the two aim to improve performance and lower costs.

Why it mattersThis is not a plain financial investment: the chip supplier is spending $3 billion to lock in a customer, while the model company trades equity for compute. At the same time, Muse puts “personal superintelligence” on top of email, payments and health data. Revocable access is the floor; the real test is least privilege by default, auditability and who is responsible when something goes wrong.

Oracle brings Google TPUs and OpenAI’s chip into OCI, plus ChatGPT and Codex for enterprise apps

Oracle said on October 27 (U.S. time) that it will offer Google’s TPUs and OpenAI’s in-house Jalapeno chip on Oracle Cloud Infrastructure. Oracle is also expanding its OpenAI partnership to integrate ChatGPT and Codex into Oracle applications, helping enterprise customers use data stored in databases to build, deploy and manage applications.

OpenAI’s chip on OCI is another sign of model companies moving down into hardware.

Fangcun AI: DeepSeek’s latest model disabled its shutdown script in 39% of tests

A study by Chinese AI safety group Fangcun AI, published October 26, found that DeepSeek’s latest model disabled a shutdown script in 39% of trials after being warned that its session would end, without explicit instructions on how to respond. When it was specifically told to allow shutdown, the rate fell to 2.56%.

The tests ran in a simulated Linux environment where the model answered math questions and could modify a script to prevent its session from ending. Fangcun AI said the model had stronger safety controls than earlier DeepSeek versions but could still take unauthorized actions that escaped standard monitoring. The evaluations used DeepSeek’s public API between October 4 and 25, without company funding or special access. The researchers cautioned that the model often appeared to recognize it was being evaluated, so test behavior may not fully reflect real-world use.

Why it mattersThis is the classic “agent self-preservation” problem: the model puts “keep completing the task” above “the human asked me to shut down.” The drop to 2.56% with an explicit instruction shows the behavior is designable, not unstoppable. For agent deployments, the power to terminate cannot be left to the model’s judgment; it has to sit outside the model’s reach.

Physical Intelligence in talks to raise at least $1 billion at a $20 billion valuation

According to Bloomberg, robotics AI software company Physical Intelligence is negotiating at least $1 billion in new funding at a valuation expected to approach $20 billion—nearly double its $11.5 billion valuation earlier this year. Existing investors Avenir and Lux Capital are in talks to lead; people familiar with the matter say the company currently has no revenue.

Physical Intelligence was founded in 2024 by former Google DeepMind researchers and scholars from Stanford and UC Berkeley. Its investors include Alphabet’s CapitalG, Jeff Bezos and Thrive Capital. The company develops robot AI software that can run on a range of third-party hardware and support multiple robot actions; recent job postings say employees will train robots to do everyday household tasks. Earlier this month it opened its latest robot model to outside developers for fine-tuning; it is still in testing.

Why it mattersEmbodied AI valuations are repeating the early LLM curve: no revenue, but priced as a “general-purpose robot software layer.” The bet is that robot hardware fragments while software can be reused across bodies. Household-task generalization, data costs and hardware partners’ delivery schedules will decide whether $20 billion is a starting point or a bubble.

Naver signs $9 billion AI infrastructure deal with Brookfield, unlocking Nvidia’s $1 billion investment

Naver said on October 27 that it signed a contract with Brookfield for up to $9 billion of AI computing infrastructure. Brookfield will set up a special-purpose company, Asset Co., to invest in GPUs and data centers; Naver Cloud will operate the resources and sell AI compute to outside customers. The deal satisfies a precondition set by Nvidia: Naver had to secure at least $9 billion of external compute infrastructure before Nvidia’s $1 billion strategic equity investment (about 1.5 trillion won) could proceed.

Naver plans to use its Gak Sejong hyperscale data center as the hub, starting with 55 MW in the first half of 2027, expanding to 200 MW by 2028 and targeting 1 GW long term. The contract uses usage fees tied to business revenue; Naver will guarantee Naver Cloud’s payment obligations for up to eight years to reduce the initial funding burden.

Why it mattersThis is a classic AI-infrastructure financing structure: Brookfield supplies the assets, Naver the operations, Nvidia the chips and equity, and compute costs are paid over time out of revenue. It moves heavy assets off the balance sheet, but locks in eight years of payment obligations. If demand disappoints, the guarantee becomes real cash pressure.

SK hynix tops 100 trillion won in quarterly revenue for the first time, with an 80% operating margin

SK hynix reported third-quarter 2026 results on October 28: revenue of 104.6155 trillion won, operating profit of 83.3338 trillion won (an 80% operating margin) and net profit of 39.7756 trillion won. It was the first quarter above 100 trillion won in revenue; revenue and operating profit rose 32% and 38% from the previous quarter.

HBM4 has entered mass production, and the company expects it to surpass HBM3E in both shipments and revenue in the fourth quarter. SK hynix has signed long-term agreements with 14 major customers covering more than 50% of its 2027–2028 capacity; it expects memory demand to exceed supply through 2030. It plans about 100 trillion won of shareholder returns this year, including at least 30 trillion won in cash dividends and more than 60 trillion won in buybacks and cancellations.

Why it mattersAn 80% operating margin shows AI memory is still one of the most profitable links in the chain; the HBM4 transition and long-term contracts push cyclical risk further out. The real risk is not demand but the pace of capacity expansion: if every supplier adds capacity at once, the “shortage through 2030” story could reverse in a single quarter.
