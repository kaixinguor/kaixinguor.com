苹果 HALO 在宇树 G1 上跑通 6 个未建图环境，360 次单项任务平均成功率 91%

苹果研究团队在 arXiv 提交论文《Perceptive Humanoid Motion from Simulation》，披露人形机器人自主控制系统 HALO，并把它部署到宇树 G1 上验证：背部加装 3D 打印外壳，内置一台 M4 Pro、64GB 统一内存的 Mac mini，负责机载推理开销较大的 Orchestrator（VLM，默认千问 Qwen3.6-35B-A3B）与 Motion Generator（DiT，千问 Qwen3.5-2B）；G1 自带芯片运行 50Hz 的 Tracker（强化学习策略）。

在 6 个未预先扫描或建图的真实环境中，团队做了 360 次单项任务测试，平均成功率 91%。长序列组合任务在单一场景各测 10 次，3 种组合平均 70%；最复杂的「俯身通过障碍—走向目标—推箱子」10 次成功 5 次，为 50%。数据侧用 250 张 H100 合成主要训练数据、耗时约 48 小时；动作生成器用 64 张 B200，跟踪器用 4 张 H100。

团队公开了数据生成、模型训练与部署代码。论文也写明局限：新增物理任务仍需人工写提示词、设计动作关键帧，部分动作依赖人类视频或动捕；机器人主要靠视觉，难以直接感知接触，物体离开视野或被遮挡时，涉及接触的任务会明显变差。

看点91% 是单项任务的成功率，70% 才是长序列的真实体感——误差会沿步骤累积，这是所有分层控制系统的共同瓶颈。更值得抄的是工程拆分：大模型放在机载 Mac mini 上跑语义与编排，实时策略留在机器人控制器，云端只是可选；再加上公开代码，复现门槛从「有机器人」降到「有 G1 加一台 Mac」。下一步的胜负手在触觉与遮挡处理，不在再刷高单项成功率。
另一个视角这套管线一次数据循环要 250 张 H100 跑两天，动作生成还要 64 张 B200；能力越依赖合成数据，账单越像训练一家模型公司，而不是造一台机器人。谁来为「每个新任务重新采一遍数据」付钱，会决定这类系统是实验室演示还是可售产品。

HyperFlow 把 31B 视频音频模型压到 13.7B，4 到 8 步出片，给 INT8 与 GGUF 两版

Video Rebirth 在 GitHub 放出 HyperFlow H3 Flash：以 MiniMax-H3 的 31B 联合视频—音频生成模型为底座，压到 13.7B，推理步数降到 4 至 8 步，同时提供 INT8 量化版和 GGUF 版。仓库把「联合生成」作为卖点——画面与声音在同一次去噪里产生，而不是先出视频再配音。

项目页给出的关键数字是参数量、步数与量化格式，没有给出与教师模型逐项对比的基准分数，也没有说明训练数据规模与许可证细节。它目前是社区发布，不是同行评审论文；所有性能数字都应视为作者自测。

看点把 31B 蒸馏到 13.7B、再把步数压到个位数，省的是推理时的显存与时间，代价通常落在细节、运动一致性和音画同步上。要判断它能不能用，别只看画质样片：先看 8 步与 4 步的差异、口型与敲击声是否对齐、INT8 掉点多少，以及 GGUF 在消费级显卡上的实际帧率。对做本地生成工具的团队，GGUF 版意味着可以绕开数据中心，但许可证和商用条款要先读清楚。

11/07 Cyber Security News ↗

Claude Code 的「不再询问」把密码写进项目配置，1,122 份公开副本含密钥

Apiiro 的扫描显示，Claude Code 在用户选择「Yes, and don't ask again」后，会把整条命令原样写进项目的 .claude/settings.local.json；如果命令里带着密码或 token，它们也以明文落盘。公开 GitHub 上有 104,185 份该文件，分布在 103,617 个仓库，其中 1,122 份（约 1.1%）至少含一个密钥，合计 1,379 个。

拆开看：379 个数据库密码、43 个公网服务器 SSH 登录、42 个 GitHub/GitLab/npm token、23 个 AWS 密钥；涉及 63 家组织。研究者追踪的 1,105 个仓库里，539 个的该文件由带 Claude 共同署名的提交改动。含密钥的文件中，一半在默认分支上已存在至少 217 天；另有 734 个历史密钥、211 个 fork 副本。把共享配置、历史版本与聊天记录都算上，共 2,539 个密钥、1,994 个仓库。

常见扫描器漏得不少：gitleaks 标出 564 份、TruffleHog 标出 643 份，两者都没标出的有 215 份，主要是写在命令行里的密码。Claude Code 首次写入时会把该文件加进本机全局 git excludes，但这个保护不随仓库走，也不会改写已经提交的历史。

看点这不是「模型泄露」，是权限交互的设计问题：为了少弹窗，工具把「允许这条命令」记成了「记住这条命令的全文」。修复顺序应该是先轮换、再清理——从仓库删除文件不会抹掉 git 历史与 fork。对做 agent 的团队，允许规则要按命令模式而不是完整命令行保存，密钥只以变量名出现；同时把 .claude/、.cursor/ 这类目录当成 .env 来扫。默认值选错一次，代价是几百个凭据。

11/07 17:40 IT Home ↗

Apple's HALO hits 91% on 360 single-task trials across six unmapped rooms on a Unitree G1

Apple's research team has posted "Perceptive Humanoid Motion from Simulation" on arXiv, describing HALO, a humanoid control system it deployed on a Unitree G1. The robot carries a 3D-printed shell and a Mac mini with an M4 Pro and 64GB of unified memory, which runs the compute-heavy Orchestrator (a vision-language model, Qwen3.6-35B-A3B by default) and the Motion Generator (a DiT, Qwen3.5-2B). The G1's own chip runs a 50Hz Tracker, a reinforcement-learning policy.

Across six real environments that had not been pre-scanned or mapped, the team ran 360 single-task trials and averaged 91% success. For long-horizon compositions, three task sequences were each run 10 times in one environment, averaging 70%; the hardest — duck under an obstacle, walk to the target, push a box — succeeded 5 out of 10. The data pipeline used 250 H100s for about 48 hours; the motion generator trained on 64 B200s, the tracker on four H100s.

The team released data-generation, training and deployment code. The paper also states the limits: new physical tasks still need hand-written prompts and keyframes, some motions rely on human video or motion capture, and the robot is vision-only, so contact-heavy tasks degrade when objects leave the camera's view or become occluded.

Why it mattersThe 91% is single-task success; 70% is what long sequences actually feel like, because errors compound across steps — the shared bottleneck of every hierarchical control stack. The more copyable part is the engineering split: the large model runs on an on-robot Mac mini for semantics and orchestration, the real-time policy stays on the robot controller, and the cloud is optional. With code released, reproduction drops from "own a robot" to "own a G1 and a Mac". The next fight is touch and occlusion, not another point of single-task success.
The other viewOne data cycle costs 250 H100s for two days, and motion generation another 64 B200s. The more capability depends on synthetic data, the more the bill looks like a model company's, not a robot company's. Who pays to re-collect data for every new task will decide whether this stays a lab demo or becomes a product.

11/07 22:16 GitHub · Video Rebirth ↗

HyperFlow squeezes a 31B video-and-audio model to 13.7B, generating in 4 to 8 steps

Video Rebirth has published HyperFlow H3 Flash on GitHub: a compressed version of MiniMax-H3, a 31B joint video-audio generation model, cut to 13.7B parameters and 4 to 8 inference steps, with INT8 and GGUF builds. The pitch is joint generation — picture and sound emerge from the same denoising loop rather than a video pass followed by a dubbing pass.

The repository states parameter count, step count and quantization formats, but gives no head-to-head benchmark against the teacher model, no training-data scale, and no full licence details. This is a community release, not a peer-reviewed paper; every performance figure is the authors' own.

Why it mattersDistilling 31B into 13.7B and pushing steps into single digits buys memory and latency, and usually charges for it in detail, motion consistency and audio-visual sync. Before trusting it, compare the 4-step and 8-step outputs, check whether lip movements line up with impacts and speech, measure the INT8 drop, and look at real frame rates for the GGUF build on consumer cards. For teams building local generation tools, GGUF means escaping the data centre — but read the licence and commercial terms first.

Claude Code's "don't ask again" writes passwords into project settings; 1,122 public copies hold secrets

Apiiro's scan found that when a Claude Code user picks "Yes, and don't ask again", the tool saves the entire command verbatim in the project's .claude/settings.local.json. If the command contains a password or token, it lands on disk in plain text. Public GitHub holds 104,185 copies of that file across 103,617 repositories; 1,122 of them — about 1.1% — contain at least one secret, 1,379 in total.

The breakdown: 379 database passwords, 43 SSH logins for public servers, 42 GitHub/GitLab/npm tokens and 23 AWS keys, spread across 63 organisations. In 539 of the 1,105 repositories the researchers traced, the file was changed by a commit listing Claude as a co-author. Half of the secret-bearing files had been on the default branch for at least 217 days; there are another 734 historical secrets and 211 forked copies. Counting shared settings, history and agent chat logs, the total reaches 2,539 secrets across 1,994 repositories.

Common scanners miss many of them: gitleaks flagged 564 files and TruffleHog 643, and neither flagged 215, mostly passwords typed inside commands. Claude Code adds the file to the user's global git excludes the first time it writes it, but that protection does not travel with the repository and does not rewrite history.

Why it mattersThis is not a model leak; it is a permissions-design failure. To reduce prompts, the tool turned "allow this command" into "store this command in full". The order of repair is rotate first, clean second — deleting the file does not remove it from git history or forks. Agent builders should save allow-rules by command pattern, keep secrets as variable names only, and treat .claude/, .cursor/ and similar directories as .env files in secret scanning. One wrong default can cost hundreds of credentials.

11/07 12:53 Beijing Youth Daily ↗

Robots get an ID: China's industry ministry consults on a "one machine, one code" rule as Beijing starts deployment

On November 6, the equipment industry department of China's Ministry of Industry and Information Technology opened public consultation on a draft measure for robot product identification codes, with comments due December 5. The code identifies a complete robot and has four segments: manufacturer code, product description code, serial-number code and other codes. It covers industrial, service and special-purpose robots.

The draft requires manufacturers to mark each unit in at least three ways — digital storage, human-readable and machine-readable — with consistent information. Marks must be clear, durable, hard to replace, fixed for life, tamper-proof and non-erasable. Annual applications are generally capped at 300% of the previous year's actual output, with extra volume requiring production evidence. Products shipped before the rule takes effect must register their own serial numbers on the platform. Beijing's economy and information technology bureau held a mobilisation meeting on November 4.

Why it mattersThis adds an identity layer to embodied AI: a common primary key for recalls, traceability, incident liability and resale. The real cost sits in production lines and data — an extra marking step per unit, changes to MES, field and API definitions on the platform, and retroactive serial registration for installed machines. For robot-software teams, the code becomes the entry point for device registration, permission binding and firmware updates. Decide now what goes into the code, what stays in the cloud, and how it is verified offline.
