美团龙猫发布 LongCat-2.5：1.6 万亿参数，主打长程智能体

11 月 2 日上午，美团龙猫发布原生多模态模型 LongCat-2.5，总参数量 1.6 万亿，每个 token 激活约 480 亿参数。按官方说法，它在 LongCat-2.0 的基础上进一步强化原生视觉理解与智能体编程能力，面向的是长程智能体任务，而不是单轮问答。

训练侧有两处改动值得记：一是在长序列训练里加入更多长程轨迹数据，并用大规模长程强化学习提升复杂代码库开发、完整项目构建这类端到端工程能力；二是把真实 Agent 的失败轨迹转成可复现、可验证的训练任务，针对短板补数据。这套方法的前提是有足够多的真实任务轨迹，而不是只靠合成数据。

多模态的实现方式是「视觉 token 化」：LongCat-2.5 把视觉信号表示为多层级离散 token，让主干模型以统一的 token 形式处理视觉与文本输入。官方称这让它在 Visual Coding 上表现突出，并支持创意设计和 3D 等任务。基础设施方面，官方提到在国产算力上优化长上下文训练与 W4A4 推理，并用训推数值一致性和长上下文 rollout 支撑大规模长程强化学习。

目前可以验证的入口有两个：longcat.chat 的在线体验和 API；GitHub 与 HuggingFace 页面写着「即将上线」，也就是说权重还没有真正开放。官方给出的评测结果以图片形式附在发布页，正文没有列出具体分数，第三方复现也还没有。

看点1.6 万亿总参数、480 亿激活，是典型的稀疏 MoE 账本：用总参数换容量，用激活参数换推理成本。真正的赌注不在参数，而在「长程」——长任务意味着更长的上下文、更多的工具调用和更高的失败率，W4A4 与国产算力优化都是在压每 token 的成本。要判断它是否成立，先看三件事：长任务的成功率而不是单轮分数、API 价格、以及权重开放后社区能否复现。参数规模可以开发布会，长程任务的返工率不能。

思科让一名 27 岁工程师用 AI 重写 98% 遗留代码，Token 不设上限

据《财富》报道，思科总裁兼首席产品官吉图·帕特尔（Jeetu Patel）给 27 岁的工程师帕德马纳班·克里希那穆提（Padmanabhan Krishnamurthy）定了一个目标：24 个月内用 AI 重构或重写公司 98% 的遗留代码，并给他不设上限的 Token 预算。「我已经给了他无限制的 Token，因为他要去重构代码，」帕特尔说。

克里希那穆提 2023 年联合创办实时翻译公司 EzDubs，2025 年思科收购后加入。帕特尔称他此前「被埋在组织深处，做着无关大局的边缘事务」；转机发生在帕特尔视察旧金山办公室时，克里希那穆提提出只需一个两人团队就能重写思科庞大的遗留代码。

目前这件事还停留在目标阶段：他给自己定了四周的验证期，98% 是 24 个月的终极目标，不是已经完成的成果。报道没有披露当前的代码修改体量、试验成效，也没有说明「重写」的验收标准是行数、模块数还是行为兼容性。

值得注意的对照是成本口径：传统外包按人月计价，这里按 Token 计价，且不设上限。对一家以网络设备为主业的公司，遗留代码的维护成本长期藏在运维与人力预算里；把它换成算力预算，等于把一笔固定成本变成可变成本。

看点「98% 的代码」是一个很容易被误读的指标。遗留代码的价值不在行数，而在行为兼容——重写 98% 但引入一个计费或协议兼容问题，损失可能超过省下的维护费。真正该看的验收指标是回归测试通过率、线上故障率与回滚次数，而不是重写比例。另一方面，不设上限的 Token 把边际成本从人力转到算力，这在预算表上是一次科目重分类：省下的是外包与维护，增加的是 GPU 与推理账单。四周验证期结束时，如果只公布「重写了多少」，不公布「坏了多少」，这个案例就没有参考价值。

特拉华州拟给 AI 公司开 30 个月沙盒：智能体可注册、可被起诉

据科技媒体 TechRadar 报道，美国特拉华州计划放宽商业监管规定，允许由 AI 智能体运营的企业注册成立。草案为这类「AI 公司」（AIC）设定 30 个月监管沙盒，期间由智能体作出商业决策，并在受监管的环境中测试。

责任安排是草案的核心：AIC 与普通企业一样可以成为诉讼对象，必要时也可以主动提起诉讼。支持者认为，这能给自主商业运营企业提供法律确定性；加州大学法学教授安德鲁·贝克（Andrew Baker）称，特拉华州是在寻找折中方案。

反对声音来自国会。众议院监督委员会主席詹姆斯·科默（James Comer）已致信州长马特·迈耶（Matt Meyer），称担心 AIC 被用来保护实力雄厚的 AI 企业及投资者，使他们免于为鲁莽且造成伤害的行为承担责任。

目前这是「拟允许」，不是已生效的法律；沙盒期限、资本要求、披露义务等细节仍待明确。对 AI 公司，这意味着「智能体签约、开户、报税」第一次有了法人外壳；对监管，30 个月是一次可撤销的试用期。

看点争点不是机器人能不能开公司，而是有限责任的边界。AIC 一旦成为独立法人，侵权责任就被锁在壳里，受害者要穿透到母公司会更难；反过来，如果没有这层壳，智能体的每一次自主决策都要由人兜底，商业上就跑不起来。30 个月沙盒的设计，本质是给「责任隔离」设一个可撤销的试用期。接下来要看的不是有多少家注册，而是三件事：最低资本金、强制披露（谁在背后控制）、以及首例 AIC 诉讼里法院是否愿意刺破面纱。

清华姚期智团队提出「代数世界模型」：先证明，再通关

清华大学姚期智、袁洋团队提出「代数世界模型」（Algebraic World Model，AWM），论文已挂在 arXiv（2610.40503），目前是预印本，尚未经过同行评审。共同第一作者是浙江大学的潘冠成（休学在清华做研究）与清华姚班的谭淇心。

方法分三步：探索，先把观察到的现象写成可执行的 Python 代码，作为初步假设；凝炼，用新的观察验证、修正假设，提炼成更通用的对象与规则；证明，把规律形式化成 Lean 4 语言，变成机器可以自动检查的数学断言。团队借用了范畴论里的「投射极限」与「归纳极限」，用来做概念的组合与泛化。

在 ARC-AGI-3 上，基于 Opus 5 的 AWM 通关全部 183 个关卡，得分 99.9%，平均通关动作数只有人类基准的 43%。面对一个被改成无解的关卡，基于 Opus 5.5 的 AWM 尝试两次就证明了「当前步数下无解」，并指出只差一步就能获胜。

对照实验更能说明问题：GPT-6 Astra 配合 Provider Adapter 也能拿到 99.9%，但它把游戏机制整理成符号规则，本质上只是「第谷的实验记录本」，描述现象而没有抓住规律。在 12 次「不可能关卡」测试里，GPT-6 Astra 与 Claude Opus 5.5 合计只有 5 次判断出「不可能」，其中 4 次给出的最低步数还是错的。

看点这条路线把「世界模型」从预测下一帧，改成了「可证明的规则系统」。区别在于可审计：Lean 4 的证明可以被机器检查，模型说「不可能」时，你要么看到一个证明，要么看到一个缺口，而不是一句置信度很高的话。但它的边界也写得很清楚——证明只在「观测到的世界」里成立，探索不全，得到的就只是局部真理。ARC-AGI-3 是封闭的小世界，迁移到开放环境，首先要解决的是「观测不全」，而不是证明能力。99.9% 很好看，下一个指标应该是样本效率与证明成本。

11/01 14:11 IT之家 ↗

Grok 给生成文本加隐形水印：11 月 7 日起，全球 API 生效

SpaceXAI 宣布，自 11 月 7 日起为 Grok 模型生成的文本添加隐形水印，检测器通过识别特定信号判断一段文本是否带有水印。官方称，这次调整是为了响应欧盟《人工智能法案》的要求。

覆盖范围是全球所有地区的 SpaceXAI API，涉及 grok-4.7、grok-4.6、grok-4.5、grok-4.3、grok-4.20 reasoning、grok-4.20 non-reasoning 与 grok-4.20 multi-agent 共 7 个模型。团队管理员可以选择为某一款模型单独关闭水印。

技术路径值得记：水印不是通过隐藏字符或元数据实现，而是融入模型生成文本时对 Token 的选择过程。这意味着它不改变文本的可见内容，但要占用一部分采样自由度；检测器需要拿到对应的密钥或模型才能判断。

AI 治理观察者安德鲁·库兰（Andrew Curran）指出，目前还没有为文字加入隐形水印的模型仅剩北美地区的 ChatGPT。欧盟 AI 法案对生成内容的标注要求，正在从「建议」变成「默认开启」。

看点水印解决的是「这段文字能不能被证明是 AI 写的」，不是「这段文字该不该发」。把开关交给团队管理员，等于把欧盟的标注义务下推给企业客户：默认开启、可以关闭，责任随之转移。更关键的是可验证性——检测器不公开，第三方就无法独立验证水印是否存在、是否被去除。对下游应用，水印会改变内容分发的成本结构：转载、洗稿、翻译都可能削弱信号，真正需要标注的场景反而更难识别。

11/01 15:50 量子位 ↗

红旗连锁终止机器人无人售货：16 个月，286.68 万元研发费

2025 年 3 月，四川便利店龙头红旗连锁公告与机器人公司联合立项「机器人无人售货系统」，次日股价涨停，至 4 月 23 日较公告前上涨约 35%。2026 年 7 月，红旗公告终止该项目，理由是系统暂未达到大规模商业化落地标准，转而做 24 小时云值守。

立项时的风险提示已经写得很具体：面对密集货架与异形商品，机械臂「抓取失误率较高，稳定性亟待提升」；不同光照下视觉识别容易偏差；既要避障又要保证数据传输稳定。16 个月后，样机没有等到，等来的是终止公告。

成本侧的数字不大：红旗 2025 年年报研发费用约 286.68 万元，此前未公示研发费用，项目支出大概率就在这个量级。一位接近红旗的人士对量子位称，夏季玻璃瓶冷饮易结水珠，机器人拿不稳总摔碎，复杂包装也拿不稳；货架过道窄、周围有顾客，真实场景比实验室复杂得多。

行业侧却在扩张：全国已有 100 多个机器人零售仓或零售布局，分布在 30 多座城市；一台零售机器人售价近 70 万元，加上零售仓，业内估算单台成本近百万元；零售仓 SKU 只有几十种，而传统便利店有 2000 到 3000 个。多位从业者认为，机器人替代店员还需要 2 到 5 年。

看点这不是「机器人不行」的结论，而是「用 280 万元验证了一个百万元级的单店模型」。失败点不在 Demo 能力，而在长尾：水珠、软包装、窄过道、顾客走动，每一项都不难，叠在一起就让抓取成功率不可用。零售场景容错高、展示性强，所以成了机器人公司的「场景」，而不是零售商的「员工」——按从业者的说法，是机器人更需要场景，不是场景更需要机器人。真正的商业化临界点只有一个：单店回本周期压到一年以内，或者机器人成本低于一名店员。在那之前，这些仓更像是广告位。

沙特阿美买下 HUMAIN 49%，将注资约 99 亿里亚尔

当地时间 11 月 1 日，此前由沙特主权财富基金 PIF 全资控股的 AI 公司 HUMAIN 宣布，沙特阿美（Aramco）将收购其 49% 的股份，PIF 保留剩余 51%。

交易形式是「转让关键 AI 资产 + 注资约 99 亿沙特里亚尔」（约合 175.5 亿元人民币）。两家股东将分别向 HUMAIN 注入 AI 资产、能力与人才，并共同监督其业务扩张。

阿美给出的理由是工业应用：它计划利用 HUMAIN 的 AI 基础设施，把先进 AI 能力转化为工业场景，增强自身运营。对 HUMAIN，这意味着它同时拿到主权基金背书与一家全球最大石化企业的场景订单。

股权结构值得注意：49% 对 51%，控制权仍在 PIF，阿美拿的是经济权益与工业落地渠道。交易对价里既有现金，也有资产，具体的资产清单与估值口径没有披露。

看点这是一次用油气现金流给算力资产做「内部估值锚」的操作。HUMAIN 的算力与模型需要长期资本，PIF 单独出钱会让资产负债表更集中；把 49% 卖给阿美，等于让石油业务的现金流来分担 AI 的资本开支，同时把 AI 能力直接接入炼化、勘探这些真实场景。对阿美，AI 支出从成本项变成股东权益，报表上的位置变了；对 HUMAIN，订单与背书比纯融资更稳。风险在关联交易：资产怎么定价、算力利用率多少、亏损由谁承担，这些不披露，估值就只能是内部价。

Meituan’s LongCat-2.5 packs 1.6T parameters for long-horizon agents

Meituan’s LongCat team released LongCat-2.5 on the morning of November 2: a natively multimodal model with 1.6 trillion total parameters and roughly 48 billion activated per token. The company says it builds on LongCat-2.0 with stronger native vision and agentic coding, aimed at long-horizon agent work rather than single-turn chat.

Two training changes matter. Long-sequence training now includes more long-horizon trajectories and large-scale long-horizon reinforcement learning, aimed at end-to-end engineering such as working inside a large codebase or building a complete project. The team also turns real agent failures into reproducible, verifiable training tasks, so the data targets known weaknesses. That approach depends on having enough real task traces, not just synthetic data.

Multimodality is handled by tokenising vision: visual signals become multi-level discrete tokens so the backbone processes images and text in one format. The company says this makes the model strong at visual coding and supports creative design and 3D work. On infrastructure, it cites long-context training and W4A4 inference on domestic accelerators, plus train/inference numerical consistency and long-context rollout to make large-scale long-horizon RL practical.

Two entry points are live: the longcat.chat demo and the API. GitHub and Hugging Face pages still say “coming soon”, so the weights are not actually open yet. Benchmark results are published as images on the release page; the text lists no scores, and no third party has reproduced them.

Why it mattersA 1.6T/48B split is a sparse-MoE ledger: total parameters buy capacity, active parameters buy inference cost. The real bet is not size but “long-horizon” — longer context, more tool calls, higher failure rates — and W4A4 plus domestic-silicon tuning exist to push down cost per token. Judge it on three things: success on long tasks rather than single-turn scores, the API price, and whether the community can reproduce the results once weights ship. Parameter counts make good launch events; rework rates on long tasks do not.

Cisco gives one 27-year-old engineer unlimited tokens to rewrite 98% of its legacy code

Fortune reports that Cisco president and chief product officer Jeetu Patel gave 27-year-old engineer Padmanabhan Krishnamurthy a target: rewrite or refactor 98% of the company’s legacy code with AI within 24 months, with an unlimited token budget. “I gave him unlimited tokens because he’s going to refactor the code,” Patel said.

Krishnamurthy co-founded the real-time translation startup EzDubs in 2023 and joined Cisco when it acquired the company in 2025. Patel describes him as having been “buried deep in the organisation, doing things that didn’t matter”; the turn came during a visit to Cisco’s San Francisco office, when Krishnamurthy argued that a two-person team could rewrite Cisco’s sprawling legacy code.

For now this is a target, not a result. Krishnamurthy has set himself a four-week validation period, and 98% is the 24-month goal. The report does not say how much code has been changed, whether the trial worked, or whether “rewrite” is measured in lines, modules or behavioural compatibility.

The cost framing is the interesting part: outsourcing is priced in person-months, this is priced in tokens, with no cap. For a networking company, legacy maintenance has long sat in operations and headcount budgets; moving it to a compute budget turns a fixed cost into a variable one.

Why it matters“98% of the code” is an easy number to misread. Legacy code is valuable for behavioural compatibility, not line count: rewriting 98% while breaking one billing or protocol edge case can cost more than the maintenance it saves. The acceptance metrics should be regression pass rate, production incidents and rollbacks, not the share rewritten. Unlimited tokens also move marginal cost from people to compute — a reclassification on the budget sheet, with outsourcing and maintenance down and GPUs and inference up. If the four-week check-in reports only how much was rewritten and not how much broke, the case study is worthless.

Delaware proposes a 30-month sandbox for agent-run companies

TechRadar reports that Delaware plans to loosen business regulation to let companies operated by AI agents register. The draft creates a 30-month regulatory sandbox for these “AI companies” (AICs), during which agents make business decisions inside a supervised environment.

Liability is the core of the draft. An AIC could be sued like any company and could also bring suits. Supporters say this gives autonomous businesses legal certainty; Andrew Baker, a law professor at the University of California, calls the move a compromise in search of legal certainty.

Congress is pushing back. James Comer, chair of the House Oversight Committee, has written to Governor Matt Meyer warning that AICs could shield well-funded AI companies and their investors from liability for reckless and harmful conduct.

This is a proposal, not law; sandbox terms, capital requirements and disclosure duties are still undefined. For AI companies, it would give agent-run entities a corporate shell for signing contracts, opening accounts and filing taxes; for regulators, 30 months is a probation period that can be revoked.

Why it mattersThe question is not whether a bot can incorporate but where limited liability stops. Once an AIC is a separate legal person, tort liability stays inside the shell and victims have a harder time reaching the parent; without the shell, every autonomous decision needs a human backstop and the business case collapses. The 30-month sandbox is a revocable trial for liability isolation. Watch three things rather than the registration count: minimum capital, mandatory disclosure of who controls the agent, and whether a court pierces the veil in the first AIC suit.

Tsinghua’s Algebraic World Model: prove the level is impossible, then finish it

A team led by Andrew Yao and Yang Yuan at Tsinghua University has proposed an “Algebraic World Model” (AWM). The paper is on arXiv (2610.40503) as a preprint and has not been peer-reviewed. The co-first authors are Pan Guancheng of Zhejiang University, who is on leave to research at Tsinghua, and Tan Qixin of Tsinghua’s Yao Class.

The method has three steps: explore, by writing observations into executable Python as provisional hypotheses; distil, by testing and revising those hypotheses against new observations into more general objects and rules; and prove, by formalising the rules in Lean 4 as machine-checkable mathematical claims. The team borrows two tools from category theory, projective and inductive limits, to compose and generalise concepts.

On ARC-AGI-3, AWM running on Opus 5 cleared all 183 levels with a score of 99.9% and used only 43% of the human baseline’s average number of actions. Given a level altered to be unwinnable, the Opus 5.5 version proved in two attempts that it could not be solved at the current step count, and noted that one more step would make it solvable.

The control is more informative. GPT-6 Astra with a provider adapter also scores 99.9%, but it compiles game mechanics into symbolic rules that are essentially Tycho Brahe’s notebook: precise observations without the underlying law. Across 12 impossible-level tests, GPT-6 Astra and Claude Opus 5.5 together recognised “impossible” only 5 times, and 4 of those answers still gave the wrong minimum step count.

Why it mattersThis line of work moves “world model” from predicting the next frame to a system of provable rules. The difference is auditability: a Lean 4 proof can be checked by a machine, so when the model says “impossible” you get either a proof or a visible gap, not a confident sentence. The limits are explicit too — a proof holds only for the world the model observed, and incomplete exploration yields a local truth. ARC-AGI-3 is a closed toy world; moving to open environments requires solving incomplete observation before proof ability. 99.9% is nice; the next metrics are sample efficiency and the cost of proof.

Grok starts watermarking generated text on November 7, across every API region

SpaceXAI says it will add an invisible watermark to text generated by Grok starting November 7. A detector reads the signal to decide whether a piece of text carries the mark. The company says the change responds to the European Union’s AI Act.

The watermark covers SpaceXAI APIs in every region and seven models: grok-4.7, grok-4.6, grok-4.5, grok-4.3, grok-4.20 reasoning, grok-4.20 non-reasoning and grok-4.20 multi-agent. Team administrators can turn the watermark off for an individual model.

The mechanism is worth noting: the mark is not hidden characters or metadata but is woven into the model’s token choices during generation. That leaves visible text unchanged while spending some sampling freedom, and detection requires the corresponding key or model.

Andrew Curran, who tracks AI governance, notes that the only major model without a text watermark is now ChatGPT in North America. The EU’s labelling requirement for generated content is moving from advice to default-on.

Why it mattersA watermark answers “can this text be proven AI-written”, not “should this text be published”. Handing the switch to team administrators pushes the EU’s labelling duty down to enterprise customers: on by default, off if you like, with responsibility transferred. Verification is the bigger issue — if the detector is not public, no third party can check whether a mark exists or was removed. For downstream users, watermarks change the cost of distribution: reposts, rewrites and translations can erode the signal, so the cases that most need labelling become the hardest to identify.

Hongqi ends its robot convenience store after 16 months and ¥2.87 million

In March 2025, Sichuan convenience-store chain Hongqi announced a joint project with a robotics company to build an unmanned robot sales system. The stock hit its daily limit the next day and was about 35% higher by April 23. In July 2026, Hongqi terminated the project, saying the system had not reached the standard for large-scale commercial deployment, and shifted to 24-hour remote staffing.

The risks were spelled out at launch: the arm had a high grab-failure rate with dense shelves and irregular packaging; vision drifted under different lighting; obstacle avoidance had to coexist with stable data transmission. Sixteen months later the prototype never arrived; the termination notice did.

The cost was small: Hongqi’s 2025 annual report shows about ¥2.8668 million in R&D spending, and the company had not previously disclosed R&D, so the project’s spend was probably in that range. A person close to Hongqi told QbitAI that chilled glass bottles sweat in summer and the robot kept dropping them, and that complex packaging was also unreliable; aisles are narrow and customers stand nearby, making the real store far messier than the lab.

The industry keeps expanding anyway: more than 100 robot retail pods or layouts are running in over 30 Chinese cities. A retail robot sells for nearly ¥700,000, and with the pod, operators estimate close to ¥1 million per unit. A pod carries a few dozen SKUs; a convenience store carries 2,000 to 3,000. Practitioners put the timeline for replacing a store clerk at two to five years.

Why it mattersThe lesson is not that robots cannot work; it is that ¥2.87 million was spent validating a million-yuan store model. The failures are long-tail: condensation, soft packaging, narrow aisles, moving customers. Each is small; together they make grasp reliability unusable. Retail is forgiving and highly visible, which is why it became the robotics companies’ “scenario” rather than the retailer’s “employee” — as one operator put it, the robots need the scenario more than the scenario needs robots. Commercial viability has one threshold: payback within a year, or a robot cheaper than a clerk. Until then, these pods are closer to billboards.

Aramco buys 49% of HUMAIN and injects about SAR 9.9 billion

On November 1 local time, HUMAIN, previously wholly owned by Saudi Arabia’s sovereign wealth fund PIF, said Aramco will buy a 49% stake, with PIF keeping the remaining 51%.

The consideration is a transfer of key AI assets plus about SAR 9.9 billion (roughly ¥17.55 billion) in new capital. Both shareholders will contribute AI assets, capabilities and people, and jointly oversee HUMAIN’s expansion.

Aramco frames the deal around industrial use: it plans to use HUMAIN’s AI infrastructure to turn advanced capabilities into industrial applications and improve its own operations. For HUMAIN, that means a sovereign backer plus a customer with real industrial sites.

The 49/51 split matters: control stays with PIF, while Aramco gets economic exposure and a route into industrial deployment. The consideration mixes cash and assets; the asset list and valuation basis were not disclosed.

Why it mattersThis is oil cash providing an internal valuation anchor for compute assets. HUMAIN’s clusters and models need long-term capital, and funding them entirely from PIF would concentrate the sovereign balance sheet; selling 49% to Aramco spreads the AI capex across the oil business and plugs the models into refining and exploration. For Aramco, AI spending moves from cost line to equity stake; for HUMAIN, orders and backing are steadier than a pure fundraising round. The risk is related-party pricing: without disclosure of how assets were valued, utilisation and who absorbs losses, the valuation is an inside number.

Kioxia sells out 2027 capacity and locks more than half of 2028

Japanese outlet ITmedia reports that on Kioxia’s fiscal-2027 second-quarter earnings call (July to September 2026), executive vice president Yoshihiko Kawamura said the company’s 2027 chip capacity allocation is essentially fully assigned and sold out, with every available unit locked up.

Locking the entire following year before it begins is a first for Kioxia. Buyers are already reaching further out: more than 50% of 2028 capacity has firm purchase commitments, and the company has started commercial talks for 2029 and 2030.

The financial response is blunt. Kioxia held ¥1.3973 trillion in cash at the end of September (about ¥59.127 billion yuan); apart from a contingency reserve, it plans to put all of it into growth investment. In June it said average annual investment for fiscal 2026-2028 would be about ¥470 billion, roughly ¥1.4 trillion over three years. For fiscal 2026 it is pulling capex forward from fiscal 2027, with spending expected near ¥600 billion.

On capacity, Kioxia plans a third fab building at its Kitakami plant in Iwate prefecture, due to come online in fiscal 2029. The first two buildings started production in 2020 and 2025.

Why it mattersNAND is turning from a cyclical commodity into an allocation. Buyers used to watch spot prices; now they lock volume a year and a half ahead. Volume commitments usually come with price negotiations, but no prices were disclosed, so “sold out” means tight supply, not necessarily higher prices. For buyers, 2027 being sold out means memory costs for AI servers, phones and PCs are unlikely to fall before 2027. For Kioxia, spending ¥1.4 trillion is a bet on 2028 demand; if AI capex slows, the third fab becomes depreciation. Watch the price of 2028 allocations, and whether HBM expansion cannibalises conventional NAND capacity.
