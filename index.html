看点这不是一份“业绩差”的财报，而是一次估值模型的切换：AI 投入从加分项变成要先回答回报期的负债。接下来每个超大规模云厂商都会被同一把尺子量——资本开支、自由现金流、搜索/广告的变现斜率。

OpenAI 重写 Model Spec：从案例法收束为五条价值观

OpenAI 10 月 29 日发布新版 Model Spec，把原先约百页、案例法式的行为规则，收束为五条价值观：忠实于人类意图、伤害最小化、诚实与透明、促进人的能动性、以关怀行事。每条价值观下仍保留 Root / System / Developer / User / Guideline 的权限层级和红线。

变化在于“规则变薄、裁量权变厚”：平台守住不可覆盖的红线，红线内的大量取舍交给开发者和企业，通过 system/developer 指令去配置。OpenAI 称这份规范已用于训练，但生产模型尚未完全符合。

这与模型从对话走向智能体有关：动作组合无法穷举，只能用高阶原则加权限层级来约束。责任也随之下移——企业需要自己定义风险、审批和回滚。

看点规范从“清单”变成“宪法”，听起来更灵活，实际是把治理成本转移给调用方。真正要看的不是五条价值观写得多好，而是 OpenAI 是否给出可量化、可复现的冲突评估基准。

纽约提出 RAISE Act 2.0：可限制算力、72 小时报告、独立审查员

纽约州参议员 Andrew Gounardes 与议员 Jordan Wright 10 月 29 日提出 RAISE Act 2.0（S.10731），在去年 RAISE Act 基础上加码：大型开发者需按季度报告 AI 研究自动化比例与算力使用，风险或自动化能力出现重大跃升须在 72 小时内报告。

新设的 DIGIT 办公室可在认定灾难性风险时限制训练算力、要求暂停部署；州总检察长可向法院申请叫停。适用范围扩至三年 AI 研发投入超 10 亿美元的公司，算力门槛对齐欧盟 AI Act。

法案还要求企业聘请州执照的独立“前沿审查员”，每年审计合规、每 90 天做灾难风险评估，报告公开；举报人保护写入法律，初犯罚款最高每天 100 万美元，再犯每天 300 万美元。

看点这是美国州层面第一次把“放慢前沿”写成可执行的行政权力：不是等事故后罚款，而是事前限制算力。它会不会被联邦优先权或诉讼挡住另说，但“算力可被监管”这个先例已经立起来了。

Alphabet loses about $500 billion in a day as AI capex meets cash flow

Alphabet fell 12.73% on October 29, its worst single-day drop since its 2004 IPO, wiping out roughly $500 billion in market value. Third-quarter revenue rose 25% to $127.6 billion and Google Cloud revenue doubled to $30.7 billion, but search revenue growth slowed to 12% from 17% a quarter earlier, at about $63.3 billion.

The trigger was cash flow: quarterly capex reached $51.4 billion, free cash flow came in at -$9.1 billion for a second straight negative quarter, far below the -$2.7 billion analysts expected. The company raised its 2026 capex guidance to $195–205 billion and signaled a significant increase in 2027.

Investors are repricing Google from a search cash cow into a high-capex, low-free-cash-flow infrastructure company. AI search gives more complete answers but weakens ad clicks; even rapid cloud growth cannot yet cover a single quarter of capex.

Why it mattersThis is less a bad earnings report than a change in valuation model: AI spending has turned from a bonus into a liability that must answer for its payback period. Every hyperscaler will now be measured with the same yardstick — capex, free cash flow and the monetization slope of search and ads.

Cohere valued at $20.5 billion with backing from Canada and Nvidia

Cohere confirmed on October 29 that it closed the first tranche of a new round at a $20.5 billion valuation, with the full round expected to reach up to $5 billion. The Canadian government committed up to $1.14 billion, and Nvidia and other strategic investors participated.

Why it mattersThe valuation anchor for enterprise AI is shifting from model capability to contract quality. Cohere has backing from both a government and Nvidia, but a $20.5 billion price tag has to be justified by long-term enterprise contracts, not demos.

Why it mattersThe bottleneck in AI infrastructure is moving from the chip to the links between chips. Whoever controls optical components controls the bandwidth and power curve of the next cluster — but the premium and integration costs may arrive before the scarce asset does.

OpenAI rewrites its Model Spec around five values

On October 29, OpenAI published a new Model Spec that condenses roughly 100 pages of case-law-style rules into five values: be faithful to human intent, minimize harm, be honest and transparent, facilitate human agency, and act with care. Each value still sits under a Root / System / Developer / User / Guideline hierarchy with red lines.

The shift is thinner rules and thicker discretion: OpenAI keeps non-overridable red lines, while many trade-offs inside them move to developers and enterprises through system and developer instructions. OpenAI says the spec is already used in training, but production models do not yet fully reflect it.

The change reflects the move from chat to agents: action combinations cannot be enumerated, so higher-level principles plus authority levels have to do the work. Responsibility moves downstream — enterprises must define risks, approvals and rollbacks themselves.

Why it mattersTurning the spec from a checklist into a constitution sounds more flexible, but it shifts governance costs to the caller. The question is not how well the five values are written, but whether OpenAI publishes measurable, reproducible evaluations for conflicts between them.

New York’s RAISE Act 2.0 would cap compute, require 72-hour reports and independent auditors

New York State Senator Andrew Gounardes and Assemblymember Jordan Wright introduced RAISE Act 2.0 (S.10731) on October 29, building on last year’s RAISE Act. Large developers would report quarterly on how much AI research is automated and how much compute they use, and report major jumps in risk or automation capability within 72 hours.

A new DIGIT office could limit training compute and pause deployments when it finds catastrophic risk; the state attorney general could seek a court order to stop dangerous activity. The law would cover companies spending more than $1 billion on AI development over three years, with compute thresholds aligned to the EU AI Act.

The bill also requires a state-licensed independent “frontier examiner” to audit compliance annually and assess catastrophic risk every 90 days, with public reports. Whistleblower protections would be codified, and penalties would reach $1 million per day for a first violation and $3 million per day thereafter.

Why it mattersThis is the first state-level attempt to write “slowing the frontier” into executable administrative power: limiting compute before an incident rather than fining after one. Federal preemption or litigation may block it, but the precedent that compute can be regulated is already set.

AgiBot unveils GO-3 Preview and Lingqu OS 1.0 — and a way to measure robot work

At its A.I.DAY on October 29, AgiBot unveiled the GO-3 Preview embodied foundation model, the WITA-OMNI multimodal interaction model, the AGILE motion-intelligence framework, Lingqu OS 1.0 and the AHA3 hardware architecture. GO-3 uses a predict-then-act decision loop and topped RoboDojo, RoboTwin and RoboColiseum; it is slated for open source by the end of 2026.

Lingqu OS 1.0 is billed as the industry’s first native operating system for embodied AI. It supports 15 categories and more than 100 peripherals, with timing error under 4 microseconds at 1,000 Hz. AgiBot plans to open-source about 200 million lines of code and more than 5,000 ecosystem packages by year-end, and donate the OS to the OpenAtom Foundation.

AgiBot also proposed Worken — active robots × autonomous working hours × output efficiency ratio — as a common yardstick for how much work a robot actually does on the job. Co-founder Peng Zhihui said first-half 2026 revenue was close to 2 billion yuan and cumulative output reached 20,000 units.

Why it mattersThe robotics industry has compared moves and parameters; AgiBot now wants to compare work hours. If customers accept Worken, selling robots becomes selling productivity packages — but only if the data is auditable and output can be verified, otherwise it is just a marketing unit.

WeRide’s Jingshuo raises tens of millions in pre-A for physical-AI data infrastructure

On October 29, WeRide disclosed that its subsidiary Jingshuo Technology raised tens of millions of dollars in a pre-A round from Jiayu Capital, Hong Kong Science and Technology Parks and Sirius Capital. The money will expand R&D and deepen coordination across its data platform, world model and skill-asset engine.

Jingshuo focuses on data and world-model infrastructure for embodied AI and large models, with a core team from WeRide’s physical-AI organization. The company says its order book exceeds several billion yuan, revenue is growing more than 100% year on year, and it serves dozens of world-model and embodied-AI companies.

Jingshuo will remain an independently operated, majority-owned subsidiary of WeRide and stay consolidated. Autonomous-driving companies are spinning out real-world data and engineering capacity to sell to the embodied-AI industry, creating a new infrastructure layer.

Why it mattersThe most expensive part of embodied AI is not the robot body but data from enough of the real world. Jingshuo is productizing a decade of WeRide’s road-test assets, but data licensing, scenario transfer and customer repeat purchase are the real barriers.
