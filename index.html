英伟达把 VLA 的推理文本从动作路径里摘掉：成功率不变，延迟最多降 3.4 倍

英伟达与纽约大学、UC 伯克利、约翰斯·霍普金斯、斯坦福的团队提出 TacitVLA：训练时照常让视觉语言主干生成推理文本，但在注意力掩码里让动作专家看不到这段文本。动作不再等待自回归解码，推理监督仍然塑造主干表示；论文与项目页已公开，代码标注为即将发布。

在 RoboMME、RMBench、LIBERO / LIBERO-Plus 和 PhysicalAI-AV 四个基准上，TacitVLA 的成功率与显式推理版本相当，单次调用延迟最多降低 3.4 倍。LIBERO 上为 147ms 对 495ms，正好卡进 250ms 的执行窗口；闭环驾驶为 127ms 对 269ms。在相同延迟下，它比不做推理训练的同一模型成功率最多高 17.1 个百分点。

绝对成绩：RoboMME 计数任务 82.7%（此前最好 66.8%），RMBench 77.5%（此前 56.5%），LIBERO-Plus 72.9%，PhysicalAI-AV 闭环场景分 52.5%。RMBench 的 Press Button 任务上 74.3%，不做推理的版本只有 7.7%，记忆条目正确率 99.7%。所有延迟数字都是单张 H100 上的均值。

看点这不是让模型「少想」，而是把推理的两种作用拆开：训练时写推理能改善主干，部署时读当前推理只会增加延迟。对做机器人的团队，最小改动是一行 attention mask，不需要新模型或调度器；真正要验证的是长程记忆任务里「动作先出、日志后写」能否稳定，以及跨机型迁移时推理监督是否还划算。
另一个视角延迟从 495ms 降到 147ms，省下的是产线节拍和 GPU 时间；如果每台机器人每天多跑三倍回合，数据回流成本会先于硬件成本成为瓶颈。

架构上，多智能体把实验拆成文献检索、试剂选择、方案设计、代码生成、硬件执行、结果分析和迭代优化；仪器能力被抽象成带自然语言说明和统一代码接口的「原子服务」，模型可以组合设备能力、生成并修正跨设备控制代码。硬件提供微米级定位和微升级液体处理，控制终端基于 OpenHarmony。

Nvidia cuts the reasoning text out of the VLA action path: same success, up to 3.4x lower latency

A team from Nvidia, NYU, UC Berkeley, Johns Hopkins and Stanford built TacitVLA. It still trains the vision-language backbone to generate reasoning text, but an attention-mask change hides those tokens from the flow-matching action expert. Actions no longer wait for autoregressive decoding, while the reasoning supervision still shapes the backbone.

Across RoboMME, RMBench, LIBERO / LIBERO-Plus and PhysicalAI-AV, success matches the explicit-reasoning counterpart while per-call latency falls by up to 3.4x. On LIBERO it takes 147 ms versus 495 ms, inside the 250 ms execution window; in closed-loop driving, 127 ms versus 269 ms. At the same latency as a model trained without reasoning, it gains up to 17.1 points.

Absolute results: 82.7% on RoboMME Counting (previous best 66.8%), 77.5% on RMBench (56.5%), 72.9% on LIBERO-Plus, and a 52.5% scene score on PhysicalAI-AV. On RMBench Press Button it scores 74.3% against 7.7% without reasoning; the ledger is correct in 99.7% of episodes. All latency figures are means on a single H100.

Why it mattersThe result separates two jobs of reasoning: writing it during training improves the backbone, while reading the current text at deployment only adds delay. For robotics teams, the minimal change is one attention mask, with no new model or scheduler. What still needs testing is whether act-first, write-the-ledger-during-execution holds over longer horizons, and whether the supervision still pays off across robot bodies.
Another viewCutting 495 ms to 147 ms buys line throughput and GPU hours. If each robot runs three times as many episodes per day, data return may become the bottleneck before hardware does.

Karpathy builds HackerTube in a weekend: 43m comments distilled into 6,454 videos

Andrej Karpathy used Claude Opus 5.5 to build HackerTube over a weekend, distilling 43 million Hacker News comments from 20 years into 6,454 videos that keep getting recommended there. The site needs no login, shows a single mid-video frame instead of an optimised thumbnail, and stores state locally in the browser.

He says Claude Code handled nearly all the work, from data analysis to server setup; his manual steps were a Google Cloud API key, a rented server and buying the domain. He then used Claude Code to one-shot TristanTube in three minutes, with 47,000 videos from a YouTube channel list.

Why it mattersThe point is not another video site but a cost structure: data cleaning, front end and deployment compressed into a weekend. What is reproducible is the task split and acceptance: let the model turn 20 years of comments into a queryable candidate set, and keep only the domain, key and deploy steps for a human. The hard part is evaluation. Recommendation quality rests on the author's judgement and has no benchmark; the more one-person products appear, the more their build logs need to be auditable.

Shanghai Jiao Tong's AutoDNA lands in Nature: multi-agent autonomous molecular-biology experiments

A team from Shanghai Jiao Tong University's IPADS lab and its School of Chemistry and Chemical Engineering built AutoDNA (Siyuan-1), an AI-native scientific lab that performs molecular-biology experiments autonomously. The work was published in Nature and featured on the journal's homepage.

The architecture splits experiments across agents for literature search, reagent selection, protocol design, code generation, hardware execution, analysis and iteration. Instrument capabilities are exposed as atomic services with natural-language descriptions and a unified code interface, so the model can compose devices and generate and repair cross-device control code. The hardware provides micron positioning and microlitre liquid handling; the control terminal runs OpenHarmony.

The team validated the system on nucleic-acid testing, enzymatic DNA synthesis and cancer detection, and demonstrated end-to-end DNA storage: encoding, writing, storage, reading and decoding. The paper is s41586-026-11228-6.

Why it mattersThe engineering value is the instrument abstraction layer: hiding differences between device models behind a model-callable service interface is what makes cross-device orchestration possible. The failure modes matter more than the demo: drift in micron positioning over long runs, contamination control, and whether a failed experiment can roll back automatically. For lab-automation teams, unify the interface and state machine before letting the model write arbitrary code; one bad call can destroy a batch of samples.

US charges three men over a $129m AI scheme impersonating executives and lawyers

The US Attorney's Office for the Southern District of New York unsealed an indictment against Jacob Saul, Sharon Hazan and Guy Shetrit. Prosecutors say that from 2024 to 2026 they used AI to fake the voices and images of executives and lawyers, persuading employees to wire money, in a scheme worth more than $129m. Saul and Hazan were arrested in Spain this summer; Shetrit was arrested in the UK in September.

The playbook had two steps: impersonate an executive to tell an employee about a sensitive financial transaction, then hand the employee to a fake lawyer at an international firm who gave the wiring instructions. Proceeds were converted to cryptocurrency and split among co-conspirators.

Disclosed victims include a Missouri hospital group that lost nearly $5m, a Rhode Island bank about $2.7m, a California hospital group about $3m, a Massachusetts mutual-fund company more than $4.6m, a Florida pet-food company about $5m and a UK tech company more than $11m. The three are charged with conspiracy to commit wire fraud, conspiracy to commit money laundering and aggravated identity theft.

Why it mattersThis is not a deepfake-detection problem but an authorisation-chain problem: employees were told to bypass normal payment checks because the caller was "the executive" or "the lawyer". The repair order is two-person approval, a callback to a known number and a payment whitelist, not a detection model. For voice and video product teams, provenance and watermarking should be on by default and verifiable downstream.

Co-Design Bench: even the best coding agent fully solves only 26.4% of tasks

Researchers from Alibaba and several Asian universities built Co-Design Bench from real user change requests in coding sessions. Agents must rework existing web apps, sometimes with images, audio, video or documents as input. Across ten models, the best, GPT-6 Astra, meets about 70% of a task's requirements on average but is fully correct in only 26.4% of tasks.

By track, GPT-6 Astra fully solves 41% of multimodal tasks and just under 18% of repository-level tasks. Claude Opus 5 and Qwen3.8-Max follow, with Grok 4.6 last. Every model gets worse when a task combines layout, design, interaction and content changes.

Grading uses two AI judges: one operates the finished app in a browser, the second reviews every positive verdict and looks for mock-ups or hard-coded results. Only features that actually work score. The harness also matters: on larger overhauls, Qwen3.8-27B can fall from 75% to 56% depending on the environment, enough to change the ranking of two models.

Why it mattersNear-perfect scores on older benchmarks no longer say whether an agent can ship. 26.4% is closer to the feel of a real project. Teams buying coding agents should test model and harness as a pair, and split acceptance into "it runs, it is not hard-coded, and multiple change types hold at once". For agent builders, the gains may come less from the model than from specs, rollback and verifiable intermediate artefacts.

Menlo Ventures: 89% of enterprise open-source model spend goes to Chinese models, Kimi takes 61%

A Menlo Ventures report says Chinese models account for 89% of global enterprise spending on open-source models. Moonshot's Kimi leads with 61%, followed by DeepSeek at 15% and Zhipu's GLM at 11%; Meta's Llama, the leading Western model, has only 7%.

In the same report, open-weight models handle only 19% of enterprise token usage, with closed models still dominant; Anthropic and OpenAI together take 86% of spend. The report was covered by the South China Morning Post and relayed by IT Home on November 8.

Why it mattersRead the numbers separately: 89% is a share of open-source model spend, not of total enterprise AI spend; 19% is the open-weight share of tokens. For model vendors, Kimi's 61% says price and Chinese-language use cases are still the main levers. For buyers, concentration is higher on the closed side: Anthropic plus OpenAI at 86% means leverage comes less from the number of models than from portable evaluations and exit costs.
