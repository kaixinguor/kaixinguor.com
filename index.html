DeepMind 用智能体找出 1024 位 RSA 的两处弱点，密码分析提速约 1600 倍

11 月 6 日（美东），Google DeepMind 公布：在人类密码学家指导下，AI 智能体用数周时间找出旧式 1024 位 RSA 的两处重大弱点。一条路径指向绕过旧式数字签名保护，另一条指向批量分解 1024 位密钥。DeepMind 科学与研究副总裁 Pushmeet Kohli 在社交平台公布了这一结果。这是研究公告，不是已经发生的入侵。

数字口径：报道称，同样的密码分析工作比过去的方法快约 1600 倍；两处弱点分别指向旧式数字签名保护与 1024 位密钥分解。研究由 Dj Dvijotham 与 Abhradeep Thakurta 带领，合作方包括加州大学圣地亚哥分校与法国 Inria。DeepMind 明确划了边界：结论不影响 RSA 2048 及更强的现代加密，也没有点名任何在用的生产系统。

另一条可复现的结论关于填充（padding）：一旦攻击者能控制签名数据块约一半以上，填充几乎不再提供额外保护。这一点比「1600 倍」更值得记录，因为它给出了一个阈值，而不是一个平均值。

看点把「读论文—写代码—跑实验」的循环交给智能体，密码分析的迭代单位从年变成周，这是能力变化，不是奇观。要判断可复现性，先看三件事：用的是哪一版模型、多少算力；1600 倍是相对哪条基线、在哪种攻击路径上成立。对下游，1024 位密钥还活在旧证书链、嵌入式固件和没人记得的长期基础设施里，清点比恐慌有用。
另一个视角发现成本降了一到两个数量级，替换成本没降：一把旧密钥背后是一台要停机、重新签发、再验证的设备。真正的时间表由设备清单和运维窗口决定，不由论文决定；这笔迁移预算会落在持有旧基础设施的运营商与公共部门账上。

MIT 给机器人记忆加「不确定性」：92% 的模糊物体能被自己纠正

MIT 团队公布一套名为 UQ-DAAAM 的系统，让机器人判断自己对物体的描述有多可信：它比较同一物体来自不同视角的多份描述，量化不确定性，再主动选择能降低不确定性的新视角，把多次观察合并成更准确的描述，写回机器人的记忆。

它建立在早前的 DAAAM 框架上——用视觉语言模型给机载相机拍到的物体生成描述，存进三维地图表示，供机器人回答问题或执行任务时检索。新系统加的是不确定性量化这一层：测试中，92% 的模糊物体被消解，描述准确率提升 5%。

据 Interesting Engineering 报道，论文与代码的公开链接尚未给出；系统在什么硬件上运行、单次纠正需要补拍几个视角，也还没有对外披露。

看点机器人出错往往不是「看不见」，而是「记错了还很自信」。把不确定性显式化、再用主动视角选择去补证据，等于把视觉语言模型的幻觉问题转成一个可执行的采集策略。5% 看起来小，但它作用在记忆层：一条错误描述会被后续几十步任务继承、放大。真正难的是校准——模型说「不确定」的时候，是不是真的不确定；这决定了补拍策略会不会白跑。

DeepMind agents find two weaknesses in 1024-bit RSA, making cryptanalysis about 1,600 times faster

On November 6 (US Eastern), Google DeepMind reported that AI agents, guided by human cryptanalysts, found two significant weaknesses in legacy 1024-bit RSA in a matter of weeks. One line of work points to bypassing older digital signature protections; the other involves factoring 1024-bit keys in bulk. Pushmeet Kohli, DeepMind's VP of science, announced the result on social media. This is a research announcement, not a live compromise.

The numbers: the report says the same cryptanalytic work now runs roughly 1,600 times faster than previous methods, with the two weaknesses pointing at legacy signature protection and at 1024-bit key factoring. The work was led by Dj Dvijotham and Abhradeep Thakurta, with researchers from UC San Diego and France's Inria. DeepMind drew the boundary explicitly: modern encryption such as RSA 2048 is not affected, and no production system was named.

The more reproducible finding concerns padding: once an attacker controls roughly half or more of the signed data block, padding provides almost no additional protection. That is worth recording because it gives a threshold rather than an average.

Why it mattersHanding the read-paper, write-code, run-experiment loop to an agent moves cryptanalysis from years to weeks; that is a capability shift, not a spectacle. To judge reproducibility, check three things: which model version and how much compute; what baseline the 1,600-times figure is measured against and on which attack path. Downstream, 1024-bit keys still live in old certificate chains, embedded firmware and long-lived infrastructure nobody remembers provisioning. Inventory beats panic.
The other view Discovery got one to two orders of magnitude cheaper; replacement did not. Behind each legacy key sits a device that has to be taken offline, re-issued and re-verified, so the real schedule is set by asset lists and maintenance windows, not by a paper. The migration budget lands on whoever still operates the old infrastructure.

MIT adds uncertainty to robot memory, letting 92% of ambiguous objects correct themselves

A team at MIT has built a system called UQ-DAAAM that lets a robot judge how much to trust its own description of an object. It compares several descriptions of the same object from different viewpoints, quantifies the uncertainty, then actively chooses new viewpoints that reduce it, merging the observations into a better description that is written back into memory.

It builds on DAAAM, an earlier framework in which a vision-language model writes descriptions of objects seen by onboard cameras and stores them in a 3D map representation for later retrieval during questions or manipulation. The new layer is uncertainty quantification: in tests it resolved ambiguity for 92% of objects and improved description accuracy by 5%.

According to Interesting Engineering, public links to the paper and code were not yet available, and it is not yet disclosed what hardware the system runs on or how many extra viewpoints a correction requires.

Why it mattersRobots usually fail not because they cannot see but because they remember wrongly and stay confident about it. Making uncertainty explicit and then choosing viewpoints to gather evidence turns a VLM hallucination into an executable sensing policy. Five points looks small, but it lands on memory, where one bad description gets inherited and amplified over dozens of downstream steps. The hard part is calibration: when the model says it is unsure, is it actually unsure? That determines whether the extra views are worth the motion.

Microsoft winds down its Oracle AI compute lease as older GPUs lose the economics

Bloomberg reported on November 7 that Microsoft is gradually ending its AI compute leasing arrangement with Oracle: it decided not to renew the contracts and has recently begun shutting down some capacity rented from Oracle. People familiar with the matter said the servers Oracle provided use an earlier generation of Nvidia GPUs, and as Microsoft's own specifications changed, that capacity became less useful.

The arrangement was announced in 2023, with Oracle giving Microsoft server access to support a new version of Bing; the full value was never disclosed. Microsoft has since signed large capacity leases with neoclouds including CoreWeave, Nscale and Nebius, committing more than $80 billion to external cloud capacity. The report says some Microsoft managers are unhappy with the cost and delivery delays of the neoclouds.

Oracle said AI infrastructure demand remains strong, including for older GPUs. Its fiscal 2026 cloud revenue was $33.99 billion, of which IaaS was $18.09 billion, up 77%, while free cash flow was negative $23.7 billion. Microsoft declined to comment.

Why it mattersA compute contract loses value with each GPU generation, and that is the hidden cost of leasing versus building: signed at scarcity prices, repriced two or three years later on process node, memory bandwidth and interconnect. Buyers should write migratable capacity specs and exit terms into contracts rather than headline chip counts; sellers still have demand for older cards, but the price anchor has moved. What model teams feel directly is that a fixed budget buys a different amount of useful compute every year.
