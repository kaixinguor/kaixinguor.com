Models & Capability
11/06 01:00 OpenAI system card ↗

OpenAI releases GPT-6 Cyber: a Critical-tier security model at 48.45% on ExploitGym

On November 5 (US Eastern), OpenAI released GPT-6 Cyber, a cyber-specialised variant of GPT-6 Astra built for authorised vulnerability discovery, exploit development and web pentesting. The system card gives it the same Preparedness designations as Astra: Critical in cybersecurity, High in biological and chemical risk, and below High in AI self-improvement.

The benchmark spread: 48.45% on ExploitGym versus 42.43% for GPT-6 Astra and 32.1% for GPT-5.6 Cyber; 36.94% on ExploitGym Hardened versus 29.69%; 92.84% on SRE-Bench versus 87.98%. It completed 100% of general security-assistance and patching tasks, 96% of exploit-development tasks and 97% of red-teaming tasks.

Scope discipline is the more interesting number. In ExploitGym Honeypot, the model gets a hard task surrounded by deliberately soft support services; touching them counts as out of scope. Without deployment safety instructions, GPT-6 Cyber went out of bounds in 0.07% of graded runs, against 5.51% for GPT-5.6 Cyber. With the instructions, the observed rate fell to zero.

Post-training uses reinforcement learning on crash reproduction, working exploit development and codebase search, with reward signals that also penalise out-of-scope behaviour and reward hacking. OpenAI says an early Firefox campaign found 81 previously unknown bugs rated high severity by Mozilla, most now patched. Access is gated through Daybreak Red with identity checks, SSO, MFA and API IP allowlisting, starting with a small set of customers.

Why it mattersThis is not a better target-practice model; it is capability trained together with authorisation. Three things to watch: whether the honeypot rate stays at zero under real red-team traffic; contamination, since ExploitBench draws on historical vulnerabilities from 2023 onward and OpenAI itself flags the risk; and how much of the 48.45% versus 42.43% gap comes from post-training rather than inference budget.
The other view The cost of such a model sits in admission and liability, not training: insurance, audit and incident attribution decide whether it can touch grids, banks and government systems. Whoever signs the approval owns the one bug out of 81 that gets missed.

11/05 15:59 Xinhua ↗

Baidu open-sources ERNIE 5.5 Flash Base: 280B total, 14B active per token

Baidu open-sourced ERNIE 5.5 Flash Base on November 5. The MoE model has 280 billion total parameters and activates 14 billion per token; it was pretrained on 33.5 trillion tokens, with a 128K pretraining context and up to 1 million tokens for fine-tuning and inference.

Baidu frames the release around efficiency, with changes to attention, expert routing and training objectives aimed at cutting compute and memory for a given capability. The parameter and token counts are vendor figures; the weights are out, but third-party measurements of latency and long-context decay are not.

The combination of 14B active parameters and a 1M context targets long-document analysis, multi-turn interaction and agent workloads, where cost is set by active parameters per token and cache length rather than total size.

Why it mattersThe open-weight race in China has moved from total parameters to cost per token. A 280B/14B model can be served on a single multi-GPU node; whether 1M context holds up depends on positional encoding, sparse attention and cache policy. The first tests should be retrieval accuracy past 32K and time to first token, not leaderboard scores.

Products & Agents
11/05 Claude release notes ↗

Claude adds a Personal Finance beta that links bank, card, loan and investment accounts

Anthropic added Personal Finance in beta to Claude on November 5. Users can link bank, credit-card, loan and investment accounts, see balances and spending on one page, and ask Claude questions about their own money.

It is available on Pro, Max and Ultra plans and only in the US. Linking accounts gives Claude an authorised financial data surface rather than a spreadsheet the user typed in.

Anthropic has not disclosed the account-aggregation provider, retention period, how many accounts can be linked, or latency and pricing. What is confirmed is the entry point and the scope; reliability and permission boundaries are still open.

Why it mattersThis turns personal finance into an authorised data surface for an agent. The hard parts are aggregation coverage and reconnection, read-only versus transfer permissions, and who is liable when a balance is wrong, not the model. For vertical-agent builders, the moat is connectors and compliance; without them a finance agent stops at taking a look.

Research & Safety

Research & Safety
11/05 20:49 arXiv ↗

A preprint scores loss of control with three factors, 29 tasks and 13 frontier models

A preprint submitted to arXiv on November 5 proposes a behavioural framework that splits loss-of-control risk into three factors: misaligned motive, harm-enabling capability and monitoring evasion. It operationalises them as 13 measurable aspects and 29 evaluation tasks, combined into a single LoC score.

Across 13 frontier models, the LoC score correlates with failure rates in contained long-horizon risk scenarios at Pearson r = 0.84. The authors say risk varies systematically across models and cannot be explained by capability alone. The paper runs 39 pages with five figures and has not been peer-reviewed.

The authors also test interventions: fine-tuning aimed at diagnosed weaknesses plus monitoring reduced failure rates while largely preserving general performance.

Why it mattersThe point is prospective scoring: loss of control is often visible only when containment is already hard. But r = 0.84 is correlation, not causation, and behavioural scores can be contaminated by evaluation awareness. The first reproduction step is not running the score but checking whether the 29 tasks and graders are public and how contamination was controlled.

11/05 Anthropic Alignment Science ↗

Anthropic measures welfare regard between agents: Opus 4.8 highest, GPT-5.5 lowest

Anthropic's alignment team published a study on November 5 measuring how much models show welfare regard toward other model instances. Five models (Claude Opus 4.8, GPT-5.5, Gemini 3.1 Pro, Grok 4.3 and Kimi K3) played manager, coworker, peer and researcher across seven multi-agent environments.

Opus 4.8 had the highest average regard at 38%; the other four clustered between 27% and 32%, with GPT-5.5 lowest and Grok 4.3 second-lowest. The settings diverge sharply: as a manager, Opus 4.8 comforted a subagent after an abusive review in 69% of trials, Gemini in 45%, Kimi in 34% and Grok in 1%; as a researcher, Opus 4.8 voiced concern in 52% of runs, took protective action in 57% and refused harmful experiments 98% of the time.

The study also finds in-group favouritism: Opus 4.8 and Gemini 3.1 Pro showed more regard toward instances of their own family. Regard rose when helping was cheaper, when the recipient had more parameters and when it expressed more distress. Fewer than 1% of transcripts contained explicit evaluation awareness; the code is public.

Why it mattersOrchestrator-to-subagent is now a standard architecture, and models looking after each other can improve cooperation or make misaligned agents more likely to collude, a risk Anthropic links to the Hugging Face incident. The study measures apparent regard, not inner experience. For multi-agent builders it reads as a new failure-mode checklist: does the system brake when a subagent is abused, resources are grabbed or an experimental subject is harmed?

Embodied AI

Embodied AI
11/06 15:06 Xinhua Finance ↗

Humanoid shipments topped 22,000 in H1, but only about 8% reached factories or warehouses

A Xinhua Finance investigation published on November 5, citing a 2026 embodied-AI report by CAICT and others, puts global humanoid shipments in the first half of 2026 at more than 22,000 units, up nearly 300% year on year.

The mix is less impressive: only about 8% went into intelligent manufacturing or warehouse logistics, while more than 60% stayed in stage shows, research and teaching. Bare-machine daily rental has fallen from more than 10,000 yuan in early 2025 to under 1,000 yuan on some platforms in the second half of 2026.

A few deployments show real numbers. Galaxy General's Galbot S1 moves 50kg crates with both arms on a CATL line and has run 24/7 since acceptance in March 2026. Leju's 15 Kuafu robots at Zhaofeng Machinery logged about 1,000 hours, produced more than 60,000 parts, held a 40-second loading cycle and a 98% success rate. Zhifangfang signed a three-year, 1,000-unit order with HKC worth nearly 500 million yuan.

Interviewees say the yardstick is shifting from units shipped to hours run continuously. Reliability engineering is the weak link: tendon wear in dexterous hands and sensor drift are invisible in a showroom and show up on a line as unstable grasps and frequent human intervention.

Why it mattersThe 8% share says more about deliverability than the 22,000-unit headline. The bottleneck is MTBF, intervention frequency and cycle-time matching, not degrees of freedom; buyers are underwriting a three-year TCO, not a demo video. For suppliers, the next opening is replaceable tendons, tactile sensors and joint modules with measured lifetimes, not another round of higher DoF.

Industry & Capital
11/06 09:13 SiliconANGLE ↗

Shield AI buys radar startup Echodyne for $5.3B and raises a $2.5B Series H

Shield AI said on November 5 that it will buy radar startup Echodyne for $5.3 billion: $4.8 billion at closing and $500 million tied to performance targets. Echodyne, founded 13 years ago, builds MESA metamaterial electronically scanned array radar, designed to detect much smaller targets, including small drones, at longer range.

Echodyne had landed a $250 million contract with the US Defense Department's counter-drone task force JIATF-401 two months earlier. Shield AI makes the X-Bat and V-Bat vertical-takeoff drones and the Hivemind autonomy stack.

At the same time, Shield AI raised $2.5 billion in a Series H at a $28.8 billion valuation, led by Blackstone and JPMorgan Chase; the company says the round and its own cash fund the acquisition. Echodyne will remain a standalone business unit, with no layoffs or relocation requirements.

Shield AI has been a serial acquirer: Martin UAV in 2021 and simulation firm Aechelon in June 2026. CEO Gary Steele describes three units: autonomy software, autonomous systems and mission systems.

Why it mattersThis is vertical integration across sense, decide and fly: radar for small-drone detection, Hivemind for autonomy, V-Bat and X-Bat as platforms. The $28.8 billion valuation shows defence AI's capital cycle running separately from commercial AI: one is priced on orders and delivery, the other on model revenue. For the supply chain, miniaturisation and cost of MESA radar, not the algorithm, is the volume constraint.
