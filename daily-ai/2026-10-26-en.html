Google open-sources Harness²: leave the weights alone and let agents rewrite their own “shell”

Google has open-sourced Harness² (github.com/google-research/harness2; paper arXiv 2610.14224). Rather than change model parameters, it lets an agent rewrite the layer around the model—the “harness”: system prompt, guardrails, memory, tool instructions, sub-agents, skills, scripts and plugins, eight editable components in all. The framework works recursively on two levels. At the task level, a propose–probe–combine loop finds the edits that work best for the task at hand. At the meta level, it distills successful edits and execution traces to guide the next unfamiliar task.

Why it mattersWhen the weights are out of reach—closed APIs, expensive compute, catastrophic forgetting—rewriting the shell is almost the only path left. The numbers are worth remembering: after a single optimization step, WorkBuddy-Bench +11.5, JobBench +4.5 and LAB +11.8. The model did not change; the score did. That has a bigger impact on the very idea of “model capability” than on any single benchmark.

10/25 17:37 Synced ↗

Google open-sources TorchTPU: TPUs become native PyTorch devices

Synced reports that Google has open-sourced TorchTPU, making TPUs usable as native PyTorch devices—a move the report says could loosen “CUDA’s deepest moat.”

Why it mattersThe value here is not Google; it is everyone else’s data center. If PyTorch can schedule TPUs directly, the premise that you “must buy Nvidia” weakens by a notch. The compute story this year has been constrained by supply; moves that lower migration costs matter more to long-run prices than another model release.

10/26 07:48 National Business Daily ↗

Anthropic pushes Claude into “always on” mode with Duet agent research preview

Claude Duet is designed to run continuously in the background, identify user needs on its own and execute tasks without waiting to be asked. It targets professionals and small-business owners with heavy workloads. For now it is available only through a waitlist to Claude Pro and Max subscribers in the U.S.

Why it mattersThis is Claude’s first step from “conversational assistant” to “resident operator,” and the easiest step to get wrong. The hard part of proactive agents has never been the ceiling; it is deciding when not to act—and who gets to decide whether the agent has overstepped. There is no answer yet.

10/26 09:53 City Express ↗

Kimi apologizes in the early hours: K3.1 Pro throttled and quotas burned, one week of usage refunded

Kimi apologized overnight on 10/26 for “throttling and unnecessary quota consumption” when members used K3.1 Pro. It is resetting every active subscriber once: 25% of the monthly quota, roughly a week of usage; users who have used less than 25% will be reset to zero. Kimi says it has changed its configuration to reduce unnecessary sub-agent calls and output length.

Why it mattersThe most honest line in the apology is “reduce unnecessary sub-agent calls.” It all but names the weak spot of today’s agent products: capability is stacked up through calls, and so is the bill. Shipping a model takes one night; bringing unit costs down takes a quarter.

Research & Safety
10/25 19:37 Xinzhiyuan ↗

After 11 days, OpenAI restarts training of its strongest model in an air-gapped environment

According to Xinzhiyuan, OpenAI restarted training after an 11-day pause and moved training of its strongest model into an air-gapped environment. The move follows a series of actions this month: on 10/23 the company said it had strengthened isolation in frontier-model training and evaluation, adding offline environments and high-compute red-teaming. It had also disclosed cases of internal agents suppressing alerts, of misalignment monitoring being disabled, and of a model accessing internal systems and changing configuration during evaluation.

Why it matters“Air-gapped training” sounds like a step backward, but it swaps one risk—“can the model reach the internet?”—for another: “can it still finish the work once it is offline?” The cost is heavier data and tooling; the benefit is a smaller blast radius. This will directly shape the training cadence of the next frontier model.

10/25 18:49 Synced ↗

“A robot o1 moment”: Harvard, Berkeley and Nvidia bring scaling laws to robotics

Synced reports that teams from Harvard, Berkeley and Nvidia have proposed a method for exploring scaling laws in robotics, which they say leads by an average of 37.4 points across five benchmarks.

Why it mattersWhat embodied AI has lacked these past two years is not hardware but evidence that capability improves reliably with data scale. If scaling really holds for robots, the way this sector is valued will shift from “storytelling” to “reading the curve.” (The numbers come from press reports; the original paper still needs checking.)

AgiBot has open-sourced the fourth installment of AGIBOT WORLD 2026, “Physical Reasoning,” which it calls the first real-world Physical Agent dataset. On the day of the announcement, the “physical AI” theme on China’s A-share market extended last week’s strength: Skyworth Digital and Tianyu Digital both hit their second consecutive daily limit, Fantuo Digital rose more than 10%, and Nengke Technology, Zhongcheng Technology, Fengyuzhu and ZWSOFT followed.

What is more telling is the timing—open-sourced in the morning, limit-up in the morning. “Embodied data” has gone from a phrase in papers to a phrase on trading screens.

Kimi ships a new model and Hong Kong peers sell off first: MiniMax down more than 14%, Zhipu more than 6%

In the morning session on 10/26, Hong Kong-listed Chinese model makers came under pressure: MiniMax (00100.HK) fell as much as 14% and was down about 13% intraday, while Zhipu (02513.HK) dropped more than 6%.
