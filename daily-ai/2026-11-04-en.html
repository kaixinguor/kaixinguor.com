Inference provider fal raises $1.3 billion at a $16 billion valuation on $800 million of annualised revenue

On November 3, fal announced $1.3 billion of new funding at a $16 billion valuation, according to Bloomberg. The company hosts and serves image, video and audio generation models behind a single API, and counts studios, advertising agencies and game developers among its customers. It has 185 employees, and the new money goes mainly to compute and hiring.

The structure: Bessemer Venture Partners led the Series F, and partner Talia Goldberg joins the board of Features & Labels Inc., fal's registered name. Nvidia, Sound Ventures, HOF Capital and T. Rowe Price also participated; fal calls Nvidia a strategic investor. Add the $350 million Series E led by Sequoia in April at an $8 billion valuation, and the two rounds come to $1.65 billion. A $140 million round last December valued the company at $4.5 billion, taking total funding past $1.9 billion.

The demand side is blunt. CEO Burkay Gur said the round is this large because demand surged and serving it takes compute: "We hit a point in summer where we were not able to serve our customers and had to rate-limit them." The company post-trains some models to run faster — video has been the focus over the past year — and pays model owners licensing fees or a share of revenue. Pretraining a model of its own is not on the near-term agenda, though Gur did not rule it out later.

On the numbers: annualised revenue passed $800 million earlier this year, according to a person familiar with the matter, which works out to roughly $4.3 million per employee. For scale, rival Fireworks closed $1.5 billion at a $17.5 billion valuation in July. Several people familiar with inference-company finances told TechCrunch that gross margins across the sector stay thin, because compute is expensive to buy or lease.

Why it mattersThe $1.3 billion does not buy a model; it buys a place in the queue. The cost structure is rent compute, sell tokens, and the spread is decided by utilisation: throughput, cache hit rates and batching decide whether margin exists at all. Once demand passes capacity there are only two moves — rate-limit, or buy short-term compute at a premium — and both hurt. $800 million of annualised revenue against a $16 billion valuation is roughly 20x sales, a bet that inference demand stays ahead of supply for years. Three numbers will tell: whether gross margin improves with scale, how far cost per token falls, and how much capacity is owned versus rented.
The other viewThe party that gets paid reliably in this chain is whoever sells the accelerators and rents the racks; an inference provider is the middle layer that absorbs upstream price swings, so its margin compresses first and its fundraising cadence ends up tracking the length of its compute contracts.

11/04 07:19 Wallstreetcn · republished by Eastmoney ↗

AMD's data center revenue nearly doubles to $8.55 billion, with a $17 billion guide for the fourth quarter

After the close on November 3, AMD reported third-quarter 2026 revenue of $13.377 billion, up 45% year on year and 15.5% sequentially — a record quarter, and slightly above the top of its own guidance range. Non-GAAP earnings per share were $2.09, up 74%; GAAP gross margin was 56% and operating income $2.832 billion, up 123%.

The mix has one engine. Data center revenue was $8.546 billion, up 97% year on year and 27% sequentially, or 64% of total sales, with segment operating income of $2.882 billion and a margin near 33.7%. AMD credits two lines: strong EPYC server CPU demand, as CPUs regain weight in training, inference and agent scheduling, and record shipments of Instinct MI450 GPUs.

Guidance is what the market watched: about $17 billion for the fourth quarter, plus or minus 3%, implying roughly 66% year-on-year and 27% sequential growth and ahead of the $16.23 billion consensus. CEO Lisa Su said growth accelerates further in the quarter as rack-scale Helios systems and the sixth-generation EPYC "Venice" CPU ramp.

The cost sits in cash flow: free cash flow fell 64% to $550 million and inventory rose by nearly $3 billion. GAAP R&D spending was $2.803 billion, up about 31%, though non-GAAP operating expenses fell to 27% of revenue. Elsewhere client revenue grew 7%, below expectations, gaming fell 40% and embedded rose 29%.

Why it mattersThe technical takeaway is that the rack has become the unit of account. Helios sells as a whole cabinet — GPUs, CPUs, switching and liquid cooling — so delivery is set by rack integration and power, not by wafer starts. Inventory up nearly $3 billion and free cash flow down to $550 million says the money is sitting in components; if the $17 billion quarter arrives it becomes revenue, and if it does not it becomes a writedown. AMD's guide usually turns before its customers' commentary does, because it is the party being ordered from in advance.

11/04 03:08 ITHome ↗

Apple Intelligence goes live in mainland China with iOS 27.3, putting Alibaba's Qwen inside Siri

On November 4, Apple shipped the iOS 27.3 developer preview to iPhone. ITHome, testing the build, found that Apple Intelligence and Siri are now available on mainland-China devices, with a matching settings page; Apple's China site lists the iPhone 15 Pro and later as compatible.

The model side is the partnership landing. Apple works with Alibaba, and some experiences call Qwen: users in China can reach it from Siri, the writing tools and visual intelligence, covering text and image understanding. Signing in with a Qwen account, or with Apple, is enough — no app switch. Alibaba has said the integration extends across iOS, iPadOS, macOS and visionOS.

The first batch is basic and high-frequency: live translation inside Messages, FaceTime and the Phone app, with face-to-face translation on AirPods 4 and AirPods Pro 2 or later; rewrite, proofread and summarise in the writing tools; visual intelligence that identifies objects and places through the camera; and a Clean Up tool in Photos for unwanted objects.

The boundaries matter. This is a developer preview, not a general release, and the list does not include cross-app actions in third-party apps. The hardware floor is the iPhone 15 Pro, so older devices are out.

Why it mattersThis is localisation of a model, not a model of Apple's own. Apple hands part of semantic understanding, writing and image understanding to Qwen while keeping the system entry point, the privacy story and the orchestration — an admission that, in Chinese, an outside model's marginal return beats building it in-house for now. For developers the question is the call path: whether system-level access comes with an API, with quotas and with a billing model. If only Siri and the writing tools can reach it, third-party apps inherit a capability gap that becomes a product gap. The hardware floor also decides how large this release really is.

Products & Agents
11/04 03:03 Fortune ↗

Grok Bot reaches 800,000 paid weekly users as Musk calls serving costs "unsustainably high"

On November 3, Fortune reported that SpaceX president Gwynne Shotwell told analysts on the October 20 earnings call that Grok Bot, the company's AI agent, had 800,000 paid weekly users in early October, double the September figure, with businesses the fastest-growing category across IT, sales, marketing and engineering.

The cost side answered quickly. Replying on X to a user whose allowance ran out four days early, Musk wrote: "The cost of serving Grok Bot was unsustainably high relative to revenue." The report says quotas were subsequently cut. Grok Bot launched in August and handles multi-step work across apps and websites: browsing, drafting email, processing invoices.

The mechanism is simple. Chatbots are billed per prompt; agents are billed per step — reading a page, calling a tool, checking a result — so tokens and tool calls scale with task length. Heavy users cost more while the subscription price stays put. Meta's Muse hit the same wall: 10 million users since launching on September 8, with Zuckerberg telling analysts in October that running it is expensive and that he expects a tenfold improvement in compute efficiency within a year.

The financial backdrop: SpaceX's Super Intelligence division booked $7.56 billion of third-quarter revenue, up from $813 million a year earlier, and $431 million of operating profit against a $1.7 billion loss. But AI capital spending in the quarter was $16.4 billion — more than twice the division's revenue and 84% of the company's $19.6 billion of total capex.

Why it mattersThis is the first public stress test of the agent business model. Subscriptions assume most users never hit the ceiling; agents break that assumption, because a heavy user does not ask a few more questions, he asks the model to run dozens of steps for him. There are three exits — usage-based pricing, quotas written into contracts, or a step cost low enough for the subscription to cover. Each changes the product: metering makes trial-and-error expensive, quotas force caching and pruning, and cost reduction goes back to inference optimisation. For anyone building agents, the useful datum is the ratio: 800,000 paid weekly users is enough to make the economics break.

11/04 02:42 TechCrunch ↗

Netflix ships an AI Discover tab that picks titles by mood

On November 3, Netflix announced an "AI Discover" tab in its TV app as a home for experimental discovery features. The first one groups titles into themes chosen by mood, viewing preferences, how much time you have and what is new or trending — "something light", "scare me", "family movie night", "quick watches" — and serves matching recommendations once a theme is picked. Users can narrow the theme further or hit "something different" to start over.

Every title carries a "Tune Your Recommendations" button next to Play and My List, so a suggestion can be saved or a show excluded from future ones. Netflix says the tab runs on a new intelligence layer it has been building that responds to what members say in real time and keeps learning, and calls AI Discover its "test kitchen" — a place to try recipes before deciding what graduates into the main app.

The timing is part of the story: Netflix recently cut about 1,000 jobs as growth slowed and viewing time fell. Replacing endless scrolling with a handful of themes is a bet on reducing the cost of deciding.

Technically, this puts content understanding and user intent on one path: the themes are human-defined buckets, while ranking and explanation come from a model, and likes and blocks feed back into the online loop, so theme buckets absorb part of the cold-start problem.

Why it mattersThe interesting change is the interface, not the recommendation quality: swapping a search box and a poster wall for a few mood labels is an admission that users often do not know what they want, and that a model can collapse a vague intent into a shortlist. For recommendation teams this pattern is copyable — use an LLM for intent, keep classic retrieval and ranking underneath, then close the loop with feedback. The real tests are cold start and legibility: who defines the buckets, how fast a new title enters one, and whether negative feedback slowly narrows what you are shown.

11/04 05:07 ABC News · Associated Press ↗

Google ties Search and Gemini to one switch: schools that reject its AI lose Search

On November 3, Google confirmed that students using its software in class will lose access to its search engine unless their school system also opts into its AI, the Associated Press reported. The default-on approach Google took in August is gone: K-12 schools can now switch Gemini, Gemini Notebook and Search on or off individually, with everything off until an administrator turns it on.

The switches are not independent. Because search is integrated with AI, a school that opts out of AI altogether also loses Google Search — which means keeping the search box requires keeping the AI. "We believe schools should have control over AI in their classrooms and in providing administrators with controls to help them manage their approach," Chris Phillips, Google's vice president and general manager of education, said in a blog post.

Context: in August, Google turned its Gemini chatbot on for many K-12 students using Classroom, having previously allowed it only for users 18 and older. The move drew pushback from educators, child-development experts and parents. Google says 150 million students and teachers use Classroom worldwide, and many students work on school-issued Chromebooks.

Google also said it is making its search tools "better for learning", including step-by-step guided learning so students cannot simply pull an answer out of the box. Criticism from educators and policymakers followed quickly.

Why it mattersThis turns product coupling into a procurement term. Some coupling is real — an AI summary is now part of the results page, so disabling the model removes that block — but pushing it to "no AI, no search" is a packaging decision, not an architectural constraint. For enterprise product teams it is a cautionary example: defaulting features on buys adoption quickly, and the exit cost gets externalised onto the customer, then returns as regulation and press. The engineering question sits between the two: can you ship a model-free results page, and how much worse is it?

A Nature review defines "modern analog computing": matrix maths on memristor arrays, and where precision runs out

On November 3, npj Unconventional Computing published the review "Modern analog computing for solving differential and matrix equations", by Zhong Sun of Peking University and Piergiulio Mannocci, Manuel Le Gallo and Abu Sebastian of IBM Research Zurich.

The paper separates modern analog computing from its mid-century predecessor and names three core primitives: solving differential equations, solving matrix equations and performing matrix-vector multiplication. It walks the hardware from discrete components through integrated circuits to resistive-memory arrays — RRAM and other memristive devices — and surveys recent progress on CMOS analog circuits and resistive arrays.

Why those three: the dominant workloads in AI and 6G are large matrix operations, and a resistive device's conductance can be rewritten by voltage pulses and then holds its value, which stores a weight as a physical quantity so that Ohm's and Kirchhoff's laws do the multiply-accumulate. Qubit's write-up offers the comparison: a 65nm CMOS analog chip in 2016 fit four integrators and eight multipliers, while a 1000x1000 resistive crossbar can pack a million multipliers at high density.

The paper does not dodge the hard parts. Precision, scalability, the relationship to in-memory computing and the computational complexity of analog machines each get their own discussion. Being a review, it collects existing results rather than reporting new experiments.

Why it mattersRedefining analog computing around matrix operations moves it out of the "solving differential equations, retro edition" drawer and into the slot marked alternative compute for AI inference. For people serving models, three engineering numbers decide the story: effective bit width (can it hold 8 bits and up, reliably), array size (yield and IR drop set the ceiling), and the energy share taken by ADC and DAC — an array can compute as fast as it likes, and the converters will eat the savings. The value of this review is shared vocabulary and boundaries, not a timeline for replacing GPUs.

11/03 21:33 Shanghai Observer ↗

Agibot runs six humanoids for 24 hours in a convenience store: 1,730 tasks, four made the cut

On the morning of November 3, six Agibot G2 humanoids finished a 24-hour shift in a working convenience store in Shanghai. Shanghai Observer reports 1,730 tasks completed across the six: 683 customer receptions, 369 item pickups and deliveries, 147 coffees made, 126 breakfast services, 277 restocking and patrol rounds and 128 table cleanings. More than two million people watched the livestream, and four of the six passed the trial.

The roles covered a store's main flow — greeting and enquiries, stocking and shelf patrol, coffee, checkout and hot food, table cleaning. Assessment was not just throughput: hours on station, customer feedback, success rate, response time and exception handling all counted. Two guest testers set problems on site, putting regular and sugar-free cola side by side and asking for the sugar-free one, scrambling the gum shelf to see if it could restore it, and standing in the aisle as a roadblock to see whether it went around or walked into them.

Some details reveal the approach: fetching a cola requires a double check — "ears understand, eyes confirm"; shelf restoration does not rely on label coordinates but on visual inventory cell by cell; the robot stops for people and then routes around them, and does the same for a box in the way. The two that did not pass were described as having minor problems with initiative and stability.

The day before, on November 2, Agibot launched the GO-3 Preview embodied model, claiming full-task success in a real store rising from 28.6% to 85.7% over five days. This was a public stress test of the same deployment path.

Why it mattersThe 24-hour stream is a stress test and a measurement test. 1,730 tasks sounds like a lot until you divide: six robots over 24 hours is about 12 tasks per robot per hour, and many are short — greeting, carrying, wiping — while the long, fine work such as coffee and hot food is a smaller share. Success rates were not broken out by task type, and four-out-of-six is an employer's judgement, not a statistic. What other embodied teams should copy is the assessment design: putting hours on station, exception handling and customer feedback into the criteria is closer to deployment than beating a simulator score. Next questions: can it repeat in a store with no camera crew, and who pays the takeover cost when it fails?

11/03 13:16 QbitAI · content provided by Xiaomi ↗

Xiaomi's embodied foundation model wins a Wuzhen award, and a humanoid tries a full eight-hour factory shift

On November 3, Xiaomi said that at the 2026 World Internet Conference in Wuzhen, the Xiaomi group received the "Outstanding Contribution" award in the innovation category, while its robotics unit's "general robot manipulation foundation model for scaled deployment" took the "Leading Technology" award. According to Xiaomi, more than 230 companies applied for the contribution award and 15 won; the technology award drew more than 430 achievements from 43 countries and territories, with 20 finalists.

On the model side, Xiaomi says its latest embodied foundation model, Xiaomi-Robotics-1, was pretrained on 100,000 hours of real-world manipulation data, is the first in China to systematically validate a scaling law for embodied intelligence, and ranks first globally on RoboCasa365 and RoboDojo. Both claims are vendor self-assessments and have not been independently reproduced.

In the factory, a humanoid took on an eight-hour shift for the first time at the centre-console side-panel sequencing station in a Xiaomi car plant, meeting the station's full-day production demand with autonomous error correction and line-level coordination. The timeline: it first entered the plant in March, working three continuous hours on a self-tapping nut station with 90.2% two-sided installation success at a 76-second takt; by July that station reached 98%, and two more complex stations were added.

At home, an agent system built on Xiaomi-Robotics-1 began a trial in a Xiaomi staff apartment, completing a chain of more than 30 continuous minutes across three rooms and more than 50 tasks: preparing tools, tidying sofa and table, clearing rubbish, sorting and returning objects, inspecting rooms, a whole-home recheck and exit.

Why it mattersThe structure is the story: one base model, two arenas. The factory station is about takt and repeatability — 76 seconds, then 90.2% to 98%, a measurable ramp. The apartment is about long horizons and generalisation — 30 minutes, three rooms, more than 50 tasks — which tests recovery from failure rather than single-point accuracy. Sharing one pretrained model is a bet that manipulation data transfers across scenes. But the 100,000 hours and the first-place rankings are the company's own numbers; the test is whether the eight-hour shift runs for a month, and how many of those 50 tasks survive a change of apartment.
