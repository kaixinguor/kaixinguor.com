11/18 Google · Introducing Gemini 4 Neon ↗

Google ships Gemini 4 Neon: 61 on the Intelligence Index, 17% cheaper per task than Carbon and 40% faster

Google DeepMind released Gemini 4 Neon on November 18 local time, the fourth Gemini 4 model in seven weeks according to Artificial Analysis. At its xhigh setting Neon scores 61 on the firm’s Intelligence Index, one point above Gemini 4 Carbon at high and third overall, behind GPT-6.2 Astra (max) and Claude Fable 5.2 (max).

Three numbers describe what kind of upgrade this is. Cost per task is $6.44 against $7.76 for Carbon at list price, about 17% less, and a task takes roughly 21 minutes instead of 36, about 40% shorter. Yet Neon spends about 15% more output tokens per task, so neither gain comes from writing less. The price is $3 for input and $15 for output per million tokens, 25% under Carbon’s standard $4/$20, and the speed comes from roughly 110 output tokens per second against 57. Cached input is billed at $0.15, a 95% discount.

Coding agents are where it closes most of the gap. In the Antigravity CLI, Neon at xhigh scores 59 on the Coding Agent Index, three points above Carbon and level with Claude Fable 5.2 (58) and Claude Opus 5.5 (58), at $6.86 per task against $12.61 and $15.47 for the two Anthropic models. Visual work improved too: 33.4% on GDP.pdf at high, five points above Carbon; 1,700 Elo overall on AA-Briefcase with 1,771 for presentation quality, 45 points ahead of the previous leader, Claude Sonnet 5.5; and a Climb win rate of 56% on Google’s internal MKA evaluation.

On specifications: a one-million-token context, text, image, video and speech input with text output, and four effort levels — low, medium, high and xhigh, the last introduced with Hydrogen last week. It is live through Google’s API and the Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform, and Pro and Ultra subscribers get it in Antigravity and the Gemini app.

Why it mattersFor anyone building agents, cost per task is closer to the real invoice than price per million tokens. Half of Neon’s 17% gap comes from price and half from speed, and the two mean different things: price sets the marginal cost of long context and retries, while speed decides how many rounds and timeouts a task needs before it converges. The second thing to question is the effort dial. On the same suite, Neon scores 33.4% on GDP.pdf at high but only 28.0% at xhigh, so the levels are not monotonic. Sweep all four against your own tasks before shipping rather than assuming more effort is better. Third, do not benchmark against Carbon’s $2/$10: that is a discount that expires on November 30.
The other angleAnthropic priced its IPO at $83 a share the same day, near a $1.5 trillion valuation. Model prices are falling while capital-market valuations rise, and both shorten the window in which a closed model can be benchmarked on price. Buyers walk into Q4 with cheaper tokens and a bigger story, which hands negotiating leverage back to them.

11/18 12:55 Phoenix Tech ↗

Tencent open-weights Hy4 flash in three variants: 220B total parameters, 12B active per token, a 1M-token window

Tencent’s Hunyuan team opened the weights of the Hy4 flash family on November 18. Hy4 flash is the instruction-tuned model, Hy4 flash-FP8 an FP8-quantised build meant to cut the compute and memory needed to serve it, and Hy4 flash-base an un-tuned pretrained model. Weights and deployment notes are on GitHub, Hugging Face, ModelScope and GitCode for local deployment, under the Tencent Hy License.

The published figures: about 220 billion total parameters, of which roughly 63.5 billion are Engram parameters, with about 12 billion activated per token. It is a mixture-of-experts model taking text and images in and returning text, with a context window of up to one million tokens. The architecture adds hybrid attention, LatentMoE and Engram, plus a DFly2 module for speculative decoding; Tencent says decoding exceeds 150 tokens per second, a figure from internal testing.

The timeline matters: Hy4 flash launched on November 16 and until now was reachable mainly through Tencent Cloud’s TokenHub and some Tencent products. The weights are what appeared today. Tencent says the rest of the Hy4 line is being prepared for release as well.

Why it matters220B total with 12B active is the classic big-capacity, small-activation trade: the floor for local deployment is set by activation size and KV cache, while weight volume follows total parameters, and the two together decide whether it fits on one card. The 63.5 billion Engram parameters are about 29% of the total; table-lookup parameters like these barely touch per-token compute but weigh heavily on file size, which is exactly what the FP8 build targets. Quantisation usually damages long-context recall before it damages short prompts, so test that yourself at the full window. Three things worth verifying: real recall at one million tokens, the actual speedup from DFly2 on your own workload, and what the Hy License permits commercially. For teams adapting domestic accelerators, open weights matter more than an API, because they let you retune scheduling in your own inference stack.

Products & Agents

11/18 20:33 IT Home ↗

DeepSeek folds server-side search and page fetching into token billing, ending the separate fee from noon on November 18

DeepSeek’s pricing page now says that from 12:00 Beijing time on November 18, 2026, server-side search and web fetching triggered by a model call are no longer billed separately; only token usage is charged. Three surfaces trigger that search: the web-browsing tool, the web_search tool in the Anthropic-compatible API, and the web_search tool in the Responses API.

The contrast is with last month. On October 17 DeepSeek launched Search and Fetch APIs, both running on its own search engine and billed per request at ¥5 per 1,000 successful calls. Search took a query and returned structured results; Fetch pulled a single page and returned it as Markdown with title and body. The same “look something up, then answer” flow that produced two bills, a request fee plus a token fee, now produces one.

Why it mattersThis changes cost structure, not price. A request fee is fixed and does not care how long the page is; a token fee moves with how much text you read. For most agents a search brings back several long pages, and the token side dominates a fraction-of-a-cent request fee, so folding one into the other is probably not a price rise. For the lookup-heavy, read-light case — polling exchange rates or inventory all day — per-request billing was cheaper. What genuinely improves is predictability: token usage can be estimated client-side, request counts cannot. Anyone maintaining an agent cost model should rebuild the retrieval line, and watch how many pages the browsing tool fetches by default and how deep it crawls, because that is the real numerator of the bill.

Research & Safety

11/18 OpenAI · Advancing chip design with our models ↗

OpenAI says its models cut Jalapeño vector-unit area by about 13%, and is applying the method to AMD hardware

OpenAI published a technical report on November 18 local time detailing how its models took part in chip design. The company started using GPT-4o on Jalapeño, its first custom inference chip, in early 2025. In the vector unit the models reworked floating-point adders, multipliers, conversion functions, reduction logic and the special-function unit, exploring alternative logic implementations and how to divide computation across pipeline stages to share hardware and shrink intermediate storage. In the matrix unit, gains accumulated across floating-point adders, multipliers and low-precision dot-product circuits.

Two single changes show the scale. One multiplier redesign used about 15% less cell area in synthesis while cutting latency from two clock cycles to one; a separate adder redesign took about 15% off area while keeping its two-cycle latency. Across the effort, the team estimates model-proposed changes reduced standard-cell area by about 13% in the vector unit and 10% in the matrix unit, and those optimisations were proven during Jalapeño’s silicon bring-up.

The same approach is being carried over to third-party hardware: the report's introduction says the team is applying it with AMD to deliver significant gains on hardware outside OpenAI's own line. To measure end-to-end design ability, OpenAI also ran successive model generations against two open-source processor designs. The next step is a partnership with Synopsys to bring the models into the EDA tools engineers already use to design and verify chips.

Why it mattersThis is not AI drawing circuits; it is a model doing local search between RTL and synthesis, judged by the old standard of power, performance and area, with silicon bring-up as the final arbiter — which is what makes the report harder than most demos. For chip teams the reproducibility question sits in the evaluation: redesigning a processor from a spec on two open-source cores is not the same as winning on a production target, and the report itself notes a model may drop things like performance counters. The part worth copying is the workflow — putting the model inside the existing EDA and verification loop rather than letting it emit a design alone. The third signal is Synopsys: once models sit inside commercial EDA, the change shifts from research to licensing, and the pricing power of the big three gets renegotiated.

11/18 21:04 Securities Times · People’s Finance News ↗

China and Singapore hold AI governance talks in Beijing: standards, safety and risk, international cooperation

China’s Cyberspace Administration and Singapore’s Infocomm Media Development Authority (IMDA) held an AI governance exchange meeting in Beijing on November 18, according to the CAC’s own WeChat account. Officials from the relevant departments discussed AI governance policy, standards and practice, AI safety and risk, and international cooperation on AI governance, and reached a consensus on deepening cooperation.

The readout lists no deliverables and no date for a next round. The useful background is that the two sides use different instruments: Singapore works through IMDA-led frameworks and testing regimes, China through CAC-led filing, security assessment and content governance. The three agenda items are precisely where those two toolkits meet.

Why it mattersAI governance is moving down from multilateral forums to bilateral ones. Multilateral venues produce principles, but implementation depends on whether two countries’ filing requirements, testing methods and incident-reporting channels can connect. For a team deploying models in both places, the practical value of a dialogue like this is whether one safety assessment can be reused on the other side — and that answer normally lives in technical annexes, not in a readout. Two things to watch: whether a working group and a recurring meeting are established, and whether any mutual recognition or joint testing appears. Without those, the consensus stays at the level of statements.

Embodied AI

11/18 21:10 China Securities Journal ↗

Pony.ai posts ¥299m in third-quarter revenue, up 75.2%, as robotaxi reaches ¥119m and the fleet hits 2,768 cars

Pony.ai reported third-quarter results on the evening of November 18: revenue of ¥299 million, a record for the period, up 75.2% year on year and 23.1% quarter on quarter, growing faster than costs. Gross profit rose 69.5% year on year. The company credits its co-built fleet model, which expands vehicle deployment while adding recurring technology-licensing revenue.

The mix is shifting. Robotaxi revenue was ¥119 million, up 165.4% and 39.9% of the total, the main growth engine. Robotruck brought in ¥120 million, up 76.3%, with port haulage and urban light-truck projects continuing to land, and smart solutions contributed ¥59.4 million. The robotaxi fleet stood at 2,768 vehicles at quarter-end, and the company aims to deploy 3,500 across 20 cities worldwide by the end of the year.

Why it mattersThe 39.9% line matters more than the 75.2%. Revenue is moving from selling solutions to running operations plus licensing technology: the first is priced per project and swings with delivery, the second spreads over fleet size and earns margin from density. Going from 2,768 to 3,500 means adding 732 vehicles in one quarter. A co-built fleet pushes capital spending onto partners at the cost of sharing per-vehicle revenue, so scale does not translate one-for-one into profit. What decides whether the business stands up are two undisclosed numbers: rides per vehicle per day, and the ratio of remote safety operators to cars. If neither improves, 3,500 cars just multiplies the cost base by 26%.

11/19 06:07 Fortune ↗

Anthropic prices its IPO at $83 a share: a roughly $1.5 trillion valuation and at least $68.5 billion raised

Anthropic priced its initial public offering at $83 a share on November 18 local time, above the $70–78 range it had guided, valuing the company at roughly $1.5 trillion. That would make it the second-largest IPO on record, behind SpaceX’s $1.77 trillion listing in June. The stock begins trading on Nasdaq on November 19 under the ticker ANT.

On size, the company had planned to sell about 825 million shares, which at $83 implies at least $68.5 billion raised. Fortune, citing Bloomberg, reported that Anthropic also increased the size of the offering on strong demand, without saying by how much. Seven strategic partners, including Google and Nvidia, agreed to buy $26 billion of non-voting stock at the offer price. It is also the first pure-play frontier model maker to list: SpaceX also builds rockets, and Google and Meta both have search and social cash flows underneath them.

The filing’s numbers: revenue of $33.2 billion for the nine months to September 30, against $2.5 billion a year earlier, and a $104 billion net loss, most of it accounting for convertible notes rather than cash leaving the business. The two things investors have watched for two years — the duration of its compute commitments and enterprise renewal rates — have never been priced in a public market.

Why it mattersAnnualising $33.2 billion of nine-month revenue puts the $1.5 trillion valuation at roughly 34 times sales. In an industry where inference costs fall every year, that multiple requires revenue to grow faster than unit prices decline. The first step in reading the accounts is separating the $104 billion loss: fair-value losses on convertible notes do not consume cash, and what matters is operating cash flow plus the maturity schedule of compute contracts, which is the rigid spending of the next three years. The second variable is the pricing environment of the same day, with Google cutting Gemini 4 Neon to $3 and $15 per million tokens, which redefines the margin band for closed models.
The other angleThe $26 billion strategic purchase overlaps customers with shareholders: Google and Nvidia both supply compute and buy stock at the offer price. Those related-party deals will be a disclosure focus in the first quarterly report, and for public markets they put the loop of AI infrastructure money — whose pocket it leaves and whose it returns to — into a public filing for the first time.

11/18 13:26 DigitalToday ↗

Nvidia stops selling H200 to China, confirmed by interim CEO on the earnings call, and adds a board committee on export controls

Nvidia has decided to stop selling H200 chips to China, according to DigitalToday citing TechCrunch on November 17 local time. Interim CEO Colette Kress confirmed the move on the earnings call, effectively ending a contested exception under which H200 sales to China ran through US transshipment, an arrangement brokered between former CEO Jensen Huang and the Trump administration with a 25% US tariff on the transactions.

The same day, Nvidia said it had created a board committee to oversee national security policy, export-control compliance and global trade risk. The policy side moved first: on November 16 Washington issued rules barring shipments of advanced US chips to overseas data centres that serve Chinese customers, closing the route through foreign facilities.

The cost is already being estimated. Jefferies expects the new rules to cut Nvidia’s data-centre revenue by $44 billion to $62 billion next year. Kress’s response was that demand remains unmet, including for Vera Rubin, and that overall revenue should not be affected; but potential customer losses in Southeast Asia — Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand and Indonesia — are flagged as a medium-term variable. Nvidia’s Singapore subsidiary reported $104 billion of revenue in 2025; the company has not disclosed the figure since.

Why it mattersThe point is not that one chip is withdrawn. It is that the basis for judging China’s compute supply moves from “policy allows it” to “the company’s compliance team allows it”. The H200 transshipment route with its 25% tariff turned political risk into a calculable cost; after the halt that cost can no longer be calculated, so buyers plan around absence — a harder constraint than any export list. For teams building training clusters, the near-term effect is second-hand H200 and rental prices, and the medium-term effect is how fast domestic accelerators and neoclouds substitute. For Nvidia, a $44–62 billion hole has to be filled elsewhere, and a board committee on national security says it now treats export compliance as a long-run operating variable rather than a quarterly disturbance.
