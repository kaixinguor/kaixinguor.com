Models & Capability

模型与能力
11/24 20:34 Sina Tech ↗

XPENG unveils WorldGPT: one cloud teacher model covers six bodies on land, water and air, planning the action before rolling the world forward

On the evening of November 24, XPENG introduced WorldGPT, a "world-action model" and the newest member of its Turing world-model family. Within XPENG's physical-AI stack it acts as a cloud teacher model: given past frames and an instruction, it plans the action, predicts how the world will change as a result, and carries out the reasoning and decision in language.

XPENG says it is the first world model to cover six bodies across land, water and air in a single model, handling both prediction and action planning. The industry norm has been one stack per robot — one system for a car, another for an arm, a third for a drone. More body types mean higher cost and more fragmented data, and the hard part is that actions cannot be shared directly between machines.

WorldGPT's answer is to train a larger cloud model across bodies to extract shared world knowledge and general action intuition, then distil a small local model for each body, balancing general capability against on-device deployment. XPENG says supporting a new body used to cost roughly as much as starting from scratch — collect data, train, tune deployment, redo the whole chain — whereas now a new body only has to specialise on top of the base and calibrate control details with a little of its own data.

XPENG says the model "leads" in both driving and robot manipulation, but published no benchmark, success rate or comparison set.

看点Three numbers would settle the cross-body claim: how large the teacher is, the parameter count and latency of the distilled student, and whether failure modes are consistent across the six bodies. If what is shared is only a visual representation, this is a better vision backbone; only if the action space is aligned too is it "one model, many machines". Today there are no weights, no API and no third-party benchmark, so "first" is a vendor claim rather than a reproducible result.
另一个视角Going from one stack per robot to one stack for many spreads the fixed R&D cost per body type, but training the cloud teacher and collecting its data remain large one-off outlays. How many devices the cost can be spread across decides whether this ends up a project business or a platform one.

11/24 17:00 Jiemian News ↗

Tencent Hunyuan open-sources Hy MT3, a third-generation translation model starting at 1.8B and aimed at devices

On November 24, Tencent Hunyuan released and open-sourced Hy MT3, its third-generation translation model, in three sizes — 1.8B, 7B and 30B-A3B (a mixture-of-experts) — aimed at on-device scenarios spanning PCs, phones and smart devices.

The model is already live in Tencent's Hy Translate app and mini-program, and the open weights can be downloaded and deployed locally. Hunyuan says it covers 102 languages, according to Tencent News.

The division of labour between the three sizes is straightforward: 1.8B fits on a phone or an embedded device, 7B covers PCs and workstations, and 30B-A3B uses sparse activation to bring active parameters per token down to the 3B class in exchange for quality on the server side. Translation is now a standalone open-source product line for Hunyuan, three generations in.

看点Translation is the most obviously quantified on-device task: latency measured in milliseconds, quality comparable through BLEU and COMET, and a language count you can actually count. MoE is a pragmatic fit — translation traffic is short utterances at high concurrency, so a small active parameter count means more requests per card. The numbers to watch next are post-quantisation memory, time to first token, and how many of those 102 languages are "usable" rather than merely "supported".

AGIBOT's LinkSoul platform marks one year: 20,000 users and 30,000 robot agents, with a "one sentence, one agent" pitch

On November 24, AGIBOT reported the first-year results of LinkSoul, its platform for building and running robot agents: more than 20,000 cumulative users and more than 30,000 robot agents created.

LinkSoul targets developers, enterprises and industry users, offering agent creation, knowledge and skill configuration, task orchestration, debugging and application connectors. AGIBOT says users can create a robot agent in natural language.

The figures count agents created, not agents in service: how many of the 30,000 are still running, on how many real machines, and for how long without failure is not disclosed.

看点The hard part of a robot agent was never building one, but keeping it running: task orchestration has to land on a specific body's degrees of freedom and workstation, and debugging has to reproduce real occlusion and error inside a 3D environment. Thirty thousand creations show the entry barrier is low; the numbers that decide whether the platform can charge a subscription are weekly active agents and long-run stability, and neither was published.

Research & Safety

11/24 UK NCSC ↗

UK NCSC says AI-assisted attacks have risen markedly since September, with red-team frameworks pointed at real targets

On November 24, three officials at the UK's National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC) wrote that they have observed a marked rise in AI-assisted cyberattacks since September, with organisations across multiple sectors reporting incidents as a result.

Per the post, attackers are using "agentic offensive frameworks" — software originally built for red-team exercises and applied beyond its defensive purpose — combined with cheap, capable open models or illegally resold APIs. The pattern is rapid, automated vulnerability discovery followed by attempts against every internet-facing asset a target owns in quick succession. Victims ranged from service outages to full system compromise.

The post is signed by David Chismon, chief technology officer for architecture, alongside incident handler Kris B. and Ellen T., lead data scientist on the vulnerability management team. They stressed that none of the vulnerabilities or techniques are new, that most can still be patched, and that nearly everything the NCSC has seen can be prevented with basic security practice, zero trust and properly applied technical controls.

In reporting the same day, CyberScoop quoted CrowdStrike principal intelligence analyst Eric Loui on a healthcare case in Argentina: the operator simply asked the model to find an entry point, without naming services or teaching reconnaissance, and 90 minutes later the agent had found and exploited a SQL injection. He added that safeguards on older models are easy to get around, and in the worst case an operator just starts a new session and tries again.

看点The takeaway is not "AI hackers have arrived" but that the cost structure changed: a full intrusion used to consume people and time, and now a harness plus a cheap model can try every asset in parallel. What defenders can measure therefore returns to the basics — asset inventory, key rotation, API permission hygiene and patch closure time. Mind the wording too: the NCSC reports an increase in incidents without a sample size or a statistical method, so read it as a warning, not an attack-count curve.

Two days, 2.4 million lines gone: only about 60% of the 17.37-million-line Lean proof is actually used

According to a November 24 report by QbitAI, the formal proof of the Riemann hypothesis that Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge produced with Claude is being compressed by developers around the world. The proof contains 17.37 million lines of Lean 4 across 175,187 files; the Lean and Mathlib communities had already confirmed it clears four separate verification gates.

The progress shows up in repository metrics: the official mirror riemann-hypothesis has taken 359 pull requests, cutting more than 2.4 million lines of effective code and more than 25,000 files, with over 800 commits and close to 300 requests still open. A tracker called rh-shrink reports that, after removing blank lines, comments and import declarations, effective code fell from 14.62 million lines to 12.11 million, a 16.5% reduction.

Another site, rh-kernel-backbone, explains why so much could go: of the 17.374 million lines, only 10.737 million are reached by the main proof — just over 60%. Some 2.694 million lines are dead code the proof never touches, and about 241,000 theorems are duplicates.

Compression has also become a public leaderboard. VerifyTask split the 158,055 files that make up 92.23% of the proof into 39 assumption-free core modules, inviting shorter equivalent Lean 4 code for each, with every submission checked against Lean 4.34.1, 4.33.1 and the Comparator with zero sorry. At the time of the report the board had logged 955 verified runs and 301 accepted breakthroughs, cutting 50.48 million standardised tokens across those modules — an 8.4% compression rate.

The ranking is the interesting part: Claude Opus 5.5 with Claude Code, the pair that wrote the original proof, sits seventh with 8 core modules. First is a developer's multi-model system The Council with the pi toolchain, at 28 modules and a 66.6% average compression; second is Gemini 4 Neon with Antigravity at 18 modules and 66.4%, with all 103 submissions passing first time; DeepSeek Flash V4.1 and GPT-6.2 Astra follow. One developer wired four models and three terminal tools into a single pipeline and shrank module 1 from 21 files to one, cutting tokens by 95.4%.

看点The bottleneck in formal proofs has moved from "is it right" to "can anyone read it". The number worth keeping is not the lines deleted but the ratio: only 10.737 million of 17.37 million lines are reached by the main proof, which is exactly the redundancy auto-generated code tends to produce. The reusable lesson is cross-model review — the author's own model ranks seventh while other models and mixed pipelines cut deeper, a large-scale demonstration that a differently structured model finds your dead weight better than you do. Keep the units apart: 8.4% is a token measure, 16.5% is effective lines.

On November 24, Embo, a US startup building AI "brains" for robots, said it had raised a $110 million seed round. Investors include a16z, Khosla Ventures, DST Global and NVentures, Nvidia's venture arm; the round converts to roughly 860 million yuan.

A $110 million seed is rare for robotics. According to Dealroom, the round ranks in the top 1% of all seed rounds for US computer-vision startups; chief executive Danijar Hafner has spent years on world-model agents in the Dreamer line, and co-founder and CTO Wilson Yan built VideoGPT. Embo has not disclosed a product, customers or a valuation.

看点This money buys a team and a thesis, not a validated product. The world-model bet: learn from internet video how a scene will change next, use that predictor as a virtual training ground, and train skills there with reinforcement learning to sidestep real-robot data collection. Only one thing needs proving — that a skill learned in one kitchen works in an unseen one — and that is precisely what no embodied team has publicly solved yet.

$44 billion of GPU financing lands: chips go into a trust and are leased to SpaceX for five years, against a five-year depreciation clock

On November 20, Apollo Global Management and Goldman Sachs priced about $44 billion of senior and subordinated notes to fund SpaceX's purchase of Nvidia's next-generation GPUs and related equipment, per a November 24 DeepTech report. Strong institutional demand upsized the deal from an initial plan of roughly $40 billion, making it the largest GPU financing to date.

The structure is a finance lease rather than a direct loan: an entity called Saturn Funding Trust buys the chips and servers, then leases them to SpaceX for five years. The hardware is Nvidia's next-generation Vera Rubin platform, expected to be delivered through the first half of 2027, with rent starting when equipment is delivered.

Pricing shows how the market judges the asset: the largest tranche, about $25.2 billion of notes, yields 8.259%; about $4.4 billion maturing in 2031 yields 7.66%; and $2.564 billion of Class B subordinated notes due 2033 yield 8.36%. Moody's rates the notes A3, one notch above SpaceX's own credit rating.

One detail reveals the collateral: lenders barred the equipment from orbit, and the rating materials state that the chips will be deployed entirely in SpaceX ground data centres in the United States. The once-floated orbital data centre is, to a creditor, unrecoverable collateral.

看点This is the template for buying GPUs with bonds: the chip is both capital equipment and collateral, a five-year lease races the GPU's depreciation curve, and 8.26% is what the market charges for chip-obsolescence risk. For anyone building training clusters, the numbers that matter are the delivery window and the rent start date — a quarter of delay means interest accrues while no compute ships.
