Nvidia pauses server shipments to four Southeast Asian countries, starting a 90-day licence clock

Nvidia has told manufacturing partners including Dell and Supermicro to pause server shipments to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia. The Financial Times reported the halt without a deadline, citing three people; Nvidia is assessing whether its regional partners meet US export requirements.

The order follows the Commerce Department's move to close the remote-computing route. Chinese firms had been renting Nvidia chips in those countries' data centres; this month Washington put that access under licence. Cloud providers now have 90 days to apply and show that no restricted company is among their end users. One executive told the FT there was no guidance on how to apply, describing "chaos".

TechCrunch reported that the Institute for Progress estimates such remote data centres supplied about 24% of China's AI compute. Financing is already pulling back: banks have halted about $500 million in loans to Singapore-based Aolani, which planned to rent capacity to ByteDance. ByteDance could walk away.

The resale market moved too. CNBC reported an unusually large number of Nvidia GPUs listed for sale after the rule, including thousands of B300s.

Why it mattersThe control now covers server deliveries and rented cloud capacity, not just chip sales. For a team renewing capacity in Southeast Asia, the 90-day licence is a hard deadline: check whether the end customer is restricted before negotiating price.
Another view: the $500 million loan pause shows policy risk is now priced into bank credit. The duration of a compute asset is set by a licence, not by a depreciation schedule.

11/27 06:12 Seoul Economic Daily ↗

China trains Qwen3-32B in orbit for 45 minutes as SpaceX wins approval for 1 million satellites

China's Academy of Information and Communications Technology has finished performance checks on a satellite cluster launched in October. One satellite, Libra, carried Alibaba's Qwen3-32B and trained for 45 minutes 33 seconds without interruption while orbiting, completing a full cycle of solving problems, analysing errors and improving.

Heat is the hard part in orbit. CAICT said liquid cooling and enlarged radiative wings from ADA Space, also known as Guoxing Aerospace, carried away heat from a 10-kilowatt-class AI computer. Google's orbital TPU prototype runs for about 15 minutes per session, according to the report.

The Star Compute project aims to deploy 2,800 satellites. The first cluster went up in May 2025 and the second in October; the satellites in orbit now provide about 50 POPS.

Separately, the US FCC approved SpaceX's Starmind constellation of up to 1 million satellites for orbital data centres. SpaceX must launch the first by November 2033, deploy 100,000 by 2035 and 500,000 by 2038, and finish by 2040. Objections from Amazon and others were dismissed.

Why it mattersOrbital data centres trade launch costs and radiator mass for land and grid power. Forty-five minutes beats 15, but that is a thermal milestone, not a cost advantage; 50 POPS is a mid-sized cluster on the ground. What matters is cost per token, downlink bandwidth and failure rates. Watch whether the next Star Compute batch trains models rather than only running inference.

Tencent open-sources DFly2, speeding long-context generation on Hy4 flash by 3.2x

Tencent Hunyuan open-sourced DFly2, a draft model, on November 26. It does not replace Hy4 flash or change its weights and output. It sits beside the target model and runs speculative decoding: a smaller model proposes tokens, and the target verifies them in a batch.

DFly2 is built for DSA-style sparse-attention models. It reads the target's cached KV and the positions its indexer selects, at most 2,048 per query. The draft layers keep no cache of their own, their attention cost does not grow with context, and they do not take part in prefill.

Tencent compared it with DFlash and DSpark on 19 public benchmarks. Long-context generation is about 3.2x faster; one AgentLongBench task reaches 4.2x, and four agent tasks average about 3.2x. Its accepted-length advantage over DSpark grows from about 4% on short text to about 18% at 128K context; on agent tasks it rises by as much as 68.5%.

The weights, training code and technical report are all open. Beyond Hy4 and Hy4 flash, DFly2 now also trains on DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash and GLM-5.3-Flash, with new MoE drafters released for Hy4 flash, DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash and GLM-5.3-Flash. The AngelSpec training framework supports context up to 256K.

Why it mattersSpeculative decoding is the cheapest latency lever available, and DFly2's real gain is zero extra KV cache. For serving, saved memory means more concurrent users; for prefill-decode separation, passing only one last-layer hidden state saves bandwidth. The 4.2x is a single best case; the average is 3.2x, and the numbers are vendor-run. Measure accepted length on your own traffic before trusting the launch chart.

11/26 12:02 DoNews ↗

InternLM open-sources Sage, a 9B full-duplex model that rejoins a conversation in 1.29 seconds

The InternLM team, supported by Shanghai AI Lab, open-sourced Sage-Interaction on November 25 and disclosed it on November 26. It is a 9B-parameter full-duplex omni model built on MiniCPM-o 4.5.

It takes streaming audio and video while generating speech. A user can interrupt, backchannel or talk over background noise without breaking the conversation. Unlike assistants that wait for the user to finish, it splits a real-time front end from a backend agent: the front end keeps talking while complex reasoning, tool calls and long tasks go to the backend, whose results are folded back into the conversation.

The project says Sage-Interaction scores 80.73 on FD-Bench v1.5 and has a turn-switch latency of 1.29 seconds on FD-Bench v1. The weights and code are on Hugging Face and GitHub.

Why it mattersThe hard part of full duplex is scheduling, not parameter count. A 1.29-second switch is close to human turn-taking, but the real test is whether the front end can avoid talking over the backend when a result arrives. The scores are the project's own. To judge fitness, test real microphones, noise and tool latency. The next signal is whether anyone puts it into phone support or a companion product.

Products & Agents

11/27 01:28 The Decoder ↗

OpenAI fixes a GPT-6.2 billing error, and Astra quotas now run out 40% faster

A billing error during the GPT-6.2 rollout gave most users the wrong discounts on GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol, OpenAI Codex engineer Thibault Sottiaux said on X. The bug is fixed. The cost is that Astra quotas now run out 40% faster than last week, and Sol 25% faster.

Sottiaux said the fixed quotas still last longer than they did on GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6.1 Sol. Other models were not affected, and OpenAI will not retroactively charge the difference. As compensation, Go, Plus, Pro and Business subscribers each get one extra saved reset; Business users get Fast Mode free through the end of the year.

At the same time, OpenAI is testing a merger of Chat and Work with a small group of Pro users. Work is more agentic, slower and more token-hungry. After the merger, all conversations run in one cloud environment, and the model decides whether to answer directly or start a full agentic workflow.

Why it mattersThis is a price correction, not a capability change. The same task now uses 40% more of an Astra quota, or 25% more of a Sol quota; the subscription price is unchanged, but the number of runs is not. Teams should re-measure cost per task rather than reading the price sheet. After the merged quota reaches more users in December, watch the share of Pro users who hit the ceiling, and whether one extra reset covers the gap.

Research & Safety

研究与安全
11/26 19:30 The Decoder ↗

Microsoft dissects ARTEX, a multi-agent pipeline that stole cloud keys in five minutes

Microsoft Threat Intelligence has published an analysis of ARTEX, an AI penetration-testing framework. A GitHub user released it in July 2026; the source was deleted in October, but copies still circulate, and the Chinese security firm ThreatBook sells a commercial Pro version.

ARTEX does not invent new exploits. It drives existing tools such as Nmap, Sqlmap, Metasploit and headless browsers through specialised agents. A goal model breaks an instruction into verifiable subgoals; a planning agent reads a knowledge graph and assigns short tasks; worker agents run the commands; a reporting agent writes up the findings.

It can connect to any large language model, including versions with safety guardrails removed. A built-in pooling feature switches models or API keys on errors or usage limits, making the activity hard for any single model provider to track.

Microsoft logged two speed samples: one agent took five minutes to find leaked credentials on GitHub and pull data from Azure Key Vault; in one Storm-0940 campaign, automated password guessing exceeded 151,000 attempts, peaking at 2,900 per minute. Microsoft recommends honeypots, tarpits and fake success signals to deceive the agents.

Why it mattersThe threat is not a new exploit but a closed loop around existing tools that switches models when one fails. Fixed IP and user-agent rules are easy to route around. Honeypots and fake wins work because the goal model needs verifiable proof of success. That advantage has a shelf life: once attackers learn to slow down and spot decoys, traffic-based defences will weaken again.

11/26 23:24 IT Home ↗

Switzerland reports five AI-agent attacks; four succeeded, hitting power, water and telecom

Switzerland's Federal Office for Cybersecurity (BACS) said on Thursday that five cyberattacks in recent days used AI agents against companies and critical-infrastructure operators. Four of the five succeeded and systems were breached.

Swiss public broadcaster SRF reported that three of the targets were critical-infrastructure operators in power, water and telecommunications. BACS did not name the attackers. It recommended that companies put protective measures in place, including regular security checks.

Reuters reported the notice on November 26. BACS did not say which agents were used, which systems were breached, or how the attacks were carried out.

Why it mattersFour successes out of five is a tiny sample, not a general success rate. The target set is the point: one each in power, water and telecom. Agents lower the cost of reconnaissance, attempts and lateral movement, so defenders should assume machine-speed trial and error. The missing piece is TTPs. Until BACS publishes them, the practical steps are segmenting OT networks, limiting credentials and shortening alert times.

Embodied AI

11/26 13:59 People's Daily Beijing ↗

Beijing's Yizhuang district offers 1,000-plus datasets and RMB 5 billion in robot scenarios

The E-Bot Day 2026 humanoid-robot developer conference was held in Beijing's Yizhuang district on November 25. The district is home to more than 300 robotics and embodied-AI companies, with an industrial chain worth more than RMB 20 billion.

Yizhuang released a plan for an international open-source community called Future Machine Domain. It promises open data-collection sites and more than 1,000 open datasets; a "machine gene factory" and an open bill-of-materials library for rapid prototyping and supply-chain coordination; and nine benchmark scenarios with nearly RMB 5 billion in application demand.

The conference also issued a training-ground quality initiative, a list of ten developer achievements for 2026, and a responsible-innovation pledge signed by more than ten organisations. The carnival had 45 booths, 33 projects and 12 university clubs.

Why it mattersEmbodied AI does not lack demos; it lacks trainable data and paying scenarios. The 1,000 datasets and RMB 5 billion demand are two lists that can be audited. For developers, check whether the datasets are downloadable and how long real-robot validation takes. For investors, check how much of the RMB 5 billion becomes orders rather than intentions. If the open BOM really shortens prototyping, it will matter more than another stage demo.

11/26 19:09 STAR Market Daily ↗

Samsung forms a fab robot team, targeting parts changes and maintenance by 2028

Samsung Electronics' Device Solutions (DS) division has set up a robot team under its global manufacturing and infrastructure headquarters to put robots to work in semiconductor fabs. The Korean outlet ETNEWS reported the move on November 26.

Industry figures put front-end automation in fabs at about 90% and back-end automation at 80%. Humans still replace parts and consumables and maintain equipment, and those are the tasks the robot team is aimed at.

Samsung plans to pick partners this year, run technical evaluations next year and begin deployment in 2028.

Why it mattersA wafer fab is one of the hardest environments for a robot: cleanroom conditions, micro-vibration and 24-hour uptime with very little tolerance for error. Samsung is starting with maintenance rather than production, which is the right order. The 2028 date suggests it knows the difficulty is reliability and force control, not the arm itself. Watch two things: whether the partners build mobile manipulators or fixed arms, and whether the first deployment lands in the front end or the back end.
