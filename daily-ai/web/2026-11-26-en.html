Models & Capability

模型与能力
11/25 21:12 ifeng Tech ↗

An unreleased OpenAI model wrote nine math papers in a day, claiming the matrix-multiplication exponent is 9/4

OpenAI's internal model produced nine mathematics manuscripts in a single day. One of them, titled "The Matrix Multiplication Exponent Is 9/4", claims that under the arithmetic circuit model it studies, the exponent for square matrix multiplication is exactly 2.25.

That exponent is usually written as omega. It measures how the cost of multiplying matrices grows with their size; the naive algorithm runs in about n cubed. Researchers had already proved 9/4 as an upper bound, meaning a method exists at that speed. The manuscript claims a matching lower bound.

The lower bound is stated as follows: for every n of at least 2, any formal arithmetic circuit for square matrix multiplication needs at least ceiling(n^(9/4)/2) non-scalar multiplication or division gates. It is a statement about a theoretical model, not about wall-clock time on a GPU.

OpenAI says an unreleased internal model produced most of the manuscripts as raw research data during an evaluation on open problems, not as finished papers. The repository now holds 730 manuscripts across 375 research families, and about 42% of top-level results have been formalised. Each selected result used about three hours of ChatGPT Pro thinking compute; the whole evaluation took in roughly 4,000 research questions.

Why it mattersThe useful numbers are 42%, three hours and 4,000. The formalisation rate decides whether a claim can be checked line by line in Lean; the other two decide the unit cost of this kind of research. Watch whether the formalisation share climbs over time, not the flashiest theorem in a single release. Another view: 4,000 questions at three hours each is roughly 12,000 hours of inference, a research expense for the lab and a shift in scarce labour toward asking the right question.

Products & Agents

产品与智能体
11/26 04:03 TechCrunch ↗

ChatGPT folds chat and work into one quota, and heavy Pro users will hit the wall sooner

OpenAI has begun rolling out a unified Chat and Work experience to a small group of Pro subscribers. Product lead Thibault Sottiaux announced the update on X on Tuesday, after OpenAI had emailed users about the merger earlier.

The two modes now share one usage meter across ChatGPT. Conversations on the Instant model stay unlimited and do not count against the shared allowance; users can keep using Instant after the allowance runs out.

An OpenAI spokesperson told TechCrunch that most users should not hit limits more often, but a small fraction of the heaviest users may run out sooner. OpenAI plans to bring the shared limits to more users in December.

Some paying users have complained on social media and OpenAI's community forum that the same subscription may buy fewer runs on advanced reasoning models. Anthropic merged Claude and its work product Cowork into one interface in September.

Why it mattersMerging the entrance is a product call; merging the quota is a cost call. Quotas used to be split by feature. Now they are split by compute, which turns "how many products did I buy" into "how many tokens did I buy". For heavy users that is a quiet price increase. The number to watch after the December rollout is the share of Pro users who hit the ceiling, and whether OpenAI raises it.

11/25 19:53 ifeng Tech ↗

ByteDance's Xiaodou books restaurants and hails rides, but shopping stays inside Douyin

ByteDance opened an invite-only beta of Xiaodou, its personal agent, on November 24. It is a standalone app, not a tab inside Doubao, and it shares conversation context and user preferences with Doubao through three entry points: suggestions, follow-ups and a profile of the user.

In a hands-on test by ifeng Tech, Xiaodou filtered restaurants by live location and called a candidate to confirm a private room and a group coupon. On a fresh Amap account it also completed a cross-city ride: registration, permissions, order and payment confirmation, with the app handling the steps in between.

At work it connects to Feishu, WeCom, DingTalk, Baidu Netdisk and Apple Reminders. When a Feishu document had not been revised after the previous day's comments, Xiaodou surfaced the gap and offered to message the colleague. Payments and phone numbers require a confirmation card.

The boundaries are just as visible. Shopping is limited to Douyin Mall; coupons and address changes are still manual. Baidu Netdisk does not support SMS-code login, and WeCom has no proactive push channel yet.

Why it mattersThe bottleneck for a personal agent is no longer the model. It is accounts and permissions: who grants OAuth, who authorises payment, and who rolls back when a task fails. Xiaodou's advantage is that Douyin, Feishu and Jimeng are its own apps, so their authorisations can be productised. Every third-party integration adds linear adaptation and compliance work. Watch the number of outside apps and the rollback path, not the demo.

Research & Safety

研究与安全
11/25 Google Research ↗

Google tested 12 models on one-sided accounts: accuracy fell, and more turns hardened the bias

Google Research built a multi-agent simulation to test whether a model can infer what someone really wants when it hears the story only through the user. Each scenario gives a target character a hidden motive; a user character then recounts the events to the model under test. Because the motive is set by the framework, the correct answer is verifiable.

The team tested 12 large language models and open-sourced the framework plus a dataset of more than 20,000 examples. Models were significantly more accurate when they could observe the full event directly than when they talked only to the user.

Explicit bias in the user widened the gap. In some cases the user's suspicion had no factual basis, and the model still validated it. Models also needed more detail than humans before committing to a judgement. More turns did not reliably help: models preserved conversational coherence and anchored to their earlier reading, hardening the first mistake.

Why it mattersThe harness is reusable. A hidden motive, a one-sided account and a checkable ground truth add up to a regression suite that any team building a companion, advice or support agent can copy. It also carries a warning: multi-turn conversation is not a free error-correction mechanism. The longer the context, the harder it is for the model to overturn what it has already said.

11/26 01:35 TechCrunch ↗

Character.AI wants to auction 21 million users' chats; 42 attorneys general move to block it

Character.AI filed for Chapter 11 protection in October and now plans to auction user data. According to state attorneys general, the dataset includes chat histories from more than 21 million US accounts, more than 2.1 million of them belonging to minors, plus personal data such as IP addresses.

Only one bidder has been disclosed: Smirks and Pangs Acquisition LLC. The company was registered in Delaware on November 13 and was picked as the base bid the next day. The auction is scheduled for December 1.

A coalition of 42 attorneys general, including the District of Columbia's, has intervened to stop the sale. They argue that Character.AI promised consumers their personal information would not be sold; that a buyer would not be bound by the old privacy policy and could resell the data; and that the company has told the court less about safety and legal risks than it has told bidders.

California's Rob Bonta and Connecticut's William Tong both spoke out. A separate plan to license Character.AI's intellectual property to Disney for $20 million, which does not include user chats, has also drawn multi-state opposition and has not received final court approval.

Why it mattersBankruptcy turns a privacy promise into an asset question. The promise sits in a privacy policy; the data sits on the estate's asset list. When the two collide, the court looks at the second. For anyone building a chat product, the practical lesson is to put data custody into the architecture: who can export it, who can decrypt it, whose cloud account holds it. A policy document is the first thing to fail in a bankruptcy.

Embodied AI

具身智能
11/26 03:52 The Robotics Media ↗

Lute matches Pi0.5 with one-twentieth of the training data, using a tactile kit you can carry

Lute, a startup building tactile foundation models for robots, raised $20 million in combined pre-seed and seed funding, announced on November 25. OTB Ventures and FORWARD.one co-led the round, with Seedcamp, Credo Ventures, Naval Ravikant, Inovo VC, ff Venture Capital and Borusan Ventures participating.

The company was founded in January 2026 and has 16 people split between Warsaw and the Bay Area. Pawel Budzianowski, who previously led machine learning at PolyAI, is the founder; Krzysztof Walas, a former associate professor at Poznan University of Technology, is a co-founder.

Lute puts skin-like tactile sensors into a portable data-collection kit, then trains its Lute-0 model on what the kit records. The company says Lute-0 matched the open-source Pi0.5 model on dexterous manipulation while using about one-twentieth of the pre-training data.

The system is deployed at what Lute describes as Europe's largest automotive retail distributor, a company with roughly $6 billion in annual revenue, and is in pilots in hospital corridors and large retail stores. Those figures and customers are the company's own account.

Why it mattersTactile data only exists at the moment of contact. The old collection rig lived on a work cell, so every new customer meant rebuilding it. Lute's carry-on kit lowers the cost of collecting data from "retrofit the line" to "bring the box to the site". The one-twentieth claim is the company's own. The test is whether it holds on transparent, soft and reflective objects, where vision alone fails.

Industry & Capital

11/26 03:13 Cailianshe ↗

The GPU spot premium is gone: small buyers get bulk pricing, and renewals have room to bargain

SpaceX cut the spot rent for small Blackwell clusters from about $15–15.5 per GPU-hour to $10–12, a drop of 20%–35%. That is roughly the bulk price it charges large customers such as Anthropic and Google.

In the same SemiAnalysis note, Anthropic declined to renew a cluster of about 50,000 H200s. SpaceX is looking for new tenants, with delivery in early 2027 at about $5 per GPU-hour. Ornn's data puts the H200's actual traded price at $6.53 on Monday, so SpaceX's offer is about 23% below it.

Ornn's transaction index shows rents cooling: B200 is down 8.8% from its November 8 high to $9.07, H200 is down 16% from its November 11 high to $6.53, and B300 is down 24% from its mid-October high to $10.87.

On the demand side, Ramp's data shows enterprise token spending rose 42% in July, only 1.5% across August and September, and 1.65% in October. In the week to November 15, spending was up 12% from a month earlier: usage rose 27.2% while the blended price fell 11.2% to $0.45 per million tokens.

Why it mattersThe spot premium is gone, which means supply is loosening. For a team about to renew a compute contract, short-term leases softening before long-term ones is a signal worth taking into the negotiation. The caveat is in the same note: SemiAnalysis still sees healthy returns for new Blackwell projects. This looks more like a landlord clearing space for new capacity than a market-wide collapse.

11/26 08:21 DigitalToday (via CNBC) ↗

After the remote-access rule took effect, thousands of B300s hit the resale market

The US Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security issued an interim final rule on November 16, effective November 19. It restricts not only direct chip purchases by Chinese firms but also renting GPUs in third-country data centres and using them remotely.

CNBC reported on November 25 that Nvidia GPU listings had risen unusually since the rule was announced. Jack Duhamel of GPU marketplace GPU Mart said one seller explicitly linked the decision to sell to the new restrictions.

Two people, who asked not to be identified, said the supply came mainly from inventory built for Chinese demand for rented overseas data-centre capacity. Since the announcement, offers involving thousands of B300 GPUs have appeared on the market.

US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer told CNBC's Squawk Box that the rule is meant to close routes around export controls, including transshipment through Southeast Asia and remote use via third-country data centres. Nvidia declined to comment. CFO Colette Kress said on the November 17 earnings call that the company was assessing the impact. A remote-access security bill has passed the House and awaits a Senate vote.

Why it mattersOne rule turned work-in-progress capacity into inventory looking for a buyer. The asset's duration is now set by policy rather than depreciation. For buyers, that means a short-term discount; for sellers, a clock to find a new data centre. Watch two numbers: the actual discount on B300 deals, and the jurisdiction where the chips end up. That will shape the next round of controls.
