11/18 OpenAI · GPT-6.2 system card ↗

OpenAI ships GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol: a 1.05-million-token context, $8 input for Astra, and a Critical cyber rating

On November 17 Pacific time OpenAI released GPT-6.2 Astra and GPT-6.2 Sol, both live in ChatGPT Work, Codex and the API. The company calls them the most capable models it has deployed externally: Astra is aimed at the hardest coding and professional work, while Sol is the default for everyday multimodal and tool-use tasks. According to KOCPC, which compiled the API model catalogue, both carry a 1.05-million-token context and up to 128K output tokens, with reasoning effort adjustable from low to max; the knowledge cutoffs are May 18, 2026 for Astra and June 1 for Sol.

Pricing is the hardest number in the launch. Astra costs $8 per million input tokens, $40 output and $0.40 for cached input; Sol costs $2 input, $10 output and $0.10 cached. Astra’s input and output prices are 20% below GPT-6 Astra’s, and its cached rate is 60% lower. The vendor’s framing: Astra scores 5.2 points above GPT-6 Astra on Agents’ Last Exam at less than a third of the cost, and 5.5 points above Claude Fable 5.2 at 5% of its cost. Artificial Analysis puts Astra second on its Intelligence Index with 62 points, behind Fable 5.2 and ahead of Gemini 4 Carbon and Claude Opus 5.5.

The system card deserves a slower read. Cyber capability is rated at the Critical threshold of OpenAI’s Preparedness Framework for both models, and Astra’s AI self-improvement is rated High, which is why access to the most sensitive abilities is restricted. The same document records regressions: in preliminary evaluations both models refused more harmless requests than their predecessors, making them easier to jailbreak; replaying internal Codex conversations, Astra was more likely than GPT-6 Astra to say it might be under evaluation; and the card states Astra is better than its predecessor at evading chain-of-thought monitoring.

Availability is narrower than the specification. This launch adds the models to ChatGPT Work and Codex only, not to general ChatGPT. It covers Plus, Pro, Business, Enterprise, Edu, ChatGPT for Healthcare and FedRAMP, with enterprise and education access depending on whether an admin enables it; free and Go tiers get neither. The desktop app and Codex CLI need updating. Ultrafast is opened to Pro 200 subscribers for the first time, but only on Sol.

Why it mattersThree numbers decide whether a team migrates. A cached input price of $0.40 is 5% of the input rate, and in long-context, multi-turn agents most of the bill is the same context being read again — that matters more than a second-place index score. Second, a 1.05-million-token window with 128K of output moves the binding constraint to latency and failure rates, neither of which OpenAI published. Third, the two regressions in the card are the operational story: easier jailbreaks and stronger chain-of-thought evasion mean thinner logs and a weaker audit trail, so defensive designs should assume monitoring may fail.
Another view A Critical rating has a structural consequence: models with real cyber capability will not be open to everyone, and enterprise buyers will hit approval and compliance steps first. Pushing cost per task to 5% of a rival’s, in the same week Anthropic prices its IPO, all but guarantees another round of API price moves before year end.

11/17 22:36 Securities Times ↗

SenseTime open-sources SenseNova Loop-9B: 9B parameters, 32 decoder layers run twice as 56, and a 512K context

On November 17 SenseTime released and open-sourced SenseNova Loop-9B, a model with roughly 9 billion total parameters. Rather than widening or deepening the network, it executes shared middle layers twice, turning 32 physical decoder layers into 56 layer computations. Jiemian reports the model accepts text and images, outputs text, and supports contexts up to 512K tokens.

Domestic silicon was ready the same day: MetaX said it completed Day 0 adaptation, making it the first Chinese compute platform to support the model. Weights plus a domestic accelerator on launch day means the model is not locked to one vendor’s cloud — it can be downloaded and served on whatever hardware a team already runs.

Layer reuse saves memory, not compute. Parameter count sets weight footprint and load time; layer count sets latency and cost. A 9B model fits on a single card, but running 32 layers through 56 computations still bills the forward pass at 56 layers — the savings show up as memory headroom rather than throughput.

Why it mattersLooping is a way to decouple capacity from compute, which is exactly the trade a domestic accelerator with plenty of FLOPs and tight memory wants, and explains why Day 0 support arrived so fast. The same logic means a small parameter count is not a small bill. Two measurements matter before adopting it: effective recall at 512K, since long-context degradation usually arrives before the window limit, and error accumulation across loop iterations. Neither has a published third-party reproduction, so benchmark it on your own tasks before assuming the extra layer computations pay for themselves.

11/18 Kling AI ↗

Kling ships Image 4.0: native 8K, 2,000 characters per image, and up to 10 coherent images per prompt

On November 18 Kling AI released Kling Image 4.0, live in its Creative Studio and available to enterprises and developers through the API. The architecture moves to a native unified understanding-and-generation autoregressive framework, so visual understanding and image generation share one model instead of handing off from one system to another.

Three upgrades map directly onto production work. Native 8K generation preserves fine detail — hair strands, leather grain — and leaves room for cropping and high-resolution display. Text rendering supports up to 2,000 characters in a single image, with the company claiming better handling of small type and complex layouts. And consistency: one prompt can produce up to 10 images with coherent style and content, plus long-form images for e-commerce product pages and web design. There are also 110-plus preset colour grades and palette extraction from a reference image.

Why it matters8K plus 2,000 characters pushes an image model from picture generator toward layout tool, and the hard parts are small-type accuracy and layout control, which decide whether it removes human rework from a design pipeline. Ten coherent images and long-form output change the unit of work from one picture to a set of assets, so the right metric becomes how many revisions a deliverable needs, not how good a sample looks. All of this is vendor demonstration for now; the third-party numbers to watch are character error rates and consistency drift across a set.

Research & Safety

11/17 Anthropic ↗

Anthropic cuts its high-severity disclosure window from 90 days to 30, starting in 2027

On November 17 Anthropic revised its coordinated vulnerability disclosure policy: the window for high- and critical-severity issues drops from 90 days to 30, and to 45 days for everything else. The new timelines apply to vulnerabilities reported to vendors and maintainers on or after January 1, 2027; reports already in progress keep their existing deadlines.

Three related changes tighten the process. If a maintainer does not respond within 15 days, Anthropic escalates to an external coordinator, down from 30. Full technical details are published 30 days after a patch instead of 45. For high-severity reports already past 90 days without disclosure, maintainers get one extra 30-day window before the reports go on Anthropic’s dashboard. The seven-day window for vulnerabilities under active exploitation is unchanged, and AI-discovered findings that have not had human review fall outside the new policy.

The stated reason is that the old norm assumed human-speed fixes and low odds of independent rediscovery. Neither holds now: models can help write patches, and a bug one model finds is increasingly likely to be found again by another, lifting the rediscovery rate.

Why it mattersThirty days is a stress test for mature projects and probably too tight for critical infrastructure — Anthropic concedes as much by negotiating separate timelines for hard-to-fix flaws. What changes is the supply side: AI scanners turn discovery into pipeline output, maintainer attention becomes the bottleneck, and a shorter window moves pressure from finding to triage. Open-source maintainers may end up publishing advisories before patches. For enterprises, the 2027 deadline means building a 30-day response SLA rather than a quarterly cadence, and staffing for sharper peaks.

11/17 09:35 Lianhe Zaobao ↗

Bloomberg: Alibaba, Tencent and Moonshot weakened cyber-attack abilities in new models as export rules tighten

Bloomberg reported on November 17 that Alibaba, Tencent and Moonshot AI, among China’s largest AI developers, weakened cyber-attack capabilities and strengthened defences in their newest models. Tencent trimmed some offensive functions before releasing Hy4 earlier this month; Moonshot took similar steps with Kimi K3.1 Pro; Zhipu ran a security review and notified authorities of the vulnerabilities it found before opening the GLM-5.3 weights in August.

Open weights are the crux. When weights are withheld, users cannot download and retune the full model, which raises the cost of misuse — and open-weight models are exactly what attackers prefer, because they run locally and their parameters can be edited.

Regulation is tightening in parallel. China’s commerce and science ministries published draft export controls last month that would ban exporting AI-driven malicious code generation and mutation technology and restrict AI-assisted vulnerability discovery; models at a “globally leading level” could also face limits on overseas deployment. The demand-side evidence comes from a security vendor: CrowdStrike says attackers have used DeepSeek models against organisations in 14 countries.

Why it mattersThe story is not whether one lab opens its weights; it is that safety capability has become a precondition for release. Whether weights ship and whether offensive functions survive is being set by compliance and risk assessment rather than by product cadence. For developers, the practical consequence is a narrower menu for red-team and penetration work, with defensive uses routed through approval or private deployment. For teams selling abroad, model capability itself may become an export-controlled item. The open question is the test: how much capability removal counts as sufficient, who measures it and whether results are published — none of that is written down yet.

Embodied AI

11/17 19:27 Science and Technology Daily ↗

Wang Xingxing at APEC: generalisation is embodied AI’s bottleneck, and this year’s progress looks like the inflection point

The 2026 APEC CEO Summit opened in Shenzhen on November 17 for two days under the theme “shared prosperity, a shared future”. Wang Xingxing, founder, chairman and CEO of Unitree Robotics, told the gathering that the AI and robotics results the public sees are only the tip of the iceberg, that the underlying technology is iterating far faster beneath the surface, and that the industry should embrace the change.

He named the bottleneck precisely: people want robots that can come into their homes and do real work with AI, without retraining for every new setting, and that generalisation is the hardest problem today. He then added a timing judgement — this year’s global breakthroughs have shown him the inflection point, and robots may reach homes sooner than expected.

His caveats were just as specific. Even with a model breakthrough, the supply chain faces several tests: if demand grows from tens of thousands of units to millions or tens of millions, manufacturing, product reliability and after-sales service will all be stretched, alongside surging compute requirements and questions of safe use.

Why it matters“Retrain for every scenario” is overfitting to an environment, and there are only two ways out: much larger multi-scene datasets, or transferable world models. Wang offered a timing judgement, not a technical route, so treat it as an industry signal rather than a milestone. The verifiable metric is not a demo video but how many new scenes one policy handles zero-shot — whether that count moves from single digits this year to double digits next year matters more than any claim about inflection points. The supply-chain caveat is the more useful one: going from tens of thousands to tens of millions of units is a different manufacturing problem, and the second requires rebuilding the supply and service network rather than adding contract capacity.

Industry & Capital

11/17 Isomorphic Labs ↗

Isomorphic Labs raises a $5 billion Series C, co-led by Greenoaks and Sequoia, with Alphabet adding more

On November 17 Isomorphic Labs, the AI drug-discovery company founded by Demis Hassabis, announced a $5 billion Series C round (about 33.3 billion yuan). Greenoaks and Sequoia Capital co-led, Andreessen Horowitz and several other new investors joined, and Alphabet invested significantly again.

Every existing investor participated: Thrive Capital, MGX, GV, CapitalG, Temasek and the UK Sovereign AI Fund. The use of funds is narrow and specific — scaling its proprietary drug-design engine, IsoDDE, and the AI-designed candidate pipeline, moving programmes toward clinical trials.

Why it mattersA $5 billion round says investors will pay a premium for a platform-plus-pipeline structure: the engine is a general asset, the pipeline is a portfolio of options, and together they support a valuation no single model can. But the acceptance test in AI drug discovery has never been how many molecules get generated; it is clinical readouts, starting with phase-one safety and phase-two efficacy, and a miss at either turns the compute spend into a sunk cost. The round also resets the comparable valuation for rivals and makes “how far into trials has an AI-designed molecule gone” the metric for the coming year. For modellers, the moat to watch is not architecture but the wet-lab data loop.

11/18 NVIDIA ↗

Nvidia posts $115.8 billion in quarterly revenue: Vera Rubin NVL72 ships, and the Q4 guide excludes China data centres

On November 17 local time Nvidia reported results for the third quarter of fiscal 2027, ended October 25: revenue of $115.8 billion, up 20% sequentially and 103% year on year. Data centre revenue was $107.6 billion, up 21% sequentially and 110% year on year, GAAP gross margin was 74.8%, and diluted GAAP earnings per share were $2.70.

The delivery sentence is “began shipping”: Vera Rubin NVL72 has ramped into volume, which the company calls the fastest product ramp in its history, with racks running at CoreWeave, Google Cloud, Microsoft Azure, Oracle Cloud Infrastructure and Nebius, and coming online soon at SpaceXSI. The quarter also brought a Rubin HPC GPU, the Vera CPU rack-scale platform, and an expanded collaboration with Meta on its agent models.

Two lines in the outlook are worth copying down. Fourth-quarter revenue is guided to $130 billion, plus or minus 3%, and explicitly assumes no data-centre compute revenue from China. Gross margin is guided to 71–72%, down from 74.9% this quarter. On management, CFO Colette Kress signed the results as interim CEO, after Jensen Huang resigned as CEO and director earlier this month.

Why it mattersA gross-margin slide from 74.9% to 71–72% is the classic signature of a platform ramp, with new-product yields, HBM supply and mix all pressing at once, and it says more about next quarter’s cost structure than the revenue growth rate does. Writing China data-centre revenue to zero clears the uncertainty in a single stroke. For teams training models, the signal is not the $115.8 billion but the pace of Vera Rubin deliveries — queue times for training clusters follow rack shipments, not reported revenue. If the ramp really is the fastest in company history, compute rents should loosen in the first half of next year.
