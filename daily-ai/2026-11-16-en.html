11/15 18:06 The Decoder ↗

Meituan opens the weights of LongCat-2.5: a 1.6T-parameter MoE that runs its sample deployment on 194 Ascend chips

The Decoder reported on November 15 that Meituan’s LongCat team released the weights of LongCat-2.5 a week after unveiling the model, under the MIT licence, which allows commercial use, modification and redistribution. It is a mixture-of-experts model built for long-horizon agent work: 1.6 trillion total parameters, about 48 billion active per token, a 1-million-token context, up to 128,000 output tokens, text, image and video input, and a reasoning toggle.

Training and supervised fine-tuning ran on Chinese accelerators. In its technical blog, Meituan says each domestic card has less than 80GB of memory, so the team used dual-shard FSDP, hybrid vocabulary parallelism, MoonEP dynamic expert replication and zero-copy communication to fit long-sequence training; single-card throughput on long-sequence SFT was 86% above a DDP baseline. For inference it quantises expert weights and activations to 4 bits (W4A4), reporting 43.3% higher prompt-processing throughput and 42.77% higher generation throughput, with accuracy “on par” with the uncompressed model.

The official sample deployment uses 194 Huawei Ascend Atlas A2 accelerators: two for image processing, 64 for prompt ingestion and 128 for generation. The self-hosting guide contains no Nvidia GPU configuration. The benchmarks are vendor-run: 88.4 on Terminal Bench v2.1, below Claude Opus 5’s 89.1 and DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash’s 90.6 and slightly above GPT-5.6 Sol’s 88.8; 38.5 on the harder v4.0, against 53.9 for Opus 5; DeepSWE rises from 9.7 to 64.6; the 91.9 on BrowseComp was measured in Meituan’s own environment. Promotional API pricing is about €0.27 per million input tokens and €1.07 per million output tokens.

On training, the team built tasks from real GitHub pull requests, cut internet access, Git history and build caches in the sandbox, used an LLM judge to catch shortcuts, and turned real agent failures into reproducible, verifiable tasks. Reinforcement learning uses asynchronous partial rollouts, with the oversampling rate adjusted by pass rate and all-pass or all-fail groups filtered out.

Why it mattersThe 1.6T open weights and the domestic-chip deployment shift the hard question from “do we have accelerators” to “can we keep 194 of them stable”. Three caveats matter: the sample deployment is inference, not training, and Meituan does not disclose the training cluster; the benchmarks are vendor-run, and BrowseComp was measured in-house; and “accuracy on par” under W4A4 comes without per-task deltas. For downstream teams the MIT licence is the concrete part — commercial use and retraining are allowed — but the memory and communication budget of a 1.6T model means most will still call an API.
Another view If the “domestic silicon + 4-bit + long context” playbook travels, next year’s price war will not only show up in API rates but in the table of how many chips each token costs.

11/15 23:42 The Decoder ↗

Claude Fable 5.2 “paints” photorealistic images in 700,000 strokes by writing Python, not pixels

The Decoder reported on November 15 that Anthropic’s Claude Fable 5.2 takes a different route to images: instead of generating pixels, the model writes a 1,000-to-2,000-line Python program that drives a paint engine simulating bristles, wet paint and canvas texture, placing hundreds of thousands of strokes on a virtual canvas. In a demo by Anthropic employee Jake Eaton, a photorealistic parking-garage scene took roughly 700,000 strokes.

In a log published by engineer Michael Graczyk, the model painted a bird at sunrise in just 111 strokes, each with a colour code, a brush width and several slightly wobbling waypoints; the first stroke was #30346a, 90 pixels wide, with five waypoints. There is no undo: every stroke stays. Between passes the model looks at the canvas and reacts — when the bird’s back came out too thin, it laid a bolder stroke over it, turning stray bristle marks into ruffled feathers.

The approach ties long-horizon planning to spatial reasoning. Anthropic says Fable 5.2 scores 64.9% on its internal RoboDojo Sim benchmark against 29.8% for GPT-6 Astra; but in researcher Aris Zhu’s test with a real robot arm painting “The Starry Night”, Astra painted faster and is so far the only one of the two to stand a domino on end. Those numbers come from the vendor and employee demos, with no independent reproduction.

Fable 5.2 was released on November 9 on the same base model as Mythos 5.2. Anthropic says it also produced results on two open problems in black-hole physics, designed three working circuits with Diode, and used more than 1,000 sub-agents to write a laptop operating system in eight days.

Why it mattersThe demo is less about whether the painting looks real than about whether the model can stay coherent across hundreds of steps and hundreds of thousands of discrete decisions: every stroke is code, nothing can be rolled back, and mistakes can only be painted over — the classic failure mode of long-horizon agents. The loop worth copying is generate, render, look, revise. The number worth questioning is cost: how many tokens, GPU-hours and dollars 700,000 strokes took is not disclosed. And half of the “photorealism” belongs to the paint engine, not the model.

11/15 21:16 IT之家 ↗

Huawei Cloud launches openPangu-2.1-Pro: 512K context and cache-hit input at 0.016 yuan per million tokens

On November 15, Huawei Cloud added the open-source Pangu model openPangu-2.1-Pro to its MaaS platform. The company frames the upgrade around three areas — agent development, AI coding and inference optimisation — naming deep research, HarmonyOS control, HarmonyOS app development, Ascend operator generation and inference-pipeline optimisation as the target scenarios.

The model list gives a 512K context, 500K maximum input, 384K maximum output and a 256K maximum chain of thought. It supports a switchable deep-thinking mode, function calling, prefix continuation and prefix caching, with default limits of 2 million TPM and 100 RPM. The only preset region so far is southwest China (Guiyang).

Pricing is the most concrete number here: 0.8 yuan per million input tokens, 3.2 yuan per million output tokens and 0.016 yuan per million cache-hit input tokens, about 2% of the input rate. Huawei says more than 100,000 task-specific examples were used to improve skill triggering, tool selection and tool calling, while coding work is tailored to HarmonyOS ArkTS and Ascend operators.

Huawei has not disclosed the parameter count, a weights download or benchmark scores. openPangu-2.0-Pro and 2.0-Flash are scheduled to go offline on November 30, so services still on the old models must migrate by then; the new model will also plug into AgentArts, CodeArts and OfficeAce.

Why it mattersThe pricing makes the bill structure explicit: cache hits cost 2% of the input rate, so repeated questions over long documents will be dominated by cache pricing, while output at four times the input rate is where coding and report-writing spend goes. The real unknown is tool-calling reliability, not price: there is no success rate, no count of callable tools and no parameter count, so teams can only blind-test 2.0 against 2.1 on the same tasks before migrating. The single preset region is another constraint — cross-region latency and data-compliance costs need to be priced in first.

Korea’s two biggest platforms turn personal agents into a checkout lane

DigitalToday reported on November 16 that Naver plans to launch a consumer AI agent this year that shops, books and pays on the user’s behalf, connecting search results directly to transactions. The company also plans to extend into logistics and offline payments and to shift its platform toward AI.

Kakao’s route is to turn KakaoTalk from an assistant that answers questions into one that executes tasks in the chat window, monetised through advertising and commerce, with a target of breaking even on AI in 2028. Korean startup Pickle already handles shopping and ticketing, though testers complain that it is slow.

The report places both moves against a global race for the personal-agent entry point: Meta’s Muse now calls US businesses for paying subscribers, while Naver and Kakao’s advantage is local payments, identity and merchant relationships — and their weakness is model capability.

Why it mattersThe battleground is shifting from “whose model is stronger” to a trio of entry point, payment and merchant integration: whoever completes the last mile of a purchase captures the transaction data and the commission. Kakao’s 2028 break-even target is an admission that it expects to subsidise the business for two years; the real cost is not inference but merchant-side integration and dispute handling. For China’s super apps, the mirror is clear — the moat is payments and merchants, not leaderboard positions.

11/16 06:00 OpenAI ↗

OpenAI opens a science blog with a 123-page lattice paper co-written with GPT-6 Astra

At 06:00 Beijing time on November 16, OpenAI launched a science blog. Its first post is a guest essay by Nathaniel Craig, a physicist at UC Santa Barbara, who worked with GPT-6 Astra on a candidate non-perturbative lattice definition of the Standard Model. The paper went up on arXiv (2610.09093) on October 6: 60+63 pages, 12 figures, with a mathematical companion and Lean formalisation materials.

Craig writes that Astra was “essential to this result”: he proposed extending Lüscher’s abelian construction to non-abelian cases with refinement methods, iterated with Astra and outside experts, and arrived at “the current Astra-led proof”. The manuscript mixes author-written sections with extensive rewrites of Astra’s proof, and the Lean verification was carried out by Astra.

Physically, the work fills a gap in the electroweak sector: gauge fields have long had a lattice regularisation, but fermions — chiral fermions in particular — have lacked a satisfactory non-perturbative definition. Craig follows Lüscher’s 1998 programme and covers a class of non-abelian chiral gauge theories that includes the Standard Model. Mathematician Peter Woit calls it “a modest advance over Lüscher”, still far from a full continuum-limit definition and not yet a way to do actual non-perturbative electroweak calculations.

Why it mattersThis is a checkable sample of “AI as a mathematician collaborator”: not a claim that a model proved a theorem, but a 123-page manuscript, Lean formalisation and arXiv/Zenodo attachments anyone can open. What engineering teams should study is the division of labour — a human sets strategy and physical judgement, the model carries long derivations and formalisation, outside experts iterate and review. That workflow is closer to reproducible science than any benchmark. The caveat is that “essential” is unquantified: how many pages Astra wrote, how many iterations it took and how often it was wrong survive only as traces in the manuscript. The blog itself is also a product: OpenAI is building a permanent venue for the “AI does science” narrative.

For buyers of compute, the near-term effect is higher compliance risk on second-hand and grey channels; for investors, Culper’s figure is a short seller’s estimate, not an audited number.

Huang reportedly explores a return as Bessent gives Nvidia 10 days on China sales

The same day, US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said Nvidia needs a “dramatic shakeup” and should look outside the company for its next CEO. He gave the board 10 days to answer whether Nvidia pushed “seemingly illicit” chip sales to China to inflate revenue. Bloomberg and CNBC both reported the remarks.

Nvidia’s board is now searching for a successor. The resignation followed an October 29 New York Times report that Huang appeared to be the target of a Chinese espionage operation; Reuters reported a third extramarital relationship. Lawmakers in both parties are also pushing to tighten AI chip exports to China.

Why it mattersThe transmission channel to the AI industry is not gossip but supply contracts: Huang was the node connecting Nvidia to governments, cloud providers and startups, and the compute deals and investment commitments under negotiation get repriced when he leaves. Bessent’s 10-day deadline turns a governance problem into an export-compliance problem — if the board admits it relaxed compliance to boost sales, Nvidia faces penalties and audits, not just reputational damage. For buyers, the immediate task is to check the export-compliance warranties and the provenance of the chips in their contracts.

11/15 18:45 The Decoder ↗

Australia’s central bank sees the first employment cracks among young workers in the most AI-exposed jobs

The Decoder reported on November 15 that Mike Major, an economist at the Reserve Bank of Australia, has built a framework for spotting the early labour-market effects of AI by focusing on two places: entry-level roles and the most AI-exposed occupations such as programming, clerical work and customer service. Since ChatGPT launched in late 2022, job postings have fallen across all groups, but the drop is steepest in the most exposed group; within it, 15-to-24-year-olds and recent graduates have weaker employment rates and higher unemployment.

The report is explicit that this is not proof of causation: it measures theoretical exposure, not actual firm-level use, and business-cycle shifts or remote work could explain the numbers. For comparison, an August 2025 Stanford study of millions of US payroll records found employment among 22-to-25-year-olds in exposed occupations down 13% in relative terms, and nearly 20% among young software developers; a March 2026 Anthropic study found hiring rates for 22-to-25-year-olds down about 14%, with no systematic rise in overall unemployment.

There is counter-evidence too. A study of 25,000 Danish workers found no measurable change in wages or hours despite a 64% adoption rate, with employees saving an average of just 2.8% of working time. A University of Pittsburgh analysis of 10.6 million LinkedIn profiles found that weakness in exposed occupations began in early 2022, before ChatGPT launched.

Why it mattersThe value of this monitoring is that it breaks “AI takes jobs” into an observable entry point: not the overall unemployment rate, but whether young people can get into exposed occupations at all. If the door narrows while headline unemployment stays flat, the macro data will look fine while the talent pipeline is already shrinking. On policy, the 2.8% time saving suggests the efficiency dividend is still thin; what is changing is hiring structure, not headcount.
