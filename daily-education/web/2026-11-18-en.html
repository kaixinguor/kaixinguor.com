11/17 12:26 China Daily ↗

A federal judge vacates the fixed-term visa rule outright: D/S survives, and transfer or a second degree is no longer barred

According to China Daily's report, updated at 12:26 on November 17, Judge Dennis Saylor of the US District Court for the District of Massachusetts issued a final judgment on November 16 vacating in its entirety the Homeland Security rule that would have ended Duration of Status (D/S). The court found the rule "arbitrary, capricious and otherwise not in accordance with law", breaching the Administrative Procedure Act.

The rule was to take effect on September 15, 2026, replacing D/S with fixed admission periods capped at four years for F-1 students and J-1 exchange visitors and 240 days for I-visa foreign media representatives, and barring international students from transferring, changing academic objectives or taking a second degree at the same or a lower level. The judgment leaves the decades-old framework intact: provided they stay enrolled full time and follow visa rules, F-1 and J-1 holders may remain for as long as their programme and authorised post-graduation training require.

The procedural record mattered as much as the principle. The rule drew about 22,000 public comments warning of processing backlogs at US Citizenship and Immigration Services, disrupted research pipelines and a loss of global competitiveness for American universities, yet DHS set the effective date for September 15. A coalition including the Presidents' Alliance on Higher Education and Immigration, NAFSA, the American Federation of Teachers, the United Auto Workers and NewsGuild-CWA sued in the District of Massachusetts on August 18; the court granted a nationwide preliminary injunction on September 14, the day before the rule was to bite, later denied the government summary judgment and refused DHS's fallback request to send the rule back for revision without vacating it, noting that D/S had worked smoothly for decades and that the contested rule never took effect.

The government already has an appeal pending before the US Court of Appeals for the First Circuit over the September injunction and is expected to seek review of the final judgment too. Universities are bracing for a second fight: an October DHS proposal would charge institutions $70,000 for recommending each student for Optional Practical Training, plus $30,000 for a STEM OPT extension.

Why it mattersThe court vacated the whole rule rather than a clause, so international offices can stop building systems and staffing for a four-year renewal clock — but an appeal is already queued at the First Circuit, so both scenarios stay on file. For families here the decision clock moves: a four-year bachelor's plus a two-year master's no longer requires planning a renewal before year four, and transferring, switching majors or taking a second degree return to the old rules. The binding constraint has not gone: the $70,000 OPT fee and $30,000 STEM extension remain in a separate proposal, so the exit is still being priced, and the enrolment decline already booked will not reverse on a court order.
Another angleThose 22,000 comments became the factual foundation of the judgment, which sets a procedural bar for the next attempt: any future change to student stays has to come with a proper cost-benefit case, a plan for backlogs and an honest look at alternatives. What universities save is compliance cost, not tuition or exchange rates; database work and legal advice built for the fixed-term rule are already in 2025/26 budgets and become sunk costs if the appeal fails.

11/17 Ministry of Education ↗

Twelve original textbooks from nine universities: philosophy and sport science join the series for the first time

The Ministry of Education announced on November 17 that 12 original textbooks written by teams at nine universities have been published. The project on original textbooks for philosophy and social sciences now extends to philosophy and sport science, part of a push to build an independent Chinese knowledge system in those fields.

Philosophy enters the series with three separate volumes of Introduction to Philosophy, edited respectively by Sun Zhengyu of Jilin University, Li Deshun of China University of Political Science and Law, and Yang Guorong of East China Normal University, each approaching the subject's basic features, concepts and research scope from a different angle. Sport science also appears for the first time, with Yang Wenxuan of South China Normal University and Chen Qi of Guangzhou Sport University co-editing an Introduction to Sport that stresses the spirit of Chinese sport and the link between sport and social development.

The remaining eight titles sit in journalism and communication, education and psychology. Journalism accounts for three: Peng Lan of Renmin University of China and Li Liangrong of Fudan University each edit an Introduction to Online and New Media, while Tu Guangjin and Zhou Yong, both of Renmin University, edit News Commentary. Education accounts for three: Wang Benlu of Beijing Normal University on curriculum and teaching theory, Wu Zhihui of Northeast Normal University on education management, and Qian Minhui of Peking University on the sociology of education. Psychology contributes two editions of Educational Psychology, edited by Liu Rude of Beijing Normal University and Mo Lei of South China Normal University.

The ministry said the journalism, education and psychology volumes are meant to distil original theory, reflect a scholarly and professional consensus and adapt to new teaching conditions. By discipline the 12 titles cover five fields, with philosophy and sport science entering the project for the first time.

Why it mattersThree competing Introduction to Philosophy volumes mean one course can now run on three different architectures, with the department choosing which to teach. That is room for the classroom and a problem for equivalence: credit transfer, make-up work for students changing major and the scope of graduate entrance exams are the first things to feel it. More telling is that all three education titles are core disciplinary courses — curriculum and teaching theory, education management, sociology of education — the backbone of teacher training, so a new textbook drags teacher handbooks, lesson cases and in-service training along with it. Nine universities delivering 12 titles also concentrates capacity in Beijing and eastern China; the real test is classroom adoption and a revised edition two years out, not the publication notice.

11/18 China Education Daily ↗

Autumn job fairs pass 10,000: 264,000 employers, 5.515 million posts, 5.291 million graduate visits

China Education Daily reported on November 18 that the 2026 national city job-fair campaign for university graduates, launched on September 20, is still running hard. More than 2,400 public employment and talent agencies, several leading private recruitment firms and industry associations have combined to stage fairs and careers guidance across sectors and regions. So far they have held more than 10,000 fairs online and offline, with 264,000 employers offering 5.515 million posts and graduates making 5.291 million visits.

From September to November, local governments used the autumn window to dig out posts in the digital, green and low-altitude economies, advanced manufacturing and modern services, and organised small, specialised fairs around key industrial chains and hard-to-fill roles so that supply and demand met at the same point in time.

Two cases give a sense of scale. On October 24 the Jiangxi human resources and social security department held an equipment-manufacturing fair at Jiangxi Vocational and Technical University, with 320 employers from inside and outside the province offering more than 21,000 posts matched to the university's programmes. During the campaign the Inner Mongolia department led a fair for the three north-eastern provinces plus eastern Inner Mongolia, where more than 300 employers offered over 10,000 posts and drew more than 4,000 jobseekers.

The campaign is driven by the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security. Next steps include gathering more posts around graduates' needs and running varied recruitment and careers services so that talent is matched over a wider area.

Why it matters5.515 million posts against 5.291 million visits is roughly 1.04 posts per visit — the total is not the shortage; the match is. The posts sit on industrial chains such as advanced manufacturing and low-altitude aviation, and graduates' subjects, preferred cities and salary expectations do not always line up. For universities, a large share of those 10,000-plus fairs were staged by public employment agencies rather than campus careers centres, which argues for moving the emphasis from bringing employers on campus to teaching students how to read a vacancy: turning a chain's competency requirements into a course and internship list is slower work than another mass fair and more useful. Jiangxi placing its fair inside a vocational university, and Inner Mongolia assembling a cross-provincial one, show segmented and regional matching replacing the all-purpose job fair.

11/17 13:42 Zhejiang Provincial People's Government (eol.cn) ↗

Zhejiang approves two Sino-foreign vocational colleges: six programmes and 1,410 places at launch

The Zhejiang Provincial People's Government published an approval on November 16, dated November 3, agreeing to set up two Sino-foreign cooperative institutions: the Zhejiang Business Technology Institute Holmesglen College of Digital and Innovative Technology and the School of Applied Digital Technology at Zhejiang Financial College. Education portal eol.cn carried the full text at 13:42 on November 17.

The Holmesglen college is run by Zhejiang Business Technology Institute with Holmesglen Institute of Australia. It is a non-independent, college-level institution licensed until December 31, 2034, offering software technology, big data and accounting, and international business. Each programme enrols 90 college students a year under the national admissions plan, for a near-term total of 810. Graduates receive the Chinese college diploma and the Holmesglen diploma.

The digital technology school is run by Zhejiang Financial College with the University of Alcalá in Spain, also non-independent and licensed until December 31, 2033. It opens with financial technology application, big data technology and digital media art design, admitting 80 students a year in each of the first two and 40 in the third, for a near-term total of 600. Graduates receive the Zhejiang Financial College diploma and an equivalent qualification from Alcalá.

The approval instructs the provincial education department to supervise both institutions under the rules on Sino-foreign cooperation, and to guide the schools towards distinctive programmes and new training models. Tuition will follow state pricing rules, and the operating licences will be issued once the Ministry of Education assigns numbers.

Why it mattersBoth institutions sit in digital technology and both are college-level and non-independent, which takes Sino-foreign cooperation down from the international stream of a bachelor's programme into vocational education. For candidates that is one more route; for the colleges it is pressure on both tuition and staffing, since the value of a foreign diploma depends on curriculum alignment and teachers actually sent, and caps of 810 and 600 places mean these can only be small, expensive programmes that never dilute cost. Admissions is the real gate: with every place inside the national plan and tuition set by the state, they compete for the same score band as ordinary vocational programmes, selling the value of two diplomas without going abroad rather than a foreign ranking. The licences run only to 2033 and 2034, which leaves a renewal review as a check on quality.

11/17 Hong Kong Baptist University ↗

HKBU launches AITA, a course-aware AI tutor: more than 100 courses and 3,000 users

Hong Kong Baptist University announced on November 17 that AITA, a course-aware AI tutor grounded in collaboratively written course materials, is now live for the 2026/27 academic year. The platform was built by a team led by Professor Xu Jianliang, head and chair professor of computer science, with Dr Zhang Ce of the same department, Dr Fu Xiaoyi of journalism, Professor Yu Chuan of the Academy of Language and Culture, and scholars from the Chinese University of Hong Kong.

Unlike general-purpose tools, AITA plugs into the university's Moodle and reads course materials to generate summaries and mind maps automatically, and to add narration to recorded lectures synchronised with the matching slides. Students can highlight text on a slide or upload a chart or screenshot and ask questions by voice in several languages; answers cite their sources. Its learning and instruction agent also guides students through tasks, generates quizzes, gives real-time assessment and feedback, and analyses engagement and difficulty to recommend improvements to instructors.

Two technical choices stand out: a course-oriented retrieval-augmented generation system that anchors output in the uploaded materials to cut hallucination, and an agent that interprets a student's question and offers a route through the work. The platform is in use in more than 100 courses with over 3,000 registered users, 2,800 of them from HKBU; the rest come from CUHK and more than 20 institutions in the mainland and overseas, including Zhejiang University, Nanyang Technological University, the University of York and the University of Melbourne.

Development began in 2025 and continues under the University Grants Committee's Fund for Innovative Technology-in-Education for 2025-2028, as the team extends the platform from pilot courses to more institutions. AITA took second place at the second AIREA international competition on AI in education and was shortlisted for the QS Reimagine Education Awards 2026.

Why it mattersBeing course-aware is the line between this kind of tool and a general chatbot: an assistant whose answers are confined to what the teacher chose can plausibly be allowed in class and in assessment, while a general model cannot. That more than 200 of the 3,000-plus users come from 20-odd outside institutions suggests universities are settling into a division of labour — one shared tutor, each campus building its own corpus — which narrows the pricing power of commercial education models in higher education. For instructors the platform bundles quiz generation, engagement analysis and improvement advice into one door: it saves preparation and marking time, and hands over part of the authority to interpret how students learn, since nobody has yet written into the workflow who checks the agent's judgment about where the difficulty lies.

11/17 17:51 HKEX filing (CFi.cn) ↗

Wisdom Education guides to RMB 15.6 million profit: supply-chain and service revenue fall RMB 24.2 million

Wisdom Education International Holdings (06068.HK) issued a profit warning on November 17. After a preliminary review of unaudited consolidated management accounts for the year to August 31, 2026, the company expects unaudited consolidated net profit of about RMB 15.6 million, against audited net profit of about RMB 38.3 million a year earlier. CFi.cn posted the full filing at 17:51 that day.

The filing attributes the drop to three items: revenue from school-related supply-chain services fell about RMB 11.2 million, revenue from integrated education services fell about RMB 13.0 million, and administrative expenses rose about RMB 7.1 million. The two revenue lines together fell about RMB 24.2 million, while net profit fell about RMB 22.7 million.

The company cautioned that the figures rest only on a preliminary review and assessment by the board using information currently available, that the unaudited accounts for FY2026 are not final and have not been reviewed or audited by the auditor or seen by the audit committee, and that further adjustment is possible. Detailed financial information will appear in the annual results announcement.

That announcement is expected on or about November 26, 2026. The filing was signed by executive director Li Jiuchang and dated Dongguan.

Why it mattersNet profit falls from RMB 38.3 million to RMB 15.6 million, about 59%, while the two revenue lines fall only RMB 24.2 million and administrative costs rise RMB 7.1 million — profit shrinking faster than revenue usually means a bigger share of low-margin business, or fixed costs that did not contract with sales. For a private education group that has treated school-related supply chains as its growth engine, this is a curve worth watching: supply-chain revenue depends on enrolment and procurement concentration, so when student numbers plateau it shows up earlier than tuition. The November 26 results will break out segments, which is when it becomes possible to tell a deliberate retreat from a loss of students, and whether the rise in administrative costs was investment or drift.
