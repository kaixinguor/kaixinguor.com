11/18 11:29 Science and Technology Daily ↗

Tianjin University wins three "PhD plus master's" dual degrees: about five students each, picked internally

On November 18, Science and Technology Daily reported that Tianjin University's dual-degree proposal had passed expert review and that its first three submissions were approved: a synthetic biology PhD paired with an AI master's, an electrical engineering PhD with a master's in cyber and information security, and a power engineering and engineering thermophysics PhD with a chemical engineering master's. Students will be selected through an internal second round.

Tianjin is among the first universities approved for the national pilot. The framework dates to December 2025, when the State Council Academic Degrees Committee (国务院学位委员会) issued the Measures for the Pilot Establishment of "PhD plus Master's" Dual-Degree Projects (《「博士+硕士」双学位项目试点设置管理办法》). That was the first national rule allowing doctoral students to take a second master's in another discipline while enrolled. In June 2026 the State Council's 15th Five-Year Plan for education named the model a national task.

The intake and the process are already fixed. Each project takes about five doctoral students in its first cohort, selected from candidates already enrolled and judged on interdisciplinary aptitude. Supervision comes from a joint team made up of the doctoral supervisor, a master's-level subject supervisor and a company mentor; each project gets its own curriculum and degree standards, and the three are tied to biomanufacturing, power and energy, and green propulsion.

China Education Daily reported the same day that AI is the most common master's side of the early pilots. Fudan University runs "academic PhD plus professional AI master's" across 10 strong disciplines — philosophy, economics, journalism and communication, mathematics and basic medicine among them — with a first cohort of 10 doctoral students chosen internally. Nanjing University has made computer science and AI its core intersection, and Xi'an Jiaotong University announced its launch on November 10. The measures require a separate thesis or practice-based result for each degree, with the two research fields overlapping and the master's work normally supporting or supplementing the doctorate.

Why it mattersA dual degree is not an extra certificate; it carves a slice out of the four to five years a doctorate already takes. An internal second round redistributes admissions quotas inside the university: a hot master's field such as AI absorbs places that would otherwise go to a department's own master's cohort, and somebody has to decide whose quota shrinks and who pays the coordination cost of a cross-faculty supervisory team. The binding constraint is at the exit. Two separate theses, two defences, and the risk of a delayed graduation sit with the student; whether the stipend, the dormitory and the registration status follow the doctoral or the master's track is left to each university.
Another angleTen disciplines taking 10 students at Fudan, and three projects of about five each at Tianjin, show that this is small-sample validation rather than expansion. For a faculty it looks more like a bid for interdisciplinary work: whoever can assemble a cross-school supervisory team and turn a company problem into a thesis topic gets the students. If the evaluation two years out only asks whether they graduated on time, departments will pick the low-risk combinations and the interdisciplinary edge will be sanded down.

11/18 14:29 Jilin Provincial Department of Finance ↗

Jilin releases RMB 58.9 million for weak links in compulsory education, with a negative list on spending

At 14:29 on November 18, the Jilin Provincial Department of Finance published a notice allocating RMB 58.9 million of 2026 central funding to improve weak links in compulsory education and build capacity (Ji Cai Jiao Zhi No. 738, 2026). The money is booked under transfer-payment revenue for shared education responsibilities (code 1100245), with spending classified under general education (20502) and as a transfer between levels of government (51301).

The notice rests on the Ministry of Finance and Ministry of Education budget document Cai Jiao No. 198 of 2026 and requires compliance with the management measures for the fund (Cai Jiao No. 127 of 2021). Localities must optimise the allocation of school places against shifting school-age cohorts and urbanisation, plan urban and rural schools together, close gaps in buildings and safety, and improve schools' capacity in science education and mental-health counselling.

The prohibitions are specific: no prestige projects or luxury construction; no spending on staff pay, budget balancing, debt repayment, interest or outbound investment; no deduction of working or management costs. The fund must also be coordinated with the long-term mechanism for rural school building safety and with capital projects approved by development and reform commissions, so that money and projects neither overlap nor leave gaps, and so that diversion, embezzlement or fraudulent claims are prevented.

The fund falls under routine supervision of transfer-payment execution: county finance bureaux must register the indicators in the budget-management system and keep the "trace" tag intact. The notice is dated November 17, 2026, and attaches the 2026 budget table and a regional performance-target table.

Why it mattersThe RMB 58.9 million is a capacity-building transfer, not per-pupil operating funding, so whether it gets spent depends on how far projects have been prepared — which is why the notice demands early preparation and faster execution. In county finance that usually means having lists of laboratories, playing fields and dormitories ready before the money arrives, or watching it turn into a carry-over at year end. The negative list closes the door on staff costs: this money can become buildings, equipment and renovation, but not teachers. Naming science education and mental-health counselling as eligible uses marks the difference from the older "weak links" wording. The real constraint is demography: because the notice ties planning to school-age cohorts, a county losing population that applies on the old building gap will spend on classrooms that will soon be empty.

11/18 21:37 Shaoguan municipal information office (via Tencent News) ↗

Guangdong's Hunan recruitment tour closes with 20,458 post listings and nearly 20,000 visitors

On the afternoon of November 18, the Hunan leg of the 2026 "A Million Talents to Guangdong" autumn recruitment campaign closed at Central South University of Forestry and Technology. The campaign is co-hosted by the Ministry of Education, the Guangdong provincial party committee and the provincial government; from November 16 to 18, Shaoguan took employers into Xiangtan University, Hunan University of Science and Technology and Central South University of Forestry and Technology.

Across the three fairs, 563 employer participations offered 20,458 post listings: 14,437 for bachelor's graduates, 3,682 for master's and 867 for doctoral candidates. Nearly 20,000 graduates attended and 9,930 CVs were collected on site. By post count, bachelor's accounted for 70.6%, master's for 18.0% and doctoral for 4.2%.

The halls were zoned by industry — state-owned enterprises and universities, advanced materials, advanced equipment manufacturing, big data and electronic information, and a general zone — mapped onto each campus's strengths: mathematics, computer science and materials at Xiangtan; engineering, chemistry and materials at Hunan University of Science and Technology; forestry engineering and ecology at Central South University of Forestry and Technology. Employers included Guangdong Dongyangguang Technology Holding, Guangdong Shaoneng Group, Shaoguan University, China Energy Engineering Guangdong Thermal Power, China Construction Fifth Engineering Bureau Southern Construction and Shenzhen Xintuo Network Technology.

Local cooperation followed the fairs: six agreements were signed, three between Shaoguan's human resources and social security bureau and the three campuses' careers services, and one each between the city's forestry bureau and the forestry college, between Shaoguan University's Marxism school and Xiangtan's, and between the city bar association and Xiangtan's law faculty. Each campus also supplied 10 Guangdong-born student "ambassadors" for the campaign. Shaoguan is building its "1+8" industrial plan around big data as the lead sector, alongside advanced materials, advanced equipment, biomedicine and forestry ecology.

Why it matters20,458 posts against nearly 20,000 visitors looks close to one-to-one, but the mix is what counts: seven posts in ten are for bachelor's graduates, fewer than one in five for master's and 4.2% for doctorates, while the three Hunan campuses are strongest in mathematics, materials, engineering and forestry. The post list matched the subject list, which is why employers reported dozens of relevant CVs in half a day. For regional universities, this kind of cross-province fair shifts competition from reputation to willingness to bear the organisational cost: Shaoguan sent city leaders, zoned the hall by industrial chain and bundled campus ambassadors with inter-institutional agreements, effectively trading administrative resources for student attention. The costs are shared too — employers pay travel and booths, campuses suspend classes and organise, students pay for the trip — and all three are needed to produce 9,930 CVs.

11/18 Liaoning Provincial Department of Education ↗

Liaoning publishes draft list of provincial industry-education projects, open for comment for seven days

On November 18, the Liaoning Provincial Department of Education published a notice inviting comment on the draft 2026 list of provincial industry-education integration projects. Under the selection notice Liao Jiao Tong No. 214 of 2026, and after institutional applications and expert review, the department released two attachments: a draft list of municipal industry-education consortia and a draft list of sectoral industry-education communities.

Comment runs from November 18 to November 24, seven days. Objections must be in writing to the department's vocational and adult education division, must be factual, carry an official seal or a real name, and include valid contact details; late or anonymous submissions will not be accepted, and the postmark date governs mailed letters.

Nationally, this is one more confirmation of the provincial layer of a larger system. China Education Daily reported on November 18 that the country has cultivated 34 national-level municipal industry-education consortia, covering advanced manufacturing, the digital economy and modern commerce. Such consortia are meant to link education, talent, industry and innovation chains, and the quality of their cooperation mechanisms shapes how deep integration actually goes.

The notice gives an address in Huanggu district, Shenyang (46-1 Chongshan East Road) and the phone number 024-86896319. The lists themselves sit in the attachments; the main text gives neither the number of projects nor the funding attached to them.

Why it mattersA provincial draft list is the most concrete link in the industry-education chain: national consortia are designated by the ministry, but provincial projects decide who gets provincial earmarked money, land and tax coordination. Splitting the list in two means municipal consortia (government, park, colleges) and sectoral communities (associations, lead firms, colleges) are judged on different criteria — a college has to know whether the lead is a government or a company before it knows which list to aim for, because the metrics and the money differ. Seven days of comment is the only window for objection, and anonymous or late filings are refused, which puts the burden of evidence on the participants: miss the week and the next round is a year away. That the main text lists neither project numbers nor amounts also makes it hard for outsiders to judge, inside the window, who was picked and on what basis.

11/18 14:46 China Government Procurement Network ↗

A county high school spends RMB 8.13 million on a smart campus, 160 AI tutoring systems included

At 14:46 on November 18, the China Government Procurement Network published the award notice for the smart-campus project at Qingshuihe County Senior High School (project 150124-HSZC-GK-20260005). Inner Mongolia Hailiang Digital Technology won with a bid of RMB 8.132266 million and a composite score of 92.09 under a price-deduction rule. The buyer is the school; the Hohhot municipal procurement centre acted as agent, and the agency fee was zero.

The itemised bid puts the explicitly AI-labelled spending in seven lines: 160 AI tutoring systems at RMB 1,900 each (RMB 304,000); 160 AI exam-preparation systems at RMB 1,900 each (RMB 304,000); 160 AI study terminals, iFlytek U10, at RMB 4,780 each (RMB 764,800); one classroom behaviour-analytics system at RMB 85,000; 12 AI edge-computing terminals at RMB 303,600; one vector-database appliance at RMB 329,800; and one AI brain-computer interface "psychology pod" at RMB 100,000. The seven lines total about RMB 2.19 million, roughly 27% of the award.

Roughly seven-tenths of the money sits elsewhere: 10 library-lending sets at RMB 975,000, a WPS education and office platform at RMB 979,000, 24 laboratory ceiling-integration sets at RMB 528,000, laboratory environment works at RMB 169,300, plus recorders, sports equipment and fitness machines. Delivery is due within 60 days of contract signature, with installation and commissioning completed.

The same day offered a contrasting sample. At 17:26, the network published Zhejiang Sci-Tech University's award for an AI smart-course learning platform: RMB 495,000 to Beijing Century Chaoxing Information Technology, composite score 89.5, with accounts to be activated within 30 working days and usable for one year from activation. One county high school bought hardware and systems outright; one provincial university rented a platform by the year.

Why it mattersWhat is RMB 8.13 million to a county high school? It is years of deferred equipment needs — laboratories, library, sports, office software — bundled into a single purchase, with AI as the roughly RMB 2.19 million layer on top. The structure matters more than the total: 160 AI terminals implies the school is configuring for at least 160 students at once, three or four classes, at RMB 4,780 a seat, with 160 licences each of tutoring and exam prep at RMB 1,900. For the county this is a one-off capital outlay; for the school, maintenance, renewals and teacher training after the 60-day delivery are absent from the award notice, and they are the real bill in year two. A single vendor covering laboratories, library, sports, software and AI saves on tender rounds, and also removes the bargaining space.

11/18 China Education News ↗

The Chinese Academy of Educational Sciences launches "Jiaozhi No.1", a research agent, before nearly 300 people

On November 15, the Chinese Academy of Educational Sciences (中国教育科学研究院) held a conference in Beijing on AI and new research paradigms, and launched an agent called "Jiaozhi No.1" (教智1号). China Education News reported the event on November 18. Li Yongzhi, the academy's party secretary and president, opened the meeting, which was chaired by vice-president Rong Lei.

The agent was developed under the academy's guidance and is operated by its audio-visual press. It is positioned as a professional research agent: it combines AI with the academy's education-research data, uses retrieval-augmented generation to ground answers, and runs on a domestic technology stack, with accuracy, security and controllability as the stated goals. It is aimed at research institutes, researchers and frontline teachers. Liu Mingtang, head of the press, demonstrated it, and the first batch of pilot co-building institutions was announced at the meeting.

Nearly 300 experts and officials from provincial research institutes, universities and schools attended. Among them were Li Jianping of the Tianjin academy, Xie Weihe of Tsinghua University, Li Zhengtao of the Chinese Society of Education and Jiang Kai of Peking University's school of education. A roundtable asked what an AI-driven research paradigm can and cannot do.

The breakout sessions converged on boundaries. Jiang Kuilin of Yichang's institute argued that research management should move from experience to data, and that the scarce resource in the AI era is the courage to pose real questions. Jiang Libing of Ningxia's teaching research office proposed pooling foundational resources and widening the pilot. Shen Jiale of Zhejiang's institute offered four rules — stop, disclose, verify, protect: define the boundary, disclose use transparently, keep results checkable, and protect privacy.

Why it mattersResearch agents are paid for by institutes and schools, not by parents, and what they sell is not generation but verifiability: only an answer anchored in project data and literature by retrieval can enter grant applications, result appraisal and teaching-research routines. The real barrier is data. A county institute rarely has a corpus of its own, so it needs a provincial platform to supply content; the first batch of "pilot co-building institutions" is therefore an exchange of data and scenarios rather than a software licence. For teachers, the tool saves time on literature reviews and data sorting, and takes away part of the question-setting: if the agent picks the topic, the subjecthood of teaching research is quietly replaced.

11/18 15:50 China Fund News ↗

Xueda Education puts RMB 50 million into Open Source China for a 1.23% stake

China Fund News reported at 15:50 on November 18 that Xueda Education (000526.SZ) had announced it would use RMB 50 million of its own funds to subscribe to RMB 469,700 of newly registered capital in Shanghai Open Source Consensus Intelligent Technology, with the remaining RMB 49.5303 million booked as capital reserve. After the increase it will hold 1.23%, the target will not be consolidated, and the investment awaits conditions for closing.

At RMB 50 million for 1.23%, the post-money valuation works out to about RMB 4.065 billion, a derived figure that does not account for other investors or closing adjustments. The timeline: the board approved the investment at its ninth meeting of the eleventh term on October 25, disclosure followed on October 27 via Cninfo, and on November 17 the company signed the capital-increase agreement with Open Source China, founding shareholders and institutional investors, together with a shareholder agreement effective from closing.

The company frames the deal as a way to capture the convergence of AI and education. It also warns that closing conditions remain, that returns depend on the target's operations, future financing and capital-market plans, and that the investment involves uncertainty. There is no related-party transaction, no major asset restructuring and no need for shareholder approval.

The accounting is worth separating out: 1.23% and no consolidation mean Open Source China's revenue and losses will not reach Xueda's income statement, and Xueda gains no control over its technology. For a company whose main business is tutoring and vocational education, RMB 50 million is a small strategic stake that does not affect current operations.

Why it mattersRMB 50 million for 1.23%, on a valuation of about RMB 4.07 billion, is an AI option: too small to move this year's profit, large enough to put "AI plus education" into an annual report and an investor presentation. The signal is in the structure and the accounting — own cash, no consolidation, 1.23% — which means Xueda takes neither the operating risk nor the technological control. Without a joint product, channel revenue share or co-built curriculum, the deal changes the core business by close to nothing. For education companies, the return on AI lies not in the size of the stake but in whether model capability can be wired into existing campuses, teachers and timetables, and that requires procurement and teaching-research budgets rather than an equity cheque.
