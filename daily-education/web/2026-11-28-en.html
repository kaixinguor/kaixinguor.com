Policy & Regulation

11/27 09:07 Inner Mongolia Finance Department ↗

Inner Mongolia sends about RMB 516 million to border schools, with RMB 54.73 million for gate schools

A public kindergarten in an Inner Mongolia border banner may get one more activity room next year, or a new set of teaching equipment. The regional finance department said on November 27 that RMB 81.84 million had been allocated in 2026 for preschool places in border banners, covering construction, expansion and equipment.

Compulsory education got two lines. Nearly RMB 103 million went to weak-link improvement, and more than RMB 205 million to the urban-rural compulsory education subsidy. The first pays for buildings and school conditions; the second covers fee waivers and subsidies, the "double reduction" policy and the cost of running schools.

Two more lines sit on the border itself: RMB 54.73 million for "gate schools" and RMB 71.11 million for high-school conditions. The high-school money is for dormitories, laboratories, multimedia kit, safety and water systems. The five lines add up to about RMB 516 million.

The money comes from central and regional budgets under the region's "1571" programme. The finance department said it would tighten supervision of payments, use and performance, and keep the funds to their designated purposes.

LookRMB 516 million is a headline figure, but the per-school amount depends on project lists that have not been published. The preschool and high-school lines are capital money, not staffing money; salaries and running costs still fall on banner budgets. Watch the county project notices, equipment unit prices, and the number of new boarding places at gate schools.
Another angleSchool-age numbers in border counties are falling, so buildings sized for a peak may sit empty. Using new space for combined middle and high schools would stretch the same money further and keep more students in the county.

11/27 10:44 CCTV News ↗

The "spring 2027" ninth-grade textbooks on sale are pirated; the real ones are not out yet

A parent who sees a "spring 2027" ninth-grade textbook on Douyin, Xiaohongshu or WeChat with six fingers on the cover should not order it. The national education rumour platform confirmed on November 27 that the books are pirated; the real edition has not been printed.

CCTV reported at 10:44 on November 27 that the books claim to cover morality and law, Chinese and history. The cover names "People's Compulsory Education Press" instead of "People's Education Press"; the ISBN cannot be checked and the copyright page is incomplete.

Physical copies share the same defects: thin paper, harsh ink and blurred print. That matches the pattern of earlier seizures: scanned plates, offset reprints, rotating shops and stores that close after selling out. The genuine ninth-grade volume is due to be delivered by schools and local education departments before the spring 2027 term, and is not yet in retail.

Regulators are putting the fake publisher's name on e-commerce blacklists and asking platforms to check licences, purchase receipts and business registrations. They are also sweeping listings with "spring 2027", "advance copy" and "colour print". Parents can report to 12390, 12315, 12377 or the national rumour platform.

LookThe issue is the channel, not the content. With the real book still outside retail, no online "advance copy" can have a legal supply chain; if platforms delete listings without checking licences, the same sellers reopen under new names. For parents, the safe test is to wait for the school delivery or buy from Xinhua or the publisher's official store. Watch whether the blacklist actually blocks new shops, and how long copycat listings survive after a sweep.

Vocational & Careers

职教与就业
11/27 10:07 Modern Higher Vocational Education Network ↗

A national vocational report names 100 standout schools and a 5-16-29 indicator grid

Whether a vocational college does industry-education integration well has usually been judged from written materials. A report released in Ningbo on November 26 tries to turn that into a comparable score.

The "Higher Vocational Education Industry-Education Integration Development Report" was compiled by the national association of vocational college presidents and published by Xinhua Press. The education portal released it at 10:07 on November 27 at the association's 2026 annual meeting; secretary-general Ren Junqing presented it and named 100 "excellent schools".

The index has three layers: five first-level, 16 second-level and 29 third-level indicators. The top layer covers support for industry, contribution to talent training, delivery of key elements, platform resources and support for firms going abroad. The bottom layer includes patent commercialisation and the share of graduates in matched jobs.

The top quarter of schools tend to tie programme changes to industry demand, run joint training with employers and have stronger "double-qualified" teachers. The bottom quarter is weak in regional industry service, platform building and taking vocational programmes overseas.

LookThe list names the top quarter as models, but the 29 bottom-level indicators will do the real work. Once provinces adopt them, colleges will shift money toward what can be counted, and humanities or general courses may become more marginal. Watch whether provincial reviews use the index, whether the 100 schools actually beat the average on patent deals and matched employment, and whether the bottom quarter publishes improvement plans.

11/27 10:21 Guizhou Provincial Education Department ↗

Guizhou shortlists 16 colleges for its 2025-27 "double high" build

Sixteen Guizhou vocational colleges have been shortlisted for the province's "double high" construction plan, with a public comment period running to December 1. For the winners, the label brings a provincial project identity for programme groups and training bases over the next three years.

The provincial education department published the list at 10:21 on November 27. The selection follows the province's 2025-27 "double high" plan; schools applied between September and October, then faced document review and site visits.

The expert panel recommended 16 institutions, including Guiyang Health Vocational University. The comment period lasts five days; objections must be filed in writing with the vocational education office, stamped for organisations and signed for individuals.

The notice does not name a funding amount. Provincial "double high" status usually comes with programme, course, training-base and teacher projects, and the money is set in a later document. For colleges, selection is the start; a mid-term review decides whether the rest of the funding arrives.

LookThe label decides which programme groups get provincial projects and equipment for three years. The window is only five days, and objections must be in writing, so teachers and employer partners who miss it will find the list hard to change. Watch the funding line in the approval document, the industry chain each group is tied to, and whether the mid-term review can remove a college. A list that never removes anyone is an honour; one that can is a planning tool.

Guangdong puts RMB 13.11 million into education IT, including RMB 7.69 million for a data centre

Guangdong has allocated RMB 13.109558 million in the second batch of 2026 education IT money across six projects. The largest, RMB 7.69 million, goes to an education big-data centre.

The provincial education department published the allocation at 10:22 on November 27; comments run to December 3, seven days. The budget line is the education development (IT and innovation) fund.

The rest: RMB 3 million to Guangzhou's education bureau for AI-in-education school scenarios; RMB 1.526488 million to a large-model and agent action involving five institutions led by South China Normal University; RMB 357,500 each to Guangdong Polytechnic Normal University and South China University of Technology for basic and advanced AI teacher training; and RMB 178,070 to finish the third phase of a graduate employment platform.

The notice says "proposed allocation", and objections must be in writing by December 3. The amounts are project budgets, not recurrent funding for schools.

LookOf RMB 13.11 million, RMB 7.69 million builds the data base, RMB 3 million buys school scenarios in Guangzhou, and teacher training gets only RMB 715,000. The weight is on platforms, not people. Once the data centre's interfaces and standards are fixed, they lock in what applications schools can buy later. For teachers, the value of a RMB 357,500 training round depends on how many it reaches and whether it counts toward promotion. Watch the number of schools connected to the centre, how many seed teachers finish, and which schools the RMB 3 million covers.

11/27 Yunnan Normal University ↗

Yunnan joins Fuguang with 281 teachers from 16 prefectures and 75 pilot schools

Two hundred and eighty-one teacher trainers and "seed" teachers from 16 prefectures in Yunnan began a residential programme at Yunnan Normal University on November 26. They will return to lead "1+2" teams in 75 project schools.

This is the Yunnan leg of Fuguang, a Ministry of Education programme run with Peking University, coordinated by the provincial education department and hosted by Yunnan Normal University, with support from Tencent's social value unit. Yunnan is one of the first five provinces in the programme.

The programme covers policy, AI literacy, subject integration and ethics. The 75 schools must start their return-to-work projects within two weeks; by the end of December, all 16 prefectures are to hold joint regional research and live demonstrations.

All teachers are also expected to complete six hours of online training through the national smart education platform. The model is "seed leads, backbone follows, everyone covered", with follow-up evaluation by the province, the university and Tencent.

LookTraining 281 people is the easy part; the test is whether 75 schools can put AI lessons on the timetable within two weeks. Yunnan ties the "1+2" teams to six hours of whole-school training, so the seeds are expected to teach colleagues, not just attend. Watch the AI hours in those schools, the number of school-based sessions each seed runs, and whether the December livestreams show real classrooms. The other risk is hardware: without enough bandwidth and computing in county schools, the training stays on paper.

11/27 HKEX filing ↗

China Science and Education Industry posts a RMB 345 million fiscal 2026 loss after a RMB 383 million tax catch-up

China Science and Education Industry earned RMB 1.535 billion in fiscal 2026, up 6.2%, but swung from a RMB 458 million profit to a RMB 345 million loss. Income tax did it.

The company filed its annual results with the Hong Kong exchange on November 27. Income tax for the year was RMB 472.1 million, against RMB 37.6 million a year earlier, a rise of 1,156.7%. Of that, about RMB 383.1 million was a prior-period adjustment after a re-assessment of how academic education income is taxed, and RMB 174.9 million was a late-payment surcharge.

Local tax authorities re-assessed the education sector in the region and told colleges to treat academic education income as taxable for certain periods, the company said. Management applied a 25% rate and booked the change as an accounting estimate. The amounts are estimates, not a confirmed tax liability or a final bill, and talks with the tax authority continue.

Operationally, the group runs three schools with 59,238 students, up 6.4%. Gross profit fell 18.7% to RMB 587.8 million and gross margin dropped from 50.0% to 38.3%. Adjusted net profit fell 49.6% to RMB 231.6 million. Capital spending rose 48.1% to RMB 576.9 million; borrowings were RMB 2.149 billion and the debt-to-equity ratio 53.9%.

The company is not the first Hong Kong-listed private educator to put income tax at the centre of a profit warning this week. On November 24, China Education Group said it expected Chinese corporate income tax to rise by RMB 250 million to RMB 290 million in fiscal 2026, an increase of 74% to 86%.

LookThe accounts expose a hidden cost in private higher education. If a local tax bureau reclassifies academic tuition as taxable, back taxes and surcharges both hit the current year. For this company, RMB 383.1 million of back tax and RMB 174.9 million of surcharges add up to RMB 558 million, close to a third of annual revenue. For the sector, whether other private colleges face the same re-assessment depends on provincial tax practice. Watch the final determination, tax provisions at other listed educators, and whether the effective rate returns to normal in fiscal 2027.
