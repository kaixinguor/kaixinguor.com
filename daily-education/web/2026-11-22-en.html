Fujian's preschool report: 6,470 kindergartens, 1.015 million children, 95.19% affordable coverage

Xinhua filed from Fuzhou at 18:18 on November 21: the previous day the standing committee of Fujian's 14th provincial people's congress reviewed the provincial government's report on preschool education. The baseline numbers are 6,470 kindergartens, 1.015 million children enrolled, a three-year enrolment rate of 99.52%, 67.92% of children in public kindergartens, and 95.19% of places classified as affordable.

The public share has a comparison point. At 67.92% Fujian sits 8.52 percentage points above the national average of 59.4% (derived). On accreditation, 38 counties and districts have passed national review for universal and affordable preschool, ninth most in the country, and Xiamen became one of the first prefecture-level cities to pass across its whole territory. The stated goal is universal, affordable preschool in every county before 2030.

On spending, the province says it built, expanded or upgraded 1,008 public kindergartens during the 14th five-year plan period, lifting the public share by 13.4 percentage points since 2020. Per-child operating funding of RMB 600 a year is fully in place, alongside subsidies for affordable private kindergartens and the fee waiver for the final pre-school year. On staffing, 28,000 kindergarten teachers have been recruited since 2021, with training rounds targeted at grassroots kindergartens.

The report also names the problems: falling births are shrinking the cohort, some private kindergartens have closed, and the redistribution of affordable places has not kept pace. Quality gaps persist between urban and rural kindergartens and between public and private ones, and some teachers need support in digital skills and in talking to families. Next steps include a monitoring and early-warning system for the school-age population, shifting places between school stages and across regions, and keeping public central kindergartens and necessary rural sites open.

Why it matters95.19% and 67.92% measure different things: the first is the share of places deemed affordable, counting public kindergartens and subsidised private ones; the second is the share of children actually in a publicly funded seat. The 27.27-point gap between them is where policy has to work (derived). With births falling, high coverage does not make adjustment easy — closing private classes is a market decision while protecting public staffing and per-child funding runs through the staffing and finance offices. At 1.015 million children and RMB 600 each, operating funding comes to roughly RMB 609 million a year (derived), a recurring county-level cost booked separately from the 1,008 construction projects. As enrolment falls, the total shrinks faster than kindergartens and teachers can exit, so unit costs rise before they fall.
Another angleA public share 8.52 points above the national average means Fujian carries affordable preschool mainly through public kindergartens: subsidised private providers get a grant, public ones get staffing quotas and per-child funding, and the long-run fiscal commitment for the same child differs by an order of magnitude. For parents, prices stay stable in the short run; for private operators the live question is whether to convert to affordable status or exit, and at county level the answer depends on how many places are still missing.

11/21 20:45 Xinhua ↗

Chang'an University: recruitment notice was misdated, not pre-decided; three officials disciplined

Xinhua carried the university's statement at 20:45 on November 21. Candidates had complained that after a November 14 preliminary interview for student-adviser posts, the university website showed results dated November 13 — a day before the interview — and suspected the list had been settled in advance.

The statement reconstructs the sequence. After the interviews on November 14, human resources staff pasted the list of candidates who had passed into a notice draft first created in the website back end on November 13, and published it at 18:55 that day. Nobody noticed that the notice's publication date still showed the draft's creation date, and reviewers missed it too. After candidates raised it, the office corrected the date at 20:31 on the 14th.

The finding: a task force led by the disciplinary committee examined the files and the server logs and confirmed that the published names were in fact those who passed the preliminary interview on the 14th. There was no list fixed before the interview.

The penalties cite the Communist Party accountability regulations and the rules on organisational handling of Party members. The section chief in charge was suspended pending review, the deputy director overseeing the work was reassigned, and the head of the human resources department received a formal admonition. The university apologised for the public concern and the damage done, and promised tighter internal checks.

Why it mattersThis is not evidence of a rigged hire; it is a failure of a content system and of review, twice over. The notice's publication date defaults to the draft's creation timestamp — a textbook case of a data field decoupled from the business event — and the pre-publication check never compared the two. Three people were held responsible, but the real fixes are a field validation on the publishing page and a second pair of eyes on any notice that publishes names. The same error can surface on any university's recruitment, admissions or scholarship page. The cost structure is worth noting: one news cycle bought three penalties and a system-wide self-check, while the list itself was sound. For candidates, procedural legitimacy and fairness of outcome are separate accounts, and the first cannot be restored by a statement issued afterwards — only by the check that should have happened before publication.

11/21 17:16 Northeast News Network ↗

Shenyang's Tiexi district names two illegal tutoring cases, one run out of a childcare licence

Northeast News Network published the Tiexi district education bureau's enforcement notice at 17:16 on November 21. The bureau reported two cases of illegal off-campus tutoring, part of a campaign against disguised and mutated training and a separate drive on teacher conduct.

The first is an unlicensed individual class on the second floor of No. 12A-4, Nanshidong Road. The operator, surnamed Zhao, held neither a school licence nor a business licence and taught academic subjects to children in compulsory education; inspectors found primary-school pupils in an offline academic class. Under Article 18 of the interim measures on administrative penalties for off-campus training, the operator was warned, pupils were dispersed and the class was ordered to stop immediately.

The second is Shenyang Jinshuo Education Consulting, at No. 32-5, Nanshiyi Road. Its business licence covers only childcare and consulting, with no school licence from the education authority, yet it taught academic subjects to compulsory-education pupils under the label of childcare; inspectors found two outside staff teaching primary pupils. The same article applied: the company was ordered to stop, its operator warned, and it was told to trade strictly within its licensed scope.

Why it mattersThe two cases are the two standard shapes — no licence at all, and a licence for something else — and both end at a warning plus a stop order, with no fine. That shows the limits of the toolbox at county level: for an individual or a tiny outfit, closure is the strongest available measure, but for a company hiding behind a childcare licence, "trade within your licensed scope" leaves childcare intact and academic tutoring one rebrand away. The cost lands on the district bureau's staff: every inspection needs people, records and paperwork, and these classes sit in residential blocks where detection depends on complaints. What would actually reduce repeat offences is not heavier penalties but routine inspection of a childcare company's lesson timetables and payment flows.

11/21 11:09 People's Daily Online ↗

Fujian Open University opens a battery-sector college with a company and a technician institute

People's Daily Online's Fujian channel reported at 11:09 on November 21 that the Fujian Open University Feimaotui New Energy College was unveiled on the afternoon of November 19 at Fujian Sanjiang Technician Institute. The three partners are Fujian Open University, Feimaotui (Fujian) Electronics and the technician institute.

The roles were set out at the ceremony. Feimaotui vice-president Jiang Zhicheng described the group's industrial footprint and the institute's results, saying the three parties would pool resources and match programmes to job requirements in the new-energy sector. Wu Jianming, a vice-president of Fujian Open University, framed the college as a "university–company–technician institute" model with two concrete tracks: a "1+X" system that links academic credit to skills certificates, connecting education, talent, industry and innovation chains; and more flexible teaching so that students can study while working.

The event included a mentorship ceremony for a "master craftsman" class and a lecture on electrical automation, and participants toured the Feimaotui Power Technology workshops and the institute's exhibition hall to review training conditions. The report describes the college as extending the open university's reach into the industrial workforce.

Why it mattersThe combination targets an old mismatch: technician institutes issue skills grades, open universities issue academic credentials, and companies provide the machines and the mentors — three systems that rarely recognise each other's hours. The hard part of a 1+X scheme is not the timetable but credit recognition: whether workshop hours convert into university credits, who signs off, and whether the converted credit counts for promotion or a settling-in permit. That decides whether "study while working" is a channel or a slogan. The cost sits with the employer: workshops used as classrooms and skilled workers used as teachers have no stable funding line, so the expense lands on the company's own labour budget — and when orders dip, mentoring hours are among the first things cut.

11/21 09:43 China News Service · Henan ↗

Zhengzhou University opens a technology business school backed by four provincial bodies

China News Service's Henan service reported at 09:43 on November 21 that on November 19 the provincial education department, the science and technology department, the state-owned assets supervision commission and Zhengzhou University jointly hosted a conference on industry-education integration and the unveiling of the Zhengzhou University Henan Technology Business School.

President Li Peng set the mission: the school is part of a strategy to build a science-education powerhouse and to train technology-transfer managers and innovation professionals — people who understand markets, technology, industry and business. The method, she said, is to build classes on real projects and to extend the faculty into industry. Vice education director Zhu Zifeng was blunter: the new school is "not simply adding a college and hanging up a plaque" but part of reworking the national university science park and integrating education, science and talent policy, with classes moved into pilot plants, factory floors and pitch rooms.

The ceremony also did several concrete things. Li Peng and Qin Shuguang, head of the provincial party personnel department's talent division, presented appointment letters to technology-transfer managers. The business school's leadership read a joint construction pledge covering coordination, joint training, research on how transfer works, landing results and sharing gains. The university's national science park released more than 100 technologies ready for transfer, and deans of technology business schools from the Central University of Finance and Economics, Hohai University and Jiangxi University of Finance and Economics spoke on goals and difficulties.

Why it mattersThe asset here is not a degree programme but the supply of a new occupation — the technology-transfer manager. Bringing the education, science and state-asset departments to one table merges three networks: university outputs, state-owned enterprise use cases and financial instruments. Who is enrolled, where classes happen and where projects come from are no longer a business school's private decisions. The cost is faculty: people who know industry are mostly outside the staffing quota, and how industry professors are paid, assessed and cleared on conflicts of interest will decide whether this is real transfer work or a plaque in three years' time. On the labour market, technology-transfer managers have no common qualification or pay band yet, so the first cohort's exit depends on whether the province's technology trading market can offer enough pilot-testing and commercialisation posts. The 100-plus technologies are inventory awaiting transfer, not signed contracts.

Harbin University of Science and Technology becomes northeast China's first "AI beacon campus"

Science and Technology Daily reported at 22:37 on November 21 that Harbin University of Science and Technology signed a strategic agreement with Alibaba Cloud and unveiled an "AI beacon campus". President Liu Xia said the university would combine its mechanical and electrical engineering base with Alibaba Cloud's work in cloud computing, AI and large models, embedding AI in teaching, research and campus governance.

Alibaba group vice-president Xu Shijun described the mechanism: the programme is run by Qianwen Office and offered to universities nationwide, aiming to build a campus AI ecosystem and a replicable model for university AI rollout. Harbin is the first university in northeast China to join, and receives seats on the Qianwen Office suite, AI course training and support for running AI competitions.

The two sides list five areas of cooperation: computing capacity, AI in teaching and research, upgrades to the smart campus, joint talent training and graduate employment. The report does not disclose how many seats, how many courses, how much money or how long the agreement runs.

Why it mattersDeals like this package general-purpose model capability as a campus service in exchange for a university's scenarios and entry points. In the short term staff and students get free seats and training, and the university gets computing capacity and a showcase label. The medium term turns on two questions: whether AI courses enter the formal curriculum — which means credits, teachers and assessment — and whether campus data leaves the campus, which brings data classification and procurement negotiation into play. The hidden cost for the university is lock-in: free seats now, paid migration later, and the price of switching in three to five years depends on how deeply courses, identity systems and workflows are wired into the platform. No term or sum was published; the real price of such partnerships usually appears at renewal.
