11/20 15:20 Xinhua ↗

China Development Bank finishes 2026 student loans: 7.53 million borrowers, RMB 116.3 billion

On November 20 Xinhua reported that China Development Bank had completed the 2026 national student loan disbursement it administers, paying RMB 116.3 billion to 7.53 million students from low-income families. Borrowers can check their disbursement in the bank's online loan system or its mobile app. Divided by headcount, the average loan comes to roughly RMB 15,500 (derived).

The lifetime figures put the year in proportion: RMB 676.8 billion lent cumulatively, reaching 71.25 million student cases, which makes the 2026 tranche about 17.2% of the running total (derived).

Terms come from China Education Daily's front page of November 21, which also carried the same cumulative numbers. An undergraduate may borrow up to RMB 20,000 a year and a postgraduate up to RMB 25,000. Interest runs at five-year LPR minus 70 basis points, the loan term is the programme length plus 15 years with a 22-year ceiling, no principal falls due while the student is enrolled and the state pays the interest in full, and a five-year grace period on principal follows graduation (China Education Daily).

What changed this year is the paperwork. The bank added remote authorisation for the co-borrower on origin-based loans, so parents sign online while students sign in person. Its social and livelihood department says it lends to every eligible applicant, works with education departments on repayment relief for the poorest students, and bundles scholarships, internships, job placement and insurance alongside the loan.

Why it mattersAt RMB 15,500 per borrower, the value of this programme is its timing, not its size: a family short of cash in the four most expensive years gets money, the state pays the interest during those years, and principal only starts after a five-year grace period. What the policy shifts is a cash-flow schedule. Against RMB 676.8 billion of lifetime lending, a RMB 116.3 billion year is growth at the margin, so the real variable is who qualifies as a low-income student and how long the programme runs — not how loudly it is announced.
Another angleInterest is pegged to five-year LPR minus 70 basis points, so a 10-basis-point cut changes the annual interest bill on 7.53 million loans by hundreds of millions of yuan — and squeezes both the fiscal interest subsidy and the bank's margin at once, which lands on two different budgets. The grace period pushes repayment to the sixth year after graduation, exactly when the 12.99 million graduates of 2027 reach mid-career, so relief and peak earnings arrive out of phase.

11/20 10:41 Hainan Provincial Department of Education ↗

Hainan clears three new degree-granting institutions and nine programmes, plus 12 that passed their own review

At 10:41 on November 20 the office of Hainan's provincial degree committee published its 2026 review of new bachelor's-degree-granting institutions and programmes. After on-site expert inspection, three institutions and their nine programmes are proposed for degree-granting authority. A further 12 programmes cleared institutional self-review and are also listed for public comment.

The legal basis is the province's revised rules on degree authorisation and conferral (Qiong Xue Wei Gui No. 1 of 2025). The two lists run on different tracks: the three institutions and nine programmes went through on-site expert inspection, while the 12 programmes were assessed by their own universities and forwarded to the provincial office.

Comment runs from November 20 to December 4, fifteen days. Objections must be in writing, signed and submitted within the window; late filings are not accepted. The contact number is 0898-65349690 and the address is Room 417, Provincial Government Office Building, 9 Guoxing Avenue, Haikou. The institution and programme names sit in two attachments; the notice gives no count of each university's existing degree points.

Why it mattersDegree authority is a gate, not a label: a college must first run the programme, then win the right to confer the bachelor's degree, and without it the first cohort graduates without their own institution's degree on the certificate. The split between on-site inspection and institutional self-review is not about how long the paperwork takes but about who signs for quality — inspectors look at labs, staffing and curriculum, while self-review means the university reaches the conclusion and the province only publishes it. The cost lands on the institutions: documents, expert visits and remedial work come out of university budgets, not a government line item. For applicants, the list matters two years out, when the first cohort's degree wording starts to appear on postgraduate, civil-service and overseas applications.

11/20 15:52 Xinhua ↗

Hubei's third provincial skills competition opens with 64 events and 100 workers honoured

On the morning of November 20 Hubei opened its third provincial skills competition, together with the fourth recognition round for outstanding skilled workers, at the Wuhan International Expo Centre. The provincial government hosts it; the human resources, trade union and education departments co-organise. Events run to November 21.

The scale: 64 competition events, 40 of them decided at the expo centre and 24 at satellite venues. Technical standards track the WorldSkills and national competitions, and the event also selects Hubei's team for the fourth national skills competition in 2027. Tasks are drawn from real job requirements in optoelectronics, intelligent manufacturing and Industry 4.0.

The awards deserve separate attention. One hundred workers were honoured by the provincial government: ten "Hubei artisans" at RMB 500,000 each, twenty provincial skills masters at RMB 100,000 and seventy provincial technical experts at RMB 20,000 — about RMB 8.4 million in total (derived). Around the competition the province ran five strands — contest, exhibition, conference, demonstration and hiring — with 20 demonstrations of rare skills and more than 200 local employers recruiting on site.

The stock numbers come from the provincial human resources department: 10.91 million skilled workers in Hubei, 3.61 million of them high-skilled, or about 33.1% (derived); 30 model industry-education skill chains; and a four-tier competition system run by the trade union that stages more than 20,000 workplace contests a year involving nine million workers. Hubei has held the provincial competition every two years since 2022.

Why it mattersSixty-four events, one hundred prizes, RMB 8.4 million: the money goes to individuals, once, while employers and colleges pick up the durable assets — equipment, standards and job offers. The 24 satellite venues are the mechanism that matters, because hosting means a college supplies the floor, the machines and the judges, and keeps them afterwards. For the budget, RMB 8.4 million is the visible line; the invisible one is the working time behind 20,000 workplace contests a year, paid by employers and workers. With high-skilled workers at one third of the 10.91 million total, the room left is not in volume but in upgrading the tier below.

11/20 Yunnan Provincial Department of Education ↗

Yunnan names five micro-major cases, from carbon accounting to low-altitude aviation

On November 20 Yunnan's education department published its selection of model "micro-major" cases for the education ministry's Double Thousand programme. The notice cites the ministry's call for cases (Jiao Jiu Ye Si Han No. 56 of 2026) and says five cases were chosen after universities nominated candidates and experts reviewed them.

The five come from five institutions: Yunnan University on carbon emissions management; Yunnan Minzu University on intelligent product design; Qujing Normal University on drone technology and applications aimed at the low-altitude economy; Dianchi College on creative writing tied to cultural tourism; and Kunming University of Science and Technology on intelligent manufacturing and digital twins. The case titles frame the method as cross-disciplinary teaching, platform support, industry-education integration and credit-for-certificate exchange.

Comment runs from November 20 to 24. Objections go in writing and under a real name to the student work department of the provincial education working committee; the number is 0871-65157667 and the address is 2 Xuefu Road, Wuhua District, Kunming, 650223. The case names and institutions sit in an attached document.

Why it mattersOnce micro-majors became an employment instrument, provincial selection became a choice of topics: the five cases map onto green energy, cultural tourism, the low-altitude economy and intelligent manufacturing — all on Yunnan's industrial list. The mechanism is cheap and awkward at once. A micro-major adds no degree and no extra year; it slots a dozen credits into an existing programme, so the cost is teaching hours and industry agreements rather than capital spending, but it has to be pushed through academic affairs. Being named a model case buys recruitment messaging and a stronger hand in later funding bids, not money, which gives universities a reason to enter and little reason to go deep. Note the shape of the list: all five are comprehensive or normal universities, with no vocational college and no prefectural institution. A tool designed for fast, employment-led response may end up rewarding the institutions that needed it least.

11/20 17:31 Beijing News · Shell Finance ↗

Tencent's WorkBuddy education edition reaches 660,000 users, with lightweight models taking 73.1% of a day's tokens

On November 20 Beijing News' Shell Finance reported that Tencent's WorkBuddy education edition had drawn about 660,000 users since launching on October 19, 2026 — one month of operation.

The model mix says more about how it is used than the headcount does. Hunyuan models account for 76.3% of the edition's token consumption, the largest share of any model family. Hunyuan Hy4 flash, released November 16, reached 3.64 times its first-day token volume by November 18 and took 73.1% of that day's tokens on its own; the report reads the shift as demand for light, fast models in day-to-day teaching work.

Alongside it, a credits scheme for teachers. Guangzhou Daily's New Flower City reported the same evening that Tencent SSV is offering 12 months of 2,000 free credits a month to K-12 teachers nationwide, claimed through the Tencent WorkBuddy WeChat account and activated within three working days of approval. The same report says the product has already been deployed across whole districts, including Laizhou in Shandong, where more than 6,000 primary and secondary teachers were given AI assistants; the education edition is built on the enterprise version's technical base and tuned for lesson planning, slide building, student-data analysis and parent communication.

Why it mattersSix hundred and sixty thousand users in a month shows that teacher-side AI spreads through free quota, not procurement. The number that changes the judgement is 73.1%: lightweight models absorbing seven in ten tokens means the daily work is short — item writing, marking, comment drafting — not long-form lesson preparation. That fixes the cost structure: cheap inference, heavy concurrency, low value per call, so the product survives only by spreading fixed costs across scale. The incentive is explicit too. Twelve months at 2,000 credits is a subsidy with an expiry date, and who pays afterwards decides what this is: if schools buy it, the money comes from per-student operating budgets and has to pass procurement; if teachers renew individually, it comes out of salaries and is judged only on whether it saves time.

11/21 CNINFO (Kaiwen Education filing) ↗

Kaiwen Education plans a RMB 3 million Xiongan subsidiary to run and sell its Zhiqi app

On November 20 Beijing Kaiwen Dexin Education Technology (002659) held its fourth board meeting of the seventh term by written vote. All seven directors attended and passed the subsidiary resolution 7-0, with no abstentions (announcement 2026-030).

The filing published on November 21 (announcement 2026-031) sets out the plan: to speed up its smart-education business and to push operation and commercial rollout of the Zhiqi app, its controlled subsidiary Beijing Zhiqi Wenhua Education Technology will set up a wholly owned company in Xiongan New Area, Hebei — Xiongan Zhiqi Wenhua Education Technology (provisional name, subject to registration) — with RMB 3 million of registered capital, paid in cash, 100% held by Zhiqi Wenhua.

The business scope covers internet information services, technical development and transfer, software development, applied AI software development, education consulting (excluding training that requires a licence), systems integration, data processing, IT consulting, management consulting and conference services, among other general items.

The company's stated rationale is to use Haidian's technology and education resources to run the Zhiqi app and to use the Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei coordination channel to support a national rollout of its smart-education business. The filing notes that shareholder approval is not required, that there is no related-party transaction, that the deal is not a major asset restructuring under the listing rules, and that it will not hurt the company's operations or finances.

Why it mattersRMB 3 million is small change for a listed company, but what it buys is standing: a separate legal entity in Xiongan can sign local contracts, bid for projects and apply for space and policy support, and Haidian's teaching resources cannot be reused across provinces without a vehicle that can invoice, hire and carry liability. The cost is not the capital but what follows — the business scope includes both internet information services and applied AI development, each of which brings filing, data and content obligations. For families and students, the terms of service, data permissions and subscription price of an app like this are usually set by the subsidiary rather than the school, so the counterparty for any complaint changes with the registration. The open question is timing: a Xiongan business model needs a first regional order, and RMB 3 million funds roughly one team for one year.
