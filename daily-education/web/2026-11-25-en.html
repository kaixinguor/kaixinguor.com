Policy & Regulation

政策与监管
11/25 08:40 Ministry of Education (Weiyan Jiaoyu) ↗

London and Beijing restart education talks after nine years, with three working groups and a nine-area pact

On November 24 the UK-China education ministers' consultation reopened in Beijing after a nine-year gap. Minister of Education Huai Jinpeng met UK Education Secretary Lucy Powell; Vice-Minister Ren Youqun and UK early-years minister Paul Waugh also took part. The ministry released the readout on the morning of November 25 through its Weiyan Jiaoyu account; CCTV News had reported the signing at 23:19 on November 24.

The mechanism agreed is a regular ministerial dialogue, with three working groups beneath it: digital education, STEM schooling and vocational training. Three directions of cooperation are named — joint training of high-level talent, industry-education integration, and two-way student mobility. Huai framed the talks as turning leaders' consensus into practical work; Powell said the UK would use the dialogue and the working-group channel.

After the meeting the two sides signed an Agreement on Strengthening Cooperation in Education covering nine fields: pre-school, basic, vocational, higher and cross-border education, lifelong learning, special needs education, language education, and digital and artificial intelligence. The text of the agreement, the membership of the working groups and their task lists have not been published.

LookA framework agreement carries no budget line; what decides delivery is whether three working groups can produce executable lists. Digital education means course resources and platform access, STEM schooling means textbooks, lab equipment and teacher training, and vocational training means certificate recognition and engineers inside schools — each sits with a different department or agency, and the groups have to settle who certifies, who staffs and who pays before a school dares to sign. For universities, cross-border education and student mobility are existing revenue lines, but joint-programme approval, credit recognition and fee-setting are not in this document. For vocational colleges, industry-education integration is the only part that can start immediately, because placing company engineers on campus needs no new approval layer. The nine-year gap is itself the signal: after the last round, friction over student visas and joint programmes never stopped, and a ministerial restart restores the conversation, not automatic recognition.
Another angleListing cross-border education and digital/AI separately opens a channel for two priced services: tuition and joint programmes on one side, education technology, assessment and course exports on the other. The new bearers are British edtech and awarding bodies and the Chinese schools and local platforms that host them — and their gatekeepers are not visa rules but data export, content review and fee filing.

11/25 06:55 China Education Daily (via ce.cn) ↗

Zhanjiang puts 864 schools on a market model for uniforms, with one code per garment and 100 approved suppliers

China Education Daily reported on page one of its November 25 edition (correspondents Jiang Yingyan and Li Juan, reporter Liu Dun) that Zhanjiang's education bureau and market-regulation bureau issued a joint notice in February opening primary and secondary school uniform supply to the market on three principles — buy on the market, control quality, purchase voluntarily — and building a traceability system with a code for every garment. All 10 counties and districts and all 864 schools that use uniforms have completed the change.

The design rule has moved from one design per school to one per county: each county or district settles a single uniform with summer, spring-autumn and winter sportswear versions. The city sets common standards for fabric, workmanship, inspection and the traceability code, and tightens breathability and abrasion requirements for Zhanjiang's long, humid, sunny summers; designs are chosen through public submission, online voting and expert review.

Traceability reaches the individual garment: each new uniform gets an electronic code that lets a parent check the manufacturer, production batch and inspection report. A city-level platform holds registration data from every county so procurement can be tracked and risk flagged. New uniforms began this autumn with entry grades — primary, junior, senior and vocational year one. A five-year transition means no pupil is forced to change; other year groups may keep the old design or buy the new one voluntarily.

The enforcement and supply numbers: inspections have covered 108 businesses around schools and 35 were ordered to fix problems; approved suppliers have risen to 100, up 270% on the pre-reform base. The paper notes that some schools had single-source supply, which created procurement risk, and that a fragmented market made quality supervision hard to coordinate.

LookThe reform moves purchasing power from the school to the parent. That cuts the school's single-supplier and integrity risk, but shifts the supervisory burden to the market regulator: 108 businesses inspected and 35 fixes is a starting volume, and the recurring cost is sampling plus platform operation, neither of which generates fee income. One design per county merges many small school-level markets into something large enough to bid for, and supplier numbers rising 270% show entry barriers have fallen. With a common design and transparent prices, competition moves from relationships to fabric, workmanship and delivery, margins compress, and the survivors are the factories with capacity and quality systems. For families, the five-year transition spreads the cost of switching. For schools, what remains is sizing, organising voluntary orders and handling after-sales — work with no establishment attached, which lands on form tutors and logistics staff.

Vocational & Careers

职教与就业
11/25 00:00 China Education Daily ↗

Shanghai adds 16 bachelor-track and 40 higher-vocational articulation points, the most in three years

China Education Daily reported on page one of its November 25 edition (correspondent Chen Zhiteng, reporter Ji Lin) that Shanghai has run vocational articulation since 2010 — 16 years — and now operates a multi-type, multi-level system. In 2026 the city added 16 bachelor-track articulation points and 40 higher-vocational points, the largest annual increase in three years. The new programmes are tied to advanced manufacturing, biomedicine and artificial intelligence, with more than 80% serving priority fields.

The stock: by the end of 2025 Shanghai had approved 81 bachelor-track points and 210 higher-vocational points in total. By 2026 the bachelor track covers 91 majors and has produced about 20,000 graduates, and the bachelor-track intake plan is 15% larger than in 2023. The city is building a "2+3+6+6" industrial system, and demand in integrated circuits, biomedicine and AI has moved from skilled operators to technical application engineers and shop-floor managers.

Governance runs through consortia. Each is built around a subject: one university leads, a consortium takes no more than five secondary schools, and each secondary school may join at most four. Shanghai Electronic Information Vocational and Technical College, a 2010 pilot, now leads five secondary schools under 13 common rules, with teaching separated from assessment and with common papers and marking for general and civics courses; more than 96% of its graduates find jobs matching their field.

The curriculum is rebuilt rather than stacked. In 2015 Shanghai Senior Technical School and Shanghai University of Engineering Science jointly applied for a bachelor-track programme in materials forming and control engineering, using a platform-plus-module structure across seven years: stamping and injection moulding run through courses, practical work and the final project, with AI added along the way. Landscape technology uses a 3+2 split, three years at one college and two at another, with a single training plan to avoid repetition.

LookArticulation moves the sorting age: the choice at 15 is deferred to 18 for higher-vocational and 21 for bachelor-track routes, and a family's question changes from whether to go vocational to which school to attend for seven years. That changes incentives inside schools — secondary schools used to compete for pupils, now they compete for programme approvals and consortium seats, because a point is an intake quota and a seat is a partner university. The cost sits in two places: teacher hours, since a seven-year curriculum needs joint lesson planning and resequencing with no extra posts, and governance, since 13 common rules, common papers and separate marking all need people to run them. Read the numbers carefully: 96% is one college's matching-employment rate, not the city's; a 15% larger bachelor-track intake plan is additional supply, not additional jobs, and the real test arrives with the first cohorts' employment and starting pay.

Technology & Product

技术与产品
11/24 19:57 Guangzhou Daily (Xinhuacheng) ↗

Seewo launches a teaching agent that learns from a teacher's past lesson slides

At its Future Education Innovation Day on November 24, Seewo launched "Seewo Yike", a teaching-specific agent, and opened a public beta running from November 24 to December 31. It follows the company's 2023 launch of a teaching model. Yang Ming, chief technology officer of CVTE (002841.SZ), the parent company, framed the goal as moving AI in schools from "available" to "actually used".

The stack has three layers: a model for understanding, reasoning and generation; context that tells the agent who and where it is teaching; and an orchestration layer that breaks tasks down, calls tools and checks results. Three capabilities follow — understanding teaching method through class-level learning data and classroom conditions; organising collaboration through task frameworks built from real teaching scenarios; and calling teaching-specific tools on top of Seewo's 17 years of classroom hardware and software.

The differentiation is memory. With authorisation, the agent distils a teacher's import routines, questioning habits and activity design from past lesson slides into a personal teaching memory, then drafts a new lesson against that lesson's objectives. Output can be edited page by page and pushed to the classroom screen. A new "infinite canvas" collects slides, H5 pages, interactive web pages and micro-lessons in one space, has the agent sequence them against teaching objectives, and sends the result to Seewo's whiteboard and interactive panels in one step.

Data links already run to the classroom feedback system, Seewo Xinge, student-evaluation products, Seewo central control and Seewo Mofang. Heads of teaching research can assemble lesson recordings and draft lecture notes; form tutors can comment from a phone, sync with the class-optimisation and comprehensive-evaluation tools and generate a first draft of pupil reports; administrators can inspect devices remotely and generate school reports. Resources cover primary, junior and senior high school, including micro-lessons, premium slides, simulation assets and interactive pages. A live demo used AR to show blood flow in a fish tail, an experiment that is hard to observe directly. Seewo says it will iterate on beta feedback and widen access after review.

LookOnce the pitch moves from generating content to remembering this particular teacher, the purchasing question changes: a school is no longer buying seats but a coupling to its existing whiteboards, central control and evaluation systems, and switching costs rise with every lesson the memory absorbs. Two costs follow. Authorisation is the first — whether past lesson slides belong to the school or to the teacher has to be settled in the beta terms, or a school cannot clear data-compliance review. Hardware is the second: on-device inference and multimodal capture assume classroom equipment that per-student funding does not cover. A beta closing on December 31 lands just before winter-break procurement and next term's budget, so the feedback will end up in the sales deck. For a local education bureau the questions that decide a regional contract are where the data sits, who owns the memory when a teacher moves or leaves, and whether it can be exported at graduation.

Industry & Capital

11/24 21:46 CFi.cn (full filing) ↗

Offcn directors extend a RMB 45-90 million share-purchase plan by six months, having bought RMB 5.0 million

On November 24 Offcn Education Technology (002607.SZ) disclosed announcement 2026-060: a group of directors, senior managers and core staff had planned to buy shares worth RMB 45 million to RMB 90 million within six months of June 11, 2026. As of the announcement they had bought 3.4062 million shares, about 0.06% of total share capital, for RMB 4,998,846 — roughly 11% of the floor.

The stated reasons are closed reporting periods, market conditions and personal funding arrangements, which make completion by the original deadline unlikely. The buyers propose extending the window by six months to June 10, 2027, with all other terms unchanged. The board approved the motion on November 24; related directors He Youli, Sun Wei and Jin Hua abstained. The change still needs approval at an extraordinary general meeting on December 10.

The buyers are director and deputy general manager He Youli, director Jin Hua, employee director Sun Wei, deputy general manager Gui Hongzhi, finance director Wang Yue, board secretary Gong Yafang, and core staff including party secretary and essay-research institute head Zhang Hongjun. The filing says none of them held shares directly or indirectly before the plan, that the purchases will not change control, and that the share distribution will remain compliant with listing requirements.

Two risks are retained: if shareholders approve the extension, market moves or unavailable funding could still prevent full execution. The company also set December 3 as the record date for the December 10 meeting, which will run on-site and online.

LookTurning an 11% completion rate into "six more months" buys time rather than cutting the pledge: the floor stays, but delivery slides into 2027 and a hard commitment becomes an option. For minority holders the signal is not in the filing but at the December 10 meeting, where related shareholders step aside and outsiders effectively price the extension. The cost shows up as a confidence discount — a RMB 45-90 million floor was a backstop expectation, and it now cannot be tested until June 2027, while management's own buying of RMB 5.0 million suggests personal funding and corporate timing are out of step. For education companies, valuation increasingly rests on cash flow and refund liabilities; insider buying is a signal and does not change contract liabilities. The next data points are fourth-quarter collections and the refund line.
