Policy & Regulation

政策与监管
11/25 18:48 Shandong Provincial People's Government ↗

Shandong publishes 22 measures for basic education: 92% upper-secondary completion by 2030, 70% of places by quota, and the high-school cut-off phased out

On November 25 the Shandong provincial government released its implementation opinions on deepening reform and speeding up high-quality basic education (Lu Zheng Zi [2026] No. 141) in its policy library. The document was signed on October 28 and made public on November 25; it contains 22 measures in three blocks covering moral education, resource allocation and safeguards. Dazhong News carried the full text at 18:48 the same day.

The admissions language is the most specific. By 2030, completion of upper-secondary schooling is to reach 92% and the share of pupils admitted to public academic high schools is to rise by more than 10 percentage points against 2025. At least 70% of places at good academic high schools are to be allocated to lower-secondary schools by quota, the minimum admission cut-off is to be phased out, and cities are to move towards balanced allocation, catchment admission and registration-based admission. Zhongkao will use fewer subjects and easier papers, with open-book exams where conditions allow. A vocational-academic transfer mechanism is to be created, and some upper-secondary schools may open comprehensive high schools focused on engineering.

Provision is being consolidated as well as expanded. In principle no new primary or secondary schools and kindergartens will be built in villages; shrinking rural schools are to be merged carefully, and small townships are encouraged to concentrate on high-quality nine-year boarding schools. Twelve-year and nine-year through schools and complete middle schools are supported, and small-class teaching is to be explored once staffing is secured. By 2030 gross preschool enrolment should reach 99%, 75% of preschoolers should be in public kindergartens, and enrolment of children with disabilities should hold at 100% under 15-year free special education.

Time inside the school day is reallocated too: one PE lesson a day and at least two hours of physical activity; primary school not starting before 8:20 and secondary school before 8:00; a "no-homework day" and fewer tests; AI literacy courses for all, 200 primary and secondary AI experiment schools by 2030, and gigabit links into schools. On staffing, the plan promises to meet establishment needs, extend county-managed teacher employment, move teachers between counties and schools, and link pay guarantees to arrears control. On funding it repeats the "two only increase" rule and requires kindergarten fees and academic high-school tuition to be reviewed every three to five years.

LookQuota allocation at 70% or more, the end of the minimum cut-off and vocational-academic transfer together change what lower-secondary schools optimise for. They used to compete on how many pupils crossed the line into a top high school; now they compete on how internal places are distributed and whether a pupil at a weak school can win a good high-school seat on class rank. Schools shift from selecting the strongest to lifting the bottom. The cost lands in three places. A daily PE lesson takes timetable space, facilities and PE teachers, and the establishment gap there is real. A public high-school admission share more than 10 points higher means more buildings and teachers, with county budgets paying the matching share. Reviewing high-school tuition every three to five years passes part of any increase to households. The evaluation clause bans promotion targets and rewards based on "top scorers" or enrolment rates, but oversight still runs through county-government performance reviews, so delivery depends on how each city splits the 92% and 70% targets down to counties.
Another angleFiscally this is expansion and restructuring at once: more public high-school places alongside the "two only increase" rule, funded mainly by provincial pooling and central budget investment with county matching. When the matching money is missing, class sizes and teacher hours give way before enrolment rates do. For households, more academic high-school places lower the vocational share and reshape spending on tuition and tutoring over the next three years. For county high schools, quota allocation redistributes good pupils, narrows the room for school-choice fees and cross-district recruitment, and ties revenue and reputation more closely to local intake quality.

11/25 15:33 Guizhou Development and Reform Commission ↗

Guizhou weighs tuition rise at public universities: undergraduate fee from RMB 4,170 to RMB 5,300 or RMB 4,990, applied to new students from autumn 2027

On the morning of November 25 the Guizhou provincial development and reform commission, together with the education and finance departments, held a public hearing on adjusting tuition at provincial public universities. The subject was the draft pricing scheme. The commission said participants broadly accepted the need for a moderate increase while questioning the details, the cost audit and how the change would be phased in; the scheme now goes back for revision and provincial government approval before it is issued.

The cost base is the starting point. According to Guizhou Radio and TV, the audited average cost of educating one student at a provincial public university was RMB 20,200 a year over 2023-2025, against a current average undergraduate fee of RMB 4,170 and a vocational-college fee of RMB 3,850. Two options were put forward; arts fees stay unchanged in both.

Option one raises the average undergraduate fee, including vocational bachelor programmes, from RMB 4,170 to RMB 5,300, or 27.1%, and the vocational-college fee from RMB 3,850 to RMB 4,500, or 16.88%. Universities on the national Double First-Class list or the former "211" list could charge 5.5% above those levels. Option two raises the undergraduate fee to RMB 4,990, or 19.66%, keeps the vocational-college fee at RMB 4,500, and allows the same top-tier institutions a 12.83% premium. A university holding both labels may use only one premium.

The phase-in follows the usual rule of old rules for current students and new rules for new ones: the revised undergraduate fee applies from autumn 2027 and the vocational-college fee from autumn 2029. Nobody now enrolled is affected, and the first cohort to pay the new rate enters in 2027.

LookAgainst a cost of RMB 20,200, a fee of RMB 4,170 leaves a gap of roughly RMB 16,000 per student that the treasury covers. Even at RMB 5,300 under option one, tuition would cover about a quarter of the cost, so this is gap-filling rather than full cost recovery. The incentive effect on universities is delayed: no extra income arrives before autumn 2027, and because current students keep the old rate, revenue takes four years to mature, which does little for near-term cash flow. For households, option one adds RMB 1,130 a year, about RMB 4,520 over a four-year degree, predictable but concentrated at enrolment. For admissions, a higher public fee narrows the gap with private providers, which could push private colleges' entry rankings up passively. Vocational colleges wait until 2029, so students on secondary-to-higher vocational pathways still see the old number when they apply, and expectations there do not move yet.

11/25 21:45 UK Department for Education (GOV.UK) ↗

UK confirms GBP 31 per pupil for the 2027-28 risk protection arrangement, with early-career teacher mentor funding paid from late November

On November 25 the UK Department for Education published its funding update for academies, local authorities and further education providers. It went live at 13:45 London time, 21:45 in Beijing, in three versions covering each group of institutions, and deals mainly with grants, procurement training and risk protection.

The sharpest number is the unit price of the risk protection arrangement, or RPA: GBP 31 per pupil for 2027-28. Local authority-maintained schools renew automatically at that rate from April 1, 2027, and academies from September 1, 2027, with no action needed; schools that want to leave must give three months' written notice before the end of their membership year.

On staffing, the department published 2025-26 allocations for early-career teacher entitlement: funding for time off timetable in the second year and for mentor support, plus money for the mentor training programme. Payments go to local authorities at the end of November 2026 and directly to academies in early December. A post-16 budget support grant starts in April 2027, consolidating the former post-16 National Insurance contributions grant and money already announced for recruitment and retention in 2027-28, and adding improved maternity provision for staff in the statutory further education sector along with schools and academies teaching 16 to 19-year-olds.

The same update points back to the 2027-28 national funding formula for schools, high-needs funding and central school services, set out in a written statement by the minister of state for school standards on November 19. Provisional allocation tables and technical notes are published, and the department will run a briefing for academy finance staff on December 2.

LookA flat GBP 31 per pupil on automatic renewal makes insurance a predictable line in the budget, but schools losing pupils lose both grant scale and insurance scale at once: the unit price holds while the headcount falls, so per-pupil management cost quietly rises. The early-career teacher money arrives at the end of November and in early December, while reduced timetables and mentor cover have been running since September, so schools carry the cost first and recover it later; the squeeze is sharpest for small schools and single-academy trusts. Consolidating the National Insurance grant with recruitment and retention money saves forms, at the price of no longer being able to track recruitment spending separately, which coarsens audit and pushes impact evaluation back onto self-reporting.

11/26 00:00 China Education Daily ↗

Shangqiu takes its career-planning contest into all 4,767 dormitories, with 726 classes and 23,332 students taking part

China Education Daily reported on page two of its November 26 edition (reporter Pang Ke) that Shangqiu Institute of Technology ran the campus round of the fourth national college career-planning competition across the whole institution: 4,767 dormitory rooms, 726 classes and 23,332 students took part, with the contest staged at dormitory, class, school and college level.

The organising model folds student self-government into the jobs service. The college appointed all 4,767 dormitory heads as employment and entrepreneurship grid workers, so form tutors cover an area and grid workers cover a room. Class rounds used themed meetings, live pitches, all-student defences and peer review, and presentations ranged from career ambitions to plans built around AI-related work. Subject teachers and careers advisers were assigned to classes to coach topic choice, goal setting, written plans and defence technique.

The numbers need their denominator. The 23,332 figure is the number of enrolled students who took part, not the number of graduates, and it is not an employment rate. The report gives no destination rate for the class of 2026; it quotes careers adviser Kong Degang saying the college has moved from selecting a few competitors to cultivating everyone, and that whole-class contests push teachers to work on day-to-day careers guidance.

LookThis spreads the reach of careers guidance onto a student-run structure: 4,767 dormitory heads are an extra grid layer outside the establishment, and coverage can reach 100% within a term. The price is professionalism, since dormitory heads have no counselling training and quality depends on how often they are trained and checked. The teacher-side cost is concrete: defences across 726 classes eat into class-meeting and free time, and coaching has no extra posts, so it comes out of lesson preparation and research. What tests the model is not the participation count but destination rates, job-match rates and starting pay three years out, none of which the college has published; those numbers should come before the next contest.

11/25 17:34 China Government Procurement Network ↗

Peking University's education school tenders RMB 1.641 million for one linked smart-classroom system, excluding imports, with bids opening December 16

At 17:34 on November 25 the China Government Procurement Network published a public tender by Peking University's graduate school of education for an integrated "boundless connection" smart-classroom system (project OITC-G260313626). The buyer is Peking University and the agent is Orient International Tendering. The budget is RMB 1.641 million, the ceiling is the same, and the purchase is for one integrated system.

The stated purpose says more about the money than a hardware list would: the room is to support international academic exchange, cross-campus collaborative teaching research, high-level policy consultation and remote practice supervision for teacher trainees in one linked space. Those four uses point away from screens in a single classroom and towards cross-campus audio and video links, recording, and integrated control that can serve both meetings and teaching.

Two kinds of supplier are excluded: imported products are not allowed, and consortia are not accepted. Bidders must bid for the whole package and may not subcontract. The contract is fixed-price, with delivery and installation within 20 days of signing. Tender documents cost RMB 600 and are on sale from November 25 to December 2; bids open at 09:30 on December 16 at the Internet Finance Centre in Haidian, Beijing.

LookThe RMB 1.641 million buys connection, not panels. Cross-campus teaching research and remote practice supervision depend on codecs, echo suppression, recording and platform interoperability, so hardware is a modest share of the cost and integration, commissioning and ongoing operation take the rest — and operation sits outside this contract. Excluding imports narrows the field to domestic integrators, realistically the few firms that have done both university recording systems and conference rooms. The timetable is the real constraint: bids open on December 16 and installation must finish within 20 days of signing, so acceptance lands in the winter break, with classroom works, network access and teacher training all needed at once; any slip pushes the remote practice course into next term.

11/25 19:45 Beijing Business Today ↗

Gaotu posts RMB 1.87 billion in quarterly revenue and RMB 15.648 million in net profit, its first third-quarter GAAP operating profit

Beijing Business Today reported on November 25 that Gaotu Techedu (GOTU) had released unaudited results for the third quarter of its 2026 financial year: revenue of RMB 1.87 billion, up 18.3% year on year; operating profit of RMB 1.092 million; and net profit of RMB 15.648 million. The report describes it as the company's first GAAP operating profit in a third quarter.

The profit came from the cost side. Operating expenses fell by about 11.3 percentage points as a share of revenue year on year. On RMB 1.87 billion of revenue and RMB 1.092 million of operating profit, the operating margin works out at roughly 0.06%. Net profit exceeded operating profit, so non-operating items and tax together added to the quarter's result. The report does not disclose segment revenue, contract liabilities or retention rates.

For education companies the third quarter is the first full quarter after the summer marketing season, so the expense ratio usually explains profit better than revenue growth does: revenue rose 18.3% while the expense ratio fell 11.3 points, meaning the improvement came from spending less rather than earning more.

LookRMB 1.87 billion of revenue producing RMB 1.092 million of operating profit shows scale effects have not arrived. The big education costs are teaching hours and content development, both of which grow with class volume; expense-ratio gains come from customer acquisition and administration, not delivery. For investors a single profitable quarter is a weak signal, because the year turns on fourth-quarter renewals and refunds, and contract liabilities do not appear in this report. For the industry, if acquisition costs fell because the channel mix changed towards livestreaming, private traffic and referrals, the gain is replicable; if they fell because marketing was cut, the next enrolment season gives it back through slower revenue growth. Earnings calls usually describe these two paths differently, so the full report is worth checking before drawing a conclusion.
