11/26 20:13 Xinhua ↗

Five ministries set a 2 million high-skilled target for technical schools and a 97% placement rate

A pupil who enters a technical school next autumn will find that, when he graduates, an advanced-worker class certificate counts as a junior-college qualification in public-sector hiring and promotion reviews. The intermediate class counts as a secondary-school qualification. The preparatory technician class counts as a bachelor's degree.

Xinhua reported the opinion on November 26. It was issued by the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security, the National Development and Reform Commission, the Ministry of Education, the Ministry of Finance and the State-owned Assets Supervision and Administration Commission. The document is Ren She Bu Fa [2026] No. 77. It was signed on October 15 and released on November 26; the full text is on the central government website.

The 15th Five-Year Plan targets are stability in enrolment and training. The graduate destination rate is to reach at least 97%. More than 2 million high-skilled people are to be trained over the period. The base is 2,410 technical schools with 4.19 million students, plus nearly 6 million training places a year for the public.

The measures are specific. Programmes are to be adjusted against industry demand and employment outcomes. Excess, low-employment and disappearing-occupation courses are to be merged or closed. A group of quality technician colleges and 100 quality programmes are to be built. About 1,000 textbooks are to be developed or selected. Technical schools will join the unified vocational admissions platform and recruit young people, migrant workers, the unemployed, veterans and people with disabilities who can work. AI literacy is to enter the curriculum.

Money and staffing follow. Central modern-vocational-education funds can be used for technical schools. Provinces may set separate per-student funding for preparatory-technician and advanced-worker classes. Schools carrying heavy training loads can have their performance-pay totals adjusted. A share of training income may go into general funds.

Look97% is a destination rate, not a jobs-matched rate. The denominator is that year's graduates, and the count includes further study, freelance work and self-employment. The harder constraint is the cull of courses: programmes with excess supply, low employment or vanishing occupations are to be cut, and schools lose the revenue attached to them. One hundred quality programmes spread across 2,410 schools is fewer than 0.05 per school, so the money will concentrate in flagship technician colleges. Watch the provincial course-filing lists next year, the per-student funding standard for technician colleges, and the number of advanced-worker graduates actually hired into public institutions.
Another angleTwo million high-skilled trainees against 4.19 million students means most of the gain must come from training, not full-time schooling. Central modern-vocational-education money can now go to technical schools; whether provinces add a separate per-student line will decide if the 100 quality programmes can keep their workshops equipped. For the labour market, once public institutions treat technician-college graduates like junior-college graduates, the pull will show first in county towns in central and western China.

11/26 16:08 Shandong Provincial Education Department ↗

Shandong ties top-up degree recommendations to first-attempt core-course grades, with an 80% weight and a top-60% shortlist

A Shandong vocational student entering in 2027 will have every first attempt at a core course counted in the ranking for a top-up degree recommendation. Retakes and resits will be handled under each college's published formula.

The provincial education department issued the notice at 16:08 on November 26. Its reference is Lu Jiao Zhi Han [2026] No. 21. It applies to students entering vocational programmes in 2027 and later.

The assessment has three parts: course grades, overall performance and internships. Course grades are calculated from credit points and must be at least 80% of the total. Major-foundation and core courses must be at least 80% of that course-grade component. Overall performance covers conduct, innovation and entrepreneurship. Internships are a key basis for recommendation.

The process is fixed to the fifth semester. Colleges must finish the calculation at the end of the final year. They must shortlist the top 60% and post the list for at least five working days. Formulas, scoring rules and proposed lists must be public and documented. Students need an appeals channel. The provincial department will run spot checks and feed the results into vocational-college quality monitoring.

One hard rule targets internships. No one may skip a placement to prepare for the exam. No one may issue a false internship certificate. Students who change majors are assessed under the receiving programme's standard.

LookTwo 80% weights narrow the exam-only route. Course grades make up at least 80% of the assessment, and major-foundation and core courses make up at least 80% of those grades. For a student entering in 2027, every core course from the first year counts; cramming in the third year no longer works. For colleges, ranking the top 60% moves competition from a provincial test to internal grade points. Students who transfer majors are judged by the receiving programme's standard, which may encourage course-shopping. Watch whether each college publishes its formula, how retakes are counted, and whether the recommended list stays up for the full five working days.

11/26 16:20 Yunnan News (Yunnan Daily) ↗

Yunnan requires a full-time counsellor in every qualifying school by 2030 and 10 mental-health lessons a term

A Yunnan secondary school with 1,000 students will need at least one full-time mental-health teacher under the new plan. By 2030, every qualifying school in the province must have one.

Yunnan News reported the plan at 16:20 on November 26. It was issued by the provincial education leading group of the Communist Party committee. The plan covers 2026 to 2030. It names three gaps: patchy monitoring, too few full-time teachers, and weak links between school, family and hospital.

The targets come in two steps. By 2028, every student above grade four should be covered by mental-health screening. 85% of the required full-time counsellor posts should be filled. By 2030, staffing should reach 100%. Primary and secondary schools must teach at least 10 mental-health lessons a term. Universities must offer at least 2 credits. Schools with more than 1,000 students need at least one full-time counsellor. Universities need a ratio of at least 1:3,000 and at least two counsellors each.

Support includes training for 500 lead counsellors, 5,000 full- and part-time counsellors and 10,000 class teachers, advisers and administrators. The province will create 100 master studios. Every prefecture must have at least one public psychiatric hospital or standalone psychiatric campus. Every county must have a psychiatric clinic in at least one public hospital. Health workers are to visit schools at least 300 times a year.

The timetable is constrained too. There is no homework on Wednesdays and a 15-minute break between classes. Pupils must get at least two hours of physical activity a day. Schools may not set subject exams in the first two weeks of term. They may not rank pupils by test scores or be evaluated on progression rates.

LookA 100% staffing target is a payroll and recruitment problem, not a training problem. Yunnan goes from 85% in 2028 to 100% in 2030, which means adding 15 percentage points in two years; across the province's schools that runs into thousands of posts. Training 500 lead counsellors, 5,000 full- and part-time counsellors and 10,000 class teachers can make people competent, not make them exist. County budgets carry the new posts, and schools must find 10 lessons a term in an already full timetable. Watch county recruitment plans for next year, the list of counselling rooms that pass inspection, and the number of actual referrals through the green channel to hospitals.

Beijing College of Economics and Management, Beijing Polytechnic University, Beijing Vocational College of Agriculture and China Civil Affairs University each have eight.

The list follows a municipal notice, Jing Jiao Han [2026] No. 300. It went through school nomination, formal review, online review and panel assessment. Objections must be in writing with evidence and sent by November 30.

11/27 08:14 Sichuan Provincial Education Department ↗

Sichuan gives every schoolteacher a free AI assistant and makes training mandatory

A Sichuan primary-school teacher who opens the provincial cloud platform next week will find a single AI assistant account issued by the province. The education department wants every teacher to activate it and use it. There is no charge.

The provincial education department published the notice at 08:14 on November 27. The assistant is a core application of the Tianfu Cloud Education platform. It is funded and run centrally, with one platform and one account.

The uses are grouped by lesson stage. Before class, teachers can prepare resources, lesson designs, slides, tiered homework and experiment plans. During class, the assistant supports scenario-building, interaction, pronunciation modelling, virtual experiments and collaborative work. After class, it assembles questions and analyses classroom data.

Sichuan Online reported the limits. Generated lesson plans must be checked against curriculum standards, textbooks and the actual class. They may not be used as they are. Teachers remain responsible for objectives, pace and values. Test items must be reviewed. Classroom analytics must lead to a testable improvement plan, not a data display.

Training becomes a required part of teacher development, school-based research and master-teacher studios. It is folded into the province-wide AI literacy programme. The department wants school-based delivery, online coaching, pairing and shadowing. Sichuan has more than a million teachers. The training launched in October 2025 uses 32 hours of online courses and is designed for more than a million educators, as Sichuan Online reported at the time.

LookFree access and a single account solve availability, not use. Making account activation the first step of training suggests that activation is the bottleneck. For teachers, an assistant that drafts lessons, assembles papers and analyses classes adds work before it removes any. For schools, making training mandatory takes time from existing professional-development sessions. Sichuan has more than a million teachers; the training launched in October 2025 is built on 32 hours of online courses and is designed for more than a million educators. Watch activation rates, monthly active use, how often teachers rewrite generated lesson plans, and how many classes actually change after the analytics report.

Guangzheng Education's profit falls 59% as high-margin services shrink

Guangzheng Education kept about RMB 14 of profit for every RMB 100 of revenue in fiscal 2026. A year earlier it kept RMB 29.

Zhitong Finance reported the results at 22:08 on November 26. The company (06068.HK) reported for the year to August 31, 2026. Revenue was RMB 110 million, down 15.67%. Profit was RMB 15.564 million, down 59.37%. Basic earnings per share were RMB 0.01.

Revenue fell in two places. School-related supply-chain revenue dropped from RMB 85.7 million in fiscal 2025 to RMB 74.5 million, a fall of 13.1%. Comprehensive education services lost RMB 13 million, or 28.8%. The supply chain now generates 67.7% of revenue.

Gross margin fell from 46.0% to 41.5% as the higher-margin services business shrank. Other income also fell. The RMB 10.9 million amortisation of a financial-guarantee contract in fiscal 2025 did not recur.

Gelonghui reported that the company said it would consolidate resources into an education-services platform. The platform covers online learning products, management services and school supplies. It will expand supply through a wholly owned subsidiary in Qingyuan. It will push ahead with a high-school project in Zhongshan and spin off high schools that hold their own operating licences.

LookRevenue fell 15.67% and profit fell 59.37%, a gap of more than three times. The mix explains it. High-margin comprehensive education services lost RMB 13 million of revenue, the supply-chain business now generates 67.7% of the total, and gross margin fell from 46.0% to 41.5%. Other income also vanished: the 2025 financial-guarantee amortisation of RMB 10.9 million did not recur. For investors, the next fiscal year turns on whether comprehensive services stop shrinking and whether the high-school spin-off closes. For the sector, moving from running schools to supplying services and goods makes revenue steadier and margins thinner. Watch the services revenue line, gross margin, and contract liabilities.
