Sichuan issues school-supplement rules: one set per subject, effective December 5

On November 6, Sichuan's education department, propaganda department and development commission issued trial rules for supplementary teaching materials entering schools (川教发〔2026〕19号). The document is dated November 5, published November 6, takes effect on December 5, 2026 and runs for two years.

Selection: prefecture-level education bureaus recommend one set per subject per textbook edition from the provincial catalogue, and students choose voluntarily; results must be filed with the provincial department within 14 working days. Free provision is capped at one set per student per subject.

Materials outside the provincial catalogue face a longer gate: at least six months' advance filing before use, then at least two months for content review. Schools may buy on students' behalf but may not profit, and must report within 45 working days of the semester start.

The rules list 15 prohibitions, including recommending materials to grades 1-2 or using parent committees to require purchases, and 10 exit conditions such as factual errors, ISBN trading or embedded advertising. Schools must post the list, prices and supervision numbers at the gate.

Why it mattersThe rules separate who pays, who chooses and who is accountable: the province controls the catalogue and price review, prefectures choose, schools only buy on behalf and may not profit. Cost shifts from parents to two places: county procurement budgets and a filing form due within 45 working days each semester. The hardest clause is the six-month gate for out-of-catalogue materials; if it holds, purchasing returns to the catalogue.
The other view For finance, free materials become an annual line item of students times subjects; for teachers, one fewer workbook does not mean less preparation, and whether saved time becomes new lesson-design work depends on how teaching is appraised.

11/06 08:43 People's Daily Online / Beijing Youth Daily ↗

Beijing sets school-meal deadline: no delivered meals by September 2027, all canteens self-operated

At a November 5 meeting in Fengtai, Beijing's education commission set four targets for September 1, 2027: end delivered meals; convert all contracted and delegated canteens to a new self-operated model; put all canteen procurement through district centralised platforms; and complete 1,000 smart canteens.

By the autumn 2026 semester, more than 50% of Beijing schools had canteens; six staple categories were already centrally procured. Fengtai's platform has logged 92,000 orders worth 150 million yuan, selected 10 full-category suppliers, capped settlement prices at 90% of supermarket retail and built 86 smart canteen sites.

Yanqing reports 100% self-operation after converting the remaining 29 schools before September 2026, 100% procurement through the district platform, 80% local sourcing for core categories, more than 50 million yuan of hardware investment and a floor of 70% of meal fees going to ingredients.

In 2027 school meals will join the city's livelihood agenda and its monthly review mechanism.

Why it mattersThe real shift is from contract to self-operation: schools stop being clients and become operators, carrying staffing, kitchens, books and food-safety liability. The 1,000 smart canteens are one-off capex; the 70% ingredient floor is the annual test, because labour, energy and depreciation must fit in the other 30%. Watch the 2027 budget and canteen headcount, not the cooking robots.

11/06 08:31 People's Daily Online / Science and Technology Daily ↗

Shanghai briefing: 400-plus through-train programmes and 90%-plus retention in integrated circuits

At a briefing, Shanghai's education commission described a system that now runs vertically from secondary vocational to bachelor's and horizontally between vocational and general education: 18 quality secondary vocational schools and 60 programmes; 12 high-level colleges and 50 programme groups, three in the national Double High plan; one vocational university and 13 vocational bachelor programmes at four general universities.

There are more than 400 through-train programme points across secondary-to-higher, secondary-to-bachelor, five-year and higher-vocational-to-bachelor tracks. Vocational experience months have run for 11 years, reaching about 100,000 students a year, with 32 class hours counted in comprehensive assessment.

The city has 14 municipal industry-education consortia, 15 sector consortia and 15 regional practice centres. In integrated circuits, Shanghai Electronic Information Vocational College and companies including AMEC mapped 20 key roles and sent more than 1,100 students into firms, with retention above 90% in key positions.

At Shanghai Nanhu Vocational College, order classes are about 32% of students and about 83% of graduates join local leaders in AI and connected vehicles. Seven Shanghai contestants won five golds and two silvers at the 48th WorldSkills competition.

Why it mattersThrough-train programmes are the main lever: 400-plus points mean secondary vocational is no longer terminal but a route upward. The binding constraint is the four general universities behind 13 vocational bachelor programmes: degrees, faculty and lab capacity. For parents, through-train delays the sorting age, but without transparent exit and transfer rules the anxiety moves later rather than away.

11/06 09:13 People's Daily Online / Inner Mongolia Daily ↗

Inner Mongolia vocational skills contest opens with 995 teams and 2,721 students across 14 categories

The 2026 Inner Mongolia vocational skills competition for secondary students opened in Chifeng on November 4. Twelve leagues and cities sent 995 teams and 2,721 students across 14 categories, including agriculture, forestry, animal husbandry and equipment manufacturing, with 57 events.

The provincial education department hosts and Chifeng's bureau organises; delegations also visited two local vocational schools. The higher-vocational leg starts on November 8 at 26 colleges including Inner Mongolia Construction Vocational University and Inner Mongolia Mechanical and Electrical Vocational College.

The competition is meant to map regional industry needs and to connect competition, teaching and certification.

Why it mattersThe event list is a snapshot of local demand: agriculture and equipment manufacturing dominate, matching the region's farm and energy-equipment base. The harder part comes after the medals: whether equipment, consumables and instructor time stay in classrooms. With 2,721 students from 12 leagues, medals tend to concentrate; the mapping between events and local jobs matters more than the table.

11/05 16:24 Beijing Daily ↗

Beijing releases AI-in-education package as Zhi Xiaoyan reaches more than 100 schools

At the 10th Beijing Education Forum on November 5, the city released a package of AI-in-education documents: a 2026 guide covering basic, higher, vocational, lifelong and special education plus AI literacy and ethics; a framework for students' AI literacy across knowing, collaborating and creating, and governing AI, with three levels; and a teaching guide for AI literacy courses from primary to high school.

The Zhi Xiaoyan teaching-agent platform opened free to schools and has been used in more than 100 schools with over 30,000 registered users. The Beijing Academy of Educational Sciences leads a city AI-in-education community with Tianjin, Xiongan, Shenzhen and Chengdu, and the city launched a national AI pilot-base industry-academia consortium.

Why it mattersBeijing is issuing a framework, a curriculum guide and a platform rather than hardware. The cost shows up in teacher training and timetable hours: who gives up class time for AI literacy, where preparation time comes from, and how it is assessed. A 100-school, 30,000-user footprint is a pilot, not coverage; watch the spring 2027 timetable and teacher workload data, and whether the literacy framework becomes another admissions metric.
