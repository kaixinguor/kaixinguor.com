UCASS opens the first college for endangered disciplines, with 80-plus institutions in its alliance

The University of Chinese Academy of Social Sciences (UCASS) opened the country's first university college for endangered disciplines on November 6, focused on ancient scripts and classical Chinese texts and taught from bachelor's through PhD in a master-apprentice model. The same day it launched a National Alliance for Endangered Disciplines with more than 80 universities, research institutes, museums and archives.

The college covers oracle-bone studies, bamboo and silk manuscripts, and Turkic, Tangut and Manchu scripts. Four teaching divisions — oracle-bone and bamboo-silk texts, northern ethnic scripts, south-western ethnic scripts, and classical and medieval civilisations — sit alongside a digital humanities laboratory for script decipherment, rare-text editing and comparative work.

Each student gets a chief supervisor plus an interdisciplinary co-supervisor. National research projects are meant to feed documents and training into the curriculum rather than sit apart from it.

Alliance members include Tsinghua, Renmin, Beijing Normal and Fudan universities, the Beijing Academy of Social Sciences and the First Historical Archives of China. The founding meeting issued a charter with six tasks: joint field bases, shared brands, a common resource platform, joint training, international outreach and a coordination mechanism.

Why it mattersThe binding constraints are posts and supervisors, not the nameplate. Four divisions need cross-department joint appointments, promotion rules and doctoral quotas; without them, "through-train" only means bolting postgraduate seminars onto an undergraduate timetable. The cost lands on UCASS's staffing cap and on admissions plans for fields that few students choose. For an 18-year-old, a track with almost no exit is a bet, and the exit rules matter more than the brochure. An alliance of 80-plus members that signs a charter but shares neither credits nor posts will stay a mailing list.
The other view For funders and evaluators, endangered disciplines pay back over decades, not within a five-year paper-and-grant cycle, so they can only be financed as a public good. Three concrete tests would show whether the alliance is serious: one shared text database, a batch of joint admissions slots, and mutual recognition of supervision workload. Missing any one, and the 80-plus are a contact list.

11/06 18:52 Shandong Provincial Department of Education ↗

Shandong names six nominees for national school-aesthetics teacher studios, four from schools

Shandong's education department published its nominees for national school-aesthetics teacher studios on November 6. The selection follows the Ministry of Education's notice on building such studios (教体艺厅函〔2026〕40号) and was made after school submissions and expert review.

Six institutions are listed. Four are primary or secondary schools: Jinan Licheng No. 2 Middle School, Qingdao No. 1 Middle School, Laizhou Bilingual Experimental School and Weihai Art School. Two are universities: Shandong University of Arts and Linyi University.

The comment period runs five working days from November 6. Objections must be in writing with evidence to the department's health, sport and arts office; institutions need an official seal, individuals a real name, and late submissions are not accepted.

Why it mattersA teacher studio is a research group outside the staffing table: one nameplate, one budget line, one lead teacher. Whether it lifts teachers across a county depends on whether the post counts toward promotion and how teaching hours are recognised. Four of the six slots going to schools signals a tilt toward the classroom, but the studio runs on a fixed weekly meeting slot, and that time can only come out of a school timetable. The cost sits with the lead teacher and members, and with whether the county-level research office will fund cover lessons.

USTB's Tianjin College launches an AI alliance with 20-plus firms and two new schools

The Tianjin College of the University of Science and Technology Beijing (USTB) held the founding meeting of an AI industry-education alliance, attended by officials from Baodi district, companies, research institutes and industry associations. The college initiated the alliance, which gathers more than 20 leading firms across cybersecurity, speech, robotics and industrial automation.

The meeting also unveiled an AI school and an AI industry school. The college runs a "2+5+N" discipline plan and has built clusters in AI, smart manufacturing, new materials and new energy; it previously set up a think tank for the Jingjin New City area and a low-altitude economy alliance for the Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei region.

Under the alliance, the college plans to put real industry projects, engineering scenarios and sector standards into teaching, moving from one-off company projects to chain-wide collaboration. Side sessions covered co-built course modules, lightweight joint courses and project-based teaching, plus a roundtable on embodied intelligence and competition-based learning.

Guests toured the intelligent systems, electronics innovation and software training labs and student projects. The college says it will set up a standing mechanism for two-way sharing of courses, technology and industry information.

Why it mattersThe value of the alliance is not the list of 20-plus companies but whether they write real projects, engineer hours and internships into the curriculum. Two gates decide it: whether company projects can replace practical credits, and whether teachers' time in industry counts as workload — and who covers their classes meanwhile. For an independent college, this is a way to fund labs with corporate resources; the cost shifts from the school's budget to company staff time and project cycles. If a partner changes sector, the course is left hanging, so the term length and exit clauses matter more than the unveiling photo.

iFlytek's teacher agent Xingguang builds interactive courseware without code

At the 88th China Educational Equipment Exhibition on November 6, iFlytek showed Xingguang, a "super agent" for teachers covering lesson preparation, home-school communication and administrative chores. Teachers need no coding or animation skills: a prompt produces an interactive H5 page ready for class.

In a demonstration built around the Tang poem "The Charcoal Seller", the agent created an immersive setting where students play the poet, townspeople and officials in Chang'an. For abstract topics it offers animated geometry, model teardowns and in-class quizzes.

The resource layer pulls from Bilibili, Douyin and Xiaohongshu — three platforms — filtering and labelling sources. Generated pages can be converted into a teaching app that collects student answers and files them into learning records. iFlytek worked with Wei Rui's team at Beijing Normal University to embed project-based learning methods into the agent, and offers a "Xingguang Academy" course ladder.

At the event, Liu Yanli of Haikou No. 1 Middle School used hydrological data from Yangpu port to build an interdisciplinary trigonometry lesson with 3D fitting and dynamic modelling. iFlytek says the agent's long-term memory stores a teacher's subject, textbook edition and preparation habits.

Why it mattersThe tool changes the division of labour in lesson prep: hours of searching for material and building animations collapse into one prompt plus a human check. Whether the saved time becomes more demonstration lessons and paperwork, or actual rest, depends on how the school counts workload — if nothing else is removed, "efficiency" just refills the hours. The real gates are copyright and subject accuracy: labelling three platform sources is only step one, and when a lesson gets a fact wrong, the teacher pays the repair cost.

11/06 23:40 China News Service ↗

A palm-sized terminal at a telecom expo scores spoken English in real time

At the 2026 Mobile Network Conference in Guangzhou on November 6, a small learning terminal drew crowds at the education stand. It has no entertainment functions; its core capability is that a student reads one English sentence aloud and the device marks pronunciation errors and suggests corrections.

The point is the feedback loop in speaking and listening. In a 40-minute class, only a handful of students usually get to read aloud; after class, parents cannot correct pronunciation and teachers cannot listen to every recording. Speech scoring turns accuracy and fluency into observable indicators and produces a class-level map that teachers can use to address common errors.

After class the same standard extends to a personal device, with practice data flowing back to teachers and parents. The system logs recurring problem phonemes and weak sentence patterns; its scoring logic is the same lineage as the machine-scored speaking section of the gaokao and zhongkao.

On deployment, 5G and edge computing support low latency, offline use and scale. For county and rural schools, that means a province-wide pronunciation standard and practice set can reach ordinary classrooms and kitchen tables; the classroom screen, the personal terminal and the parent app form three data entry points.

Why it mattersScoring turns "speaking up" into a markable item, which first helps students who were afraid to speak. But the finer the indicators, the more a class can revolve around them: pronunciation becomes score-chasing while logic and composure go ungraded. The cost sits in two places — who buys the terminals, and whether teachers get time to read the dashboards. For county schools, a provincial standard narrows the resource gap; the real divide is whether device refresh and connectivity can enter a recurring budget rather than a one-off expo demo.

Xueda finishes buyback: 2.95 million shares, 2.42% of capital, about 98.9 million yuan

Xueda Education (000526.SZ) told an investor relations event on November 6 that the buyback it began in June 2026 is complete: 2,949,700 shares, or 2.42 per cent of total share capital, for about 98.9 million yuan.

The shares are held in the company's dedicated buyback account. Under the disclosed plan they may be sold between 12 months and three years after the completion notice, and any unsold portion will be cancelled. The company says the buyback is meant to protect shareholder value.

On those figures the average price is about 33.5 yuan a share. Total share capital is unchanged for now, so any per-share earnings benefit only arrives with cancellation or disposal.

Why it mattersA buyback is an alternative to paying out cash: nearly 100 million yuan moves from the balance sheet into a securities account, and the share count does not fall until cancellation. For a company whose main spending is campuses and teachers, the same money could have gone to refurbishment, pay or curriculum; choosing the buyback implies management thinks the shares trade below reinvestment returns. The disclosure on disposal in mid-2027 will say more than the completion notice — selling and cancelling have opposite effects on per-share metrics.
