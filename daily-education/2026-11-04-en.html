11/03 17:20 Jiangsu Provincial Department of Education ↗

Jiangsu proposes three new degree-granting institutions and 17 majors, most of them vocational

At 17:20 on November 3 the Jiangsu Provincial Office of the Academic Degrees Committee (江苏省学位委员会办公室) published its proposed additions for 2026 to the province's bachelor's degree-granting institutions and programmes. The comment period runs from November 3 to November 16, and written objections must arrive by 17:30 on the closing day.

The list has two halves. Three institutions would be authorised to confer bachelor's degrees, among them Lianyungang Normal College (连云港师范学院). Seventeen majors would be authorised: five at Lianyungang Normal College, including pre-school education; six at Jiangsu Vocational University of Construction (江苏建筑职业技术大学), including intelligent construction engineering; and six at Changzhou Vocational University of Information Technology (常州信息职业技术大学), including software engineering technology. Jiaohuidian news, reporting at 21:32 on November 3, put the two vocational universities at 12 of the 17 majors.

The legal basis is the province's measures on bachelor's degree authorisation and conferral. The notice is unusually specific about how to object: an institution must stamp its letter and give a contact name, postal address and telephone number; an individual must sign a real name, ID number, employer and contact details. Two numbers are provided, 025-83335660 and 83335057.

What the notice does not publish matters too. The annex is a Word file of names; there are no figures on faculty ratios, per-student funding or practical teaching hours, so an outsider can see who is on the list but not what qualified them.

Why it mattersDegree-granting authority is the last mile for a vocational undergraduate university. Such a school can enrol students and issue diplomas, but until it is authorised, its graduates hold a graduation certificate rather than a bachelor's degree. Publishing institutions and majors as separate lists moves the unit of review from the school down to the programme: at one university a major may confer degrees while a neighbouring one cannot. Twelve of the 17 majors sit with two vocational universities, so the growth is going to vocational education rather than to new general colleges. The test comes with the first cohort to graduate — who supervises the thesis or practical project, against which standard, and whether the sample inspection passes.
The other angleFor applicants and parents this is an observable signal. For the same major, a college with degree-granting authority removes a step of credential checking in postgraduate applications, civil-service and public-institution screening, and points-based settlement in some cities. Once the list is settled in mid-November, the phrase "bachelor's degree" in a 2027 prospectus will have a paper trail, and application choices will follow it.

11/03 Yunnan Provincial Department of Education ↗

Yunnan proposes 20 pilot counties, 60 training bases and 600 priority schools for school sport

On November 3 the Yunnan Provincial Department of Education (云南省教育厅) published its proposed list for campus football, basketball and volleyball (校园「三大球」): 20 counties and districts as pilot counties, 60 schools as training bases and 600 schools as priority schools. Objections may be filed from November 3 to 6.

The list follows the province's 20 measures on reviving football and its student fitness programme for 2026-2030. Schools applied, prefecture and city education bureaus recommended, and selection drew on where youth training is placed, results from the past three years of the provincial four-tier school leagues in the three sports, and progress in the counties piloting school-sport reform.

The structure is neat. The 60 bases split evenly across the sports, 20 each; the 600 priority schools split 200 each and are then allocated by prefecture — Kunming gets 28 basketball, 32 football and 35 volleyball schools, Nujiang 6, 1 and 6.

The 20 pilot counties sit in 15 prefectures and Diqing has none, yet the prefecture holds three of the 60 base places, at Diqing Prefecture No. 1 Middle School and Shangri-La Middle School. Objections go in writing to the department's sport and health office; the number is 0871-65149031.

Why it mattersWhat this list allocates is not prestige but pitches, coaches and competition slots. A pilot county means county leagues, teacher training and funding run to a pilot standard; a priority school means a team that trains year-round and is expected to deliver results. Three tiers, three levels of investment, three sets of assessments. The imbalance is explicit: Kunming holds 35 volleyball priority schools, Nujiang exactly one football school — which is what allocating by three years of league results produces. The strong get more, and the weak catch up only through the pilot-county channel. Diqing has no pilot county but does hold base places; whether it gains a county next round is the test of whether the mechanism actually favours the weak.

Vocational & Careers
11/03 10:17 Changsha Human Resources and Social Security Bureau ↗

Ministry's regional jobs tour closes in Changsha: 7,100 jobs, 5,000 graduates, 1,623 intentions

The 2026 "students to the regions" stop in Changsha (百校学子地方行) and the city's "backpack" talent drive ran at the Central South University gymnasium on October 31, the final stop of the education ministry's 2026 series. The Changsha human resources bureau (长沙市人力资源和社会保障局) organised the graduate fair, inviting 100 universities and more than 4,000 students, with 182 employers recruiting on site.

The pay structure is the hard part of this story. The 182 employers advertised over 7,100 jobs, and the online section listed 541 employers and 13,000 positions. On-site roles carried average advertised pay of 180,000 yuan a year; 21% topped 300,000 yuan, the best approached one million, and 41.1% required a master's degree or doctorate.

By the close, more than 5,000 graduates had come through, 6,043 CVs were collected, 8,813 interviews were logged and 1,623 first-round intentions recorded. Online, 4,438 people registered, 10,242 CVs arrived, and AI tools ran more than 1,200 CV reviews, mock interviews and career analyses.

The fair ran seven zones — industry display, talent policy, sector recruitment, job-hunt coaching, on-site signing, livestreamed recruiting and city visits — mapped to 11 of Changsha's industrial chains. Fifteen provincial career advisers, recruiters and start-up mentors staffed the coaching desks.

Why it mattersRead the four numbers as a filter: 5,000 attendees, 6,043 CVs, 8,813 interviews, 1,623 intentions — about one intention per three graduates, though the unit is person-times rather than people, and a start date still depends on medical checks and tripartite agreements. Average pay of 180,000 yuan and a 41.1% master's-and-above share say who the city is bidding for: postgraduates, well above the bachelor's market. What government organising adds is sequence — industry exhibits first, interview booths second — not volume. The cost lands at both ends: universities carry travel and duty of care for students crossing provinces, and the city keeps paying housing subsidies and settlement quotas long after the fair.

11/03 China Education Daily ↗

Ningbo job fair draws 56 employers and 400 jobs; 97% of a Geely cohort is already interning

The Hangzhou Bay Automotive College (杭州湾汽车学院) of Ningbo University of Technology held the first large autumn job fair of the season for its 2027 cohort at the Qianwan campus. Fifty-six employers brought more than 400 positions, 90% of them local to Ningbo, spanning new-energy vehicles, high-end equipment, new materials and semiconductors — the industries of the Qianwan New Area.

Recruiters included national "little giant" specialists, municipal specialist firms and single-champion manufacturers, with roles running from R&D and design to smart manufacturing, quality control and process optimisation. Zhejiang Lingyuan Brain-Computer Technology, Ningbo's first brain-computer company, recruited on campus for the first time; its core laboratory sits inside the college.

The harder numbers come from the Geely arrangement. In the "four-in-one" dual-study cohort for digital automotive engineers run jointly by the college and Geely, 97% of the first graduating class are already interning at Geely. The college, one of the first national modern industry colleges, runs the dual-study system with the company, and 100% of student final-year projects come from real Geely work.

The college operates on a "campus plus industrial park plus R&D park" model and runs a programme that sends staff to 100 companies with three checklists, holding company-specific recruitment sessions throughout the year.

Why it mattersThe 97% tells you more than the 400 jobs. The selection inside an order-based cohort happens after enrolment and before the factory gate: the college spends three years proving to an employer that these students can do the work, which is why the employer hands over workstations and projects early. The price is written into the timetable. With every final-year project sourced from Geely, the teacher's job shifts from setting the question to vetting it, and supervision becomes joint — hours the college has to fund. The dependence is real too: if a production line is retooled or a project is cut, the cohort is the first thing affected, and the students' study plan has already been built around it.

11/03 11:06 Jiemodui ↗

Haidian's Zhiqi platform reaches 80-plus schools with 3,000 agents and 150,000 users

The Zhiqi (智启) AI-plus-education public service platform in Beijing's Haidian district has grown from a five-school pilot to more than 80 participating schools, with 3,000-plus agents serving over 150,000 teachers and students, Jiemodui reported on November 3, citing Haidian News of October 23.

The platform is run by Beijing Zhiqi Wenhua Education Technology (智启文华). Its registered capital is 50 million yuan: Kaiwen Education (凯文教育) contributed 32.5 million yuan for 65%, Zhipu (智谱华章) 10 million yuan for 20%, and Hongzhi Ruishi 7.5 million yuan for 15%.

Technically it draws on the Haidian teacher training college and the district's teaching research institute, runs on Zhipu's GLM-5, and packages lesson plans and question banks into agents for teaching, learning and administration. The primary school attached to Renmin University built a cross-disciplinary lesson-design agent with Zhiqi; teachers at Haidian Jixiu Experimental School coach theirs on when to intervene in a lesson; and at Beijing No. 57 Middle School teachers encode their subject expertise and methods into the agent.

Kaiwen Education's third-quarter report puts nine-month revenue at 271 million yuan, up 8.44% year on year, with net profit attributable to shareholders of 1.86 million yuan, up 94.33%, and recurring net profit of 1.45 million yuan, up 554.43%. Operating cash flow was 103 million yuan, up 5.0%.

Why it mattersThree thousand agents across 80-odd schools is close to 40 per school. Supply is no longer the constraint; review and maintenance are. Someone has to decide that an agent has explained a topic wrongly, update it when a textbook changes, and answer for what it says. Haidian's route is to put researchers and star teachers in front, making the agent a container for teaching research rather than a generic model bought off the shelf — which is also why the platform started with teacher development before student-facing tools. For schools the budget line has to move from equipment and software to teaching-research services. For teachers, writing expertise into an agent is new invisible labour that is not counted in teaching hours and has no evaluation standard yet.

11/03 Hexun · Gelonghui ↗

Chenlin Education to buy 51% of a GaN packaging firm, paid in cash and new shares

Chenlin Education (01593.HK) announced on November 3 that its wholly owned subsidiary, Chenlin Education Technology (Jiangxi) Group, had signed a framework acquisition agreement with Jiangxi Yuhongjin Materials Technology (the seller), Jiangxi Yuhongjin Electronic Technology (江西誉鸿锦电子技术, the target) and a guarantor, Gelonghui reported. The seller would transfer 51% of the target.

On completion the buyer would hold 51% and control, with 49% staying with the seller. Consideration is to be a mix of cash and newly issued shares, and the seller undertakes a profit guarantee covering the target's net operating profit, excluding non-recurring items, over a commitment period.

The target designs and makes chips, discrete semiconductors and electronic components, concentrating on packaging, testing and module assembly for gallium-nitride power devices. Its portfolio covers TO, QFN and LGA/BGA formats, and it has developed wire-bond-free through-hole and system-in-package processes that cut packaging cost and shrink device and module size.

The announcement calls GaN a knowledge-intensive industry and says the target badly needs facility, equipment and process engineers, describing a broad field of cooperation with the group. That sentence is the only place where the notice points directly at the education business.

Why it mattersA private higher-vocational group buying a chip packaging plant has to be read on two ledgers. Tuition cash flow is steady but slow-growing; a semiconductor asset changes the revenue mix and ties the school's balance sheet to an unfamiliar production line. Paying in new shares dilutes existing holders, while a profit guarantee defers the question of real profitability by three years — and if the target misses, the write-down lands on the listed company's accounts, which is to say on the school's operating surplus. The line about the target needing facility, equipment and process engineers is where the deal actually touches education: if the line becomes a placement site for students and a practice post for teachers, it is industry-education integration; if it is only consolidation, the school has bought a new story. A framework agreement still has due diligence and regulatory approval ahead of it; only the direction is settled.

11/03 China Education Daily ↗

Shanghai senior-learning fair opens with 102 exhibitors, 500-plus courses and an AI focus

The second Shanghai Senior Learning Course Exhibition (上海老年学习课程展) opened at the Shanghai Exhibition Centre on November 2 for a three-day run, with 8,928 square metres of floor space, 102 participating organisations and more than 500 courses on show.

The opening published Shanghai's first batch of flagship senior-education courses and an "AI Literacy 100" course adapted for older learners, and relaunched the One-Net Learning (一网通学) platform in a 2.0 version. The 2026 flagship list leans heavily on AI, from a core course on AI literacy and practice to applied sessions such as writing a life story with AI and making heritage crafts with AI tools.

Under the theme "learning without boundaries, age without limits", the fair groups courses into four categories — good courses near home, interesting courses for daily life, courses for late-life ventures and new AI literacy courses. Shared classrooms and a stage were built into the hall, and taster sessions were open to the public by booking.

The fair is guided by the National Open University for the Elderly, the China Association of Universities for the Third Age and the China Adult Education Association, hosted by Shanghai Open University and the city's learning-society centre, and organised by Shanghai Education Television and Shanghai Senior University.

Why it mattersThe constraint in senior education has never been demand; it is how supply is organised. District senior universities and community schools allocated places by street and taught mostly calligraphy and singing. Bringing 102 organisations into one hall turns course provision from internal scheduling into purchasing from the market. AI literacy is a smart wedge because it answers two problems at once — older people who cannot use phones to get things done, and a curriculum that needs refreshing — but teachers remain scarce: someone who understands AI and wants to teach older learners usually has to be found among university staff and corporate volunteers. Then comes who pays. Public purchase, charitable investment or learner fees: the choice decides whether the course is still on the floor next year.
