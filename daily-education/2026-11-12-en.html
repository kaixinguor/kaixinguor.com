11/11 15:34 Xinjiang HR bureau (carrying China Labour and Social Security News) ↗

Seven ministries set continuing-education rules for engineers: six chapters, 29 articles, hours valid nationwide from January 1, 2027

China Labour and Social Security News reported that seven Chinese ministries had jointly issued the Regulations on Continuing Education for Engineering and Technical Personnel in recent days. The signatories are human resources and social security, industry and information technology, natural resources, ecology and environment, housing and urban-rural development, transport, and the cyberspace administration. The text runs to six chapters and 29 articles, covering principles, administration, content and providers, hour accounting and supervision, and takes effect on January 1, 2027.

Study is split into general and specialist subjects and is meant to happen in engineering practice. The rules recognise ten forms of continuing education, among them online and offline training, academic and technical exchange, commercialising patents and monographs, and competitions. Employers and training bodies are encouraged to explore "AI plus" models and to build an all-domain self-directed learning system for engineers. Coverage extends to engineering and technical staff and their managers in enterprises, public institutions and social organisations, across the engineering occupations listed in the new edition of the national occupational classification.

Hours are registered and converted at rates set by format. Anything logged in the national continuing-education information system for professional and technical personnel is valid across the country. The rules also require a mechanism linking continuing education to appointment and promotion, and a funding model shared by government, society, employers and individuals, with the human resources ministry coordinating alongside industry regulators.

Why it mattersThe operative phrase is "valid nationwide". Until now an engineer who changed province or employer often had to redo hours locally, with the cost falling on the individual; a national register removes that duplicated cost and turns "did this person study" from an internal HR matter into a checkable public record. The second lever is the link to appointment and promotion, which does not fix a threshold but hands standard-setting to industry regulators and employers - and whoever sets the threshold decides which training market gets served.
Another angleFor training providers the demand side is being re-cut: with exchange, patents and contests all convertible into hours, the old model of selling seat time is diluted. For employers, cross-employer verification means a candidate's continuing-education record is visible at hiring, which lowers the information asymmetry in training spend.

Schools & Governance

学校与治理
11/12 China Education Daily ↗

"Mama canteens" reach 78 of Liling's 128 schools with kitchens: 319 parents behind the counter, satisfaction above 95%

China Education Daily reported on November 12 that Liling, a county-level city in Hunan, has been hiring parents to staff school canteens since the start of this autumn term. The model began at Dongjiang Middle School, a public school on the urban fringe whose rebuild pushed enrolment past 5,000: its first open recruitment in February drew two applicants per place. Hired parents pass health checks, food-safety training, skills tests and background screening, then work across receiving, washing, cooking and serving - and now make up more than 45% of canteen staff there.

In April the city government issued a plan to spread the model, writing numerical targets into annual school appraisals: at least 30% of canteen staff to be parents, and parent satisfaction at 95% or above. The city also moved the network fee for kitchen surveillance, water testing and parents' health-certificate costs into the municipal budget, and pays small schools with fewer than 200 pupils a subsidy of 1,000 yuan a month.

Oversight runs on two sets of numbers: across all school and kindergarten canteens the city claims nine full-coverage requirements, from named official accountability and price-checked procurement to open tendering, kitchen cameras, quality upgrades, special audits and joint party-discipline audits. Every school must form a parent meal-supervision committee of at least seven people, hold a weekly parent inspection day and one or two open-kitchen days a month, and respond to complaints within five working days. Dongjiang Middle School appraises its parent staff monthly and dismisses the lowest-ranked, with six-month contracts.

Citywide, 78 of the 128 schools with canteens now run a standardised "mama canteen" and 319 parents work in kitchens, with combined student and parent satisfaction steady above 95%. Menus are co-designed from student surveys and parent suggestions, fixed at two meat dishes, one vegetable and one soup.

Why it mattersThe model flips who bears the cost: schools used to buy a service while parents complained from outside the gate; now parents trade labour for oversight and the city pays to standardise. The 1,000-yuan monthly subsidy is aimed precisely at schools under 200 pupils - the ones contractors most readily drop. But the binding constraint is not sentiment, it is the nine coverage requirements and the five-day response clock: without a traceable process, putting parents in the kitchen makes responsibility blurrier, not clearer. It travels only where the city treasury takes on those three cost lines instead of squeezing them out of school operating budgets.

11/12 China Education Daily ↗

Kunming opens 50-plus free autumn-holiday activities on November 13-15, deliberately unhooked from assessment

China Education Daily reported on November 12 that Kunming's education and sports bureau has organised a programme of out-of-school activities for the 2026 autumn holiday, running November 13 to 15. More than 50 practical projects are offered free of charge, and pupils and parents across the city may book places as families under their real names, voluntarily.

Sites span both banks of the Dianchi greenway, a movable pontoon bridge, the Kunming youth centre, rivers flowing into Dianchi and city ecology study points. The rules are explicit: participation is voluntary, families pick the nearest site, schools and families coordinate, no group transport is arranged centrally, no one is required to tick off every site, and nothing is linked to student assessment.

Courses are banded by age and built around bridge culture, engineering construction and art-making, with fitness challenges and science activities - the pitch being that one bridge is a classroom. The ecology strand is open, self-directed study at sites including the Dianchi treatment science centre, Cuihu Park, the Mingtong and Jinzhi river nature centres and Haihong wetland, led by professional guides who walk children through native plants, animals and the lake's cleanup.

Why it mattersThe hard part of an autumn holiday is never the holiday: it is where the children go for three days, who pays and who is responsible. Kunming answers all three in one design - free (cost shifted to the public purse), named voluntary booking (liability boundary clear), and no assessment link (no new comparable metric). That lowers the middle-layer cost for schools: no central organising, no attendance reporting, no extra teacher duty. What remains is coverage: 50-plus is a count of projects, not of places, so whether families can actually book, and how slots at popular sites are allocated, decides whether this is universal or first come, first served.

Vocational & Careers

11/11 10:10 Yunnan Daily / Yunnan.cn ↗

Yunnan's vocational skills contest closes with 9,862 competitors from 169 schools, run along 42 industry tracks

Yunnan Daily reported on November 11 that the 2026 provincial vocational skills contest for college students closed in Baoshan. The competition covered all 16 prefectures and cities in the province and 169 schools, with 1,652 teams and 9,862 competitors entered.

For the first time the contest was organised as tracks rather than isolated trades: 42 tracks were mapped onto provincial industry priorities including green energy, biomedicine, highland agriculture and hot-spring health tourism, in an explicit attempt to align programme design with what firms actually need.

Baoshan sent 153 teams - 916 teachers and students - into 31 tracks and came away with 181 awards, 45 of them first prizes, for 249 points. That put the city first in the province on total awards, total points and number of first prizes, its best result at a provincial contest.

Why it mattersThe track system changes who writes the questions, not just the labels: if tasks are derived backwards from technical standards that firms accept, colleges can no longer win by drilling past papers, and programmes are pushed to line up with links in the industrial chain. What decides the reform is the afterlife of the contest - whether the 42 tracks feed course standards back into everyday teaching, or leave behind only a cohort of medallists. Spread across 169 schools, 9,862 competitors is under 60 per school: competition is still a minority activity, and the test of the next round is whether track standards become practical training for every student.

Autumn recruitment adds 131 job-service events: 87 industry fairs, 32 livestreamed sessions, 9 policy briefings

Guangming Daily reported on November 12 that the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security had released a third batch of 131 job-service events under the 2026 national city job fair for graduates, confirming the figures on November 11.

The batch breaks into four kinds: 87 online and offline campus or industry fairs aimed at biomedicine, new energy, automobiles, finance, retail and robotics; three cross-regional travelling fairs; 32 livestreamed recruiting sessions; and nine policy briefing and careers-guidance sessions.

Graduates and employers can find details and join events through the main portal hosted by the China National Talent Network.

Why it mattersOnly three of the 131 events cross regional lines; the rest sit inside a campus or an industry, which suggests vacancy supply is still tightly bound to where a college happens to be, and that the information cost of applying across provinces has not really been flattened by a national campaign. The share that deserves watching is the 32 livestreamed sessions - nearly a quarter of the batch. They push the cost of applying close to zero and hand the screening cost to employers, so the number that matters is the signing rate, and the release does not give it.

Technology & Product

11/11 Office for Students ↗

England's regulator: AI has left unsupervised coursework unable to show what students know, and wants more AI-free assessment

On November 11 the Office for Students and Advance HE published joint research, "Making AI work for students: reflections on institutional practice" (OfS 2026.56). The fieldwork ran from June to September 2026 and drew on more than 300 students and staff at over 120 providers.

The central finding is that assessment has stopped doing its job. Many institutions still set unsupervised work that students described peers completing with a large language model, sometimes in minutes, for grades similar to those earned by students who did the work themselves; existing AI-detection tools do not reliably identify use either. The report frames this as a risk to the credibility of awards and to sector reputation, and as a disincentive to engage in learning at all. A Chinese-language summary of the findings ran on November 11.

The regulatory response has three parts. Providers must ensure assessment is a valid, reliable measure of knowledge and skills, which in many cases means secure, AI-free formats - closed-book exams, presentations, practicals and performances. Where evidence shows shortfalls, the OfS can impose specific registration conditions, levy fines, or suspend access to student support and grant funding. Alongside, it has published guidance on how existing conditions apply to AI. The research also found AI-literacy training for students is uneven, staff training is usually optional with low take-up, and most staff participants said AI had not yet delivered significant efficiency gains. Advance HE will launch an AI-focused Change Academy in early 2027.

Why it mattersThe report puts "how we examine" back at the centre of teaching reform: once an assignment cannot prove who wrote it, the anchor has to move to performance in controlled conditions - and the cost of that lands almost entirely on teacher hours and exam logistics. Closed-book means invigilation, oral means one-to-one time, practicals mean space, and none of it gets cheaper because of AI. The same arithmetic applies to Chinese universities: before buying detection software, a department should cost out how many teaching hours it takes to convert one course to invigilated, in-person assessment. The other idea worth borrowing is procedural - the regulator added no new registration condition for AI, only explained how existing ones apply, keeping discretion on its own side.
