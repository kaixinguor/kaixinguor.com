Policy & Regulation

11/10 Ministry of Education ↗

Rural-revitalisation admissions track confirmed: 160 national plus 390 provincial counties, applied from 2030

The Ministry of Education, the Ministry of Public Security and the Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs have jointly issued a notice creating a rural-revitalisation admissions track for universities (Jiaoxue 2026 No. 3, dated October 27, 2026 and posted on November 10). From the 2030 intake, the programme that since 2012 has channelled students from rural and poor areas into key universities will be reorganised, with a three-year transition: 2027 to 2029 continues under the existing rules.

Eligible areas are split into a national and a local list. The national list covers 160 national priority counties for rural revitalisation plus 390 provincial priority counties in central and western China, together with areas designated for Tibet and Xinjiang; degree programmes there are offered by universities under central ministries and by provincial public undergraduate institutions. The local list is drawn up by each province and served mainly by its own public universities. Both lists get entry and exit criteria and periodic review, with changes published in advance and filed with the ministry.

Eligibility keeps the "three matches" but tightens them: the applicant must be a graduating high-school senior; the student and at least one parent or legal guardian must hold continuous local household registration in the area; the student must have continuous enrolment and actual attendance at a senior secondary school run by that county; and registration, enrolment and attendance must each run at least three years up to the gaokao year. A ministry question-and-answer spells out two shifts: rural status is no longer required, so urban and rural students at the same county high school both qualify, while the graduating-senior and parental-registration requirements are new, aimed at repeat candidates and speculative applications.

Admissions run as a separately listed plan inside the university's regular undergraduate batch. "Double First-Class" universities must in principle keep their cut-off no lower than the provincial special-type control line, unfilled places go to a supplementary round, and universities may not limit recruitment to parts of a county. Students on the track get equal treatment on changing majors and on training, with academic tracking, a designated teacher and information on loans, scholarships and grants. Provincial implementation plans need cabinet approval, must be filed with the ministry and must be published by May 2027.

Why it mattersReplacing "rural status" with "studied at a county high school" changes the target, not just the wording. Eligibility now rests on enrolment and attendance, which closes two arbitrage routes at once: borrowing a place at a strong urban high school and coming back for the quota, and repeat candidates collecting the discount more than once. The cost lands on county high schools, which must keep good students to keep their quota. The real bottleneck is verification, because registration, enrolment and attendance have to be cross-checked by education, police and agriculture departments, and empty enrolment is the weakest link. Provinces have until May 2027 to publish plans, and that interval is when counties, universities and schools work out which counties qualify, how many places are allocated and which majors are opened.
The other angleFor universities, a separately listed plan plus a floor at the special-type control line means these places cannot be filled by lowering scores; admissions offices have to plan major capacity in advance and accept supplementary rounds. For families, the cost is not the application but what follows: equal treatment on major changes, academic support and aid packages decide whether students who get in also stay in.

11/10 China Education Daily ↗

A year of the school science-education plan: 134 pilot districts, 1,246 pilot schools, 3,000-plus resources

In October 2025 seven ministries including the Ministry of Education issued guidance on strengthening science education in primary and secondary schools, setting six tasks around subject integration, engineering practice and open collaboration, and a 2035 target of a fully built science-education ecosystem. A China Education Daily stocktake on November 10 finds the ministry has added a science-education section to the national smart-education platform, with more than 3,000 resources, 112 "Zhuiguang" science-literacy lessons and a nationwide call for 1,000 teaching cases, all free to schools and teachers.

On organisation, the ministry has set up a science and STEM pilot within its basic-education reform programme, working with 12 provinces including Beijing, and has built 134 national pilot districts and 1,246 pilot schools. Shanghai has made all 16 of its districts pilot areas, with one plan per district and one policy per school, producing more than 9,300 project-based learning cases across about 1,400 compulsory-education schools. Beijing has issued subject-specific teaching guidance and a "4+1" framework for experimental teaching, and Weifang in Shandong runs a ten-programme package that hires 100 PhDs, trains 100 science vice-principals and 100 science coaches.

Teachers are the heaviest line item. The ministry has created expert committees for technology, science and interdisciplinary teaching and has trained school principals, county education chiefs and teaching-research heads nationwide. Shenzhen recruits science vice-principals from universities, institutes and leading technology firms, with academicians, professors and company engineers in the pool; Chongqing has built a four-mentor system of university supervisors, class teachers, undergraduate mentors and parents, with more than 2,500 mentors registered.

Rural gaps get a section of their own. Suzhou district in Jiuquan, Gansu, built a shared digital resource platform and used rotating postings and visiting teachers to lift the share of rural science teachers qualified in their own subject from under 15% to 78%. Zhejiang runs a rural science-education programme promoting low-cost projects such as smart greenhouses and intangible-heritage engineering, while Jinjiang in Fujian links local firms into a three-year "industry plus" plan and Pingtang in Guizhou builds county-level science bases around the FAST radio telescope.

Why it mattersScience education slides easily into one more subject, one more activity and one more plaque. Only three inputs in this stocktake can be checked: resources (3,000-plus items, 112 lessons), organisation (134 districts, 1,246 schools, 12 provinces) and people (science vice-principals, a 2,500-mentor pool). The first two can be bought with a budget and a platform; the third cannot. Most science vice-principals are part-time and visit once or twice a term, and what counties lack is full-time science teachers. Suzhou's jump from under 15% to 78% came from rotating and visiting teachers, not new hires, which raises the matching rate by moving urban teachers' time elsewhere. The indicators worth watching are therefore not plaque counts but how many lab-based lessons each school runs per week, who marks the lab reports, and which budget line pays for the consumables.

11/09 Zhejiang Education Department ↗

Zhejiang opens its 2027 graduate job season: 1,000-plus employers, 29,000 posts linked

On November 5 the provincial education department and the human resources department opened Zhejiang's 2027 graduate employment and entrepreneurship season with a school-enterprise matching event at Zhejiang University of Technology, publicised by the department on November 9. A general job fair ran alongside it, publishing post demand by county and student-supply data by institution; more than 1,000 employers registered and 29,000-plus posts were linked. The event is part of the Ministry of Education's national autumn campus recruitment campaign.

Chen Feng, deputy party secretary of the provincial education working committee, split the work into a jobs side and a teaching side. The jobs side is to expand vacancies, run campus recruitment, use start-ups to create employment and help hard-to-place graduates, and to use big data and AI so that posts find students rather than the reverse. The teaching side is to adjust disciplines and majors faster and to adopt an employer-oriented view of what graduates can do.

Zhan Maowei, deputy director of the provincial human resources department, listed four tasks: expand and upgrade the pool of quality posts; send job information, career guidance, policy advice and start-up services into campuses; roll out youth job-readiness camps alongside a first employment lesson and workplace visits; and vet employer credentials and postings, guard against fake recruitment, training loans and job scams, and run the winter wage-arrears campaign.

The provincial education development centre, Ningbo University, Taizhou University and Zhejiang Mechanical and Electrical Vocational Technical College released 2027 graduate supply data for the province and their own institutions. Zhejiang University of Technology, China Jiliang University and Jinhua Vocational and Technical University exchanged practice with Leapmotor and Jingsheng Mechanical and Electrical, while the provincial human resources market and the Yuhang and Yiwu human resources bureaus pitched platform services, local industries and start-up policy.

Why it matters"Posts finding students" sounds like an algorithm problem but is really a data-permission problem: the vacancy database sits with human resources, the enrolment and student records sit with education, and matching only works when the two can be joined. Twenty-nine thousand posts is a credible number for one provincial fair, but a mismatch between post mix and major mix does not dissolve at a matching event. Putting the jobs side and the teaching side in the same hall is the easy part; the test is whether majors follow demand, which means moving staff, admissions plans and whole faculties. For students, the readiness camps and the anti-fraud warnings are two ends of one chain, because autumn recruitment is peak season for both vacancies and training-loan pitches.

11/09 10:31 EOL (China Education Online) ↗

Zhejiang health and elderly-care consortium launched with 80-plus members and three chairs, told to become a real entity

A provincial industry-education consortium for health, medical and elderly care was launched in Jinhua on November 7 and 8, guided by the national health vocational education teaching committee, hosted by Jinhua Vocational and Technical University and co-organised by Senyu Holding Group and Hangzhou Normal University. More than 150 delegates from over 80 vocational colleges, companies and medical institutions attended.

The board has three chair members — Jinhua Vocational and Technical University, Senyu Holding Group and Hangzhou Normal University — plus 11 vice-chair members including Gannan Health Vocational College and 13 standing council members including Jiangxi Medical College. Four keynote sessions ranged from the place of health and elderly care between an active government and an effective market to the overhaul of health programmes for the "medical and care combined" model, and to rebuilding care-related programme clusters and how students are trained.

The three chairs represent three kinds of actor: a vocational undergraduate university, a company working in dendrobium and traditional Chinese medicine health products, and a teacher-training university. Zhang Yanwen, deputy director of the national health vocational education teaching committee, told the meeting the consortium must become a real entity: a working council and governance structure, a clear operating roadmap, and flagship projects on shared standards, joint training, shared bases and joint research, so that it becomes a body that shares benefits and risks.

Why it mattersMost industry-education consortia end up as a plaque and one annual meeting. Whether this one becomes an entity can be judged on three things: an independent budget and a standing secretariat, who writes the programme standards, and whether internships are scheduled against companies' actual staffing plans. Giving the chairs to a vocational undergraduate university, a company and a teacher-training university puts the degree-awarding body, the employer and the teacher trainer at one table, which is the right structure. The friction comes next: how credits transfer, whether internship rotations match a company's peak season, and whether a company's contribution can be booked as a cost. Until those are settled, the consortium is a meeting that changes city each year.

Technology & Product

11/09 Huazhong Agricultural University ↗

Huazhong Agricultural University launches Tiansi, a subject-specific teaching model with 17 smart courses and 2,000 users

On November 6 the College of Animal Science and Technology and the College of Veterinary Medicine at Huazhong Agricultural University released "Tiansi", a teaching model for animal science and veterinary medicine, at the fifth national symposium on AI-enabled talent development. It was launched jointly with the Hubei association of higher education, the university, Zhihuishu and leaders from Wuhan Polytechnic University and Jianghan University; the university published the news on November 9.

The model is built on a domestic general-purpose foundation model, with a subject knowledge graph and a dedicated knowledge base, 17 smart courses, three core functions — subject dialogue, guided enquiry and problem search — and seven types of specialised agent including animal-disease screening, subject-frontier expert and experiment-design assistant. It spans teaching, learning, administration and research, and draws on the university's two A-plus Double First-Class disciplines, animal husbandry and veterinary medicine.

By launch it had served more than 2,000 students and staff, with 48,700 student uses, and is being piloted with several other universities. The design targets hallucination: answers are traceable to the knowledge base, and coverage extends to explaining experimental principles, analysing results, recommending resources, personalising study, designing training plans and monitoring teaching quality.

Why it mattersThe value of a subject-specific model is not that it can chat but that it can be trusted inside assessment: a wrong answer in a tutorial can be corrected, a wrong experiment design or disease screening suggestion cannot. That is why the knowledge base is pinned to course concepts and their relations, trading coverage for traceability. The real cost is upkeep. A 17-course graph has to be updated with textbooks and industry standards, and who does that, whether it counts as teaching workload, and who revises it when a graduating class changes textbooks decide whether the model outlives its launch. Two thousand users and 48,700 queries are a reasonable start; the ceiling is the data boundary, because a model confined to one university and one discipline is a good teaching assistant, and the replicable question is whether pilot universities can share the knowledge base.
