Policy & Regulation

11/08 11:58 Guancha ↗

Schools push back arrival and scrap morning reading: Jiangyin high schools at 7:20, a Chengdu primary at 8:40, Chongqing's youngest at 9:45 for first period

On November 8, the topic "multiple regions delay school arrival and cancel morning reading" topped Weibo's trending list. The report rounded up announced changes: from November 1, several Jiangsu middle and high schools began adjusting start times and shortening evening study; from November 5, Jiangyin's schools, including high schools, moved to 8:00 for primary, 7:40 for middle and 7:20 for high school, with implementation planned within the week.

Chengdu's Montpellier Primary told parents that from November 7, the start of winter, arrival would move to 8:40. Chengdu Liewu Middle School scrapped morning reading for all three middle-school years from the spring term, with arrival no earlier than 7:50 and classes at 8:00. Ningbo Qianwan New Area Middle School dropped its 7:30–7:50 morning reading, asking students to arrive by 8:00. Chongqing's Ba'nan Yudong No. 2 Primary moved grades 1–2 to a 9:00 arrival and 9:45 first period; the pilot has run smoothly for nearly two years.

Why it mattersPushing arrival back changes three things at once: how long children sleep, how families split the morning, and how schools fit the timetable. The first has solid evidence behind it; the latter two are implementation costs. For working parents, "drop the child then commute" can become "cannot drop the child before commuting", and after-school care and evening study must be rescheduled too. To judge whether a change is real, look past the notice: arrival records, first-period attendance and lateness, sleep surveys, and whether urban and rural schools in different districts follow the same standard. Move the clock without cutting homework and fixing transport, and half an hour of extra morning sleep gets eaten by an hour of later bedtime.

Vocational & Careers

职教与就业
11/07 People's Daily ↗

NPC vocational education law inspection team meets to discuss its report

People's Daily reported on November 7 that the NPC Standing Committee's vocational education law inspection team held its second plenary meeting on November 6 to discuss the draft inspection report. Vice chairpersons Cai Dafeng, Tie Ning and Losang Jamcan attended.

After hearing reports and suggestions, Cai said the team should fully recognise progress in vocational education, clearly grasp the new situation and challenges in implementing the law, sum up proven practices, identify the sticking points and deeper causes, and offer practical recommendations. It should complete the remaining tasks, revise the report, strengthen follow-up oversight and push for full and effective implementation.

Why it mattersAn inspection is neither legislation nor a policy document; it produces a report, review opinions and follow-up on rectification. What matters is whether the report spells out three accounts: whether per-student funding has arrived, how large the shortage of vocational teachers is, and whether incentives for firms to participate in schooling are actually paid. After the law's revision, local implementing rules and departmental division of labour are the main bottlenecks. If the report stops at "notable progress", the next inspection will meet the same problems. Schools and companies should watch the rectification list, not the meeting itself.

11/08 11:16 China News Service ↗

2026 US-China Education Summit at Columbia puts AI education and exchange at the centre

On November 7 local time, the 2026 US-China Education Summit was held at Columbia University in New York under the theme "A Dialogue Across a Century", drawing nearly 100 people from education, psychology, art and technology. Representatives came from dozens of universities including Peking, Tsinghua, Nanjing, Beijing Normal, Xiamen and Sichuan Film and Television, as well as Columbia, Stanford, Chicago, Northwestern and Minnesota.

The opening took place at Columbia's Teachers College, with remarks from Yu Jin of the China Institute, Lin Xiaodong of Columbia's EPIC centre and Dean Thomas Bailey. After keynote speeches, two plenary dialogues addressed "AI-enabled future education" and "US-China educational and people-to-people exchange", with parallel forums on youth development, art education and AI education innovation. The event was co-hosted by Columbia EPIC, the China Institute and the Asia Education Beijing Forum.

Why it mattersThis is classic track-two dialogue: no binding power, but it can build a shared language on AI education — what standards to use when assessing students' AI use, whether teacher training counts toward hours, and how academic integrity is defined for generated content. The test is what survives the summit: joint courses, credit recognition, teacher exchanges, joint research. If it ends with panels and photos, it will not resolve the two countries' differences over AI-use rules; if one discipline produces a shared rubric, that will outlast any joint statement.
