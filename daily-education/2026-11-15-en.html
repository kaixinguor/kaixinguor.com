11/13 Ministry of Education ↗

3.47 million sign up for China's 2027 postgraduate exam, sat on December 19 and 20

On November 13 the Ministry of Education said 3.47 million people have registered for the 2027 national postgraduate entrance examination, to be held on December 19 and 20, 2026. The ministry met the inter-ministerial joint conference on national education examinations for a security briefing; Vice-Minister Xiong Sihao spoke, and the Cyberspace Administration of China, the Central Secrecy Commission (State Secrecy Bureau), the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology and the Ministry of Public Security set out their parts of the plan.

The meeting divided the work between papers and rooms. On the paper side it asked for the value orientation and scientific quality of questions to be guarded, with all-round risk screening and closed-loop management of question papers from drafting to delivery. On the room side it called for stronger security systems, entry checks and patrols throughout each exam, joint campaigns against cheating, better explanation of the rules and reasonable accommodations for candidates.

The meeting framed exam security as a matter of selecting talent for the country, holding the line on fairness and keeping social stability. Beijing, Liaoning and Nanjing University gave presentations. Member units of the joint conference met at the main venue; provincial admissions committees and education departments joined from branch venues.

Why it mattersWith 3.47 million candidates, the two days in December require every province to line up rooms, invigilators, entry screening and paper transport at the same time. The briefing keeps question security and exam-hall security as separate items, which says the risk sits not only in the room but in the drafting and movement of papers. For the universities and schools that host test centres, that labour does not appear in per-student funding; it comes out of teaching time at the end of term.
Another angle The size of the postgraduate cohort is a lagging indicator for the job market. The notice does not split the 3.47 million into first-time candidates and repeat sitters, but the larger the pool, the more concentrated the divide between those who get in and those who do not becomes between late December and March, when careers services and counselling inside university departments, not exam authorities, absorb the pressure.

11/14 China Education Daily ↗

World Chinese Language Conference opens with 210 million learners and HSK past a million candidates

The 2026 World Chinese Language Conference opened in Beijing on November 13 under the theme "Chinese, more than a language", drawing about 2,000 government officials, scholars, university heads and representatives of international organisations, China Education Daily reported. Vice-President Han Zheng addressed the opening with four proposals: strengthening the capacity of international Chinese teaching, deepening people-to-people ties, promoting mutual learning among civilisations, and using Chinese to support international cooperation.

His speech supplied the scale: 210 million people now learn or use Chinese outside China. More than 82 million foreign nationals entered or left China last year, up 26.4%, and the figure rose another 20.6% year on year in the first half of 2026. Han called for support to other countries in running Chinese teaching and for systematic services at every stage of learning.

Three releases followed. The first was the Yufei vertical model for international Chinese education, which offers learners instant assessment and on-demand tutoring, gives teachers multi-turn tools to generate material for preparation, classroom interaction and after-class work, and provides institutions with standard interfaces. The second was a set of 30 case studies in three categories: "Chinese plus" programmes that link the language to vocational training and industry, immersion teaching where subjects are taught in Chinese, and partner innovations built on the iChinese platform. The third was a poetry short film, "Tianya Gong Ci Shi", jointly guided by the National Radio and Television Administration and the Ministry of Education and produced by five bodies including China International Communications Group and regional broadcasters in Henan, Shandong and Chengdu.

A parallel HSK global partners meeting the same day reported 1,522 test centres in 169 countries, the widest country coverage of any language test, and more than one million candidates in 2026 for the first time. A survey of over 100,000 HSK candidates in 27 countries found more than 70% want to study in China. Among Chinese and China-linked multinational employers, more than 90% state a Chinese requirement in recruitment; more than 15% of employees with stronger Chinese were promoted in the past year, and 48% had been given wider responsibilities because of the language. Sixty-seven new centres were announced for 2026.

Why it mattersDemand is no longer the constraint: 210 million learners and a million candidates a year settle that. The bottleneck is supply - test centres, textbooks, teachers and assessment. The releases point in two directions at once. Yufei serves learners, teachers and institutions from one model, and one of the 30 case categories is "Chinese plus vocational", which is where the language stops being preparation for a degree and becomes a skill that can be hired. If those two strands connect, the model shortens preparation time and the cases turn into course packages; if they do not, the model is a convenience and the cases are a showcase. The risk sits in assessment: 169 countries and 1,522 centres put the weight on item banks and marking consistency, and 67 new centres mean invigilator training has to expand at the same pace, paid for by host countries and partner institutions.

11/14 Yangtze Evening News (via Xinhua Jiangsu) ↗

Jiangsu bans tests for two weeks after the new autumn holiday and written homework for the first two grades

Jiangsu's provincial education department issued an autumn-holiday charter and ten prohibitions on November 13, the Yangtze Evening News reported. The first autumn holiday began on November 14 in Nanjing and other cities.

The charter asks pupils to spend at least an hour a day outdoors in the autumn weather, walking, running, cycling, skipping or playing ball games, and to keep regular hours rather than sleeping late and scrolling. It restates the "double reduction" rule: no written holiday homework in grades one and two, strict caps on volume and time in other grades. It also encourages housework, study trips and community volunteering.

The ten prohibitions cover providers, schools and fees. Study bases, tutoring companies, attractions, restaurants and childcare providers may not operate without a licence, mislead on price or advertise falsely; childcare and study trips may not abandon their non-profit character or charge extra; tutoring companies may not sell disguised academic classes. Schools may not eat into the holiday with outings or sports days, or use self-study rooms, childcare or off-campus practice as cover for teaching, revision or tests; may not run extra classes for top pupils; may not force pupils into childcare or refuse those who apply voluntarily; may not charge in the holiday's name or route fees through parents' committees.

Two clauses are the most concrete: no written homework in grades one and two during the holiday, with strict limits for other grades, and no academic test of any kind in the two weeks after it ends.

Why it mattersThe two-week test ban is the hard clause. It turns the autumn holiday from a window for catching up into a buffer, and pushes the pressure onto the timetable that follows: schools either compress the syllabus or move tests earlier, and both mean re-planning. The prohibitions bind schools, not the market. The clause on disguised academic tutoring and the one on childcare fees depend on inspections, and inspections need staff. For working parents the practical question is not whether the holiday exists but where the children go while it lasts.

11/13 16:01 People's Daily Online ↗

Beijing's Xicheng district promises a specialist science adviser for every school within three years

Xicheng district in Beijing launched its drive to become a pilot district for science and technology education, People's Daily Online reported on November 13. The district will expand its pool of experts and link it to a "100 scholars in schools" programme, aiming to give every primary and secondary school at least one specialist adviser within three years.

Xicheng was picked in 2024 for the first national batch of science-education pilot districts and again in October 2026 for the first batch of "science and technology education" pilot districts. Cai Dongmei, head of the district education commission, drew the distinction: science education adds, bringing inquiry-based courses to every pupil; technology education multiplies, mixing science, technology, engineering and maths and feeding early talent development.

The launch published a three-year action plan for 2026-2029 and set up an 18-member expert committee drawing on Tsinghua, Peking University, the Beijing Institute of Education and the Beijing Academy of Educational Sciences, covering aerospace, life sciences and artificial intelligence. On resources, the district will use its two science museums as twin hubs, build two off-campus student centres and ten joint innovation laboratories, and draw on 130 partner organisations. Beijing Technology and Business University, the district education commission, the district market regulator and No. 161 Middle School signed a four-party agreement on a food-safety and health laboratory for secondary-school students.

Why it mattersThe difficulty is not recruiting advisers but timetabling them: how often an expert can come, who pays the fee, and whether the hours count towards a teacher's workload decide whether the adviser becomes a fixed period on the timetable or a speech at the opening ceremony. Xicheng has produced a list - 130 partner organisations, ten laboratories, two off-campus centres - and a list becomes a curriculum only if each school has someone who can turn outside resources into teaching hours. That coordinator is usually harder to find than the expert.

11/13 21st Century Business Herald ↗

Hong Kong PolyU opens an aerospace institute with HK$4.3 billion of research spending behind it

Hong Kong Polytechnic University announced the Polytechnic Aerospace Research Institute (PARA) on November 13, a university-level platform that houses four centres: deep-space exploration, the COMAC-PolyU large aircraft institute, the low-altitude economy centre, and a joint centre for space manufacturing technology.

Zhao Ruheng, senior vice-president for research and innovation, said PolyU spent HK$4.3 billion on internal research between 2022/23 and 2025/26, with priority given to deep space, large aircraft, the low-altitude economy and space manufacturing. He said PolyU is the only Hong Kong university to have taken part in the Chang'e 3 to Chang'e 6 lunar missions and the Tianwen-1 Mars mission.

Yu Hongyu, executive director of the institute and a professor of mechanical engineering, said the deep-space centre dates from 2021, has worked on the Chang'e and Tianwen missions, runs a space-resources laboratory for lunar soil and is extending into asteroid exploration. The aircraft institute, set up in November 2024, runs more than ten projects in intelligent design, precision machining and maintenance. The low-altitude centre works on airspace management and drones in dense cities; its master's programme in the low-altitude economy has 70 students registered for 2026/27. The space-manufacturing centre is co-built with the Chinese Academy of Sciences' space application centre and helped develop China's first space 3D printer. Four new platforms are planned: satellite and payload integration, space and extreme-environment health, space governance, and advanced propulsion.

Policy is moving the same way. Hong Kong's first five-year plan calls for industrialising space technology and the low-altitude economy, and the 2026 policy address has a section on supporting deep-space exploration and commercial space, with a new round of space-technology projects and simpler licensing for low-orbit satellites.

Why it mattersThe HK$4.3 billion is total internal research spending over four years, not a new budget line for the institute, which makes PARA first a consolidation: four scattered strands are put under one heading so that new money can be argued for. The most transferable detail for mainland universities is the master's programme, with 70 students registered for 2026/27 - a degree scales faster than a research centre. Space manufacturing depends on a joint centre with the CAS space application centre, which points to where Hong Kong is thin: fabrication and engineering validation.

11/13 12:07 The Paper ↗

Dong Mingzhu defends Gree's technical school: 100 million yuan a year, 300 places and more than 8,000 applicants

Dong Mingzhu, principal of the Zhuhai Gree Technical School and chair of Gree Electric, responded on CCTV Finance's "Dialogue" programme on November 12 to the suggestion that the company runs the school to make money. Gree has to put in 100 million yuan a year to keep the school running well, she said; the point of education is not a diploma but a skill that lets a young person stand on their own feet.

The school, founded by Gree, opened its first enrolment in August and took applications until mid-September, with Dong as principal. The prospectus offered 300 places at 9,000 to 15,000 yuan a year to junior-high graduates aged 15 to 18 from across the country, a three-year course with nine majors mapped to intelligent manufacturing, electrical technology and information technology. As a full-time private technical school it drew more than 8,000 applicants for those 300 places.

At the first class on October 19, Dong laid down rules on daily life: a campus shop selling oranges at 1.5 yuan was told it was fleecing students and could not operate; hairdressers were brought in at weekends because pupils' hair was too long. She told the new intake that skilled workers have choices too - "we can train another Zhang Xue" - and that a diploma does not equal ability, while Gree needs people who understand both technology and sales.

Why it mattersThe arithmetic is simple: 300 places at 9,000 to 15,000 yuan a year brings in 2.7 million to 4.5 million yuan, a rounding error against a 100 million yuan subsidy. Gree is buying people who will still be there in three years, not tuition income. With more than 8,000 applicants for 300 places, the admission rate is about 4%, narrower than most vocational colleges, which shows that a company-run school is a small pilot and no substitute for public provision. For a pupil, the value lies not in the certificate but in a guaranteed route into a job - if the promise that anyone who wants to stay can stay is kept.

11/13 13:36 Sichuan government procurement ↗

Pengzhou buys an AI classroom for 6.13 million yuan, with teacher training at 2% of the contract

Sichuan's public resources trading site published the award on November 13 for the Pengzhou education technology and equipment centre's "education informatisation and equipment - 2026 central-hall AI classroom" project. The budget was 6.16 million yuan; the contract went to Chengdu Jianjiang Information Technology at 6.13 million yuan, with a score of 92.15.

The item list is built around iFlytek's Changyan Zhiai platform for primary and secondary AI teaching: five teaching systems at 89,800 yuan each, five theme resource packs and five practice resource packs at 99,800 yuan each, six subject-integration packs at 49,800 yuan, six large-model resource packs at 59,800 yuan, five AI supercomputing systems at 69,800 yuan, one education platform at 455,080 yuan, 128 robot kits at 6,290 yuan and 104 learning terminals at 1,380 yuan.

Alongside the hardware and furniture there is 126,000 yuan for training. The rest of the list includes 150 hexagonal pupil tables at 2,390 yuan, 750 stools at 108 yuan, 128 practice kits, 12 Go-playing robots, 12 AI science kiosks and five charging carts. The agency fee of 58,000 yuan is paid by the winning supplier; the Pengzhou finance bureau supervises the contract.

Why it mattersMost of the 6.13 million yuan buys things that can be checked off at delivery: the 128 robots come to 805,000 yuan, the five supercomputing systems to 349,000 yuan, the platform to 455,000 yuan. Training is 126,000 yuan, about 2% of the contract. When the equipment is depreciated over three years, replacement and consumables are not in this contract and will have to come out of the next budget. For the school, the real test is whether 128 robots fit into the timetable: one period a week, or a demonstration on open days, decides whether the money bought teaching or a showpiece.
