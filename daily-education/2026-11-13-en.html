Policy & Regulation

政策与监管
11/12 16:07 Xinhua ↗

China consults on a skills-evaluation regulation: seven chapters, 63 articles and eight grade levels

On November 12 the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security published a draft Regulation on the Evaluation of Skilled Talent and opened it to public comment until December 12. Xinhua reported that the text runs to seven chapters and 63 articles and folds three activities - occupational qualification assessment, skilled-worker grade certification and special vocational competence testing - into a single rulebook covering standards, providers, examination practice, certificates and supervision. According to First Financial, the skilled workforce exceeds 220 million people, including more than 72 million in high-skill roles.

Entry rules differ by type. Occupational qualifications are managed by catalogue, and nothing outside it may be created or licensed; grade certification works by filing, with providers required to register with provincial or higher human resources authorities within 10 days of starting; special competence testing is organised by provincial human resources departments together with industry regulators. Colleges and technical schools that assess their own students are treated like employer-based assessment bodies, and firms may set their own occupational scope, job grades, job standards and methods.

The ladder runs through eight levels, from apprentice and junior worker (level five) up to senior technician (level one), then technician-in-chief and chief technician, the last two appointed by employers. The draft also proposes two-way equivalence between qualifications, skill grades, professional titles and academic credentials, credit transfer between formal and non-formal learning, and a national qualifications framework. Qualification assessment must keep training and testing apart: an assessing body may not run the courses it tests.

Enforcement is spelled out. Certificates are to be searchable nationwide; providers may not advertise falsely, assess beyond their filed scope, leak exam papers or sell personal data. Operating without filing brings an order to correct and then a fine of 10,000 to 30,000 yuan, or 30,000 to 100,000 in serious cases; breaching provider duties means confiscating the gains plus one to three times their value, rising to two to five times and loss of licence.

Why it mattersThe draft shifts the gatekeeping question from prior approval to filing plus liability, moving regulatory cost from the entrance to the aftermath. For vocational colleges the operative clause is that schools may assess their own students: the right to certify moves onto campus, but assessors, item banks and workshops must be paid for out of training facilities rather than a new earmarked fund. If academic and skill credentials really become comparable, technical-school graduates gain a route into the professional-title system, and one standard argument against enrolling weakens.
Another angleWith 220 million skilled workers, more than 72 million of them in high-skill roles, what a certificate is worth is settled in pay, and the draft only encourages employers to link wages to grade. What decides the training market is whether that encouragement becomes a line in job adverts.

11/12 Ministry of Education school planning centre ↗

Wuhan forum splits universities into three tracks as 13 countries and 16 school-enterprise deals sign up

The 13th International Forum on Industry-Education Integration opened in Wuhan on November 12, according to the Ministry of Education's school planning and development centre. The theme was "strengthening scientific and talent support for a modern industrial system: the mission of universities in a new phase of classified reform". Vice-Minister of Education Du Jiangfeng, Hubei vice-governor Chen Ping and Wuhan mayor Xiong Zhengyu attended the opening.

Du set out three tracks: research universities serving national strategic needs, applied universities serving regional economies, and skills-oriented universities tied closely to the production front line. He also asked institutions to let industry set the teaching agenda and to speed up the digital upgrading of disciplines. The forum materials describe this year as the start of classified reform.

The programme ran an opening session, keynote reports, a dialogue and four sub-forums: classified reform in practice, industry-education-research integration for regional growth, digital and AI-era talent training, and institutional opening in international cooperation. At the opening, the centre and China Great Wall Internet Information Centre released an upgraded China Education Think Tank network, and 16 school-enterprise projects were signed, spanning think-tank services, international cooperation, the digital economy, advanced manufacturing and green chemicals.

Participants came from universities, governments and industry bodies in 13 countries, including the United States, France, Russia, Finland, South Korea, Indonesia and Kazakhstan, along with provincial education departments, the applied universities alliance, colleges, vocational schools and companies; more than 30 media outlets covered the event.

Why it mattersThe lever is not how many labels are handed out but whether funding and evaluation follow them: national projects for research universities, regional industrial contribution for applied ones, job fit for skills-oriented ones. That means three different tables for hiring, research assessment and per-student funding - otherwise the reform is a new plaque on the gate. The 16 signed projects lean towards think-tank work and international cooperation, which is additional resource rather than core teaching money; whether they turn into courses, placements and joint programmes will show up in next year's teaching plans, or not at all.

Schools & Governance

学校与治理
11/12 12:45 China News Weekly (via Shangyou News) ↗

Guizhou county concedes "the punishment was excessive" after a teacher beat an eight-year-old with a ruler

China News Weekly reported that an eight-year-old pupil in Renhuai, in Guizhou's Zunyi city, was beaten with a ruler by her form teacher after forgetting her Chinese textbook, leaving bruises across her body. The family says the child was struck for 20 minutes on November 4, vomited and was taken to hospital.

On November 12 the Renhuai education and sports bureau told the magazine it had opened a review. A county official said the school, the bureau and local police had all intervened, and that the preliminary finding was that "excessive discipline did exist". The teacher, surnamed Wang, who also taught Chinese, took the pupil to an office and used a ruler because she had repeatedly failed to finish homework and had not brought the book.

The office had no camera, the official said, so the details are still being verified and a statement will follow. The child was visited by the school and the bureau and given counselling; the teacher has been suspended pending investigation and will be dealt with according to the findings.

Why it mattersThe rules on school discipline that took effect in March 2021 list permitted measures, from pointed criticism to after-class tutoring, and require proportionality, but say nothing about whether the place where discipline happens has to be recordable. The hard fact in this case is the missing camera: with only two accounts to compare, the school has lost the ability to show it followed procedure. The cheapest fix is not another prohibition but a rule that discipline takes place where a third adult is present or a record exists - and that teachers are told where the line sits.

Vocational & Careers

职教与就业
11/12 19:38 Yangcheng Evening News ↗

Guangdong's 596 vocational schools hold more than three million students, and are still asking who fits where

Guangdong's vocational and technical education is among the largest in China, the Yangcheng Evening News reported on November 12: seven vocational undergraduate universities, more than any other province; 43 technician colleges covering 21 cities; and 596 vocational schools, technical colleges included, with more than three million students, the biggest system in the country. Since China first entered the WorldSkills competition in 2011, Guangdong has taken 48 golds, 16 silvers, 15 bronzes and 28 merit awards, leading the country on both golds and total medals.

The showcase partnership is the "Huixin class" run by Shenzhen Institute of Information Technology and Inovance: courses mapped to the company's product lines, practicals built around real engineering projects, and recruits who work independently within one to two months, against a typical three to six months for other graduates. More than 254 of the institute's students are now in post at Inovance and its suppliers, concentrated in industrial automation commissioning, robot application and maintenance, smart-manufacturing technical support, and industrial control software. Guangzhou City Construction College has moved China Post's 11183 call centre onto campus as a factory-in-school; during the 2025 Singles' Day peak, 69 human-resources students took 36,188 calls and served more than 36,000 customers.

At county level the movement is consolidation. Maoming merged more than 30 secondary vocational schools into 12, directing four colleges and the 12 schools to serve local industries with distinct specialisms; Fogang in Qingyuan has built an industry college with JD.com that trains at least 50 digital-commerce students a year. Guangdong City Technician College uses "micro-majors" with firms including CATL, and 88 students went straight into jobs at its May recruitment fair.

Why it mattersGuangdong has settled supply; the open question is fit. That 254 students sit in four job families shows the order-class model works but is narrow. Maoming's merger shifts programme decisions from what schools happen to have to what counties need, at the cost of redeploying teachers and lengthening pupils' commutes - two costs that never appear in a match-rate table. The 48 golds are an elite metric; they reach ordinary students only when contest standards come back as practical training for everyone.

Technology & Product

技术与产品
11/12 19:35 Shangyou News ↗

Kuaishou's charity publishes 30 AI classroom cases, six in ten from rural schools

Kuaishou's philanthropic arm launched a casebook of AI teaching practice in Beijing on November 12, publishing 30 frontline examples of AI woven into lessons. It also started a co-building programme that will set up one three-way unit - education bureau, school, foundation - in each of 10 counties, with 10 case schools getting a bespoke plan each.

The application numbers show the reach and its limits: about 10,000 schools and 20,000 teachers were aware of the call, which drew 135 valid applications from 21 provinces. Eighty-six of them, more than 60%, came from rural schools. The forms contained 1,390 stated difficulties, 1,227 answers to "what did AI change" and 1,112 to "what did I do with AI"; the practice described adds up to 79,834 days.

The frontline figures are more concrete. A boarding school for ethnic minorities in Wasai township, Jiuzhi county, Golog prefecture, Qinghai, uses AI for bilingual teaching, tiered instruction and after-school support; teachers there say preparation for a single lesson fell from three hours to 40 minutes and marking time by 50%. Fanjia primary in Lizhou district, Guangyuan, Sichuan, used one phone and free tools to run movement analysis and fitness tiering for the whole school, cutting sports injuries by 60% and producing its tracking file in 20 minutes instead of three days. Jiuduhe primary in Beijing's Huairou district built a matrix of agents on a no-code platform: 108 pupils in grades three to six worked asynchronously, 780 progress records were kept for 127 of them, and the year group's average Chinese score rose by about four to five points.

The project says it plans to reach more than 2,000 rural schools, 30,000 teachers and over five million student attendances in three years. A first open class on December 23, 2025 drew 384 teachers and school social workers from 18 provinces.

Why it mattersWhat this kind of programme actually delivers is not a casebook but teacher time. If the Golog numbers - preparation from three hours to 40 minutes, marking halved - reproduce elsewhere, each teacher gets back more than ten hours a week, which is the scarcest resource in a rural school and worth more than another device. But 135 valid applications against roughly 10,000 schools reached suggests very few teachers can yet use these tools well. Whether 2,000 schools happens depends on whether training is delivered county-wide or one showcase school per county gets the support.
